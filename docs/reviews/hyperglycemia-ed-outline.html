Hyperglycemia: ED Treatment & Bridge Care

A dedicated adult ED consult for the patient who needs a safe treatment plan today and cannot readily see primary care. Proposed categories: Emergency Medicine and Nephro/Rheum/Endo. Proposed ID: hyperglycemia-ed; entry: hg-start.

The opening screen

First: crisis, insulin deficiency, or stable hyperglycemia? Screen for dehydration, vomiting, altered mentation, ketosis/acidosis, catabolic symptoms, pregnancy, suspected type 1 diabetes, and pump failure. Route urgent presentations to monitored treatment and the appropriate definitive consult.

After safety screening: New diagnosis / Out of medication / On medication but uncontrolled / Already taking insulin. Each route reaches a specific treatment and follow-up plan.

Adult, nonpregnant bridge care is the main treatment population. Pediatric, pregnant, suspected type 1, and crisis presentations do not receive routine type 2 outpatient dose outputs.

Seven decision modules

ModuleWhat the ED physician gets
1. Safety & crisis exclusionCurrent DKA/HHS criteria, mixed DKA/HHS and euglycemic DKA recognition, type 1/pump interruption assessment, and explicit escalation paths. Link to DKA and pediatric resources. Glucose alone never clears a patient for discharge.
2. Labs & causeBedside glucose and targeted chemistry/renal assessment; when to obtain beta-hydroxybutyrate, VBG, osmolality, HbA1c and pregnancy testing. Infection, ischemia, steroids, medication omission, and new diabetes considered by clinical clues. Clarify confirmatory testing and HbA1c limitations; avoid blanket cultures or imaging.
3. ED treatment & reassessmentVolume status, oral versus IV hydration, electrolyte/renal constraints, reassessment after treatment, and monitoring after insulin. Determine what must be corrected now versus managed longitudinally. No automatic admission or discharge based on one glucose cutoff.
4. Start or adjust noninsulin treatmentFormulation-specific metformin initiation/titration and renal/acute-illness restrictions, distinguishing initiation from continuation at reduced eGFR. Avoid initiation during AKI/dehydration. Compare SGLT2, GLP-1/GIP-based therapy, DPP-4, sulfonylureas and pioglitazone by comorbidity, affordability, contraindications, monitoring and practical ED suitability. Separate long-term preferred therapy from what can safely be started today; SGLT2 initiation is not an acute-illness, ketosis or fasting rescue strategy. Address medication access before calling treatment a failure.
5. When and how to start insulinSymptoms, catabolism, A1c above 10%, or glucose at least 300 mg/dL prompt consideration of insulin, not an automatic prescription or admission. Separate likely insulin deficiency from uncomplicated type 2 diabetes. Distinguish basal-only initiation from total daily basal-bolus dosing. Compare U-100 basal analogs, NPH, rapid analogs, regular insulin and premix, including meal timing, hypoglycemia and affordability tradeoffs. Concentrated products and pump conversions require a separate reviewed plan.
6. Titrate & cover mealsUse fasting versus post-meal patterns and hypoglycemia history to identify which dose needs adjustment. Avoid escalating basal to fix meal excursions. Explain basal-plus versus basal-bolus, prescribed carbohydrate ratios and correction factors, and insulin stacking. Dose reductions and holds take priority when intake or kidney function changes.
7. Disposition & bridge prescriptionA complete plan: named medication/formulation, instructions, monitoring supplies, needles, hypoglycemia rescue, teach-back, sick-day rules, return precautions, and a named follow-up route. Assess food, storage, vision/dexterity, caregiver support, language and ability to obtain medication. If safe treatment cannot be arranged, use case management, observation or admission rather than an unsupported discharge.

Toolbar: depth without a wall of buttons

LabsMedsInsulinMeal + CorrectionDischargeMore

The main buttons open focused tools with tabs. Calculators remain accessible directly from the relevant decision card.

  • Labs: test-selection checklist, DKA/HHS criteria, effective/total osmolality and corrected-sodium references.
  • Meds: start/titrate cards with indication, formulation, renal constraints, maximum labeled dose, adverse effects, sick-day holds and affordability considerations.
  • Insulin: basal starter, basal titration, basal-plus/prandial initiation, and insulin-type comparison. Each dosing pathway names the medication and regimen before accepting weight or glucose logs.
  • Meal + Correction: two visibly separate calculations, then a clinician-reviewed total. Teaching examples and a compact carbohydrate-counting guide using labels and portion sizes.
  • Discharge: editable prescription checklist, supplies and access checks, monitoring/follow-up plan, and patient instructions.
  • More: renal restrictions, sick-day care, hypoglycemia rescue, low-cost alternatives, steroid-related hyperglycemia, crisis links and the consult roadmap.

Meal insulin is not correction insulin

Meal coverage: carbohydrate grams divided by the patient's prescribed insulin-to-carbohydrate ratio.

Correction: glucose above the prescribed target divided by the patient's prescribed insulin-sensitivity factor.

The tool will show each component before considering a combined dose. It must account for recent insulin, planned versus actual intake, hypoglycemia, units and product. It will not infer carbohydrate grams from a meal name or photograph.

Example arithmetic for review: a prescribed ratio of 1 unit per 15 g covers 60 g with 4 units. With glucose 250 mg/dL, prescribed target 150 and sensitivity 50 mg/dL per unit, the arithmetic correction is 2 units. A 6-unit total is only eligible after the safety checks, not an automatic instruction. These are illustrative inputs, not default prescriptions.

Estimated 500/TDD and 1800/TDD values will be clearly separated from established patient ratios and require clinician confirmation. TDD means all basal and bolus insulin over a representative day, not basal insulin alone. Unreliable TDD, unknown recent doses, or a pump-managed dose plan will prevent an automatic combined recommendation.

Calculator safety and verification

  • Specific regimen selectors, locked evidence-based weight factors, appropriate rounding, product/concentration and route displayed. No generic free-form dose/kg calculator as the primary path.
  • Automatic maximums where the chosen regimen has a supported maximum. Insulin has no universal safe maximum: unsupported high-risk results require review, rather than an invented dose ceiling.
  • No automated titration from one isolated ED value. Require sufficient glucose pattern data and assess low readings, missed doses, access, intake, kidney function and previous dose changes.
  • Check realistic and boundary cases: new type 2, suspected type 1, ketosis, renal impairment, missing meals, recent bolus, low glucose, zero/invalid TDD, annual versus daily medication errors, and high-dose/concentrated insulin.
  • Dr. Kitlowski clinical audit of final content and every dose/formula; browser testing of all branches, toolbar tools, phone/tablet layouts and offline loading; full Supabase and cache deployment workflow after approval.

No new clinical images are proposed. The consult can be efficient with structured cards and tables.

Evidence and existing content review

  • ADA Standards of Care 2026: pharmacologic treatment: medication selection and insulin escalation. Some full-text access was blocked during initial review; final dose-level implementation requires direct verification.
  • 2024 hyperglycemic-crisis consensus: crisis diagnosis and definitive-care routing.
  • ADA 2026: hospital care: transitions and discharge support. Hospital dosing protocols will not be presented as interchangeable outpatient protocols.
  • DailyMed metformin ER label: formulation-specific dosing, kidney restrictions and contrast precautions. Every selected drug will get a current product-label check before implementation.
  • AACE comprehensive diabetes guideline: carbohydrate ratios, correction factors and individualized education.
  • Driver et al., ED discharge-glucose randomized trial: selected discharged adults, excluding type 1. This does not establish safety for crisis, insulin deficiency, or patients without a viable discharge plan.

The app's existing Diabetes Management consult will remain available. Relevant cross-links and any shared calculator changes require focused review. Its older wiki labels cannot substitute for current verification; the stored DKA “any 2 of 3” summary is not suitable for reuse.
