Beta-Blocker Overdose: Antidotes Before Pressors

Adults in the ED with suspected or confirmed beta-blocker overdose. Excludes isolated pediatric ingestions (glucose-driven presentation differs), pure calcium-channel-blocker toxicity, and mixed cardiotoxic co-ingestions — each modifies the pathway.

⚠ Cardiac arrest = give everything at once. High-dose epinephrine, glucagon 5-10 mg, calcium chloride 1-3 g, and Intralipid 20%. Beta-blocker arrest is potentially reversible — run CPR 30-60+ min and call ECMO early.
⚠ HIET is NOT DKA dosing. Insulin 1 unit/kg bolus then 1-10 units/kg/hr. You MUST co-administer D10/D25 dextrose and replete potassium aggressively (10-40 mEq/hr) — hypoglycemia is the main risk, keep D50 at the bedside.
⚠ Propranolol widens the QRS. With QRS >100 ms give sodium bicarbonate 1-2 mEq/kg IV push (target pH 7.50-7.55) and treat it like a TCA. Avoid phenytoin for seizures.
⚠ Sotalol prolongs the QT. Give magnesium 2 g IV prophylactically if the QTc is prolonged and use overdrive pacing for Torsades. AVOID amiodarone — it prolongs the QT further.
⚠ Glucagon is first-line. 3-10 mg IV bolus, then 2-10 mg/hr infusion — it bypasses the blocked beta receptor via adenylate cyclase.
⚠ Calcium supports contractility. Calcium chloride 1 g IV or calcium gluconate 3 g IV, given simultaneously with glucagon.

Cardiac arrest — give ALL simultaneously
High-dose epinephrine (standard 1 mg may be inadequate when beta-blocked) · glucagon 5-10 mg IV · calcium chloride 1-3 g IV · Intralipid 20% 1.5 mL/kg bolus, may repeat. Run CPR 30-60+ min — this arrest is reversible — and call ECMO early.[1]
Refractory shock — escalate the ladder
Add HIGH-DOSE norepinephrine (0.5-3 mcg/kg/min, far above sepsis dosing), then Intralipid 20% for lipophilic agents, then vasopressin 0.04 units/min. If still failing → VA-ECMO. Avoid dobutamine (beta-blocked) and dopamine (less effective).[2]
Propranolol + wide QRS — sodium bicarbonate
QRS >100 ms → sodium bicarbonate 1-2 mEq/kg IV push, repeat q3-5 min until the QRS narrows, target pH 7.50-7.55 (same as TCA). For seizures give lorazepam — avoid phenytoin.[1]
Sotalol + long QT — magnesium
Magnesium sulfate 2 g IV prophylactically if the QTc is prolonged; for Torsades add overdrive pacing (rate 100-120). Avoid amiodarone. Observe 24-48 h for delayed arrhythmias.[1]
Standard toxic patient — antidote sequence
Atropine (one try) → glucagon 3-10 mg bolus + infusion → calcium → HIET 1 unit/kg then 1-10 units/kg/hr. Add high-dose pressors as a bridge while HIET takes effect.[4]

Critical pitfalls

Do NOT treat HIET like DKA. Doses are 10-100× higher — insulin 1 unit/kg then 1-10 units/kg/hr with dextrose and K⁺.

Do NOT stop resuscitating early. Beta-blocker arrest is reversible — CPR 30-60+ min and call ECMO.

Do NOT give amiodarone in sotalol toxicity — it prolongs the QT further. Use magnesium + overdrive pacing.

Do NOT discharge an extended-release ingestion early — delayed, prolonged toxicity needs 24-48 h; psychiatric clearance is mandatory for intentional ingestions. Poison Control 1-800-222-1222.
