Thromboelastography (TEG) — Goal-Directed Trauma Transfusion

Bleeding trauma patients in the ED/trauma bay whose transfusion is being guided by the TEG 6s trauma-cartridge assay. Assay-specific: rTEG and citrated-kaolin TEG 5000 use different cutoffs and are out of scope. PlateletMapping content covers elective/peri-procedural antiplatelet assessment only, not the acute bleed.

⚠ Assay-labeling is mandatory: TEG 6s, rTEG, and CK-TEG 5000 cutoffs are NOT interchangeable — this spec uses TEG 6s trauma-cartridge thresholds only.
⚠ Viscoelastic-guided transfusion in trauma is a conditional recommendation on VERY LOW quality evidence (EAST 2020); the largest RCT (ITACTIC 2021) was negative for its primary outcome — use TEG to support judgment, not replace it.
⚠ Correct fibrinogen (low CFF-MA) BEFORE attributing a low CRT-MA to platelets — cryoprecipitate also raises the CRT-MA.
⚠ Standard TEG is thrombin-driven and cannot see aspirin/P2Y12 antiplatelet effect — a normal CRT-MA does not exclude an antiplatelet effect.
⚠ The TEG sample is warmed to 37°C — this masks the patient's true hypothermia-driven coagulopathy.
⚠ TXA given >3 h from injury may increase bleeding death (CRASH-2) — timing changes the recommendation, do not give reflexively.

When to use TEG

TEG measures real-time clot formation → strengthening → lysis in whole blood, mapping the cell-based coagulation model at the point of care. Early values return in ~5–10 min — fast enough to direct goal-directed component therapy instead of fixed-ratio resuscitation.

See TEG Basics: When to Use & Limits, The 4 Trauma Parameters, Treatment Ladder, and the Do NOT list.

Assay matters
Cutoffs differ between TEG 6s (cartridge), rapid-TEG (rTEG), and citrated-kaolin TEG 5000. This spec uses TEG 6s trauma-cartridge thresholds only — do not apply them to another analyzer.
Evidence quality
Viscoelastic-guided transfusion in trauma is a conditional recommendation on VERY LOW quality evidence (EAST 2020); the largest RCT (ITACTIC 2021) was negative for its primary outcome. Use TEG to *support* judgment, not replace it.

Low CFF-MA → replace fibrinogen (FIRST)

A low CFF-MA (<14 mm) means inadequate fibrinogen contribution to the clot. Functional fibrinogen is the first deficit to correct — a low overall clot strength (CRT-MA) is frequently driven by low fibrinogen, and cryo also raises the CRT-MA.

Target: fibrinogen >150 mg/dL (>200 mg/dL if TBI/CNS injury).

IV

Low CRT-MA → platelets (after fibrinogen is adequate)

A low CRT-MA (<52 mm) reflects weak overall clot strength. The MA is ~80% platelets, ~20% fibrinogen — so confirm CFF-MA is adequate (≥15 mm) first. If fibrinogen is low, correct it and recheck before giving platelets.

Fibrinolysis phenotype & the TXA decision

Post-injury fibrinolysis is a spectrum with U-shaped mortality — both extremes are worse than physiologic. Cut-points are from the Denver rapid-TEG cohort (Moore 2016, n=2,540). On the TEG 6s trauma cartridge, lysis >2.6% exceeds the upper limit of normal and should be treated as a fibrinolysis signal.

Shutdown caveat
The idea that TXA *harms* shutdown patients is the Denver hypothesis — contested by CRASH-2/CRASH-3/WOMAN. Do not withhold TXA from a bleeding patient on LY30 alone; decide on the whole picture and timing.

Heparin effect — CK-R vs CKH-R

The TEG 6s runs a heparin-neutralized channel (CKH, kaolin + heparinase) alongside the standard CK channel. Comparing the two isolates a heparin effect.

CK-R prolonged + CKH-R normal (CK-R > ~1.5× CKH-R, or Δ >2 min) → circulating heparin.
Both prolonged → factor deficiency/anticoagulant, not heparin → give plasma, not protamine.

Protamine
25–50 mg IV, once, titrate
IV
Reverses unfractionated heparin; partial for LMWH. Only if CKH-R corrects the CK-R prolongation.
Cardiac-derived, not trauma-validated
The R-vs-heparinase → protamine logic is derived from cardiac surgery / ICU practice, not validated in trauma. Use clinical judgment.

PlateletMapping — antiplatelet assessment

PlateletMapping quantifies platelet inhibition in the aspirin (AA / thromboxane) and P2Y12 (ADP) pathways. Standard TEG is thrombin-driven and overrides platelet inhibition, so PlateletMapping is required to see antiplatelet effect.

Channels: MA-Thrombin (max function) · MA-ActF (fibrin-only floor) · MA-AA (aspirin) · MA-ADP (P2Y12).

Thresholds:
• Aspirin effect: AA %inhibition ≥50% (or AA-MA <51 mm)
• P2Y12 effect: MA-ADP <~47–50 mm

Use
Elective / peri-procedural antiplatelet assessment, clopidogrel-resistance, reversal decisions. NOT for the acute bleed (slower turnaround; large treated/untreated overlap).

Normal TEG but still bleeding

A normal TEG in a bleeding patient is a meaningful result: the clotting machinery is working. The bleeding is usually anatomic/surgical, or from something TEG cannot see.

  • Surgical / structural bleed — TEG cannot detect anatomic bleeding. Pursue source control and transfuse 1:1:1.
  • Aspirin / P2Y12 platelet inhibition (thrombin-driven assay overrides it) — consider PlateletMapping.
  • Von Willebrand disease (high-shear vWF adhesion not captured).
  • Hypothermia — sample warmed to 37°C, masking true cold coagulopathy. Actively rewarm.
  • Acidosis & hypocalcemia — correct ionized calcium and pH; both impair hemostasis independent of the TEG.

When to use TEG

Cutoffs differ between TEG 6s (cartridge), rapid-TEG (rTEG), and citrated-kaolin TEG 5000. This spec uses TEG 6s trauma-cartridge thresholds only — do not apply them to another analyzer.

Viscoelastic-guided transfusion in trauma is a conditional recommendation on VERY LOW quality evidence (EAST 2020); the largest RCT (ITACTIC 2021) was negative for its primary outcome. Use TEG to *support* judgment, not replace it.

Read the tracing — 4 trauma parameters

Read the live tracing — you can usually call a weak clot by ~10 min, before the full ~50-min trace completes. Tap the abnormal parameter to get its targeted treatment.

Parameter | Normal (TEG 6s) | Abnormal | Reflects | Action

CK-R | 4.6–9.1 min | >10 min | Clotting factors | Plasma or 4F-PCC

CFF-MA | 15–32 mm | <14 mm | Fibrinogen | Cryoprecipitate FIRST

CRT-MA | 52–70 mm | <52 mm (esp. <45) | Overall strength (platelets + fibrinogen) | Platelets, after fibrinogen

CK-LY30 | 0–2.6% | >2.6% | Fibrinolysis | TXA

Prolonged CK-R → replace clotting factors

A prolonged CK-R (>10 min) means delayed clot initiation — factor deficiency or a circulating anticoagulant. If on heparin, check the heparin effect (CK-R vs CKH-R) first → protamine, not plasma.

Plasma (FFP): 15–30 mL/kg IV bolus, reassess IV — ~4 units in a 70 kg adult. Each unit (~250 mL) raises factors only ~2.5%.

4-Factor PCC: 25–50 IU/kg IV, once (max 5000 IU) IV — Off-label in trauma. Low-volume alternative to plasma; thrombotic risk.

Repeat TEG (CK-R) in 30–60 min. Maintain temperature, ionized calcium, and pH. Watch for TACO/TRALI with plasma. Reassessment targets.

Low CFF-MA → replace fibrinogen (FIRST)

A low CFF-MA (<14 mm) means inadequate fibrinogen contribution to the clot. Functional fibrinogen is the first deficit to correct — a low overall clot strength (CRT-MA) is frequently driven by low fibrinogen, and cryo also raises the CRT-MA.

Target: fibrinogen >150 mg/dL (>200 mg/dL if TBI/CNS injury).

Cryoprecipitate: 10 units (≈6 g fibrinogen), bolus and reassess IV — Or fibrinogen concentrate 4–6 g. Raises fibrinogen ~50–100 mg/dL per 10 U. Target CFF-MA ≥15 mm / fibrinogen >150 mg/dL.

Repeat TEG (CFF-MA) and/or quantitative fibrinogen in 30–60 min. Correct fibrinogen BEFORE attributing a low CRT-MA to platelets.

Low CRT-MA → platelets (after fibrinogen is adequate)

A low CRT-MA (<52 mm) reflects weak overall clot strength. The MA is ~80% platelets, ~20% fibrinogen — so confirm CFF-MA is adequate (≥15 mm) first. If fibrinogen is low, correct it and recheck before giving platelets.

Platelets (apheresis): 1–2 units, bolus and reassess IV — CRT-MA <45 mm → 1–2 units; 45–50 mm → 1 unit. Only after CFF-MA ≥15 mm.

Standard TEG is thrombin-driven and cannot see aspirin/P2Y12 inhibition — a normal CRT-MA does not exclude an antiplatelet effect. If the patient is on antiplatelets and bleeding, consider PlateletMapping and empiric platelets.

Elevated CK-LY30 → antifibrinolytic (TXA)

An elevated CK-LY30 (>2.6%) signals hyperfibrinolysis — the clot is being broken down faster than it forms.
