Postpartum Hemorrhage: Stop the Bleeding

Postpartum patients within 24 h of delivery (primary PPH) in the ED or L&D. Excludes secondary/late PPH (>24 h to 12 weeks). Does not replace immediate OB and anesthesia consultation.

⚠ Call for help early — OB, anesthesia, blood bank, charge nurse. Mortality triples when diagnosis or escalation is delayed.
⚠ Never give oxytocin or TXA by IV push. IV-push oxytocin causes profound hypotension and can trigger cardiac arrest.
⚠ Methylergonovine is contraindicated in HTN / preeclampsia / CAD; carboprost is contraindicated in asthma. Screen before every dose.
⚠ Uterine inversion: STOP all uterotonics first, replace the fundus (Johnson maneuver), then resume — a contracted cervical ring blocks replacement.
⚠ If Shock Index >1.0 or EBL ≥1500 mL: activate the Massive Transfusion Protocol and give emergency-release O-negative blood — do not wait for crossmatch.

ACOG / reVITALize (2017): Cumulative blood loss ≥1000 mL OR blood loss with signs of hypovolemia within 24 h of birth — any delivery route. Visual EBL underestimates true loss by 30–50%.[1]

Massive Transfusion Protocol
Transfuse 1:1:1 pRBC : FFP : platelets (or closest institutional equivalent). Cryoprecipitate 10 units if fibrinogen <200 mg/dL. Give TXA, correct pH, and treat the lethal triad — hypothermia, acidosis, coagulopathy.[18]

7 · Disposition, Rh & pitfalls

Disposition
ICU if ≥4 units transfused, hysterectomy, hemodynamic instability, coagulopathy, or amniotic fluid embolism. OB step-down if bleeding controlled, stable vitals, and <4 units transfused. Monitor tone and vitals intensively for 4–24 h — re-bleeding is common.[1]

Rh-negative mother with an Rh-positive or unknown newborn: give Rh Immune Globulin 300 mcg IM within 72 hours of delivery; larger doses if a large fetomaternal hemorrhage is suspected (Kleihauer-Betke guides dosing).[1]
