Digoxin Toxicity: Potassium Drives DigiFab

Adults in the ED with suspected acute or chronic digoxin toxicity. Excludes pediatric accidental ingestion dosing nuances, pregnancy, and plant/animal cardiac-glycoside exposures (oleander, toad venom) — DigiFab still applies but dosing differs.

⚠ K+ >5.5 mEq/L in digoxin toxicity = DigiFab indicated immediately
⚠ Avoid cardioversion if possible - can precipitate refractory VF. Use lowest effective energy if absolutely necessary
⚠ Magnesium sulfate 2g IV over 2-5 min first-line for ventricular arrhythmias
⚠ DigiFab dosing: 10-20 vials empiric (acute) or 3-6 vials (chronic), or calculate based on ingestion/level
⚠ Acute ingestion with normal K+ can appear deceptively well before cardiovascular collapse
⚠ PAT with block and bidirectional VT are pathognomonic for digoxin toxicity

1 · Recognize & classify

Narrow therapeutic index — the classic triad
Digoxin inhibits Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase → intracellular Ca²⁺ rises (automaticity) and vagal tone climbs (AV block). GI (nausea, vomiting, anorexia — earliest) + visual (yellow-green halos / xanthopsia) + CNS/cardiac (confusion, arrhythmia). Toxicity can occur at therapeutic levels in chronic use.[1][2]

Acute phenotype
Acute patients may feel fine despite lethal levels. Peak effect is 6–12 h post-ingestion. A normal K⁺ now does not mean safe — watch for sudden cardiovascular collapse.[2][4]
Chronic phenotype
Insidious onset on maintenance therapy. Drivers: renal insufficiency, age, hypokalemia, hypomagnesemia, hypercalcemia, hypothyroidism, and interacting drugs (amiodarone, verapamil, quinidine, macrolides, azoles).[2][4]

2 · ECG — "dig can do anything"

Effect ≠ toxicity
Digoxin effect: "scooped" ST depression (Salvador Dali mustache), shortened QT — this is NOT toxicity. Toxicity: any brady- OR tachyarrhythmia.[1][2]

3 · Risk stratification — potassium drives DigiFab

Enter Serum K⁺ above. Hyperkalemia is the single most important acute prognostic marker — a K⁺ over 5.5 mEq/L in a dig-toxic patient means DigiFab now.[3][4]

DO THIS NOW — K⁺ > 5.5 mEq/L
Give DigiFab immediately — it restores pump function and lowers K⁺ at the source. Pre-DigiFab mortality with K⁺ >5.5 was >50%. Add sodium bicarbonate, insulin/glucose, and albuterol to shift K⁺. Reserve IV calcium for life-threatening hyperkalemia only when DigiFab is delayed.[3][4][5]
DO THIS NOW — K⁺ 5.0–5.5 mEq/L
Borderline. Recheck K⁺, prepare DigiFab, and treat any arrhythmia. Do NOT push K⁺ up — and do not overcorrect a low K⁺.[3]
DO THIS NOW — K⁺ ≤ 5.0 mEq/L
Proceed with supportive care and arrhythmia management. Correct hypokalemia cautiously to low-normal (3.5–4.0 mEq/L) — overcorrection causes rebound hyperkalemia as digoxin redistributes.[2]

5 · DigiFab dosing — three methods

Indications for DigiFab
Life-threatening arrhythmia (VT/VF, high-grade AV block, atropine-refractory bradycardia) · hemodynamic instability · K⁺ >5.5 mEq/L · acute level >10 ng/mL · chronic level >6 ng/mL with symptoms.[2][3]
Method 1 — known serum level
6 vials
IV over 30 min
Vials = (level ng/mL × weight kg) ÷ 100, rounded up. Enter level + weight above. Example: 8 ng/mL × 70 kg ÷ 100 = 5.6 → 6 vials.
Method 2 — known amount ingested
16 vials
IV over 30 min
Vials = (mg ingested × 0.8) ÷ 0.5, rounded up. Enter amount ingested above. Example: forty 0.25 mg tablets = 10 mg → 16 vials.
Method 3 — empiric (acute)
10–20 vials initially. Use the high end (20 vials) for cardiac arrest or massive ingestion. Round up in severe toxicity — better to over-treat.[3]
Method 3 — empiric (chronic)
3–6 vials initially — smaller total body load. Start low and redose in 1–2 h; rapid full reversal can unmask AF with rapid ventricular response or heart-failure exacerbation.[3]
  1. Reconstitute each vial with 4 mL sterile water; dilute in 100–250 mL NS.
  2. Infuse IV over 30 minutes (IV push if cardiac arrest).
  3. Response in 30–60 min; full effect up to 4 h. Reassess and redose at 1–2 h if inadequate.
  4. Do NOT follow digoxin levels post-Fab — assays measure bound + free and read falsely elevated for days. Follow the clinical response.
Dialysis does not work
Digoxin has a large volume of distribution (5–7 L/kg); hemodialysis removes negligible drug and does not enhance Fab clearance.[2]

Critical pitfalls

**Do NOT wait on hyperkalemia.** K⁺ >5.5 in a dig-toxic patient is a DigiFab indication by itself.

**Do NOT cardiovert electively.** It can precipitate refractory VF — use the lowest effective energy only if unavoidable.

**Do NOT overcorrect hypokalemia.** Target low-normal (3.5–4.0 mEq/L); overshoot causes rebound hyperkalemia as digoxin redistributes.

**Do NOT trust a well-appearing acute patient.** Normal K⁺ can precede sudden collapse.

**Do NOT follow digoxin levels post-Fab** and **do NOT dialyze** for digoxin removal — both are useless.
