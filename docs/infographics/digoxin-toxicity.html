Digoxin Toxicity: Recognize, Risk-Stratify, Reverse

Adults with suspected or confirmed digoxin toxicity (acute ingestion or chronic accumulation) in the ED or ICU. Excludes pediatric ingestions and pure plant-glycoside poisoning (oleander, foxglove) — same antidote, different ingestion kinetics.

⚠ K+ >5.5 mEq/L in digoxin toxicity = DigiFab indicated immediately. Pre-DigiFab mortality with K+ >5.5 was >50%.
⚠ Avoid cardioversion if possible — can precipitate refractory ventricular fibrillation in the digitalis-toxic heart. If absolutely necessary, use the lowest effective energy and have DigiFab ready.
⚠ Do not withhold calcium if hyperkalemia is immediately life-threatening (peaked T waves progressing to widening QRS) and DigiFab is delayed or unavailable — give a standard dose slowly. Otherwise DigiFab corrects the hyperkalemia at its source and calcium is unnecessary.
⚠ Correct hypokalemia cautiously — overshooting to normokalemia risks rebound hyperkalemia as digoxin redistributes off the Na+/K+-ATPase.
⚠ Digoxin levels are meaningless after DigiFab — standard assays measure total (bound + free) digoxin and will read falsely elevated for days. Follow clinical response, not levels.
⚠ Hemodialysis is NOT effective — digoxin has a large volume of distribution (5-7 L/kg).
⚠ Acute ingestion can look deceptively well before sudden cardiovascular collapse, even with a lethal level.

Recognition

Digoxin has a narrow therapeutic index — toxicity can occur at therapeutic levels, especially in chronic use with predisposing factors [1][2]. It inhibits Na+/K+-ATPase, raising intracellular Ca²⁺ and increasing vagal tone — the result is that any arrhythmia can occur ("dig can do anything").

Classic triad
GI: nausea, vomiting, anorexia (often earliest). Visual: yellow-green halos (xanthopsia), blurred vision, photophobia. CNS: confusion, fatigue, weakness, delirium.

Risk factorsIntentional/accidental ingestionRenal insufficiency, age, hypoK/hypoMg, hyperCa, hypothyroid, drug interactions (amiodarone, verapamil, quinidine, macrolides, azoles)
ECG: effect vs toxicity
Digoxin effect (therapeutic): scooped ST depression ("Salvador Dalí mustache"), shortened QT — NOT toxicity.

Toxicity arrhythmias: PAT with block (pathognomonic), bidirectional VT (pathognomonic), accelerated junctional rhythm, sinus/junctional bradycardia, AV blocks, ventricular ectopy. Regularization of atrial fibrillation in a digitalized patient suggests toxicity.

Treatments to Avoid

Cardioversion
Can precipitate refractory ventricular fibrillation in the digitalis-toxic heart. If absolutely necessary (unstable VT/VF), use the lowest effective energy and have DigiFab ready [2].
Calcium — the “stone heart” question
The traditional teaching that IV calcium causes irreversible “stone heart” rests on a handful of 1930s-50s case reports; a retrospective cohort and porcine model found no association with death or malignant arrhythmia [5][4]. The reason to avoid routine calcium is lack of proven benefit — DigiFab corrects hyperkalemia at its source — not proven harm. Do not withhold calcium if hyperkalemia is immediately life-threatening and DigiFab is delayed or unavailable.
Temporary pacing
Can trigger VF in sensitized myocardium. If needed, transvenous is preferred over transcutaneous, using the lowest capture threshold; ideally defer until after DigiFab [2].
Beta-blockers and calcium channel blockers
Worsen bradycardia and AV block in digoxin toxicity — hold these medications [2].

Tachyarrhythmia → magnesium, avoid cardioversion — Magnesium sulfate 2 g IV first-line for ventricular arrhythmias. Avoid cardioversion — can precipitate refractory VF; use lowest effective energy if unavoidable.

Potassium level? — K+ >5.5 mEq/L indicates severe Na+/K+-ATPase poisoning and is a critical finding — DigiFab is indicated immediately.

DigiFab indicated? — Indications: life-threatening arrhythmia, hemodynamic instability, K+ >5.5, acute level >10 ng/mL, or chronic level >6 ng/mL with symptoms.

DigiFab indicated — Onset of effect 30-60 minutes; full clinical response may take up to 4 hours.

DigiFab dosing method? — Choose based on what is known: ingested amount, serum level, or neither (empiric).

Known ingestion → formula dose — Vials = (mg ingested × 0.8) / 0.5. Round up in severe toxicity.

Known level → formula dose — Vials = (level ng/mL × weight kg) / 100. Level must be ≥6h post-ingestion for accuracy.

Empiric dosing — Acute: 10-20 vials. Chronic: 3-6 vials, start low and redose PRN at 1-2 hours.

Post-DigiFab management — Response in 30-60 min, full effect up to 4h. Half-life 15-20h. May repeat in 1-2h if inadequate response. Digoxin levels are meaningless post-Fab.

Disposition? — Dialysis is NOT effective — digoxin has a large volume of distribution (5-7 L/kg).

ICU admission — Any significant arrhythmia, DigiFab given, hemodynamic compromise, or K+ >5.5. Continuous telemetry ≥24h after last arrhythmia.

Telemetry / step-down — Mild symptoms, stable, normal K+, no significant arrhythmia. Continuous telemetry 12-24h; escalate to ICU for any new arrhythmia.

Arrhythmia & Supportive Treatment

Bradyarrhythmias result from enhanced vagal tone and AV nodal suppression [2].

Atropine: 0.5-1 mg IV, may repeat q3-5 min (max 3 mg) — Temporizing measure. May be ineffective in severe toxicity — prepare DigiFab.

Tachyarrhythmias result from enhanced automaticity from intracellular calcium overload [2]. Avoid cardioversion if possible — can precipitate refractory VF.

Magnesium Sulfate: 2 g IV over 2-5 min IV, once, may repeat — First-line for ventricular arrhythmias in digoxin toxicity.

Lidocaine: 1.5 mg/kg IV push, then 1-4 mg/min infusion — Class IB — does not worsen AV conduction. Use with or after magnesium.

For mild toxicity: GI decontamination, cautious electrolyte correction, hold digoxin and other AV-nodal blockers [2].

Activated Charcoal: 1 g/kg PO, within 1-2 hours of ingestion — Max 50 g. Enterohepatic circulation makes multidose potentially beneficial in severe cases.
