Suicidal Patient: Screen, C-SSRS Stratify, Restrict Means, Safety-Plan, Disposition

Adults and adolescents (ASQ validated ages 10+) presenting to the ED who screen positive for suicidal ideation or present after a suicide attempt. Excludes patients whose only concern is unrelated psychiatric crisis with no suicidal ideation, and excludes formal inpatient psychiatric treatment planning beyond ED disposition.

⚠ Immediate safety comes before any interview. 1:1 constant visual observation, remove all ligatures/sharps/medications from the room, secure windows and exits, patient in gown. Do NOT leave the patient alone — even for tests or imaging.
⚠ High risk — active ideation WITH method: continue 1:1 observation, obtain psychiatric consultation, address means restriction, complete a safety plan. Do NOT discharge without a psychiatry evaluation, a documented safety plan, means restriction addressed, and follow-up within 24-72 hours.
⚠ Imminent risk — intent, plan, or a recent attempt (within 3 months): highest-risk tier, requires admission. STAT psychiatry consult, continuous 1:1 observation, search for concealed means. Voluntary psychiatric admission is preferred; use an involuntary hold only if the patient refuses AND meets criteria. Do NOT discharge.
⚠ Involuntary hold: state-specific criteria generally require danger to self due to mental illness, inability to care for self, and refusal of voluntary treatment. Document specific risk behaviors/statements, the mental illness diagnosed or suspected, and less-restrictive alternatives considered. The hold is time-limited, typically 72 hours, and the patient retains the right to a hearing/review and legal representation.

1 · Universal screening & immediate safety

Joint Commission NPSG 15.01.01 requires screening ALL patients with a validated tool. 50% of suicide decedents seen in healthcare within 30 days of death, and 80% deny suicidal ideation at last visit — universal screening detects at-risk patients who would otherwise be missed.

Immediate safety — do FIRST
1:1 constant visual observation; remove ligatures (clothing, sheets, cords), sharps (needles, razors, glass), medications (all, including the patient's own), and plastic bags from the room; secure windows and exits; patient in gown (remove belt, shoelaces); metal detector wand if available. Do NOT leave the patient alone — even for tests or imaging.

ASQ (Ask Suicide-Screening Questions)4 questions, <2 min, ages 10+
C-SSRS (Columbia Protocol)6 questions, more detailed
PHQ-9 Item 9 positiveAlready screened via PHQ-9 — route to C-SSRS
  1. In the past few weeks, have you wished you were dead?
  2. In the past few weeks, have you felt that you or your family would be better off if you were dead?
  3. In the past week, have you been having thoughts about killing yourself?
  4. Have you ever tried to kill yourself?
  5. If ALL NO — ask the acuity question: "Are you having thoughts of killing yourself right now?"

Assess in order — stop at first NO. Also assess preparatory behaviors (giving away possessions, writing notes), lifetime history of attempts, and a recent attempt within the past 3 months.

3 · What to do now — risk tier

Low acute risk — low risk does NOT mean no risk
Protective factors to assess: strong social support, engaged in treatment, problem-solving skills, reasons for living, children at home, religious/spiritual beliefs. Still required: brief counseling on warning signs, crisis resources (988 Lifeline), and a scheduled follow-up appointment.
Moderate risk — passive or non-specific active ideation
Elevating factors: recent loss, social isolation, substance use, prior attempts (strongest predictor), family history of suicide, access to lethal means, hopelessness, impulsivity. Protective factors: engaged in treatment, future orientation, social support, reasons for living.

Imminent risk — intent, plan, or recent attempt

4 · Means restriction counseling

Lethal means restriction saves lives. 90% who survive an attempt do NOT die by suicide later — reducing access to lethal means prevents impulsive deaths. CALM approach: Clarify if means are accessible, Ask about willingness to secure, Listen to barriers, Make a plan together.

MethodCase fatality
Firearms~90% case fatality
Drowning56%
Hanging/suffocation53%
Poisoning/overdose~2%

Firearms account for 50% of US suicide deaths. Ask directly: "Do you have access to any guns?" Counsel non-judgmentally: many people in crisis find it helpful to temporarily store firearms outside the home — trusted friend/family, gun shop or shooting range, law enforcement (some jurisdictions), or a gun safe with a trusted keyholder. Document counseling provided and the patient's response.

Opioids

TCAs10-day supply can be lethal

Counseling: limit quantities prescribed, lock up or store with a trusted person, consider dispensing weekly, naloxone for opioid-prescribed patients. If discharging a patient at risk: prescribe minimal quantities (7-day supply) and avoid high-lethality medications if alternatives exist.

5 · Safety planning intervention

  1. Warning signs — what tells you crisis is building?
  2. Internal coping — what can you do alone? (walk, music, breathing)
  3. Social contacts — who can distract you? (friends, places)
  4. Family/friends to contact — who can you call for help?
  5. Professionals to contact — therapist, doctor, crisis line
  6. Making environment safe — means restriction plan
  1. "What thoughts, feelings, or situations tell you that a crisis may be developing?"
  2. "What are things you can do on your own to take your mind off problems?"
  3. "What places can you go or people you can be around that provide distraction?"
  4. "Who are family or friends you can contact when you need help?"
  5. Professionals: therapist/counselor, prescriber, 988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline, local crisis line
  6. "How will we make your environment safer?"

Collaborative process — the patient completes the plan with the provider, writes it down, and keeps a copy. Review it with the patient before discharge.

Discharge with safety plan
Before discharge, ensure: safety plan completed and reviewed; means restriction addressed and documented; follow-up appointment within 24-72 hours; crisis resources provided (988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline, 741741 Crisis Text Line — text HOME, local crisis number); family/support notified with patient consent; prescriptions limited to safe quantities. Caring Contacts: a brief follow-up call/text within 24-48 hours reduces suicide attempts.
Voluntary psychiatric admission
Explain the treatment plan and expected duration, complete medical clearance per facility requirements, transfer to a psychiatric facility or unit, document the patient's voluntary agreement, notify family with consent. Preferred over involuntary admission when the patient is willing — better therapeutic alliance.
Involuntary hold
State-specific criteria — generally: danger to self due to mental illness, unable to care for self, refuses voluntary treatment. Documentation required: specific behaviors/statements demonstrating risk, mental illness diagnosed or suspected, less restrictive alternatives considered, time-limited hold (typically 72 hours). Patient rights: right to hearing/review, right to legal representation, right to communicate with an attorney. Complete the state-specific form (e.g., 5150, M-1, Baker Act).
Medical admission
Stabilize the medical condition first, continue 1:1 sitter observation, psychiatry consult for ongoing assessment, transfer to psychiatry when medically clear. Common post-attempt presentations: overdose requiring monitoring/treatment, self-inflicted wounds requiring surgery, aspiration pneumonia, rhabdomyolysis.
Crisis stabilization unit
Alternative to inpatient admission if available. Criteria: moderate-high risk but not imminent, voluntary participation, no acute medical needs, benefit from brief stabilization (24-72h). Provides crisis intervention, safety monitoring, brief therapy, connection to outpatient resources. Advantages: less restrictive, shorter stay, maintains community connections.
