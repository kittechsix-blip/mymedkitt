Pulmonary Embolism in Pregnancy — ED Management

Pregnant or ≤6-week-postpartum patients in the ED with suspected or confirmed pulmonary embolism. Excludes non-pregnant adults and pediatrics. Hemodynamically unstable (massive) PE routes straight to resuscitation + thrombolysis consideration, bypassing confirmatory imaging.

⚠ Massive (unstable) PE: UFH bolus 80 units/kg then 18 units/kg/hr IV infusion — UFH preferred over LMWH in massive PE (short half-life, reversible with protamine).
⚠ DOACs are CONTRAINDICATED in pregnancy — cross the placenta, teratogenic. Warfarin is CONTRAINDICATED in the 1st trimester — teratogenic (warfarin embryopathy).
⚠ Thrombolysis is NOT contraindicated in pregnancy for massive PE — maternal death from untreated massive PE is 25-65%; tPA does not cross the placenta.
⚠ Post-thrombolysis: hold anticoagulation 2-4h then restart UFH — do not resume anticoagulation immediately after alteplase.

Recognition

PE is a leading cause of maternal mortality — responsible for 10% of maternal deaths in developed countries. Pregnancy is a hypercoagulable state: 5-10x increased VTE risk vs. non-pregnant, with risk persisting 6 weeks postpartum and peaking in the 3rd trimester and postpartum.

Symptoms overlap normal pregnancy (dyspnea, edema, tachycardia) and D-dimer is normally elevated. Missing PE is more dangerous than diagnostic radiation.

  • Hypotension (SBP <90 mmHg)
  • Syncope
  • Severe hypoxia
  • RV dysfunction on POCUS
  • Cardiac arrest

Diagnostic workup

Pregnancy-adapted YEARS Algorithm. Criteria: clinical signs of DVT, hemoptysis, PE most likely diagnosis. 0 criteria + D-dimer <1000 → PE excluded; 1+ criteria + D-dimer <500 → PE excluded; otherwise imaging is required. D-dimer normally rises throughout pregnancy — YEARS uses fixed thresholds, not trimester-adjusted cutoffs.

ModalityFetal doseNotes
CTPA0.1 mGyHigher sensitivity, breast dose ~3-10 mGy, preferred in most centers
V/Q scan0.5 mGyLower breast radiation than CTPA, more non-diagnostic results (25-50%)
Teratogenic threshold50-100 mGyBoth modalities far below this threshold

V/Q result interpretation: normal = PE excluded; high probability = treat as PE; non-diagnostic = need additional testing. Ventilation phase can be omitted (perfusion-only) to reduce radiation further.

If DVT symptoms are present, starting with leg ultrasound avoids radiation entirely — roughly 30-50% of PE patients have concurrent DVT. A negative leg US does NOT rule out PE.

Options: serial leg ultrasounds (days 1, 3, 7); alternative imaging (V/Q if CTPA inconclusive, or vice versa); empiric anticoagulation if clinical probability is high; repeat CTPA with optimized protocol.
