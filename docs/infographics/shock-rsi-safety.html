RSI

Physiologically Difficult Airway

Before, during, and after the tube

Paralysis removes compensation. Fix shock, oxygenation, acidosis, RV strain, hyperkalemia, and cerebral perfusion before induction whenever time allows.
1

Before induction

Build reserve before you remove it.

Shock
Support MAP before the push. Treat the shock phenotype, confirm reliable access, and have the indicated vasopressor infusion running. Do not give a routine fluid bolus unless fluid responsiveness or hypovolemia is likely.
Oxygen
Head-up preoxygenation. Use NIPPV for severe hypoxemia; use medication-assisted preoxygenation if agitation prevents an effective mask seal. Prepare two-person BVM, PEEP valve, suction, and rescue airway.
Acidosis
Record the compensation. Note respiratory rate, tidal volume, ETCO2, PaCO2, and HCO3. Avoid intubation when the patient is still ventilating adequately and the cause can be rapidly reversed.
Brain + K
Prevent the second hit. In TBI, avoid hypoxemia and hypotension. Check potassium and screen for burn, denervation, major trauma, upper motor neuron injury, immobility, or myopathy before choosing succinylcholine.
Push-dose epinephrine bridge 5-20 mcg IV 10 mcg/mL concentration = 0.5-2 mL. Prefer a pharmacy-prepared or prefilled syringe. If bedside dilution is allowed, independently verify the stock concentration and final label. Bridge only, not a substitute for an infusion. Never give the 1 mg arrest dose to a patient with a pulse.
Evidence limit SCCM found insufficient evidence that peri-intubation vasopressors are superior to IV fluid in hypotensive critically ill adults. Use shock physiology and local protocol, not a universal prophylactic bolus.
2

During induction

Reduce hypnotic burden. Keep paralysis effective.

Etomidate 21 mg 0.3 mg/kg IV. Reasonable in profound shock. Ketamine 35-70 mg 0.5-1 mg/kg IV, titrated to physiology. Rocuronium - full RSI dose 84 mg 1.2 mg/kg IV. Do not underdose the paralytic because the patient is shocked.
Ketamine nuance: Ketamine is not contraindicated solely because of TBI or elevated ICP. It is also not guaranteed to be hemodynamically neutral. In the 2025 RSI trial, cardiovascular collapse occurred more often with ketamine than etomidate (22.1% vs 17.0%), with no 28-day mortality advantage.
No succinylcholine when hyperkalemia risk is present Avoid with known hyperkalemia, skeletal muscle myopathy, or after the acute phase of major burns, multiple trauma, extensive denervation, or upper motor neuron injury. Use rocuronium.
Ventilate between induction and laryngoscopy when appropriate Two-person BVM with an airway adjunct, PEEP 5-10 cm H2O, about 10 breaths/min, and the smallest volume that produces chest rise reduced severe hypoxemia in PreVent. The trial excluded patients considered at very high aspiration risk.
3

After the tube

The procedure is not over at waveform capture.

First 2 minutes

  • Confirm continuous waveform ETCO2 and tube depth.
  • Cycle BP every minute; treat MAP below 65 and reassess the shock cause.
  • Start analgesia and sedation immediately.
  • Check bilateral ventilation, PEEP, plateau pressure, and auto-PEEP risk.

DKA / severe metabolic acidosis

  • Use 6-8 mL/kg predicted body weight.
  • Set respiratory rate and flow to approximate the pre-intubation minute ventilation.
  • Do not default to RR 12. Get an urgent ABG and use ETCO2 trends.
Expected PaCO2 range 18-22 mmHg

TBI / elevated ICP

  • Avoid hypotension: SBP at least 100 mmHg at age 50-69; at least 110 mmHg at age 15-49 or over 70.
  • Target PaCO2 about 35-45 mmHg and avoid hypoxemia.
  • Do not use prolonged prophylactic hyperventilation. Temporary hyperventilation is a bridge only for active herniation while definitive therapy is mobilized.
Pause before paralysis, if time permits, when oxygenation is not optimized, shock is unsupported, or severe metabolic acidosis is still being maintained by extreme spontaneous ventilation.
