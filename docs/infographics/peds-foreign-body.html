Pediatric Foreign Body: Airway & Esophageal

Children (typically 6 months–5 years peak, but any pediatric age) presenting with witnessed or suspected foreign body aspiration or ingestion in the ED. Excludes adults and caustic/chemical ingestions.

⚠ Witnessed choking + sudden cough, age 6 months–5 years = aspiration until proven otherwise — a normal chest X-ray does NOT rule it out.
⚠ Effective cough (can cry/speak, normal color) = "watch, don't touch" — never a blind finger sweep.
⚠ Esophageal button battery = OR within 2 hours regardless of symptoms.
⚠ Honey 10 mL PO q10min up to 6 doses only if age ≥12 months, awake, and <12 hours since ingestion — NEVER give honey under 12 months of age (infant botulism risk).
⚠ Multiple magnets, or a magnet plus metallic co-ingestion, = surgical consult at any location in the GI tract.
⚠ Rigid bronchoscopy is the gold standard for tracheobronchial FB — pursue it for high clinical suspicion regardless of imaging findings.
⚠ Cannot ventilate, cannot extract = activate ENT, anesthesia, OR, and PICU immediately; never delay for imaging.

A normal chest X-ray does NOT exclude airway FB — most aspirated objects are radiolucent.

2 · What to do now

Acute choking — begin BLS now
Assess cough effectiveness first. Effective cough (can cry/speak, normal color) = watch, do not intervene. Ineffective cough (no sound, cyanotic, decreasing LOC) = act now with age-appropriate maneuvers.
Failed BLS — critical airway
Cannot ventilate, cannot extract FB. Activate ENT, anesthesia, OR, PICU NOW. Bridge: direct laryngoscopy + Magill forceps if visible; BVM between attempts; consider pushing the FB into one mainstem to ventilate the other lung; surgical airway is a last resort. Definitive: emergent rigid bronchoscopy in OR.
Proceed to airway FB evaluation
All survivors of a choking event, plus stable suspected aspiration and chronic/occult presentations (recurrent same-lobe pneumonia, persistent wheeze), need a full airway FB work-up — retained particulate is common even after an object is expelled.
Ingestion — triage by object

Button battery — TRUE EMERGENCY

Single magnet
Esophageal + symptomatic → endoscopic removal <2h; asymptomatic → ≤24h. Beyond the esophagus, asymptomatic → observation with serial X-rays q4-6h and strict isolation from other magnets/metal.
Multiple magnets / magnet + metal
Attraction across bowel walls risks pressure necrosis, perforation, fistula. Esophageal/gastric → urgent endoscopic removal. Beyond the stomach + symptomatic → surgical consult. Neodymium ("Buckyball") magnets carry the highest risk.
Esophageal coin
Symptomatic → endoscopic removal <2 hours. Asymptomatic → <24 hours, or an observation trial with repeat X-ray at 16-24 hours (25-30% pass spontaneously, more likely with distal location and age >5yr).
Sharp / long object
Esophageal sharp object → endoscopic removal regardless of symptoms — perforation risk. Symptomatic → <2h; asymptomatic → <24h. Long objects (>5cm in <1yr, >6-10cm in older child) won't round the duodenal sweep and need removal.
High suspicion → rigid bronchoscopy

Moderate suspicion → observation
Inpatient or extended ED observation with pulmonology/ENT consultation. Low threshold to escalate to bronchoscopy if symptoms persist.
Low suspicion → discharge
Asymptomatic, normal exam, negative imaging → discharge with explicit return precautions.

4 · Airway (tracheobronchial) FB

  1. Assess cough: effective (cry/speak, normal color) → watch, do not intervene; ineffective (no sound, cyanotic, decreasing LOC) → act now
  2. Infant <1yr, conscious: 5 back blows, then 5 chest thrusts; repeat until object out or loss of consciousness
  3. Child ≥1yr, conscious: 5 abdominal thrusts (Heimlich); repeat until object out or loss of consciousness
  4. Any age, unconscious: start CPR (chest compressions first — may dislodge FB); look in mouth before each breath; remove visible object only — never a blind finger sweep
  1. Activate ENT, anesthesia, OR, PICU now
  2. Direct laryngoscopy + Magill forceps if FB is supraglottic and visible
  3. BVM ventilation between attempts
  4. Consider intentional right mainstem intubation to push the FB distal, ventilating the other lung
  5. Surgical airway (cricothyrotomy/tracheostomy) — last resort
  6. Definitive: emergent rigid bronchoscopy in OR

5 · Esophageal FB

Battery generates hydroxide at the negative pole; pH >12 within 15 minutes causes liquefactive necrosis → perforation, fistula. Lithium 3V cells are most dangerous; ≥20 mm batteries carry the highest impaction/injury risk. Complications even after removal: esophageal stricture (38%), tracheoesophageal fistula (48%), aortoesophageal fistula (can occur up to 28 days post-removal).

6 · Disposition & prevention

Admit
Any post-bronchoscopy/post-endoscopy patient ×24h; button battery regardless of clinical course (ICU level); multiple magnet ingestion during observation; persisting respiratory symptoms post-extraction.
Discharge if
Asymptomatic, normal exam, normal imaging; object known to have passed or very low-risk gastric position; family understands return precautions; follow-up arranged (GI/pulm/ENT as appropriate) per pathway norms.
  • Difficulty breathing, persistent cough or wheeze
  • Drooling, refusing PO, vomiting, abdominal pain
  • Fever, blood in stool, hematemesis
  • Chest or back pain

Foods to avoid under age 4: whole grapes, hot dogs, popcorn, nuts, hard candy, raw carrots — cut into small pieces. Keep button batteries, magnets, coins, small toys out of reach. CPSC Reese's Law (2024) requires child-resistant battery compartments. High-powered magnet sets are banned by CPSC (2022) — remove from home.
