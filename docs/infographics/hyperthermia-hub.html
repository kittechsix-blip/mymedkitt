Hyperthermia Hub: Core Temp First, Then Route to the Cause

Adults in the ED with hyperthermia, a hot patient, or altered mental status concerning for failed thermoregulation. Routing/triage hub — differentiates true hyperthermia (failed heat dissipation) from ordinary fever (raised set point) and directs to the correct cause-specific consult (heat stroke, serotonin syndrome, NMS, thyroid storm, anticholinergic toxidrome, salicylate toxicity, sepsis). Carries shared cooling principles and disposition criteria; does not duplicate each cause's full management, which lives in its own consult.

⚠ Get a CORE temperature (rectal/esophageal/bladder) — peripheral/oral/axillary temps underestimate. Core >40°C with altered mental status is a cooling emergency: start active cooling within minutes, before the cause is known.
⚠ Antipyretics (acetaminophen/NSAIDs) do NOT work in true hyperthermia — the hypothalamic set point is normal; the problem is failed heat dissipation, not a raised set point. NSAIDs add renal/GI/coagulopathy risk on top of being ineffective.
⚠ Match the dominant clue quickly, but do not anchor on the first one: heat exposure, serotonergic drug + clonus, antipsychotic + rigidity, hyperthyroid history + tachyarrhythmia, dry/mydriatic delirium, tinnitus/mixed acid-base picture, and an infectious source each point to a different life-threatening cause.
⚠ Heat stroke: core >40°C with CNS dysfunction after heat/exertion is a true emergency. Cold-water immersion is the fastest cooling method for exertional cases; stop active cooling near 38.5-39°C to avoid overshoot hypothermia.
⚠ Serotonin syndrome: clonus (especially lower-limb) and rapid onset (often <6h) distinguish it from NMS. Stop the serotonergic agent, benzodiazepines, active cooling, cyproheptadine for moderate-severe disease.
⚠ NMS: lead-pipe rigidity, bradyreflexia, and onset over days (the opposite pattern of serotonin syndrome). Bromocriptine is contraindicated in serotonin syndrome — confirm the diagnosis before giving it.
⚠ Thyroid storm: give iodine only ≥1h AFTER the thionamide (PTU/methimazole). Iodine given first can fuel new thyroid hormone synthesis and worsen the crisis.
⚠ Anticholinergic toxidrome: the skin cannot sweat, so passive cooling fails — use active evaporative cooling. Physostigmine is only for pure anticholinergic delirium with a clear agent and a normal ECG; avoid with mixed/TCA ingestion or any conduction delay (seizure/asystole risk).
⚠ Salicylate toxicity with hyperthermia signals uncoupled oxidative phosphorylation and is severe. Intubation is dangerous — it can collapse the protective hyperventilation. Pursue early hemodialysis for severe toxicity (AMS, hyperthermia, level >90-100, renal failure, refractory acidosis).
⚠ Fever and true hyperthermia can coexist. Running the sepsis pathway does not exclude a hyperthermic toxidrome, NMS, or thyroid storm if the temperature is disproportionate or rigidity/clonus appears.
⚠ Cause-specific antidotes are not interchangeable — cyproheptadine (serotonin syndrome), dantrolene/bromocriptine (NMS), thionamide + beta-blocker + iodine (thyroid storm). Confirm the diagnosis before giving any of them.
⚠ Do not discharge undifferentiated hyperthermia with altered mental status, rigidity, rhabdomyolysis, or hemodynamic instability. Core >40°C, AMS, rigidity, rhabdo/AKI, DIC, or hemodynamic/respiratory instability is an ICU admission.

  4. Thyroid storm — fever, tachyarrhythmia, agitation, goiter/Graves history.

3 · Cause-specific first minutes

Heat stroke / heat illness
Defining feature: core >40°C with CNS dysfunction after exertional or environmental heat exposure. Skin may be sweating (exertional) or dry (classic/elderly). Cold-water immersion is fastest for exertional heat stroke; evaporative + ice packs to groin/axillae/neck for classic. Stop cooling at ~38.5-39°C. Cold IV fluids; monitor for rhabdo/AKI/DIC. Full pathway: Heat Stroke.[4]
Serotonin syndrome
Defining features: serotonergic agent + clonus (spontaneous/inducible/ocular), hyperreflexia worse in the lower limbs, mydriasis, rapid onset (within 24h, often <6h). Stop the serotonergic agent, benzodiazepines for agitation/myoclonus, active cooling, cyproheptadine for moderate-severe. Distinguish from NMS (rigid + bradyreflexic + slow onset). Full pathway: Serotonin Syndrome.[5]
Neuroleptic malignant syndrome
Defining features: dopamine antagonist exposure (antipsychotic, antiemetic) or abrupt dopamine-agonist withdrawal + lead-pipe rigidity, hyperthermia, autonomic instability, AMS, very high CK. Onset over days. Bradyreflexia (opposite of serotonin syndrome). Stop the offending agent, aggressive cooling, IV fluids, benzodiazepines; dantrolene and/or bromocriptine for severe cases. Bromocriptine is contraindicated in serotonin syndrome — confirm the diagnosis. Full pathway: NMS.[6]
Thyroid storm
Defining features: known/undiagnosed hyperthyroidism + hyperthermia, tachyarrhythmia (often AF), agitation/delirium, GI/hepatic dysfunction, often a precipitant (infection, surgery, iodine load, DKA). Use the Burch-Wartofsky score. Beta-blockade (propranolol), thionamide (PTU/methimazole), then iodine ≥1h AFTER thionamide, hydrocortisone, active cooling, treat the precipitant. Full pathway: Thyroid Disorders.[7]
Anticholinergic toxidrome
"Hot as a hare, dry as a bone, red as a beet, mad as a hatter, blind as a bat." Hyperthermia with DRY/flushed skin, mydriasis, urinary retention, absent bowel sounds, delirium, tachycardia. Active evaporative cooling (the skin cannot sweat, so passive cooling fails); benzodiazepines; IV fluids; monitor for rhabdo. Physostigmine may be considered for pure anticholinergic delirium with a clear agent — avoid if mixed/TCA ingestion or any conduction delay on ECG (seizure/asystole risk). Get an ECG to screen for sodium-channel blockade before considering physostigmine. Discuss with toxicology/poison center.[3][8]
Salicylate toxicity
Defining features: tinnitus, hyperpnea/tachypnea, nausea, AMS, and the classic mixed acid-base picture (primary respiratory alkalosis + primary anion-gap metabolic acidosis). Hyperthermia signals UNCOUPLED oxidative phosphorylation and marks severe, life-threatening toxicity. Check level + VBG/ABG, serum alkalinization (sodium bicarbonate) with potassium repletion, dextrose even if euglycemic if AMS, and EARLY hemodialysis for severe toxicity (AMS, hyperthermia, level >90-100, renal failure, refractory acidosis). Intubation is dangerous — it can collapse the protective hyperventilation. Full pathway: Salicylate Toxicity.[9]
Sepsis / fever physiology
When this is true fever, not hyperthermia: an infectious source, rigors, the set point is raised so antipyretics help, and skin can still sweat. But fever and true hyperthermia can coexist (e.g. infection precipitating thyroid storm or NMS). Lactate, cultures (do not delay antibiotics), early broad-spectrum antibiotics, fluids for hypoperfusion, source control, vasopressors for persistent hypotension. Reassess for a second hyperthermic process if temp is disproportionate or rigidity/clonus appears. Full pathway: Sepsis Management.[10]

Admit / observation
Admit or observe when hyperthermia is resolving with cooling, the cause is identified and reversible, and there is no end-organ injury. Serial core temperature, CK, renal function, and coagulation studies. Confirm the offending agent is stopped. Return/escalation precautions: rising temperature, new rigidity or clonus, falling urine output or dark urine (rhabdo), confusion, bleeding, or hemodynamic instability.[1][2]

7 · Do NOT

Critical pitfalls

Do NOT treat true hyperthermia with antipyretics — the set point is normal; acetaminophen/NSAIDs are ineffective and NSAIDs add risk.

Do NOT trust a peripheral temperature — oral and axillary temps underestimate core. A normal peripheral reading does not exclude life-threatening core hyperthermia; measure rectal/esophageal/bladder temp.

Do NOT wait for a diagnosis before cooling — core >40°C with altered mental status is a cooling emergency; start active cooling within minutes.

Do NOT give bromocriptine without confirming NMS — it is contraindicated in serotonin syndrome.

Do NOT give iodine before the thionamide in thyroid storm — iodine first can fuel new hormone synthesis; give it ≥1h after PTU/methimazole.

Do NOT give physostigmine with mixed/TCA ingestion or any conduction delay on ECG — seizure/asystole risk. Confirm a narrow QRS first.

Do NOT use succinylcholine for intractable hyperthermia with suspected rhabdomyolysis/hyperkalemia — use rocuronium or vecuronium instead.
