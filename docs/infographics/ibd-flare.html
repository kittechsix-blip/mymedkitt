IBD Flare: Assess, Treat, Dispose

Adults with known or new-onset inflammatory bowel disease (ulcerative colitis or Crohn's disease) presenting to the ED with an acute flare. Assumes standard ED labs/imaging are available. Excludes pediatric dosing, pregnancy-specific IBD management, and outpatient biologic-initiation decisions (biologic starts are a GI/outpatient decision, never made in the ED).

⚠ Toxic megacolon (colonic dilation >6cm transverse colon + systemic toxicity) is a surgical emergency — avoid opioids, anticholinergics, antidiarrheals, and colonoscopy (all can worsen dilation or precipitate perforation); surgical consult STAT.
⚠ Perforation (peritoneal signs, free air on imaging) is a surgical emergency — hold immunosuppressants (infection risk, impaired healing) and prepare for OR; surgery consult STAT.
⚠ Do NOT scope (colonoscopy) in the ED for a severe or fulminant flare — perforation risk.
⚠ Do NOT give empiric steroids for new/undifferentiated IBD until C. difficile and other infections are ruled out, unless the patient is critically ill and needs treatment regardless.
⚠ Avoid NSAIDs — they trigger IBD flares.
⚠ JAK inhibitors (tofacitinib, upadacitinib) carry an FDA boxed warning for increased VTE/PE and death at 10mg BID (ORAL Surveillance trial); ACG 2025 positions them only after anti-TNF failure.
⚠ VTE risk is HIGH in hospitalized IBD flares — do not skip DVT prophylaxis.
⚠ Do NOT start biologics in the ED — hold if active infection, abscess, or planned surgery; biologic initiation is a GI/outpatient decision.

  • Current medications (biologics, steroids, immunomodulators)
  • Prior hospitalizations, surgeries
  • Recent med changes or non-adherence

2 · What to do now

Toxic megacolon — surgical emergency
NPO/NGT, aggressive IVF, IV steroids (methylprednisolone 60mg or hydrocortisone 100mg q8h), broad-spectrum antibiotics, DVT ppx, serial abdominal exams q6-8h. Avoid opioids/anticholinergics/antidiarrheals/colonoscopy. Surgical consult STAT.
Perforation — emergent surgery
NPO/NGT, large-bore IV + aggressive IVF, broad-spectrum antibiotics, blood products PRN. Hold immunosuppressants. Surgery consult STAT, prepare for OR.
Small bowel obstruction
NPO/NGT if vomiting, IVF + electrolyte correction, CT abdomen/pelvis with contrast, surgery consult. Trial IV steroids if inflammatory stricture; surgery if complete or fibrotic.
Intra-abdominal abscess
IV antibiotics (ciprofloxacin + metronidazole OR piperacillin-tazobactam 4.5g IV q6h), IR drainage if >3cm and accessible, NPO if concurrent obstruction, surgery consult, hold biologics/immunosuppressants.
Mild flare — usually outpatient
UC: mesalamine 4.8g/day ± topical therapy if distal. Crohn's: budesonide 9mg/day (ileal/right colon) or prednisone 40mg/day if more extensive. Avoid NSAIDs. GI follow-up 1-2 weeks.
Moderate flare — may need admission
IVF, oral prednisone 40-60mg/day if tolerating PO, or IV methylprednisolone 40-60mg/day / hydrocortisone 300mg/day divided if not. C. diff mandatory (oral vancomycin 125 mg PO QID if positive). DVT ppx.
Severe UC — admit, IV steroids
NPO initially, IV steroids (methylprednisolone 60mg/day or hydrocortisone 100mg q8h), IVF, DVT ppx, C. diff, daily labs + AXR. Reassess day 3 — salvage (infliximab, cyclosporine, surgery) if not improving. Early surgical consult.
Severe Crohn's — admit, IV steroids
Rule out surgical complications first. IV steroids methylprednisolone 40-60mg/day, IVF/electrolytes, nutrition support (TPN if prolonged NPO), DVT ppx, CT/MR enterography, C. diff/CMV workup.
New/undifferentiated IBD — workup pathway
Rule out infection first (C. diff, bacterial), assess severity, start supportive care, GI consult for expedited workup. Do NOT give steroids until infection ruled out (or patient critically ill).

4 · Surgical complications

Toxic megacolon — diagnostic criteria
Radiographic colonic dilation (>6cm transverse colon) PLUS ≥3 of: fever >38.6°C, HR >120, WBC >10.5, anemia. PLUS ≥1 of: dehydration, AMS, electrolyte disturbance, hypotension.
  1. NPO, NG tube if vomiting
  2. IV fluids — aggressive resuscitation
  3. IV steroids: methylprednisolone 60mg or hydrocortisone 100mg q8h
  4. Broad-spectrum antibiotics (metronidazole + fluoroquinolone or ceftriaxone)
  5. DVT prophylaxis
  6. Serial abdominal exams q6-8h
  7. Surgical consult STAT

7 · Disposition

Admit ICU
Toxic megacolon, perforation/pending surgery, sepsis/shock, massive hemorrhage. NPO, IV steroids if appropriate, broad antibiotics, aggressive IVF/pressors, serial exams. GI (urgent) + surgery (stat if toxic megacolon/perforation) + IR (if abscess) consults.
Admit floor
Severe flare, unable to tolerate PO, dehydration, need for IV steroids, anemia requiring transfusion, obstruction/abscess. Diet NPO → clear liquids → low residue. VTE risk is HIGH — do not skip prophylaxis. Daily AXR if severe UC.
Discharge
Mild flare responding to treatment, tolerating PO, stable vitals, C. diff negative/treated, close GI follow-up arranged. Return immediately for fever, worsening pain, bloody diarrhea >6x/day, inability to tolerate PO, dizziness/syncope.

Crohn's Severity — Mild-Moderate: ambulatory, tolerating PO, no complications. Severe-Fulminant: persistent symptoms despite steroids, fever, vomiting.
