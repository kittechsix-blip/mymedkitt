Magnesium Disorders: Hypomagnesemia, Hypermagnesemia, Renal Caveats

Adults in the ED/ICU with an abnormal serum magnesium or a high-risk clinical syndrome (torsades, refractory hypokalemia/hypocalcemia, eclampsia MgSO4 exposure). Always interpret magnesium together with renal function, ECG, reflexes, respiratory status, potassium, calcium, and phosphate. Not a pediatric or obstetric-dosing-specific tool.

⚠ Torsades, seizure, or life-threatening arrhythmia: give IV magnesium immediately — even before the level returns.
⚠ Refractory hypokalemia or hypocalcemia often will not correct until magnesium is repleted.
⚠ Hypermagnesemia with loss of reflexes, respiratory depression, hypotension, bradycardia, or heart block: stop magnesium and give IV calcium.
⚠ Renal failure converts magnesium therapy from routine replacement to monitored drug administration.
⚠ Dialysis is definitive for severe magnesium toxicity with renal failure or persistent cardiopulmonary compromise.

1 · Recognition

Interpret magnesium with renal function, ECG, reflexes, respiratory status, potassium, calcium, phosphate, and recent magnesium exposure. Route by low vs. high Mg, but always read the level in that full clinical context.

  1. Assess ECG, reflexes, respiratory rate, BP, renal function, and paired electrolytes.
  2. Low Mg with torsades, seizure, severe arrhythmia, or severe symptoms: IV magnesium now.
  3. Low Mg without instability: replace IV or PO based on severity, GI tolerance, and renal function.
  4. High Mg / toxicity: stop sources, give IV calcium if symptomatic, support airway/hemodynamics, enhance elimination.
  5. Dialysis for severe toxicity with renal failure or persistent cardiopulmonary compromise.

Dangerous hypomagnesemia is clinical: torsades or prolonged QT, ventricular ectopy, seizures, tetany, severe weakness, refractory hypokalemia, refractory hypocalcemia, alcohol withdrawal, DKA, diarrhea, diuretics, amphotericin/cisplatin, or PPI-associated renal/GI wasting. A commonly used lab threshold for severe deficiency is Mg <1.2 mg/dL.

Most clinically significant hypermagnesemia occurs with renal failure plus magnesium exposure: antacids/laxatives, supplements, obstetric MgSO4, missed dialysis, lithium/milk-alkali overlap, or massive tissue injury. Clinical toxicity matters more than the number: hyporeflexia, weakness, somnolence, hypotension, bradycardia, AV block, widened QRS, respiratory depression, or arrest.

2 · What to do now

Torsades / seizure / unstable arrhythmia — Mg now
Magnesium Sulfate 2 g IV over 5-15 min; repeat or continue infusion per rhythm response and local protocol. Give even if serum magnesium is normal when torsades is present. Correct K to high-normal, stop QT-prolonging drugs, treat bradycardia with overdrive pacing/isoproterenol when appropriate, and defibrillate unstable sustained/pulseless rhythms.
Severe / symptomatic hypomagnesemia
If symptomatic but stable: Magnesium Sulfate 1-2 g IV over about 1 hour; more aggressive repletion often requires scheduled 2-4 g doses or infusion when renal function is normal. For Mg <1.2 mg/dL or severe total-body depletion, plan repeated dosing — serum Mg rises quickly but intracellular repletion is slower. Reduce dose and increase monitoring in renal impairment.
Mild-moderate, stable hypomagnesemia
Oral magnesium is reasonable when stable, mild-moderate, and able to tolerate GI effects. Use slower IV replacement when oral therapy is not tolerated, ongoing losses are high, or inpatient paired-electrolyte correction is needed. Discharge requires the cause addressed, no dangerous ECG/symptoms, renal-appropriate dose, and follow-up labs when ongoing losses or medications remain.
Hypermagnesemia toxicity — stop Mg, give calcium
Stop all magnesium sources. Support airway, ventilation, circulation, and continuous ECG monitoring. Give Calcium Gluconate 1-2 g IV over 5-10 min; repeat for recurrent cardiopulmonary toxicity. Use Calcium Chloride 1 g IV over 5-10 min via central access if peri-arrest. If renal function is adequate: isotonic fluids plus loop diuretic may enhance excretion. If renal failure, severe level, persistent hypotension/bradycardia/respiratory depression, or missed dialysis: emergent hemodialysis.
Mild, asymptomatic hypermagnesemia
If mild, asymptomatic, and kidney function is adequate: remove exogenous magnesium and monitor. Recheck Mg, Ca, K, phosphate, creatinine, and ECG if the level is meaningfully elevated or symptoms could evolve. Reconcile medications specifically for magnesium-containing antacids, laxatives, supplements, bowel prep, and obstetric magnesium infusion.
Renal failure — magnesium becomes a monitored drug
Avoid large empiric magnesium loads unless treating torsades, eclampsia, seizure, or clear severe deficiency. Use lower doses, longer intervals, and serial levels. For toxicity with renal failure, dialysis is definitive. Calcium antagonizes toxicity transiently but does not remove magnesium.

6 · Renal caveat & pitfalls

Renal impairment increases toxicity risk from both replacement and exposure. Avoid large empiric magnesium loads unless treating torsades, eclampsia, seizure, or clear severe deficiency. Use lower doses, longer intervals, and serial levels.

  • Do NOT wait for the Mg level before treating torsades.
  • Do NOT keep chasing potassium or calcium without correcting magnesium.
  • Do NOT give large magnesium replacement doses to oliguric/anuric patients without a monitoring plan.
  • Do NOT miss magnesium-containing laxatives/antacids in renal failure.
  • Do NOT assume calcium fixes hypermagnesemia; it antagonizes toxicity temporarily while elimination/dialysis is arranged.
