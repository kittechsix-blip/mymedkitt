Oncological Emergencies: Triage, Recognition, Time-Critical Management

Adult ED patients with known or suspected malignancy at the time of presentation with one of the time-critical complications enumerated in the flow: febrile neutropenia, tumor lysis syndrome, hypercalcemia of malignancy, malignant spinal cord compression, SVC syndrome, leukostasis/hyperviscosity, malignant pericardial tamponade, cytokine release syndrome, ICANS, or immune-checkpoint-inhibitor toxicity. The chemotherapy toxicity reference (cardiotoxicity/pulmonary/renal/neurotoxicity, nadirs, extravasation) is background reference material, not a branching pathway.

⚠ Febrile neutropenia (ANC <500 + temp ≥38.3°C once or ≥38.0°C sustained ×1h): empiric antibiotics within 60 minutes of triage — mortality drops from >70% untreated to <10% with prompt treatment.
⚠ TLS: aggressive IVF FIRST (NS 2-3 L/m²/day); rasburicase 0.2 mg/kg IV if uric acid >8 or rapid rise — check G6PD if time allows but do not delay treatment for a true emergency. Never alkalinize urine.
⚠ Hypercalcemia >14 mg/dL or symptomatic: NS 200-300 mL/hr + calcitonin 4 IU/kg SC q12h + denosumab 120 mg SC (2022 Endocrine Society first-line) OR zoledronic acid 4 mg IV — avoid loop diuretics.
⚠ Suspected MSCC: dexamethasone 10 mg IV STAT BEFORE imaging if high suspicion — every minute of cord compression risks permanent deficit; emergent MRI whole spine within 24h.
⚠ SVC syndrome with cerebral edema or airway compromise (Yale grade 4): head elevation, avoid upper-extremity IV access, dexamethasone, urgent IR consult for stent — do not delay for tissue diagnosis if life-threatening.
⚠ Leukostasis (WBC >100k with symptoms): hydration + cytoreduction (hydroxyurea or leukapheresis) — HOLD non-essential pRBC transfusions. Suspected APL: NO leukapheresis (precipitates DIC) — start ATRA 45 mg/m²/day immediately on clinical suspicion.
⚠ Malignant pericardial tamponade with instability: avoid intubation/PEEP if possible, vasopressors + cautious IVF, emergent US-guided pericardiocentesis — do NOT drain in aortic dissection.
⚠ CRS Grade 2+: tocilizumab 8 mg/kg IV (max 800 mg); add dexamethasone 10 mg IV q6h for Grade 3+; ICU for Grade 3-4. CRS and sepsis are clinically indistinguishable — pan-culture and start empiric antibiotics regardless of grade.
⚠ ICANS Grade 2+: dexamethasone 10 mg IV q6h + levetiracetam prophylaxis; methylprednisolone 1000 mg IV daily for Grade 4. Tocilizumab does NOT cross the blood-brain barrier and does NOT treat ICANS — steroids only.
⚠ ICI myocarditis (25-50% mortality if missed): methylprednisolone 500-1000 mg IV daily × 3-5 days starting within 24h, ICU, cardiology consult immediately; abatacept 10 mg/kg IV if steroid-refractory — avoid infliximab (increases mortality in CHF).
⚠ Rasburicase is contraindicated in G6PD deficiency (hemolysis) — screen when time allows but do not delay treatment in a true TLS emergency.

1 · Triage & Management Algorithm

Cancer patients are ~5% of ED visits but carry disproportionate morbidity — the shared principle across all 10 oncologic emergencies below is early recognition + rapid time-critical action prevents death or permanent disability.

Always ask: cancer type/stage; chemo/targeted/immunotherapy/CAR-T agent and last dose; last ANC/platelets/electrolytes; ECOG performance status.

Select the presenting pattern to jump to its management ladder — every branch below is treated as time-critical until proven otherwise.

2 · Febrile Neutropenia

IDSA/NCCN definition: single oral temp ≥38.3°C (101°F) OR sustained ≥38.0°C (100.4°F) for ≥1 hour, PLUS ANC <500 cells/mm³ (or <1000 with predicted decline to <500 within 48h). Profound neutropenia = ANC <100 (highest infection risk).

Up to 50% of febrile neutropenic patients have an established or occult infection; bacteremia in 10-25%. Mortality without treatment exceeds 70%; with prompt antibiotics it drops to <10%. Antibiotics within 60 minutes of triage — every hour of delay increases mortality.

Add vancomycin only if: hemodynamic instability/sepsis, suspected catheter infection, skin/soft-tissue infection, pneumonia (until MRSA ruled out), severe mucositis, or known MRSA colonization — not routine.

Low-Risk FN — Possible Outpatient Therapy
Requires MASCC ≥21, no high-risk features, reliable patient/transportation/phone, oncologist agreement, and 24-48h follow-up arranged.

Oral regimen: ciprofloxacin 750 mg PO BID PLUS amoxicillin-clavulanate 875/125 mg PO BID (PCN-allergic: cipro + clindamycin 600 mg PO TID). ED observation 4-24h before discharge. Default to admission if any uncertainty.
Afebrile but Neutropenic — Still High Risk
Neutrophils are required to mount a fever response — life-threatening infection can exist without fever. Look for: catheter site pain/erythema, perirectal pain, new mucositis pain, subtle abdominal pain (typhlitis), sinus pain (mucormycosis), new cough/hypoxia. Empiric antibiotics if any localizing finding; admit if ANC <100 or unstable.

4 · Hypercalcemia of Malignancy

Most common metabolic complication of cancer (20-30% of patients); usually advanced disease, median survival 30 days untreated. Mechanisms: PTHrP/humoral (~80% — squamous, renal, breast, ovarian), osteolytic mets (breast, myeloma, lymphoma), 1,25-vit-D (lymphoma, granulomatous), ectopic PTH (rare). Corrected Ca = measured Ca + 0.8 × (4 − albumin).

Severe/Moderate HCM — IVF + Calcitonin + Antiresorptive
Step 1: normal saline 200-300 mL/hr until euvolemic, then 100-150 mL/hr; goal UOP >100 mL/hr. Avoid LR (contains calcium). Avoid loop diuretics routinely — forced calciuresis is not evidence-supported and can worsen depletion.

Step 2 — calcitonin 4 IU/kg SC/IM q12h — drops Ca 1-2 mg/dL within 4-6h; tachyphylaxis by 48-72h, bridge only.

Step 3 — antiresorptive: denosumab 120 mg SC (2022 Endocrine Society first-line preference — no renal clearance, use in CrCl<30) OR zoledronic acid 4 mg IV over 15-30 min (alternative first-line; avoid if CrCl<30). Either is acceptable; many centers still default to zoledronic acid on cost/availability.

Vit-D-mediated (lymphoma/myeloma/granulomatous): prednisone 40-60 mg PO daily.

Hemodialysis if: Ca >18 with neuro/cardiac symptoms, renal failure preventing IVF, or refractory to medical therapy.

Disposition: ICU if severe/AMS/arrhythmia, telemetry/stepdown if moderate. Repeat Ca q6-12h; expect 1-2 mg/dL/day decline. Endocrinology + oncology consult.

5 · Malignant Spinal Cord Compression

Affects 5-10% of cancer patients; first sign of cancer in 20%. Ambulatory at presentation predicts ambulatory at 30 days — paraplegia is largely irreversible. Top primaries: lung, breast, prostate (50% of cases). Location: thoracic 60-70%, lumbosacral 20-30%, cervical 10%, multi-level in 30%.

  • New or worsening back pain (often precedes neuro signs by weeks)
  • Pain worse at night/recumbent or on Valsalva/cough/sneeze
  • Saddle anesthesia
  • Bowel/bladder dysfunction (retention or incontinence)
  • Leg weakness (usually symmetric, gradual)
  • Sensory level on exam
  • Hyperreflexia or upgoing toes (UMN signs)
  • Spinal tenderness on percussion
Red Flags Present — Steroids First, Then Image
Dexamethasone 10 mg IV bolus, then 4 mg IV/PO q6h — give BEFORE imaging if high suspicion. Some protocols use a 96 mg loading dose for severe deficit (similar efficacy, more side effects).

Emergent MRI of the WHOLE spine with gadolinium (multi-level disease in 30%); CT myelogram if MRI unavailable — CT alone is insufficient.

Consult from the ED: neurosurgery, radiation oncology, medical oncology.

Surgery + radiation (Patchell 2005): ambulatory or recently non-ambulatory (<48h), ≥3-month life expectancy, single-level, stable spine — 84% vs 57% ambulation preserved vs RT alone. Radiation alone: non-surgical candidates, multi-level disease, radiosensitive tumors (lymphoma/myeloma/SCLC/germ cell), life expectancy <3 months.

Disposition: admit neurosurgery (surgical) or oncology (RT-only); telemetry if high thoracic/cervical lesion (autonomic risk); logroll precautions until spine cleared; Foley if retention; DVT prophylaxis (mechanical pre-op, then chemical post-op). Ambulatory at presentation → 70-80% remain ambulatory at 30 days; non-ambulatory → only 20-30% regain ambulation. Median survival post-MSCC: 3-6 months (depends on primary).

6 · SVC Syndrome

Cancer causes >60% of SVC syndrome (lung 50%, lymphoma 25%); indwelling catheter thrombosis is the leading non-malignant cause. Classic: facial/neck plethora (Pemberton sign), distended neck/chest veins, upper-extremity edema, dyspnea/cough, hoarseness/dysphagia, headache/dizziness/visual changes, AMS/syncope (cerebral edema), stridor (airway compromise).

Leukostasis — Hydration + Cytoreduction
Step 1: IV fluids (avoid LR); allopurinol 300 mg PO daily OR rasburicase if uric acid >8 (anticipate TLS as cells lyse); strict I/O.

Step 2 — cytoreduction: hydroxyurea 50-100 mg/kg/day PO divided BID-TID (drops WBC 50-80% in 24-48h) OR leukapheresis for severe leukostasis (drops WBC 30-60%/session) — CONTRAINDICATED in suspected APL (M3 AML), can precipitate severe DIC.

Disposition: ICU. Heme/onc + apheresis service consults from the ED.

8 · Malignant Pericardial Tamponade

Most common malignant causes: lung (~33%), breast (~25%), lymphoma, leukemia, melanoma, mesothelioma. Malignant effusions accumulate slowly and can reach >1 L before tamponade physiology develops. Beck's triad (full classic <30%): hypotension + distended neck veins + muffled heart sounds. Pulsus paradoxus (SBP drop >10 mmHg with inspiration) is >80% sensitive in non-COPD patients.

  • RA systolic collapse — most sensitive
  • RV diastolic collapse — most specific
  • IVC plethora (>2.1 cm, <50% inspiratory collapse)
  • Septal bounce / D-shaped LV in inspiration
  • Mitral inflow respiratory variation >25%
Tamponade with Instability — Bedside Pericardiocentesis
Avoid: intubation/positive-pressure ventilation if possible (drops preload → can precipitate PEA arrest — use ketamine + no PEEP + gentle hand-bagging if unavoidable), beta-blockers, vasodilators/nitrates, diuretics, high PEEP/high tidal volumes.

Support: norepinephrine or epinephrine for hemodynamics; cautious IV bolus (250-500 mL) — temporizing only, do not over-resuscitate.

Pericardiocentesis (US-guided when time allows): identify largest fluid pocket (subxiphoid/parasternal/apical), apical/parasternal often preferred (subxiphoid traverses liver); 18g needle under direct visualization, aspirate continuously. Confirm intra-pericardial position (fluid does not clot; agitated saline bubbles in pericardial space, not chamber). Pull off 50-100 mL initially — even small volumes dramatically improve hemodynamics; leave catheter 24-72h.

KEY EXCEPTION — do NOT drain in aortic dissection (increases shear, worsens dissection) — emergent CT-surgery instead.

Always: CT-surgery + oncology consult; repeat echo within 24h to assess re-accumulation; goals-of-care discussion (malignant pericardial effusion median survival 2-4 months); cytology results turnaround 24-72h.

Disposition: ICU (post-drainage pulmonary edema, recurrent effusion, dysrhythmia risk).
Stable Effusion (± Tamponade Physiology) — Workup + Plan
Formal echo, CXR, ECG (low voltage, electrical alternans), CBC/BMP/coags/troponin/BNP, identify underlying malignancy. Telemetry + serial vitals; avoid IV fluids beyond maintenance; HOB 30-45°.

Definitive by etiology: malignant → pericardial drain + window (surgical or balloon) ± intrapericardial chemo (cisplatin/bleomycin/thiotepa); bacterial → drainage + IV antibiotics + ID; TB → drainage + RIPE + steroids; uremic → dialysis intensification; aortic dissection → do NOT drain, emergent CT-surgery.

Disposition: telemetry/stepdown if moderate-large; admit oncology (expedited outpatient reasonable if small/asymptomatic + reliable). CT-surgery + oncology consult; repeat echo within 24h; GOC discussion (median survival malignant effusion 2-4 months); cytology results turnaround 24-72h.

9 · Cytokine Release Syndrome (CAR-T / Bispecific)

CRS is the dominant acute toxicity of CAR-T cells and bispecific T-cell engagers (blinatumomab, mosunetuzumab, teclistamab, talquetamab, glofitamab, epcoritamab). Onset: CAR-T hours-14 days (peak day 1-7); bispecifics most common with first dose/step-up dosing. ASTCT 2019 grading requires fever ≥38.0°C plus the hypotension/hypoxia criteria below. CRS and concurrent infection are clinically indistinguishable — always pan-culture and start broad-spectrum antibiotics empirically with any febrile presentation.

11 · Immune Checkpoint Inhibitor Toxicities (irAEs)

PD-1 (pembrolizumab, nivolumab, cemiplimab, dostarlimab), PD-L1 (atezolizumab, durvalumab, avelumab), CTLA-4 (ipilimumab, tremelimumab), LAG-3 (relatlimab). Combination therapy (ipi/nivo) carries 2-3× the irAE rate of monotherapy. irAEs can occur weeks to months after the last dose — always ask about ICI exposure in the past 6-12 months.

1 · Triage & Management Algorithm

Cancer patients are ~5% of ED visits but carry disproportionate morbidity — the shared principle across all 10 oncologic emergencies below is early recognition + rapid time-critical action prevents death or permanent disability.

Always ask: cancer type/stage; chemo/targeted/immunotherapy/CAR-T agent and last dose; last ANC/platelets/electrolytes; ECOG performance status.

Select the presenting pattern to jump to its management ladder — every branch below is treated as time-critical until proven otherwise.

Cancer Patient — Suspect Oncologic Emergency — ~5% of ED visits. Ask cancer type/stage, agent + last dose, last ANC/lytes, ECOG.

5 · Malignant Spinal Cord Compression

Affects 5-10% of cancer patients; first sign of cancer in 20%. Ambulatory at presentation predicts ambulatory at 30 days — paraplegia is largely irreversible. Top primaries: lung, breast, prostate (50% of cases). Location: thoracic 60-70%, lumbosacral 20-30%, cervical 10%, multi-level in 30%.

  • New or worsening back pain (often precedes neuro signs by weeks)
  • Pain worse at night/recumbent or on Valsalva/cough/sneeze
  • Saddle anesthesia
  • Bowel/bladder dysfunction (retention or incontinence)
  • Leg weakness (usually symmetric, gradual)
  • Sensory level on exam
  • Hyperreflexia or upgoing toes (UMN signs)
  • Spinal tenderness on percussion

Dexamethasone 10 mg IV bolus, then 4 mg IV/PO q6h — give BEFORE imaging if high suspicion. Some protocols use a 96 mg loading dose for severe deficit (similar efficacy, more side effects).

8 · Malignant Pericardial Tamponade

Most common malignant causes: lung (~33%), breast (~25%), lymphoma, leukemia, melanoma, mesothelioma. Malignant effusions accumulate slowly and can reach >1 L before tamponade physiology develops. Beck's triad (full classic <30%): hypotension + distended neck veins + muffled heart sounds. Pulsus paradoxus (SBP drop >10 mmHg with inspiration) is >80% sensitive in non-COPD patients.

  • RA systolic collapse — most sensitive
  • RV diastolic collapse — most specific
  • IVC plethora (>2.1 cm, <50% inspiratory collapse)
  • Septal bounce / D-shaped LV in inspiration
  • Mitral inflow respiratory variation >25%

Avoid: intubation/positive-pressure ventilation if possible (drops preload → can precipitate PEA arrest — use ketamine + no PEEP + gentle hand-bagging if unavoidable), beta-blockers, vasodilators/nitrates, diuretics, high PEEP/high tidal volumes.

Support: norepinephrine or epinephrine for hemodynamics; cautious IV bolus (250-500 mL) — temporizing only, do not over-resuscitate.

Pericardiocentesis (US-guided when time allows): identify largest fluid pocket (subxiphoid/parasternal/apical), apical/parasternal often preferred (subxiphoid traverses liver); 18g needle under direct visualization, aspirate continuously. Confirm intra-pericardial position (fluid does not clot; agitated saline bubbles in pericardial space, not chamber). 13 · Always-Do Disposition Threads

Always: involve oncology (primary team knows the patient/prognosis/goals of care); goals-of-care discussion; standard opioid principles for pain; ondansetron + dexamethasone antiemetics (avoid prochlorperazine if seizure risk); chemical DVT prophylaxis unless active bleeding; document performance status (ECOG/Karnofsky).
