Anaphylaxis: Epinephrine First, Every Time

Anaphylaxis of any severity in the ED, all ages — weight-based dosing covers pediatric and adult patients throughout. Excludes isolated ACE-inhibitor or hereditary (bradykinin-mediated) angioedema without urticaria, which does not respond to epinephrine and needs a different pathway.

⚠ There is NO absolute contraindication to epinephrine in anaphylaxis, and the risk of NOT giving it always exceeds the risk of giving it. Give IM epinephrine in the anterolateral thigh immediately — do NOT delay for IV access, antihistamines, or steroids.
⚠ NEVER give the cardiac-arrest IV push dose of epinephrine (1 mg) to a patient who still has a pulse — this is a 61-fold dosing error versus the IM dose and causes hypertensive crisis, arrhythmia, and death. In a patient with a pulse, IV epinephrine is an infusion only.
⚠ Angioedema WITHOUT urticaria may be ACE-inhibitor-induced or hereditary (bradykinin-mediated, C1-esterase inhibitor deficiency) — epinephrine is ineffective for these and the management pathway differs.
⚠ Glucagon for beta-blocked anaphylaxis is DISCOURAGED — weak evidence (case reports only), it bypasses only beta-1 (not beta-2) receptors, and carries a high emesis/aspiration risk. Reserve as an absolute last resort.

4 · Fluids & airway

Distributive shock — up to 35% of intravascular volume extravasates within minutes. Adults: 500-1000 mL NS/LR bolus, repeat aggressively, may need several liters in the first hour. Pediatrics: 20-30 mL/kg bolus, repeat to 60 mL/kg+. LR preferred for large-volume resuscitation.

Airway edema can progress rapidly. Signs of upper airway involvement: stridor, hoarseness, voice change, tongue/lip swelling, drooling. Angioedema WITHOUT urticaria suggests ACE-inhibitor or hereditary angioedema (bradykinin-mediated) — epinephrine is ineffective.

Bronchospasm management
Persistent wheezing despite adequate epinephrine. Albuterol 2.5-5 mg nebulized, continuous if severe. Terbutaline 0.25 mg SQ, may repeat in 15-30 min. Ensure adequate epinephrine infusion — epinephrine is itself a bronchodilator via beta-2 receptors.
Critical airway — prepare for intubation
Prepare for a difficult airway — call anesthesia/ENT early. Video laryngoscopy first attempt, smaller ETT than usual, surgical airway kit at bedside. Temporizing: nebulized racemic epinephrine 0.5 mL of 2.25% in 4.5 mL NS (or L-epinephrine 5 mL of 1:1000). Do NOT delay intubation if the patient is deteriorating.

Critical pitfalls

**Do NOT delay epinephrine** for IV access, antihistamines, or steroids — it is always first-line and there is no absolute contraindication.

**Do NOT discharge** before symptom-free ≥4 hours or without an epinephrine autoinjector ×2 prescription and allergist referral.
