Anaphylaxis — Adult ED Treatment Protocol

Adults ≥18 in the ED with suspected anaphylaxis. Excludes pediatrics and pregnancy-specific dosing.

⚠ There is NO absolute contraindication to epinephrine in anaphylaxis. Delayed epinephrine is the #1 cause of death.
⚠ Give epinephrine IM in the anterolateral thigh, NOT subcutaneous (slower/erratic absorption) and NOT IV push for routine cases. IV push causes arrhythmia; IV epinephrine is for refractory shock as an infusion only.

Recognition

Diagnose anaphylaxis when there is acute onset with involvement of ≥2 organ systems (skin/mucosa, respiratory, CV/hypotension, GI), OR hypotension after a known allergen.

Treatment algorithm

Anaphylaxis criteria met?
Skin/mucosa (hives, angioedema), respiratory (dyspnea, wheeze, stridor), CV (hypotension, syncope), or GI (cramping, vomiting).
Give epinephrine 0.01 mg/kg IM (max 0.5 mg), anterolateral thigh; repeat q5-15 min as needed
IM anterolateral thigh gives fast, reliable absorption. Subcutaneous is slower and erratic. IV push is reserved for refractory shock as an infusion; IV push causes arrhythmia.

First-line: epinephrine

—Weight-based dose for this patient

Use the 1 mg/mL ("1:1000") ampule. The maximum single dose is 0.5 mg IM.

Adjuncts (after epinephrine)

Adjuncts follow epinephrine and never replace it. IV fluids: normal saline 1-2 L bolus for hypotension. Albuterol nebulized for bronchospasm. H1/H2 antihistamines and steroids are adjuncts only; they do not treat airway or cardiovascular compromise.

Refractory anaphylaxis

No response after 2-3 IM doses: start an epinephrine IV infusion. Add glucagon if the patient is on a beta-blocker.

Disposition

After response, observe 4-6 h for biphasic reaction. At discharge, prescribe 2 epinephrine autoinjectors and arrange allergy referral.
