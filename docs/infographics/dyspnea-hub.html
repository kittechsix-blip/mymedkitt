Dyspnea Hub — Sick Check, Exclusions, Rescue, Disposition

Adults ≥18 in the ED presenting with acute shortness of breath. This is a triage/routing hub — definitive dosing and step-by-step management live in the linked cause-specific consults (asthma, COPD, CHF/SCAPE, PE, pneumothorax, pneumonia/sepsis, anaphylaxis, ACS/arrhythmia, CO/methemoglobinemia). Excludes pediatrics and isolated chronic dyspnea without an acute change.

⚠ Tension pneumothorax — unilateral absent breath sounds + shock: decompress before imaging.
⚠ Pulmonary embolism — hypoxia, RV strain, clear lungs, risk factors.
⚠ Acute pulmonary edema / cardiogenic shock — crackles, hypoxia, hypertension or hypotension; NIV early.
⚠ Anaphylaxis / airway angioedema — stridor, urticaria, exposure: IM epinephrine now.
⚠ Toxic/metabolic dyspnea (CO, salicylate, DKA, severe acidosis) — co-oximetry, ABG, gaps.
⚠ Do not send an unstable dyspnea patient away from resus without airway, oxygen, pressor, and procedure plan.
⚠ Normal pulse ox does not exclude CO poisoning.

Sick Check First

  • Tension pneumothorax — unilateral absent breath sounds + shock: decompress before imaging.
  • Pulmonary embolism — hypoxia, RV strain, clear lungs, risk factors.
  • Acute pulmonary edema / cardiogenic shock — crackles, hypoxia, hypertension or hypotension; NIV early.
  • Anaphylaxis / airway angioedema — stridor, urticaria, exposure: IM epinephrine now.
  • Toxic/metabolic dyspnea (CO, salicylate, DKA, severe acidosis) — co-oximetry, ABG, gaps.
  • Can they speak? Single words, tripod, diaphoresis, exhaustion, agitation, cyanosis?
  • Work of breathing: accessory muscles, paradoxical breathing, silent chest.
  • SpO2 is not enough: check waveform, consider CO/methemoglobin, and watch ventilation.
  • Monitor, IV, ECG, CXR when stable, POCUS early when diagnosis is unclear.
  • Position upright.
  • If impending failure: call airway help early, prepare NIV vs intubation while treating cause.

▶ What to do now — by exclusion category

Obstructive crisis — asthma / COPD
Open Asthma Exacerbation or COPD Exacerbation.

Next 5 minutes: continuous albuterol for severe asthma physiology, ipratropium, systemic steroid, magnesium for severe asthma, NIV for COPD/hypercapnic failure when cooperative and protecting airway.

Intubation warning: severe obstructive disease is a physiologically difficult airway — use low rate, long expiratory time, permissive hypercapnia, and disconnect/allow exhalation if peri-arrest auto-PEEP occurs.
SCAPE / acute pulmonary edema
Open CHF Exacerbation and consider SCAPE High-Dose Nitroglycerin.

Next 5 minutes: upright, NIV now, high-dose nitrates when hypertensive SCAPE physiology is present, ECG/troponin, POCUS for B-lines/LV function, avoid reflex large fluid boluses.

Pitfall: wheeze can be cardiac asthma — diffuse B-lines + hypertension + acute distress should push you toward NIV/nitrates, not just nebulizers.
Pulmonary embolism
Open PE Treatment.

Next 5 minutes: oxygen, monitor, IV, bedside echo if unstable, anticoagulation if PE likely and bleeding risk acceptable, CT pulmonary angiography when stable. Shock + RV strain = massive PE pathway and PERT/intervention discussion.

Do not send a crashing obstructive-shock PE patient to CT without resuscitation, pressor, airway, and lysis/intervention plan.
Pneumothorax / tension
Open Pneumothorax.

Next 5 minutes: if hypotension, severe distress, or peri-arrest — decompress before imaging. If stable — lung POCUS and CXR. Watch ventilated patients and severe asthma/COPD closely because positive pressure can convert small PTX into tension.
Pneumonia / sepsis physiology
Open Pneumonia or Sepsis Management.

Next 5 minutes: oxygen, lactate if sepsis physiology, blood cultures if it will not delay antibiotics, antibiotics based on severity and risk, cautious fluids if shock but reassess lungs/IVC/heart frequently.

POCUS can identify consolidation, pleural effusion, diffuse B-lines, and cardiac dysfunction when the CXR is lagging behind the patient.
Anaphylaxis
Open Anaphylaxis Management.

Next 5 minutes: IM epinephrine now for airway/breathing/circulation involvement; repeat q5-15 min as needed; oxygen, fluids, albuterol for bronchospasm, early epinephrine infusion for refractory shock.

Pitfall: antihistamines and steroids are adjuncts — they do not rescue airway, bronchospasm, or shock.
Cardiac dyspnea / ACS / arrhythmia
Open STEMI, NSTEMI, A-Fib RVR, or CHF Exacerbation based on the trigger.

Next 5 minutes: ECG, monitor, troponin/BNP as helpful, POCUS for LV/RV/pericardium/lungs, treat dysrhythmia instability immediately, and keep PE/dissection in the differential when dyspnea and chest pain travel together.
CO / methemoglobinemia / dyshemoglobin
Open CO Toxicity or Methemoglobinemia.

Next 5 minutes: 100% oxygen if CO possible; co-oximetry because standard SpO2 may mislead; look for exposure history, smoke/fire, generator/heater, topical anesthetic/dapsone/nitrate exposure, cyanosis out of proportion to PaO2.

Pitfall: normal pulse ox does not exclude CO poisoning.
No immediate exclusion — initial bundle and reassess
Default bundle while diagnosis clarifies: upright positioning, oxygen target per hypercapnic risk, monitor/IV/ECG, CXR when stable, lung/cardiac POCUS when diagnosis uncertain or patient is sick, VBG/ABG when ventilatory failure, severe COPD/asthma, tox, or altered mental status is possible. Nebulizers if wheeze, NIV if COPD/CHF physiology and no contraindication, antibiotics if infection/sepsis, epinephrine if anaphylaxis.

Reassess every 5-15 minutes in severe dyspnea: work of breathing, mental status, RR, ETCO2/VBG trend, oxygen requirement, and hemodynamics.

Disposition

Disposition follows oxygen requirement, work of breathing, trajectory, and cause.

ICU / resus admission
ICU/resus for NIV or intubation, persistent severe work of breathing, shock, rising CO2/acidosis, massive PE, severe asthma/COPD with exhaustion, SCAPE not rapidly improving, anaphylaxis needing infusion, or unclear diagnosis with unstable physiology.
Admit / observation
Admit or observe for new oxygen requirement, persistent tachypnea, recurrent symptoms after treatment, pneumonia with risk features, COPD/CHF needing repeated therapy, PE workup/treatment, social inability to manage therapy, or diagnostic uncertainty that remains actionable.
Discharge checklist
Discharge only when: stable room-air oxygenation at rest and with ambulation as appropriate, work of breathing resolved or mild, clear diagnosis, treatment response durable, no high-risk alternate diagnosis, meds/equipment available, and return precautions given.

Return precautions: worsening dyspnea, chest pain, syncope, fever/rigors, hemoptysis, new confusion, inability to speak full sentences, or oxygen saturation below patient-specific threshold.

Dyspnea Hub Algorithm

Sick Check First — Work of breathing and mental status outrank pulse ox alone. Position upright, monitor/IV/ECG, oxygen target by hypercapnic risk.

Time-critical exclusion? — Sort the airway/ventilation problem first, then the cause.

Obstructive crisis (asthma/COPD) — Silent chest, exhaustion, AMS, severe air-trapping. Maximize bronchodilation and NIV early; avoid rushed intubation unless failing.

SCAPE / acute pulmonary edema — Hypertensive, frothy, diffuse B-lines, severe distress. NIV + nitrates for hypertensive pulmonary edema phenotype.

Pulmonary embolism — Pleuritic, syncope, hypoxia, RV strain, VTE risk. Unstable PE is a resuscitation diagnosis.

Pneumothorax / tension — Unilateral absent sounds, sudden pleuritic pain, trauma/procedure. Tension physiology is treated clinically before imaging.

No exclusion — initial bundle — Default bundle while diagnosis clarifies: position, oxygen target, monitor/IV/ECG, CXR/POCUS, treat cause in parallel.

Pneumonia / sepsis physiology — Fever, cough, infiltrate, sepsis physiology. Treat hypoxia and sepsis early while checking for cardiac/PE mimics.

Anaphylaxis — Wheeze with allergen, hives, angioedema, hypotension, GI symptoms. IM epinephrine first — do not delay for adjuncts.

Cardiac dyspnea / ACS / arrhythmia — Chest pain, ischemic ECG, arrhythmia, shock. ECG plus POCUS guides cardiac dyspnea quickly.

CO / methemoglobinemia — Cyanosis with normal SpO2, smoke/exposure, chocolate blood. Use co-oximetry when exposure or saturation gap is plausible.

Reassess after bundle — The dyspnea patient can improve quickly or crash quickly. Reassess physiology, not just the diagnosis label.

Imaging strategy — CXR plus POCUS first for many; CT/CTA only when stable enough and actionable.

Disposition — Disposition follows oxygen requirement, work of breathing, trajectory, and cause.

ICU / resus admission — Escalate early. Dyspnea deterioration is often abrupt.

Admit / observe — Use cause-specific consult for final destination and orders.

Discharge checklist — Document ambulation/room-air status when relevant.
