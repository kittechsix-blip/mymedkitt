Shortness of Breath: Sick Check, Then Cause

Adults ≥18 in the ED with undifferentiated shortness of breath. This is a triage hub, not a single-disease protocol — each named cause links to its own consult. Excludes primary pediatric airway, trauma-primary resuscitation, and pre-hospital management.

⚠ Work of breathing and mental status outrank pulse ox alone — a normal SpO₂ does not mean the patient is safe.
⚠ Tension pneumothorax: unilateral absent breath sounds + shock → decompress before imaging.
⚠ Anaphylaxis / airway angioedema: stridor, urticaria, exposure → IM epinephrine now, before adjuncts.
⚠ Normal pulse ox does not exclude CO poisoning — use co-oximetry whenever exposure is possible.
⚠ Do not transport crashing dyspnea to CT without an airway, oxygen, pressor, and procedure plan.

3 · Time-critical exclusions — pick the best fit

Sort the airway / ventilation problem first, then the cause. Tap a box in the pathway diagram to focus that cause's first-five-minutes bundle here.

Obstructive crisis — asthma / COPD
Continuous albuterol for severe asthma, ipratropium, systemic steroid, magnesium for severe asthma, and NIV for COPD / hypercapnic failure when cooperative and protecting the airway. Intubation warning: severe obstruction is a physiologically difficult airway — use a low rate, long expiratory time, permissive hypercapnia, and disconnect to allow exhalation if peri-arrest auto-PEEP occurs. Opens Asthma Exacerbation or COPD Exacerbation.[6][7]
SCAPE / acute pulmonary edema
Upright, NIV now, high-dose nitrates when hypertensive SCAPE physiology is present, ECG/troponin, POCUS for B-lines and LV function; avoid reflex large fluid boluses. Pitfall: wheeze can be cardiac asthma — diffuse B-lines + hypertension + acute distress push toward NIV/nitrates, not nebulizers. Opens CHF Exacerbation and SCAPE High-Dose Nitroglycerin.[8]
Pulmonary embolism
Oxygen, monitor, IV, bedside echo if unstable, anticoagulation if PE is likely and bleeding risk is acceptable, CT pulmonary angiography when stable. Shock + RV strain = massive PE pathway and a PERT / intervention discussion. Do not send a crashing obstructive-shock PE patient to CT without resuscitation, pressor, airway, and a lysis/intervention plan. Opens PE Treatment.[4]
Pneumothorax / tension
If hypotension, severe distress, or peri-arrest: decompress before imaging. If stable: lung POCUS and CXR. Watch ventilated patients and severe asthma/COPD closely — positive pressure can convert a small pneumothorax into a tension. Opens Pneumothorax.[3]
Pneumonia / sepsis physiology
Oxygen, lactate if sepsis physiology, blood cultures if they will not delay antibiotics, antibiotics by severity and risk, cautious fluids if shock but reassess lungs / IVC / heart frequently. POCUS can find consolidation, effusion, B-lines, and cardiac dysfunction when the CXR is lagging behind the patient. Opens Pneumonia or Sepsis Management.[5]
Anaphylaxis
IM epinephrine now for airway / breathing / circulation involvement; repeat q5–15 min as needed; oxygen, fluids, albuterol for bronchospasm, early epinephrine infusion for refractory shock. Pitfall: antihistamines and steroids are adjuncts — they do not rescue airway, bronchospasm, or shock. Opens Anaphylaxis Management.[9]
Cardiac dyspnea / ACS / arrhythmia
ECG, monitor, troponin/BNP as helpful, POCUS for LV / RV / pericardium / lungs, treat dysrhythmia instability immediately, and keep PE and dissection in the differential when dyspnea and chest pain travel together. Opens STEMI, NSTEMI, A-Fib RVR, or CHF Exacerbation.[9]
CO / methemoglobinemia / dyshemoglobin
100% oxygen if CO is possible; co-oximetry because standard SpO₂ may mislead; look for smoke/fire, generator/heater, or topical anesthetic / dapsone / nitrate exposure and cyanosis out of proportion to PaO₂. Pitfall: a normal pulse ox does not exclude CO poisoning. Opens CO Toxicity or Methemoglobinemia.[9]

4 · Rescue bundle + reassess

  1. Upright positioning.
  2. Oxygen target: 94-98% for most; 88-92% for hypercapnic risk.
  3. Monitor, IV, ECG.
  4. CXR when stable; lung/cardiac POCUS when the diagnosis is uncertain or the patient is sick.
  5. VBG/ABG when ventilatory failure, severe COPD/asthma, toxic cause, or altered mental status is possible.
  6. Treat in parallel: nebulizers for wheeze, NIV for COPD/CHF physiology without contraindication, antibiotics for infection/sepsis, IM epinephrine for anaphylaxis.
Reassess every 5-15 minutes
In severe dyspnea, reassess every 5-15 minutes: work of breathing, mental status, RR, ETCO₂/VBG trend, oxygen requirement, and response to therapy. Reassess physiology, not just the diagnosis label — the patient can improve quickly or crash quickly.[9]

6 · Disposition

Disposition follows oxygen requirement, work of breathing, trajectory, and cause — not the label alone.

ICU / resus
NIV or intubation, persistent severe work of breathing, shock, rising CO₂/acidosis, massive PE, severe asthma/COPD with exhaustion, SCAPE not rapidly improving, anaphylaxis needing infusion, or unclear diagnosis with unstable physiology.
Admit / observe
New oxygen requirement, persistent tachypnea, recurrent symptoms after treatment, pneumonia with risk features, COPD/CHF needing repeated therapy, PE workup/treatment, social inability to manage therapy, or actionable diagnostic uncertainty.
Discharge checklist
Discharge only when: stable room-air oxygenation at rest and with ambulation, work of breathing resolved or mild, clear diagnosis, durable treatment response, no high-risk alternate diagnosis, and meds/equipment available.

  • Hemoptysis
  • New confusion
  • Inability to speak full sentences
  • Oxygen saturation below the patient-specific threshold
