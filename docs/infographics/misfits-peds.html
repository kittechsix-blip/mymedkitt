THE MISFITS: Approach to the Sick Neonate (0-28 Days)

Neonates 0-28 days old presenting critically ill or with nonspecific findings (lethargy, poor feeding, apnea) in the ED. THE MISFITS is a differential-diagnosis mnemonic, not a single linear protocol — use the category selector below to jump to the suspected diagnosis. Excludes well-appearing febrile infants managed by outpatient risk-stratification pathways and infants >28 days old.

⚠ Check glucose IMMEDIATELY in any sick neonate — treat glucose <50 mg/dL; <40 mg/dL or symptomatic is critical.
⚠ Non-accidental trauma is a mandated-reporting diagnosis — bruising in a non-mobile infant, patterned bruising, or multiple injuries in different healing stages requires skeletal survey, non-contrast head CT, and dilated ophthalmologic exam.
⚠ CAH triad — hyponatremia + hyperkalemia + hypoglycemia: give Hydrocortisone immediately if suspected; do NOT wait for 17-OHP/cortisol/ACTH confirmation.
⚠ Suspect an inborn error of metabolism with unexplained encephalopathy, hyperammonemia, high-anion-gap acidosis, or hypoglycemia refractory to treatment — stop protein intake, start IV dextrose, consult genetics/metabolism emergently.
⚠ Neonatal sepsis: obtain blood culture before antibiotics if feasible, but do NOT delay empiric Ampicillin + Gentamicin to get it.
⚠ HSV can kill without any vesicles present (20-40% of cases) — start Acyclovir empirically on any concern; do NOT wait for PCR results.
⚠ NEC: pneumoperitoneum, a fixed dilated bowel loop, or deterioration despite medical management are surgical emergencies — NPO, NGT decompression, broad-spectrum antibiotics, and an immediate surgical consult.
⚠ BILIOUS (green) VOMITING IN A NEONATE IS VOLVULUS UNTIL PROVEN OTHERWISE — bowel can go from viable to necrotic in hours; do not delay emergent upper-GI series and surgical consult, or go straight to OR if unstable.
⚠ Neonatal seizures are often subtle — eye deviation, lip smacking, cycling movements, apnea — correct metabolic causes first (glucose, calcium, magnesium), then Phenobarbital loading dose.
⚠ Empiric antibiotics for any sick neonate: Ampicillin + Gentamicin, and add Acyclovir if any HSV risk factor is present — do not wait on antibiotics while pursuing an LP.

2 · What to do now

Select a primary concern above
Or use the algorithm below — pick Triage, work through THE MISFITS category by category, or jump straight to a suspected diagnosis.
Trauma / NAT — mandated reporting
High index of suspicion for non-accidental trauma. Red flags: inconsistent/changing history, delay seeking care, bruising in a non-mobile infant, multiple injuries in different healing stages. Get a skeletal survey, non-contrast head CT, dilated fundoscopic exam. Physicians are mandated reporters.
Heart / CHD — check 4-extremity sats and femorals
Cyanosis not improving with O2, differential cyanosis, weak/absent femoral pulses (coarctation). If ductal-dependent CHD suspected, start PGE1 0.05-0.1 mcg/kg/min BEFORE echo confirmation. SVT: HR >220-240 in infant — vagal maneuvers then adenosine 0.1 mg/kg.
CAH — give hydrocortisone now, don't wait for labs
Classic triad: hyponatremia + hyperkalemia + hypoglycemia. NS boluses for shock, D10 for hypoglycemia, Hydrocortisone 25 mg IV (neonates) or 2 mg/kg immediately — do not delay for 17-OHP confirmation.
Hypoglycemia — D10W bolus, recheck in 30 min
Glucose <50 mg/dL requires intervention; <40 mg/dL or symptomatic is critical. D10W 2 mL/kg IV push (200 mg/kg), follow with D10W maintenance infusion, recheck glucose in 30 minutes, target >50 mg/dL.
IEM — stop protein, start dextrose, call genetics
Suspect with unexplained encephalopathy, recurrent vomiting, high-anion-gap acidosis, hyperammonemia, or hypoglycemia refractory to treatment. Stop feeds, start D10W at 1.5× maintenance, treat hyperammonemia, emergent genetics/metabolism consult.
Sepsis — cultures then empiric antibiotics, don't delay
Risk factors: PROM >18h, maternal GBS colonization, chorioamnionitis, prematurity. Ampicillin 50 mg/kg + Gentamicin 4 mg/kg; add Acyclovir 20 mg/kg if any HSV risk. LP if stable.
HSV — start acyclovir now, do not wait for PCR
Mortality approaches 80% untreated; vesicles absent in 20-40% of cases. Acyclovir 20 mg/kg IV q8h immediately if suspected. Duration: SEM 14 days; CNS/disseminated 21 days.
Formula mishap — check mixing technique
Over-dilution → water intoxication/hyponatremia/seizures. Under-dilution → hypernatremia/dehydration. Ask exact scoops-to-water ratio; correct electrolytes slowly; involve social work.
NEC — NPO, antibiotics, surgical consult
Pneumatosis intestinalis is pathognomonic; portal venous gas is severe; pneumoperitoneum is a surgical emergency. NPO, NGT decompression, IV fluids, Ampicillin 50 mg/kg + Gentamicin 4 mg/kg + Metronidazole 15 mg/kg, surgical consult.
Volvulus — bilious vomiting = OR until proven otherwise
BILIOUS VOMITING IN A NEONATE IS VOLVULUS UNTIL PROVEN OTHERWISE. Bowel can go from viable to necrotic in hours. NPO, NGT, aggressive fluids, emergent upper-GI series, immediate surgical consult — go straight to OR if unstable.
Toxins — detailed exposure and medication history
Breast-milk drug transfer (opioids, benzodiazepines), environmental exposures, medication dosing errors, neonatal abstinence syndrome. Toxicology screen (urine, meconium), poison control consultation.
Seizures — correct metabolic causes, then phenobarbital
Often subtle: eye deviation, lip smacking, cycling movements, apnea. Correct glucose/calcium/magnesium first, then Phenobarbital 20 mg/kg loading (may repeat 10 mg/kg ×2, max 40 mg/kg total). If refractory: Fosphenytoin 20 mg PE/kg or Levetiracetam 40-60 mg/kg.
Unstable — resuscitate, access, empiric antibiotics
ETT 3.0-3.5 if intubating. Fluid boluses 10 mL/kg (smaller than older peds). D10 2 mL/kg for glucose. Epinephrine 0.01-0.03 mg/kg IV/IO. Empiric Ampicillin + Gentamicin ± Acyclovir. All sick neonates are admitted.

7 · Unstable — Resuscitation, Access, Antibiotics, Admit

  1. AIRWAY: ETT size 3.0-3.5 for term newborn; video laryngoscopy if available
  2. BREATHING: target SpO2 >90% (accept lower if CHD suspected); avoid hyperoxia in preterm infants; PPV 20-25 cmH2O if needed
  3. CIRCULATION: peripheral IV, UVC, or IO; fluid boluses 10 mL/kg NS (smaller than older pediatric 20 mL/kg); avoid aggressive fluid if cardiac cause suspected
  4. DRUGS: glucose D10 2 mL/kg; antibiotics Ampicillin + Gentamicin ± Acyclovir; PGE1 if CHD suspected; epinephrine 0.01-0.03 mg/kg IV/IO (0.1 mg/kg ETT)
  5. TEMPERATURE: neonates lose heat rapidly — warm room, radiant warmer, warm fluids

THE — Trauma, Heart, Endocrine — Trauma/NAT, Heart/CHD, CAH, hypoglycemia.

MIS — Metabolic, Inborn Errors, Sepsis — IEM, sepsis, HSV — the most common serious diagnoses in a sick neonate.

FITS — Formula, Intestinal, Toxins, Seizures — Formula mishap, NEC/volvulus, toxins, seizures.

Trauma / NAT — Red flags: bruising in non-mobile infant, patterned bruising, injuries in different healing stages. Mandated reporting.

Heart / CHD — PGE1 0.05-0.1 mcg/kg/min if ductal-dependent; SVT HR >220-240, adenosine 0.1 mg/kg.

CAH / Adrenal Crisis — Triad: hyponatremia, hyperkalemia, hypoglycemia. Hydrocortisone 25 mg (neonate) or 2 mg/kg immediately.

Hypoglycemia — D10W 2 mL/kg IV push (200 mg/kg), recheck in 30 min, target >50 mg/dL.

Inborn Errors of Metabolism — Stop protein, D10W at 1.5x maintenance, treat hyperammonemia, emergent genetics consult.

Sepsis — Ampicillin 50 mg/kg + Gentamicin 4 mg/kg; add Acyclovir 20 mg/kg if HSV risk; LP if stable.

Toxins — Breast-milk drugs, environmental exposures, dosing errors, neonatal abstinence syndrome.

Unstable — Resuscitation, Access, Antibiotics, Admit — ETT 3.0-3.5, fluid boluses 10 mL/kg, epinephrine 0.01-0.03 mg/kg, empiric antibiotics, all sick neonates admitted.

  • HSV PCR if risk factors present

6 · FITS — Formula, Intestinal, Toxins, Seizures

Over-dilution: water intoxication, hyponatremia, hypochloremia, seizures. Under-dilution: hypernatremia, dehydration, can cause intracranial hemorrhage. Contaminated formula: Cronobacter (powdered formula) can cause meningitis, NEC. Ask exact scoops-to-water ratio; correct electrolytes slowly; involve social work.

  • Surgical consult

  • Aggressive fluid resuscitation
  • EMERGENT surgical consult
  • Broad-spectrum antibiotics
  • Prepare for OR — go straight to OR if unstable, do not wait for imaging

Toxin categories and evaluation

  • Breast milk drug transfer: opioids, benzodiazepines, antidepressants, methadone, alcohol, cocaine/amphetamines
  • Environmental exposures: lead (rare in neonates), carbon monoxide, methemoglobinemia (well water nitrates)
  • Medication errors: wrong drug, dosing errors, topical toxicity (benzocaine, lidocaine)
  • Neonatal abstinence syndrome: irritability, high-pitched cry, tremors, poor feeding — Finnegan scoring, treat with morphine/methadone if severe
  • Evaluation: detailed medication/substance history, urine/meconium toxicology, poison control consult

  • EEG (confirm seizures)
  • MRI brain
  • Consider ammonia, lactate, IEM workup

7 · Unstable — Resuscitation, Access, Antibiotics, Admit

  • AIRWAY: ETT size 3.0-3.5 for term newborn; video laryngoscopy if available
  • BREATHING: target SpO2 >90% (accept lower if CHD suspected); avoid hyperoxia in preterm infants; PPV 20-25 cmH2O if needed
  • CIRCULATION: peripheral IV, UVC, or IO; fluid boluses 10 mL/kg NS (smaller than older pediatric 20 mL/kg); avoid aggressive fluid if cardiac cause suspected
  • DRUGS: glucose D10 2 mL/kg; antibiotics Ampicillin + Gentamicin ± Acyclovir; PGE1 if CHD suspected; epinephrine 0.01-0.03 mg/kg IV/IO (0.1 mg/kg ETT)
  • TEMPERATURE: neonates lose heat rapidly — warm room, radiant warmer, warm fluids

Epinephrine: 0.01-0.03 mg/kg IV/IO (1:10,000) IV/IO preferred over ET — Repeat every 3-5 minutes; flush with 1-3 mL NS. ET route needs a higher dose (0.05-0.1 mg/kg) — unpredictable absorption.

Peripheral IV | First choice — hand, foot, scalp veins, typically 24g

UVC | Available in first week; identify single large thin-walled vein; insert 5F catheter 2-4 cm until blood return; confirm above diaphragm on XR

Standard | Ampicillin 50 mg/kg q8h + Gentamicin 4 mg/kg q24h

NEC / abdominal | Add Metronidazole 15 mg/kg IV q12h

Late-onset (>7 days) | Consider adding Vancomycin 15 mg/kg IV q8-12h if central line, prior NICU admission, or Staph concern

Scenario | Duration

Culture-negative, improving | 48-72h

Bacteremia | 10-14 days

Meningitis | 14-21 days

Disposition | Criteria

NICU | Prematurity; respiratory support beyond nasal cannula; TPN required; cardiovascular support (pressors, PGE1); active resuscitation needs; complex congenital anomalies; seizures requiring monitoring
