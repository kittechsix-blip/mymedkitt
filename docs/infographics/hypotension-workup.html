Undifferentiated Hypotension: RUSH, Classify, Resuscitate

Adults ≥18 in the ED with undifferentiated hypotension or shock (MAP <65 or SBP <90). Excludes pediatric-specific dosing beyond the weight-based examples shown, pregnancy-specific pathways, and primary trauma resuscitation — those follow their own protocols.

⚠ Time is perfusion. Each hour spent at MAP <65 raises mortality by ~5%. Resuscitate in parallel with your workup — do not wait for a diagnosis to start.
⚠ Norepinephrine can start peripherally. Do NOT delay a first-line pressor waiting for central access; run it through a good proximal IV and place a central line once the patient is stabilized.
⚠ Cardiogenic, obstructive, and PE shock: AVOID aggressive fluid loading. A dilated RV or a failing LV does not tolerate volume — small boluses only, guided by POCUS.
⚠ Anaphylaxis: epinephrine IM comes FIRST, before antihistamines or steroids. Adults 0.3–0.5 mg IM (1:1000) anterolateral thigh, repeat q5–15 min.
⚠ Tension pneumothorax and tamponade are clinical/echo diagnoses — decompress or drain at the bedside; do not wait for a chest x-ray or CT.
⚠ Refractory shock + steroid history = adrenal crisis until proven otherwise. Give hydrocortisone 100 mg IV; do not delay for cortisol results.

1 · Recognize shock

What counts as shock
Absolute hypotension: SBP <90 mmHg or MAP <65 mmHg. Relative hypotension: a SBP drop >40 mmHg from baseline. Shock Index = HR ÷ SBP; a value >1.0 suggests significant instability even when the SBP still looks acceptable.[1][2]

Triad of hypotension + bradycardia + warm, dry skin from loss of sympathetic tone (spinal cord injury, usually T6 or above). Adrenal crisis — hydrocortisone stat, do not wait for labs
Suspect in shock refractory to fluids and vasopressors with a steroid-use history, known adrenal insufficiency, or recent steroid taper. Give hydrocortisone 100 mg IV immediately, then 200 mg/24h (or 50 mg IV q6h). Draw cortisol/ACTH first if feasible — but do NOT delay treatment. Give D50 if hypoglycemic; expect hemodynamic improvement in 1–2 h.[7]
Cardiogenic — support the pump, don't flood it
Norepinephrine is first-line (maintains coronary perfusion); add dobutamine for low output. AVOID aggressive fluids. Treat the cause: STEMI → emergent PCI, arrhythmia → cardiovert/pace; escalate to mechanical circulatory support for refractory cases.[8]
Hemorrhagic — blood, not crystalloid
Activate the Massive Transfusion Protocol (target 1:1:1 pRBC:FFP:platelets), give TXA 1 g IV over 10 min within 3 h of injury then 1 g over 8 h, and pursue source control. Run permissive hypotension (SBP 80–90) until surgical control — except in traumatic brain injury, where you keep the MAP up.[9]

Non-hemorrhagic hypovolemia — crystalloid + reassess
LR 20–30 mL/kg bolus (avoids hyperchloremic acidosis), then reassess after each bolus with exam, IVC, and urine output. Treat the driver: DKA/HHS, burns (Parkland), pancreatitis, severe GI losses.
Tamponade — drain it
Echo-guided pericardiocentesis (subxiphoid) is the fix; even a small drainage can transform hemodynamics. Bridge with IV fluid to raise preload and norepinephrine for BP; avoid intubation if you can — positive pressure worsens filling.[10]
Tension PTX — decompress it
Clinical diagnosis: needle decompression with a 14–16G catheter at the 5th intercostal space, anterior axillary line (or 2nd ICS midclavicular), then a chest tube immediately after.
Massive PE — lyse, don't flood
AVOID intubation and aggressive fluids (the RV is already dilated). Give high-flow O₂, norepinephrine for BP, and activate PERT. Alteplase 100 mg IV over 2 h (standard) or a 50 mg bolus in cardiac arrest.[11]

4 · Vasopressors → target MAP ≥65

Norepinephrine is first-line for essentially every shock type once fluids are inadequate. Titrate to **MAP ≥65** (higher if chronically hypertensive).{{ref13}}

Norepinephrine (first-line): 0.01–0.05 mcg/kg/min, titrate to MAP ≥65 (max ~0.5–1) IV — peripheral OK to start — Do not delay for a central line. Titrate every 2–5 min.

Vasopressin (second-line, add-on): 0.03–0.04 U/min (fixed, no titration) IV — Add when norepinephrine exceeds ~0.25–0.5 mcg/kg/min (decatecholaminization).

Epinephrine (low output + bradycardia): 0.01–0.2 mcg/kg/min IV infusion — Useful when the LV is failing; expect tachycardia and a lactate bump.

5 · One-glance algorithm

Undifferentiated hypotension (MAP <65 / SBP <90) — Shock Index >1 flags instability even with a borderline SBP. Each hour at MAP <65 adds ~5% mortality.

Resuscitate + workup in parallel: 2 large-bore IVs · monitor · ECG · lactate — Do not wait for a diagnosis to begin fluids and monitoring.

RUSH exam (HI-MAP) — Heart, IVC, Morrison's/FAST, Aorta, Pneumothorax in 2–5 min.

Which POCUS pattern? — Match the heart + IVC picture to one of four shock categories.

Distributive: septic, anaphylactic, neurogenic, or adrenal? — Hyperdynamic LV + flat IVC. Septic is most common; anaphylaxis, neurogenic (SCI), and adrenal crisis are the alternatives.

Septic: Hour-1 bundle + empiric antibiotics — Lactate, cultures, antibiotics, 30 mL/kg crystalloid, pressors to MAP ≥65.

Anaphylactic: epinephrine IM first — 0.3–0.5 mg IM, repeat q5–15 min; aggressive fluids; adjuncts after.

Cardiogenic: norepinephrine ± dobutamine; avoid fluids — Depressed LV + full IVC. STEMI → PCI; escalate to MCS if refractory.

Hypovolemic: bleeding? — Small kissing LV + collapsed IVC. Free fluid on FAST points to hemorrhage.

Non-hemorrhagic: crystalloid 20–30 mL/kg, reassess — LR preferred; treat DKA/HHS, burns, GI losses.
