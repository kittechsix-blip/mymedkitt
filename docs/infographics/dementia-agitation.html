Dementia & Agitation: Safety, Capacity & Texas Legal Pathway

Adults with dementia / major neurocognitive disorder presenting with agitation in the ED, Texas practice setting. Excludes primary psychiatric agitation without an underlying neurocognitive disorder, pediatrics, and isolated acute intoxication without dementia (see the Delirium and Organic vs Psychiatric Emergency consults for those pathways).

⚠ Do not default dementia agitation to psychiatric detention; screen for delirium and reversible medical triggers first — dementia diagnosis alone does not remove capacity, and capacity is decision-specific and time-specific.
⚠ Restraints/seclusion require an immediate physical safety indication, the least restrictive effective method, a time-limited order, monitoring, reassessment, and discontinuation as soon as possible.
⚠ Use antipsychotics only when danger, severe distress, or blocked essential care justifies the risk; avoid routine benzodiazepines in dementia/delirium unless withdrawal, seizure, catatonia, severe stimulant toxicity, or a specific anxiety/panic indication is driving the agitation.
⚠ All antipsychotics carry an FDA boxed warning for increased mortality (1.6-1.7×) in elderly patients with dementia-related psychosis — use only when nonpharmacologic measures are inadequate.
⚠ DO NOT combine parenteral benzodiazepines with IM olanzapine — case reports of fatal respiratory depression and hypotension.
⚠ Lewy body/Parkinson dementia: avoid or use extreme caution with haloperidol, droperidol, risperidone, metoclopramide, and prochlorperazine — high neuroleptic sensitivity risk; prefer quetiapine if medication is unavoidable.
⚠ Falls/head strike, anticoagulation, new headache, vomiting, seizure, focal deficit, or unexplained depressed consciousness should redirect toward neurologic evaluation — subdural hematoma and nonconvulsive seizure can present as agitation in dementia.
⚠ Medical incapacity uses implied emergency consent or Texas Health & Safety Code Chapter 313 surrogate consent — it is NOT automatically a psychiatric hold, and Chapter 313 surrogate consent does not authorize voluntary inpatient mental-health services or ECT.
⚠ Do not label the medical incapacity pathway as a psychiatric hold unless Texas Chapter 573 criteria are independently met.
⚠ Texas Chapter 573 emergency detention requires qualifying mental illness plus substantial risk of serious harm because of that illness, and insufficient time to obtain a warrant — dementia/neurocognitive disorder alone is excluded from the Texas statutory mental illness definition.

1 · Overview

Agitation in dementia is a medical, neurologic, ethical, and legal problem. Do not default to a psychiatric hold before screening for delirium, pain, medication toxicity, infection, urinary retention, constipation, trauma, hypoxia, hypoglycemia, and caregiver/environmental triggers.

  • Is there immediate danger requiring rescue sedation or restraint?
  • Is this delirium or a reversible medical trigger on top of dementia?
  • Does the patient have decision-specific capacity for the current decision?
  • Is this a Texas mental-health emergency detention case, or a medical incapacity/surrogate-consent case?
  • What is the least restrictive safe plan?

2 · What to do now

Immediate danger — safety first
Call adequate staff/security; remove weapons, sharps, cords, and excess equipment. One calm lead communicator; reduce stimulation. Check glucose, oxygenation, temperature, vitals, trauma, and medication exposure while de-escalating. If danger prevents essential care, use targeted rescue medication and/or restraint — immediate physical safety indication, least restrictive effective method, time-limited order, monitoring, reassessment, and discontinuation as soon as possible.
PO/ODT accepted — lowest effective oral dose

IM/IV rescue needed
Haloperidol 0.5-1 mg IM/IV or olanzapine 2.5-5 mg IM at the lowest effective dose; reserve lorazepam for withdrawal, seizure, catatonia, or specific severe anxiety/stimulant indications. DO NOT combine parenteral benzodiazepines with IM olanzapine.
Lewy body / Parkinson dementia — high neuroleptic sensitivity
Avoid or use extreme caution with haloperidol, droperidol, risperidone, metoclopramide, and prochlorperazine. Prefer quetiapine 12.5-25 mg PO if cooperative. Consider NMS/malignant catatonia if rigidity, fever, or autonomic instability follows recent dopamine-blocker exposure.
Neurologic red flag — redirect to neuro workup

Reversible trigger found — treat before escalating

Has capacity — respect the decision

Lacks capacity — surrogate/emergency consent, not automatic psych hold

Screen for Chapter 573 vs medical incapacity

5 · Reversible trigger screen

The common error is treating the behavior while missing the driver.

Trigger categoryHigh-yield actions
Pain / retention / constipationBladder scan, rectal/abdominal exam, treat pain directly, reassess before escalating medication
Infection / metabolic / hypoxiaGlucose/SpO2/temp/vitals/EKG; CBC, CMP (Na/Ca/renal), Mg, UA, CXR, VBG/ABG as indicated
Medication / intox / withdrawalStop nonessential anticholinergics/sedatives/opioids; treat withdrawal specifically
Trauma / neuro eventFall/head strike, anticoagulation, new headache, seizure, focal deficit → head CT/neuro pathway
No reversible trigger foundNonpharmacologic dementia care plan
Do not assume agitation is psychiatric or dementia progression when neurologic red flags exist: Fall/head strike, anticoagulation, new headache, vomiting, seizure, focal deficit, severe hypertension, new gait inability, or unexplained depressed consciousness. Consider ICH, SAH, Status Epilepticus, or Cervical Spine.

Nonpharmacologic first: reduce noise/light/crowding, family/caregiver at bedside, hearing aids/glasses/interpreter, food/fluids/toileting/sleep protection, validate distress. For chronic outpatient treatment, brexpiprazole (FDA approval May 2023) and dextromethorphan-bupropion (FDA approval April 2026) are approved for agitation associated with dementia due to Alzheimer disease, but they are not ED rescue sedatives and require outpatient prescribing/monitoring context.

6 · Capacity & consent

Capacity is decision-specific and can fluctuate. Dementia diagnosis alone does not remove capacity; agitation alone does not prove incapacity. Identify the exact decision now: leaving, refusing test/treatment, accepting medication, transfer, discharge plan, or consent for procedure.

AbilityWhat is assessed
UnderstandingGrasps the relevant facts about the decision
AppreciationApplies those facts to their own situation
ReasoningManipulates the information logically to reach a choice
CommunicationConsistently communicates a stable choice

Respect the decision, even if it is medically unwise. Provide informed refusal: risks, benefits, alternatives, return precautions. Offer a safer alternative plan. Do not use dementia diagnosis alone to override refusal, and do not use a Texas mental-health hold unless statutory mental illness criteria and danger criteria are independently met. Document the four abilities — understanding, appreciation, reasoning, communication — and the actual patient responses.

Immediate medical emergency: treat under implied emergency consent when delay risks serious harm, using least restrictive means to prevent elopement or injury. Non-emergent: look for medical power of attorney, guardian, or advance directive. Texas HSC Chapter 313 allows listed adult surrogates when no guardian/medical POA is reasonably available, in order: spouse, adult children, parents, nearest living relative; if none available, another uninvolved physician may concur with treatment. Surrogate consent under Chapter 313 does not authorize voluntary inpatient mental-health services or ECT. If conflict or no safe surrogate: involve social work, case management, ethics, legal/risk, APS, guardianship pathway.

Treat reversible causes first when the decision can safely wait; reassess after pain control, hydration, retention relief, glucose/oxygen correction, medication washout, or sleep. Use interpreters, hearing aids, glasses, family/caregiver support. Raise the rigor for high-stakes decisions such as refusal of admission, surgery, imaging for head bleed, dialysis, or safe discharge. Consider Capacity Assessment, psychiatry, neurology, geriatrics, ethics, or legal/risk.

7 · Texas legal pathway

Texas mental-health emergency detention requires a mental illness plus substantial risk of serious harm because of that mental illness and insufficient time to obtain a warrant. The Texas Health & Safety Code definition of mental illness excludes dementia — therefore, dementia/neurocognitive disorder alone is excluded from the Texas statutory mental illness definition, and dementia agitation alone is usually a medical incapacity/safety/surrogate problem, not a Chapter 573 psychiatric detention problem. A separate qualifying mental illness may coexist and can change the pathway.

Legal categoryPathway
Dementia/neurocognitive disorder onlyMedical incapacity/surrogate/safety pathway
Coexisting mental illness + dangerMay meet Texas emergency detention criteria
Neglect, abuse, exploitation, unsafe homeAPS/social work/guardianship pathway
Transfer or refusal issueEMTALA/capacity/stabilization documentation

Use this pathway for dementia, delirium, intoxication, brain injury, metabolic illness, or another medical/neurocognitive condition without separate Texas mental illness criteria. Continue EMTALA medical screening/stabilizing evaluation; if lacks capacity and unsafe to leave, document medical incapacity and why leaving creates serious medical risk; use sitter/family/security/elopement precautions and least-restrictive restraint only if immediate safety requires it; identify surrogate/guardian/medical POA; treat reversible drivers; admit medical/neurology/geriatrics, arrange safe discharge, or transfer. Do not label this a psychiatric hold unless Chapter 573 criteria are independently met.

Texas Chapter 573 emergency detention may apply only if: the person has qualifying mental illness, and because of that mental illness there is substantial risk of serious harm to self or others unless immediately restrained, and there is insufficient time to obtain a warrant. Examples that may qualify if danger criteria are met: dementia plus separate acute psychosis, mania, severe depression with suicidal intent, or dangerous command hallucinations not explained solely by dementia/delirium. Dementia/neurocognitive disorder alone is excluded from the Texas statutory mental illness definition. Always follow local hospital legal/risk and law-enforcement workflow.

Federal rules do not create a generic dementia hold. EMTALA: Provide a medical screening exam for agitation/AMS, stabilize emergency medical conditions or arrange appropriate transfer. CMS restraints/seclusion: only for immediate physical safety when less restrictive interventions are ineffective, least restrictive effective method, not standing/PRN orders, discontinue at the earliest possible time, monitor and document. Antipsychotics used only to restrict movement/behavior can count as chemical restraint unless they are standard treatment/dosage for the condition.

Escalate beyond ED medication when the environment is unsafe: suspected abuse, neglect, exploitation, abandonment, medication theft; recurrent ED visits because care needs exceed home/facility capacity; no surrogate, family conflict, refusal of necessary placement, or high-risk discharge disagreement. Actions: social work/case management early, APS report per local policy, ethics consult for best-interest conflicts, legal/risk and guardianship pathway when ongoing incapacity prevents safe care decisions.

8 · Disposition & documentation

Disposition should match medical risk, capacity, caregiver capability, and legal pathway. The safest plan may be medical admission even when psychiatry is not the right destination.

Admit medically when: delirium or reversible medical trigger is suspected or confirmed; medication/restraint was needed and ongoing monitoring is required; unsafe elopement risk with lack of capacity; no safe surrogate/caregiver plan; ongoing pain, infection, retention, metabolic, trauma, seizure, or medication-toxicity management is needed; or the facility refuses return without a medical stabilization plan.

Discharge may be reasonable only when: no untreated emergency medical trigger is suspected; agitation is controlled without unsafe sedation or unresolved restraint need; capacity is intact for the discharge decision, or a valid surrogate accepts the plan; responsible caregiver/facility can supervise, administer meds, remove hazards, and return if worse; medication changes are conservative and communicated; clear follow-up is arranged (PCP/geriatrics/neurology/memory clinic/psychiatry).

  • Fever, fall/head strike, worsening confusion, new focal deficit, seizure
  • Chest pain / dyspnea, urinary retention, inability to eat/drink
  • Violence, caregiver unable to keep patient safe, medication adverse effects

Document: baseline mental status source and acute change timeline; reversible trigger assessment and treatments given; safety behaviors observed (objective threats, elopement attempts, violence, self-harm risk); capacity assessment using understanding, appreciation, reasoning, communication; legal pathway (medical incapacity/surrogate vs Chapter 573 criteria if used); surrogate/guardian/POA contacted and rationale; medication indication/dose/response/adverse-effect monitoring and boxed-warning risk/benefit; restraint/seclusion indication, alternatives attempted, order/monitoring, reassessments, time discontinued; disposition safety plan and who accepted responsibility.

For a semi-cooperative patient who still requires medication. Before giving antipsychotics when feasible: assess QTc/EKG risk, Parkinson/Lewy body dementia, prior adverse reactions, falls risk, aspiration risk, anticholinergic burden, and goals of care.

Quetiapine: 12.5-25 mg PO PO — Frail elderly or Parkinson/Lewy body concern; slower onset, less EPS than typical antipsychotics.

Risperidone: 0.25-0.5 mg PO/ODT PO/ODT — Mild antipsychotic effect needed and no Parkinson/Lewy body concern. (Lower, dementia-specific starting dose than drug-store's general "Mild agitation / delirium" 1-2 mg indication — frailty-adjusted per this consult.)

Olanzapine: 2.5-5 mg PO/ODT PO/ODT — More sedation needed. Avoid parenteral benzodiazepines near IM olanzapine. (Lower, dementia-specific PO dose than drug-store's general "Acute agitation / delirium" 5-10 mg PO indication.)

All antipsychotics carry mortality warnings (**1.6-1.7×**) in elderly patients with dementia-related psychosis; use only when symptoms are dangerous, severe, or cause significant distress after nonpharmacologic steps are inadequate.

Use the lowest effective dose and reassess frequently.

Haloperidol: 0.5-1 mg IM/IV IM/IV — May repeat cautiously. Avoid or use extreme caution with Parkinson/Lewy body dementia, prolonged QTc, severe EPS history.

Olanzapine: 2.5-5 mg IM IM — DO NOT combine parenteral benzodiazepines with IM olanzapine because of respiratory/hypotension risk.

Lorazepam: 1-2 mg IV/IM IV/IM — Reserve for alcohol/benzodiazepine withdrawal, seizure, catatonia, or specific severe anxiety/stimulant indications; otherwise benzodiazepines can worsen delirium, falls, and respiratory depression.

Weight-based lorazepam if a reserved indication applies (0.02 mg/kg, max 2 mg): 1.4 mg

After sedation: continuous safety observation, airway/aspiration precautions, repeat vitals, reassess pain/urinary retention/trauma, and restart the diagnostic pathway.

Suspect Lewy body/Parkinson dementia if visual hallucinations, fluctuating cognition, REM sleep behavior disorder, parkinsonism, or known diagnosis. Avoid or use extreme caution: **haloperidol, droperidol, risperidone, metoclopramide, and prochlorperazine** — high-potency D2 blockade.

Quetiapine: 12.5-25 mg PO PO — Lowest D2 receptor affinity of all antipsychotics — least likely to worsen motor symptoms.

Olanzapine: 2.5 mg IM (caution only) IM — Only with caution if IM rescue is necessary and benefits outweigh motor/neuroleptic sensitivity risk.

If severe rigidity, fever, autonomic instability, or recent dopamine-blocker exposure: consider [NMS](#/tree/nms) or malignant catatonia and stop dopamine blockers.

5 · Reversible trigger screen

The common error is treating the behavior while missing the driver.

Trigger category | High-yield actions

Pain / retention / constipation | Bladder scan, rectal/abdominal exam, treat pain directly, reassess before escalating medication

Infection / metabolic / hypoxia | Glucose/SpO2/temp/vitals/EKG; CBC, CMP (Na/Ca/renal), Mg, UA, CXR, VBG/ABG as indicated

Medication / intox / withdrawal | Stop nonessential anticholinergics/sedatives/opioids; treat withdrawal specifically

Trauma / neuro event | Fall/head strike, anticoagulation, new headache, seizure, focal deficit → head CT/neuro pathway

No reversible trigger found | Nonpharmacologic dementia care plan

Do not assume agitation is psychiatric or dementia progression when neurologic red flags exist: **Fall/head strike, anticoagulation, new headache, vomiting, seizure, focal deficit, severe hypertension, new gait inability, or unexplained depressed consciousness**. Consider [ICH](#/tree/ich), [SAH](#/tree/sah), [Status Epilepticus](#/tree/status-epilepticus), or [Cervical Spine](#/tree/cervical-spine).

Nonpharmacologic first: reduce noise/light/crowding, family/caregiver at bedside, hearing aids/glasses/interpreter, food/fluids/toileting/sleep protection, validate distress. For chronic outpatient treatment, brexpiprazole (FDA approval **May 2023**) and dextromethorphan-bupropion (FDA approval **April 2026**) are approved for agitation associated with dementia due to Alzheimer disease, but they are not ED rescue sedatives and require outpatient prescribing/monitoring context.

6 · Capacity & consent

Capacity is decision-specific and can fluctuate. Dementia diagnosis alone does not remove capacity; agitation alone does not prove incapacity. Identify the exact decision now: leaving, refusing test/treatment, accepting medication, transfer, discharge plan, or consent for procedure.

Ability | What is assessed

Understanding | Grasps the relevant facts about the decision

Appreciation | Applies those facts to their own situation

Reasoning | Manipulates the information logically to reach a choice

Communication | Consistently communicates a stable choice

Respect the decision, even if it is medically unwise. Provide informed refusal: risks, benefits, alternatives, return precautions. Offer a safer alternative plan. Do not use dementia diagnosis alone to override refusal, and do not use a Texas mental-health hold unless statutory mental illness criteria and danger criteria are independently met. Document the four abilities — **understanding, appreciation, reasoning, communication** — and the actual patient responses.

Immediate medical emergency: treat under implied emergency consent when delay risks serious harm, using least restrictive means to prevent elopement or injury. Non-emergent: look for medical power of attorney, guardian, or advance directive. Texas HSC Chapter 313 allows listed adult surrogates when no guardian/medical POA is reasonably available, in order: **spouse, adult children, parents, nearest living relative**; if none available, another uninvolved physician may concur with treatment. Surrogate consent under Chapter 313 **does not authorize voluntary inpatient mental-health services or ECT**. If conflict or no safe surrogate: involve social work, case management, ethics, legal/risk, APS, guardianship pathway.

Treat reversible causes first when the decision can safely wait; reassess after pain control, hydration, retention relief, glucose/oxygen correction, medication washout, or sleep. Use interpreters, hearing aids, glasses, family/caregiver support. Raise the rigor for high-stakes decisions such as refusal of admission, surgery, imaging for head bleed, dialysis, or safe discharge. Consider [Capacity Assessment](#/tree/capacity-assessment), psychiatry, neurology, geriatrics, ethics, or legal/risk.

7 · Texas legal pathway

Texas mental-health emergency detention requires a mental illness plus substantial risk of serious harm because of that mental illness and **insufficient time to obtain a warrant**. The Texas Health & Safety Code definition of mental illness excludes dementia — therefore, **dementia/neurocognitive disorder alone is excluded from the Texas statutory mental illness definition**, and dementia agitation alone is usually a medical incapacity/safety/surrogate problem, not a Chapter 573 psychiatric detention problem. A separate qualifying mental illness may coexist and can change the pathway.

Legal category | Pathway

Dementia/neurocognitive disorder only | Medical incapacity/surrogate/safety pathway

Coexisting mental illness + danger | May meet Texas emergency detention criteria

Neglect, abuse, exploitation, unsafe home | APS/social work/guardianship pathway

Transfer or refusal issue | EMTALA/capacity/stabilization documentation

Use this pathway for dementia, delirium, intoxication, brain injury, metabolic illness, or another medical/neurocognitive condition without separate Texas mental illness criteria. Continue EMTALA medical screening/stabilizing evaluation; if lacks capacity and unsafe to leave, document medical incapacity and why leaving creates serious medical risk; use sitter/family/security/elopement precautions and least-restrictive restraint only if immediate safety requires it; identify surrogate/guardian/medical POA; treat reversible drivers; admit medical/neurology/geriatrics, arrange safe discharge, or transfer. Do not label this a psychiatric hold unless Chapter 573 criteria are independently met.

Texas Chapter 573 emergency detention may apply only if: the person has qualifying mental illness, and because of that mental illness there is substantial risk of serious harm to self or others unless immediately restrained, and there is **insufficient time to obtain a warrant**. Examples that may qualify if danger criteria are met: dementia plus separate acute psychosis, mania, severe depression with suicidal intent, or dangerous command hallucinations not explained solely by dementia/delirium. Dementia/neurocognitive disorder alone is excluded from the Texas statutory mental illness definition. Always follow local hospital legal/risk and law-enforcement workflow.

Federal rules do not create a generic dementia hold. EMTALA: **Provide a medical screening exam for agitation/AMS**, stabilize emergency medical conditions or arrange appropriate transfer. CMS restraints/seclusion: only for immediate physical safety when less restrictive interventions are ineffective, least restrictive effective method, not standing/PRN orders, discontinue at the earliest possible time, monitor and document. Antipsychotics used only to restrict movement/behavior can count as chemical restraint unless they are standard treatment/dosage for the condition.

Escalate beyond ED medication when the environment is unsafe: suspected abuse, neglect, exploitation, abandonment, medication theft; recurrent ED visits because care needs exceed home/facility capacity; no surrogate, family conflict, refusal of necessary placement, or high-risk discharge disagreement. Actions: social work/case management early, APS report per local policy, ethics consult for best-interest conflicts, legal/risk and guardianship pathway when ongoing incapacity prevents safe care decisions.

8 · Disposition & documentation

Disposition should match medical risk, capacity, caregiver capability, and legal pathway. The safest plan may be medical admission even when psychiatry is not the right destination.

Admit medically when: delirium or reversible medical trigger is suspected or confirmed; medication/restraint was needed and ongoing monitoring is required; unsafe elopement risk with lack of capacity; no safe surrogate/caregiver plan; ongoing pain, infection, retention, metabolic, trauma, seizure, or medication-toxicity management is needed; or the facility refuses return without a medical stabilization plan.

Discharge may be reasonable only when: no untreated emergency medical trigger is suspected; agitation is controlled without unsafe sedation or unresolved restraint need; capacity is intact for the discharge decision, or a valid surrogate accepts the plan; responsible caregiver/facility can supervise, administer meds, remove hazards, and return if worse; medication changes are conservative and communicated; clear follow-up is arranged (PCP/geriatrics/neurology/memory clinic/psychiatry).

  • Fever, fall/head strike, worsening confusion, new focal deficit, seizure
  • Chest pain / dyspnea, urinary retention, inability to eat/drink
  • Violence, caregiver unable to keep patient safe, medication adverse effects

Document: baseline mental status source and acute change timeline; reversible trigger assessment and treatments given; safety behaviors observed (objective threats, elopement attempts, violence, self-harm risk); capacity assessment using **understanding, appreciation, reasoning, communication**; legal pathway (medical incapacity/surrogate vs Chapter 573 criteria if used); surrogate/guardian/POA contacted and rationale; medication indication/dose/response/adverse-effect monitoring and boxed-warning risk/benefit; restraint/seclusion indication, alternatives attempted, order/monitoring, reassessments, time discontinued; disposition safety plan and who accepted responsibility.

Highest-yield pitfalls (source critical-action list)

**Do not default dementia agitation to psychiatric detention** — screen for delirium and reversible medical triggers first.

**Dementia diagnosis alone does not remove capacity** — capacity is decision-specific and time-specific.

**Texas Chapter 573 emergency detention requires qualifying mental illness plus danger** — dementia is excluded from the statutory mental illness definition.

**Use antipsychotics only when danger, severe distress, or blocked essential care justifies risk** — avoid routine benzodiazepines.

**Restraints/seclusion require immediate safety indication, least restrictive method, monitoring, reassessment, and earliest discontinuation.**
