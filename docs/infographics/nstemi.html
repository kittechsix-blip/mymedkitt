NSTEMI: Anticoagulate, Risk-Stratify, Time the Cath

Adults ≥18 in the ED with confirmed NSTEMI (troponin rise plus ischemia). Excludes STEMI (needs emergent reperfusion), pregnancy, and pediatrics — each needs a modified pathway.

⚠ Aspirin 162–325 mg chewed immediately (mortality benefit). 12-lead ECG within 10 minutes, serial troponins q3–6h. Nitroglycerin 0.4 mg SL — rule out RV infarction first. Morphine ONLY for refractory pain (may increase mortality); O₂ only if SpO₂ <90%.
⚠ Pick ONE anticoagulant and never switch — switching agents increases bleeding. Enoxaparin preferred; UFH if CrCl <30 or PCI within 24h; fondaparinux for conservative strategy (lowest bleed).
⚠ Screen for emergent cath (<2h) — cardiogenic shock, refractory chest pain, sustained VT/VF, acute MR/VSD, or new pulmonary edema. Emergent features override the TIMI-based timing.
⚠ Avoid prasugrel if prior stroke/TIA, age ≥75, or weight <60 kg.

DAPT duration
Standard 12 months post-PCI (Class 1, 2025 ACC/AHA). High bleeding risk: DAPT × 1 month then ticagrelor monotherapy (ULTIMATE-DAPT / T-PASS) — halves BARC 2–5 bleeding without more MACCE. Extend beyond 12 months if high ischemic risk (PEGASUS).[8]

7 · Discharge: 5 pillars + special populations

  1. DAPT: Aspirin 81 mg + P2Y12 inhibitor × 12 months
  2. Statin: Atorvastatin 80 mg — target LDL <70
  3. Beta-blocker: Metoprolol succinate — continue if EF <40% or hypertension
  4. ACEi/ARB: start within 24h if EF ≤40%, hypertension, or diabetes
  5. Aldosterone antagonist: add if EF ≤40% + HF symptoms or diabetes (EPHESUS)
AF + stent → triple therapy
Shortest possible triple therapy (OAC + aspirin + P2Y12): 1 week–1 month, then OAC + Clopidogrel 75 mg. DOAC preferred over warfarin (AUGUSTUS).[9] Avoid prasugrel in triple therapy.

1 · Confirm NSTEMI & immediate actions

NSTEMI = **troponin rise** PLUS at least one of: ischemic symptoms, new ST-depression / T-wave inversion, or a wall-motion abnormality. Get a 12-lead **ECG within 10 minutes** and serial troponins q3–6h.{{ref1}}

Aspirin — chew immediately: 162–325 mg PO chewed — Mortality benefit — give at first medical contact.

Nitroglycerin: 0.4 mg SL q5min × 3 SL — Rule out RV infarction FIRST. Morphine only for refractory pain (may increase mortality). Supplemental O₂ only if SpO₂ <90%.

2 · Anticoagulation — pick ONE, never switch

Choose **one** anticoagulant and stay with it — switching increases bleeding.{{ref3}} Enter **weight** and **CrCl** above; the indicated agent's live dose appears below. Toggle **PCI within 24h** to switch the recommendation to UFH.

Enoxaparin (preferred for most): 1 mg/kg SC q12h SC — Reduce to once daily if CrCl 15–30 mL/min.

UFH bolus (CrCl <30 or PCI ≤24h): 60 units/kg IV (max 4,000) IV bolus — Preferred when going to cath within 24h — titratable in the lab.

UFH maintenance infusion: 12 units/kg/hr (max 1,000) IV — Titrate to aPTT.

Fondaparinux (conservative — lowest bleed): 2.5 mg SC daily SC — Lowest bleeding risk (OASIS-5). If the patient goes to PCI, supplement with UFH to prevent catheter thrombosis.

5 · One-glance pathway

NSTEMI confirmed — Aspirin 162–325 mg chewed, NTG 0.4 mg SL (rule out RV infarct first), ECG in 10 min, serial troponins. Morphine only if refractory.

Anticoagulation selection — Enoxaparin preferred; UFH if CrCl <30 or PCI within 24h; fondaparinux lowest bleed. Do NOT switch agents.

Emergent features present? — Cardiogenic shock, refractory pain, sustained VT/VF, acute MR/VSD, or new pulmonary edema.

Emergent cath (<2 hours) — Activate cath lab. UFH preferred. Load ticagrelor 180 mg or prasugrel 60 mg. Bedside echo for wall motion / mechanical complications.

TIMI risk score? — 0–2 conservative · 3 delayed invasive (25–72h) · 4–7 early invasive (<24h).

Conservative (ischemia-guided) — Fondaparinux, metoprolol, atorvastatin 80 mg, clopidogrel 300 mg load. Stress test before discharge.

Delayed invasive (25–72 h) — Cath 25–72h. P2Y12 loading may be deferred until anatomy known. Continue beta-blocker, statin, anticoagulation.

Early invasive (<24 h) — Ticagrelor 180 mg preferred (PLATO NNT 54). Continue anticoag without switching. Atorvastatin 80 mg. NPO.

Post-PCI P2Y12 selection — Ticagrelor default; prasugrel if high ischemic risk (CI prior stroke); clopidogrel if on OAC. Or CABG by anatomy.

CABG indicated — Left main / multivessel + diabetes. Hold ticagrelor ≥3d, clopidogrel ≥5d, prasugrel ≥7d. Continue aspirin; restart P2Y12 24–48h post-op.

DAPT duration & population — Standard 12 mo. High bleed risk: 1 mo DAPT → ticagrelor monotherapy (ULTIMATE-DAPT/T-PASS). Extend >12 mo if high ischemic risk.

MINOCA — non-obstructive — ≤50% stenosis, 5–10% of MI. Standard ACS regimens may be harmful: spasm → CCB/nitrate; SCAD → conservative; Takotsubo → avoid catecholamines.

Discharge — 5 pillars — DAPT, high-intensity statin, beta-blocker, ACEi/ARB, aldosterone antagonist if EF ≤40%. PPI with DAPT (not omeprazole).

Risk-factor modification — BP <130/80, LDL <70, HbA1c <7%, smoking cessation (NNT 12), cardiac rehab 36 sessions (25% CV mortality reduction). AF? EF ≤40%?

Triple therapy — AF + stent — Shortest triple therapy (1 wk–1 mo), then OAC + clopidogrel 75 mg. DOAC preferred (AUGUSTUS). Avoid prasugrel.

Follow-up plan — Cardiology 1–2 wk, PCP 2–4 wk, cardiac rehab referral, lipid panel 4–12 wk. Do NOT stop P2Y12 without cardiology.

Post-MI HF (EF ≤40%) — GDMT before discharge: ACEi/ARB, beta-blocker, aldosterone antagonist, SGLT2 inhibitor. Reassess EF at 3 mo; if ≤35% evaluate ICD (≥40d post-MI).

6 · P2Y12 selection & DAPT duration

Agent | When to use | Caution

Ticagrelor 90 mg BID | Default / standard | Aspirin ≤100 mg/day required

Prasugrel 10 mg daily | High ischemic risk (DM, stent thrombosis) | CI if prior stroke/TIA; avoid age ≥75 or <60 kg

Clopidogrel 75 mg daily | High bleeding risk or on OAC | Weaker platelet inhibition

Standard **12 months** post-PCI (Class 1, 2025 ACC/AHA). High bleeding risk: DAPT × 1 month then **ticagrelor monotherapy** (ULTIMATE-DAPT / T-PASS) — halves BARC 2–5 bleeding without more MACCE. Extend beyond 12 months if high ischemic risk (PEGASUS).{{ref9}}

7 · Discharge: 5 pillars + special populations

  • **DAPT:** Aspirin 81 mg + P2Y12 inhibitor × 12 months
  • **Statin:** [Atorvastatin](#/drug/atorvastatin) 80 mg — target LDL <70
  • **Beta-blocker:** [Metoprolol](#/drug/metoprolol) succinate — continue if EF <40% or hypertension
  • **ACEi/ARB:** start within 24h if EF ≤40%, hypertension, or diabetes
  • **Aldosterone antagonist:** add if EF ≤40% + HF symptoms or diabetes (EPHESUS)

Shortest possible triple therapy (OAC + aspirin + P2Y12): 1 week–1 month, then OAC + [Clopidogrel](#/drug/clopidogrel) 75 mg. DOAC preferred over warfarin (AUGUSTUS).{{ref10}} Avoid prasugrel in triple therapy.

ACEi/ARB, beta-blocker, aldosterone antagonist, and SGLT2 inhibitor before discharge. Reassess EF at 3 months; if ≤35%, evaluate for ICD (≥40 days post-MI).

Secondary-prevention targets

BP <130/80 mmHg · LDL <70 mg/dL · HbA1c <7% · smoking cessation (NNT 12) · cardiac rehab 36 sessions over 12 weeks (25% relative reduction in CV mortality).{{ref1}}

MINOCA — when standard therapy can harm

MINOCA (≤50% stenosis on angiography, 5–10% of MI): standard ACS regimens may be **harmful** depending on etiology. Coronary spasm → CCB/nitrate, not beta-blocker; SCAD → conservative, anticoagulation may extend dissection; Takotsubo → avoid catecholamines, supportive care.{{ref11}}
