Massive Transfusion: Ratio, Calcium, TXA in Time

Adults in the ED / trauma bay with life-threatening hemorrhage meeting massive-transfusion criteria (trauma, GI bleed, postpartum hemorrhage, ruptured AAA, surgical bleeding). Excludes pediatric and neonatal resuscitation — weight-based product volumes differ.

⚠ Activate MTP within 5 minutes — do not wait for lab confirmation of coagulopathy. Transfuse a balanced 1:1:1 ratio (6 units pRBCs : 6 units FFP : 1 platelet).
⚠ TXA only within 3 hours. Give 1 g IV over 10 min within 3 hours of injury/onset. Given after 3 hours, TXA INCREASES death from bleeding (CRASH-2) — do NOT give late.
⚠ Replace calcium. Citrate in blood products chelates ionized calcium → coagulopathy, hypotension, arrhythmia. Give CaCl 1 g IV per 4 units of blood products; keep iCa >1.1 mmol/L.
⚠ Limit crystalloid to 1–2 L. Excess crystalloid causes dilutional coagulopathy — resuscitate with blood, not saline.
⚠ Guard against the lethal triad (hypothermia + acidosis + coagulopathy): warm every product, keep core temp >36°C.
⚠ Factor VIIa is NOT routine — no mortality benefit and increased thrombosis (CONTROL). Reserve for refractory bleeding after surgical control, balanced ratios, and TXA.

1 · Recognize & activate

What triggers MTP
Definition: ≥10 units pRBCs in 24h, OR ≥4 units in 1 h with ongoing hemorrhage, OR replacement of >50% blood volume in 3 h.[1] Activate early — waiting for lab confirmation of coagulopathy costs lives. Clinical gestalt + prediction scores drive activation.[3]

Score ≥2 → activate
An ABC Score ≥2 predicts need for MTP with sensitivity 75%, specificity 86%.[4] Other triggers: SBP <90 mmHg with suspected hemorrhage, or penetrating torso trauma with hypotension.[5]

Permissive hypotension (except TBI): in penetrating trauma and ruptured AAA, target SBP 80-90 mmHg until surgical/aortic control — over-resuscitation "pops the clot."[6] Limit crystalloid to 1–2 L to avoid dilutional coagulopathy.[7]

4 · Prevent the lethal triad

Lethal triad of trauma
Hypothermia + acidosis + coagulopathy feed each other.[16] Warm every product, use active rewarming (Bair Hugger, warm fluids), and keep core temp >36°C — cold enzymes will not clot even with normal factor levels.
Why calcium matters
Citrate anticoagulant in blood products chelates ionized calcium → impaired coagulation, cardiac dysfunction, and vasodilation.[11] Replace Calcium Chloride 1 g IV per 4 units of blood products. Target iCa >1.1 mmol/L.

—Calcium chloride (1 g per 4 units)
—OR calcium gluconate (3 g per 4 units)

Cryoprecipitate 10 units if fibrinogen <150 mg/dL.[14] Hyperkalemia: stored blood is K⁺-rich (cell lysis) — monitor and treat if symptomatic or >6.0 mEq/L.
