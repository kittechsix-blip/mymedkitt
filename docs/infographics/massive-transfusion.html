Massive Transfusion Protocol: Activation, Ratios, and Goal-Directed Resuscitation

Adults with life-threatening hemorrhage from trauma, GI bleed, postpartum hemorrhage, ruptured AAA/vascular injury, or surgical bleeding meeting MTP criteria (≥10 units pRBCs/24h, ≥4 units pRBCs/1h with ongoing hemorrhage, or >50% blood volume replaced in 3h). Adult dosing only — pediatric MTP weight-based dosing, anticoagulant-reversal-during-MTP, and Jehovah's Witness/bloodless-medicine alternatives are separate consult sections not covered here.

⚠ MTP definition: ≥10 units pRBCs in 24h, OR ≥4 units pRBCs in 1h with ongoing hemorrhage, OR replacement of >50% blood volume in 3h — activate on clinical gestalt, do not wait for labs
⚠ TXA must be given within 3 hours of injury/onset — CRASH-2: given after 3h, TXA increased death from bleeding (4.4% vs 3.1%); do NOT delay MTP activation to give TXA, give concurrently
⚠ Limit crystalloid to 1-2 L total — excess crystalloid causes dilutional coagulopathy
⚠ Ruptured AAA: permissive hypotension SBP 70-90 mmHg until aortic control — avoid over-resuscitation ("popping the clot")
⚠ Do NOT stop MTP prematurely — a patient may appear stable while still compensating; watch for rebleeding in the first 6-12 hours
⚠ Factor VIIa is NOT routinely recommended — CONTROL trial showed increased thrombosis risk with no mortality benefit

1 · Activate early — definition & etiology

MTP definition
Transfusion of ≥10 units pRBCs in 24h, OR ≥4 units pRBCs in 1h with ongoing hemorrhage, OR replacement of >50% blood volume in 3h. Activate on clinical gestalt plus prediction scores (ABC Score ≥2) — waiting for lab confirmation of coagulopathy costs lives.[1][2][3]

Set the etiology, units transfused, within-3h, and Rh status toggles above — the pathway below highlights and the doses recompute live. Tap any flow box for bedside detail.

Trauma — activation criteria
Activate MTP if ANY: ABC Score ≥2, Shock Index >1.0 with hemorrhage, SBP <90 mmHg with suspected hemorrhage, penetrating torso trauma with hypotension, or clinical gestalt — "this patient is bleeding out." ABC components: penetrating mechanism, SBP ≤90, HR ≥120, positive FAST (score ≥2: sensitivity 75%, specificity 86%).[4][5]
GI hemorrhage — activation criteria
Activate for hemodynamic instability despite initial resuscitation, transfusion requirement >2 units pRBCs/hour, active hematemesis/hematochezia with shock, or Shock Index >1.0. Often older patients on anticoagulation — call GI early, endoscopy is definitive treatment. Glasgow-Blatchford Score 0-1 identifies very-low-risk patients safe for outpatient management per the ACG guideline; higher scores warrant admission and endoscopy.[6]
Postpartum hemorrhage — activation criteria
Definition: blood loss ≥1000 mL OR signs of hypovolemia regardless of volume. Activate for EBL >1500 mL with ongoing bleeding, hemodynamic instability, or developing coagulopathy. Give O-negative pRBCs initially if Rh status unknown.[7][8]
Ruptured AAA / vascular — priorities
Activate MTP immediately — these patients die from exsanguination. Permissive hypotension: SBP 70-90 mmHg until aortic control. Avoid over-resuscitation ("popping the clot"). Call vascular surgery STAT.[9]
Surgical bleeding — activation criteria
Activate for EBL >50% blood volume, ongoing transfusion >4 units pRBCs/1h, inability to achieve surgical hemostasis, or developing microvascular bleeding. Damage control surgery: abbreviated operation → packing → ICU resuscitation → return to OR.[1][2]

3 · Resuscitate — TXA & calcium (time-critical)

  • Large-bore IV access: 2 × 16-18G peripheral or IO
  • Type and crossmatch STAT (rapid sample tube if available)
  • Crystalloid: 1-2 L LR or NS — avoid excess (dilutional coagulopathy)
  • Consider permissive hypotension (SBP 80-90) in trauma, except TBI
  • Warm fluids — prevent hypothermia
  • Send: CBC, BMP, coags (PT/INR, PTT, fibrinogen), ABG with lactate, TEG/ROTEM if available

4 · Blood products — 1:1:1 & cooler contents

PROPPR trial — 1:1:1 ratio
1:1:1 (pRBC:FFP:platelets) improved hemostasis and reduced death from exsanguination compared to 1:1:2 at 24h (9.2% vs 14.6%). No difference in 24h or 30d mortality overall. Practical 1:1:1: 6 units pRBCs : 6 units FFP : 1 apheresis platelet (or 6-pack random donor).[12]

Whole blood option: Low-titer O whole blood is increasingly available and contains all components in physiologic ratios — may be superior in military/prehospital settings. Check institutional availability.[13]

2 · Activation pathway

Massive Transfusion Protocol — Activate early on clinical gestalt + ABC Score ≥2 — do not wait for labs to confirm coagulopathy.

What is the etiology? — Trauma, GI hemorrhage, postpartum hemorrhage, ruptured AAA/vascular, or surgical bleeding — each has specific activation criteria.

Trauma — ABC ≥2 or SBP<90/HR≥120 — ABC Score components: penetrating mechanism, SBP ≤90, HR ≥120, positive FAST. Score ≥2 = sensitivity 75%, specificity 86% for MTP need.

GI bleed — unstable or >2 units/hr — Endoscopy is definitive. Anticoagulation is common — may need reversal. Glasgow-Blatchford 0-1 = very-low-risk, outpatient-eligible.

PPH — EBL ≥1000 mL — TXA 1 g within 3h of delivery + uterine massage + uterotonics in parallel. O-negative pRBCs if Rh unknown.

Ruptured AAA — permissive SBP 70-90 — Permissive hypotension SBP 70-90 mmHg until aortic control. Avoid "popping the clot." Vascular surgery STAT.

Surgical — EBL >50% blood volume — Damage control: abbreviated operation, stop bleeding + contamination, ICU resuscitation, return to OR in 24-48h.

Initial resuscitation — access, T&C, TXA, CaCl — 2 large-bore IVs or IO, type & crossmatch STAT, max 1-2L crystalloid, TXA within 3h, CaCl if anticipating MTP, warm fluids.

Blood products 1:1:1 (PROPPR) — 6 units pRBC : 6 units FFP : 1 apheresis platelet pack. PROPPR: 1:1:1 reduced death from exsanguination at 24h vs 1:1:2.

MTP cooler — switch type-specific ASAP — Initial cooler: 6 pRBC + 6 FFP + 1 platelet pack. Subsequent coolers q15-30 min while MTP active. Factor VIIa not routinely recommended.

Calcium replacement — citrate chelation — Citrate in blood products chelates ionized calcium. Target iCa >1.0 mmol/L (ideally >1.1). Check with every ABG during MTP.

TXA — within 3 hours! — CRASH-2: given within 3h, TXA reduced death from bleeding (4.9% vs 5.7%). After 3h, TXA increased mortality (4.4% vs 3.1%).

ABO / Rh / RhoGAM reference — O-neg/emergency-release → type-specific (~10-15 min) → fully crossmatched (~45-60 min). RhoGAM if Rh-positive blood given to an Rh-negative female of childbearing age.

TEG/ROTEM-guided resuscitation — Results in 10-15 min. Prolonged R → FFP. Prolonged K/low alpha → Cryo. Low MA → Platelets. High LY30 → TXA.

When to terminate MTP — Stop when: bleeding controlled, MAP >65 & HR <100 without ongoing transfusion, lactate/base deficit improving, TEG normalizing. Do not stop prematurely.

Complications — lethal triad — Hypothermia + acidosis + coagulopathy. Also: hypocalcemia, hyperkalemia, citrate toxicity, TACO, TRALI.

Post-MTP labs & monitoring — Targets: Hgb >7 (>8 cardiac), platelets >50k (>100k CNS), INR <1.5, fibrinogen >150-200, iCa >1.0, K+ <5.5, core temp >36°C. Watch for rebleeding 6-24h.
