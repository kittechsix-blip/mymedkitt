Hypertonic Saline Protocol: Herniation Rescue vs Severe Symptomatic Hyponatremia

Adults with either (a) acute neurologic injury with elevated ICP/impending herniation, or (b) severe symptomatic hyponatremia (seizure, coma, severe encephalopathy). Excludes routine/chronic/asymptomatic hyponatremia and routine hypernatremia — neither is an indication for hypertonic saline.

⚠ Hypertonic saline treats two distinct emergencies only — severe symptomatic hyponatremia with cerebral edema/seizure/coma, or elevated ICP/impending herniation. It is not a treatment for routine hypernatremia.
⚠ Herniation/elevated ICP rescue: call neurosurgery/neurocritical care immediately, keep the head of bed elevated and neck midline, avoid hypotension/hypoxia/fever, and treat seizures while definitive therapy is arranged.
⚠ Severe symptomatic hyponatremia: bolus 3% NaCl to raise serum sodium 4-6 mEq/L and reverse symptoms — do not aim to normalize sodium in the ED.
⚠ Do not give hypertonic saline to correct hypernatremia. If hypernatremia coexists with herniation/elevated ICP, treat as competing life threats and call neurocritical care/neurosurgery immediately — avoid hypotonic fluid boluses in acute cerebral edema.
⚠ Avoid overcorrection: keep the 24-hour sodium rise under 8 mEq/L in high-ODS-risk patients (alcoholism, malnutrition, liver disease, hypokalemia, very low starting sodium) and under 10 mEq/L in most other patients.

1 · Two emergencies, one drug

What HTS is actually for
Hypertonic saline is used for two distinct emergencies: severe symptomatic hyponatremia with cerebral edema/seizure/coma, or elevated ICP/impending herniation from acute neurologic injury. It is not a treatment for routine hypernatremia.[1][2][3]

If a hypernatremic patient is herniating, manage as a neurocritical emergency with specialist input — hyperosmolar rescue may still be used when benefits outweigh sodium/osmolality risk.

Do not use 3% saline for mild, chronic, or asymptomatic hyponatremia. 8 · Access / monitoring

3% NaCl bolus can generally be given peripherally through a reliable IV. Continuous 3% infusion and 23.4% NaCl should follow local central-line policy. Use an infusion pump and label the line clearly.

  • Neuro exam and vital signs during bolus
  • Serum sodium/osmolality q2h during active correction
  • Strict I/O and volume status
  • Avoid hypotension, hypoxia, fever, and hypoglycemia in neurocritical patients
