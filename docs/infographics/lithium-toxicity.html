Lithium Toxicity: Levels Lie, Treat the Patient

Adults in the ED with known or suspected lithium toxicity — acute lithium-naive ingestion, acute-on-chronic overdose, or chronic accumulation. No pediatric-specific thresholds are validated here. Poison Control: 1-800-222-1222.

⚠ Seizure, coma/decreased LOC, or a life-threatening dysrhythmia = hemodialysis recommended regardless of the lithium level. Do not wait on a repeat level to act.
⚠ Impaired kidney function plus lithium >4.0 mEq/L = hemodialysis recommended. Call nephrology and toxicology now.
⚠ Lithium >5.0 mEq/L, confusion, or a predicted time to <1.0 mEq/L >36 hours = discuss dialysis urgently with toxicology/nephrology rather than waiting for deterioration.
⚠ Activated charcoal does not bind lithium. Use it only for a genuine co-ingestant, and never let it delay serial levels, fluids, or the dialysis screen.
⚠ Whole-bowel irrigation is only for a large acute sustained-release ingestion with a safe or protected airway — do not force it in a confused, vomiting, or airway-risk patient.
⚠ Serial lithium levels are mandatory. Chronic or acute-on-chronic toxicity can be far more dangerous than a single level suggests — treat the patient, not the number.
⚠ After dialysis, monitor for rebound for at least 12 hours and repeat hemodialysis if the level rebounds or symptoms recur — do not discharge from the dialysis chair.

1 · Recognize & classify the exposure

Core ED problem: lithium has a narrow therapeutic index, no antidote, renal clearance, delayed CNS distribution, and poor correlation between a single serum number and clinical severity.[3][4][5]

  • Airway / mental status / seizure check.
  • ECG and cardiac monitor.
  • Hold lithium immediately.
  • Ask: acute ingestion, acute-on-chronic, or chronic accumulation?
  • Check renal function, sodium/volume status, interacting meds, and co-ingestants.
Danger pattern
Chronic or acute-on-chronic toxicity with neurologic findings — these patients can be very sick at levels that look only moderate.[3][5]
Sick now: resus first

Acute ingestion: early nausea/vomiting/diarrhea/abdominal pain/tremor. Neurologic toxicity can be delayed as lithium distributes into CNS/tissue over time. Do not discharge based on one early level — sustained-release tablets and large ingestions can have delayed, prolonged, or rising levels.[1][3][5]

Chronic / acute-on-chronic: the high-risk pattern
Clearance failure, not a huge dose. Common triggers: vomiting/diarrhea, febrile illness, poor intake, heat exposure, AKI/CKD, heart failure, nephrogenic DI, new/increased NSAID, ACEi/ARB, thiazide, loop diuretic, spironolactone, or a low-sodium diet. Clinical clues: coarse tremor, ataxia, dysarthria, nystagmus, hyperreflexia/myoclonus, confusion, delirium, seizures, coma. Treat the patient, not the number.[3][4][5]

2 · Workup, level bands & precipitants

  • Serum lithium level now, then serial q4-6 h until clearly falling.
  • BMP/CMP: Na, K, HCO3, BUN/Cr, glucose, calcium.
  • Magnesium/phosphate if severe symptoms or renal disease.
  • ECG and continuous monitor if symptomatic or high level.
  • APAP, salicylate, ethanol, VBG/ABG/lactate/osmolality for intentional or unclear ingestion.
  • Urine output tracking; Foley if severe toxicity, AKI, or dialysis consideration.
  • Specimen pitfall: lithium heparin tubes can falsely elevate the level — confirm tube type if a result doesn't fit.

GI: nausea, vomiting, diarrhea, abdominal pain — prominent early in acute overdose. Neuro: coarse tremor, ataxia, dysarthria, nystagmus, hyperreflexia, myoclonus, confusion, delirium, seizures, coma. Renal/fluid: AKI, polyuria/polydipsia, nephrogenic DI. Cardiac: T-wave changes, QT prolongation, bradycardia, AV block. The miss: chronic lithium toxicity masquerading as stroke, sepsis, dementia, alcohol withdrawal, or sedation.[3][4]

3 · Decontamination & fluids

There is no lithium antidote, and charcoal does not bind lithium. Decontamination only helps selected acute ingestions before absorption completes.[3][4]

WBI selected cases only; charcoal is not lithium therapy
Whole-bowel irrigation: large acute sustained-release ingestion, early presentation, alert/cooperative or airway protected, no ileus/obstruction/perforation/shock. Do not force WBI in a confused, vomiting, or airway-risk patient. Activated charcoal: use only for a genuine co-ingestant with appropriate timing — it does not meaningfully bind lithium and must never delay serial levels, fluids, or the dialysis screen.[3][4]

Primary non-dialysis treatment: stop lithium and restore intravascular volume/sodium. Default: isotonic crystalloid for dehydration, vomiting/diarrhea, or volume-depletion AKI. Goals: euvolemia/eunatremia, adequate urine output without forced diuresis, improved renal lithium clearance. Avoid: forced diuresis, routine diuretics, kayexalate as a removal strategy. Caution: CKD, CHF, pulmonary edema, hypernatremia, and nephrogenic DI need toxicology/nephrology-guided fluids.[3][4]

  • Sodium: hypernatremia suggests water loss/NDI or inadequate free-water access.
  • Creatinine/eGFR and urine output.
  • Potassium/magnesium/calcium if vomiting, WBI, arrhythmia, or renal disease.
  • Foley: reasonable for severe toxicity, AKI, dialysis consideration, or unreliable urine output.

4 · Hemodialysis screen & decision

Call toxicology/poison center + nephrology early if any dialysis trigger is possible. EXTRIP separates dialysis into recommended vs suggested indications — clinical toxicity and kidney function matter as much as the number.[1][2]

EXTRIP: hemodialysis recommended
Kidney function is impaired AND lithium >4.0 mEq/L, OR decreased level of consciousness, seizure, or life-threatening dysrhythmia is present regardless of the level. Nephrology + toxicology now; prepare for intermittent hemodialysis; do not delay dialysis waiting for a repeat level if the patient is clinically severe.[1][2][4][5]
EXTRIP: hemodialysis suggested
Lithium >5.0 mEq/L, OR confusion, OR the expected time to lithium <1.0 mEq/L is >36 hours despite optimal management. Discuss with toxicology/nephrology early — chronic/acute-on-chronic patients with neuro findings deserve a lower threshold for the dialysis discussion. If deferred, document the reasoning, serial-level plan, and escalation trigger.[1][2][6]
  • Hold lithium and interacting meds; continue isotonic fluids if volume depleted.
  • Repeat lithium q4-6 h until clearly falling; repeat renal function/electrolytes with each level.
  • Continue ECG/monitor if symptomatic, high level, renal disease, or ECG abnormality.
  • Reassess neuro exam frequently — gait/ataxia, tremor, dysarthria, confusion, myoclonus.
  • Escalate to the dialysis discussion if the level rises/plateaus, renal function worsens, confusion appears, or clearance looks slow.
Modality & rebound
Preferred modality: intermittent hemodialysis; CRRT if unavailable/not tolerated. Stopping target: lithium <1.0 mEq/L or clear clinical improvement — if the level can't be measured rapidly, EXTRIP recommends at least 6 hours of ECTR. Rebound is expected as lithium redistributes from tissue back into serum. After dialysis, monitor serial levels for at least 12 hours and repeat dialysis if the level rebounds or symptoms recur. Do not discharge from the dialysis chair.[1][2][5]
