Cardiac Arrest: Rhythm-Routed ACLS

Adult non-traumatic cardiac arrest in the ED or prehospital setting. Excludes neonatal and pediatric arrest and pregnancy-specific care (perimortem cesarean) — each needs a modified protocol.

⚠ Confirm arrest in ≤10 seconds, then start CPR. If you are unsure whether a pulse is present, start compressions — it is safer to do CPR on a patient with a pulse than to withhold it from one without.
⚠ Torsades / polymorphic VT: give Magnesium 2 g IV — do NOT give amiodarone. Amiodarone prolongs the QT and worsens Torsades de Pointes.
⚠ Epinephrine 1 mg IV/IO every 3-5 minutes — give ASAP after the 1st cycle in non-shockable rhythms; after the 2nd shock in shockable rhythms. Do NOT use high-dose epinephrine (Class 3: No Benefit).
⚠ Search for a reversible cause (H's & T's) at EVERY pulse check. Tension pneumothorax and hyperkalemia are immediately fatal and immediately treatable.
⚠ POCUS only during the scheduled <10-second pulse check — never interrupt compressions to scan. No cardiac activity after adequate resuscitation is a strong futility signal.
⚠ Termination of resuscitation: ETCO₂ <10 mmHg after 20 min of high-quality CPR with no reversible cause supports stopping.

1 · Scene & confirm arrest

Before you start: obvious death?
Do not initiate CPR if any are present: decapitation, hemicorporectomy, dependent lividity, rigor mortis, decomposition, obviously non-survivable traumatic injury, or a valid DNAR/POLST (verify the document and confirm identity). Otherwise, proceed to confirm arrest.[3]

Confirm arrest in ≤10 seconds: no carotid pulse and no breathing (or only agonal gasps). If unsure — start CPR. It is better to do CPR on a patient with a pulse than to withhold it from one without.[3]

  • Call for the defibrillator / AED
  • Assign roles: compressor, airway, timer, medications, recorder
  • Designate a team leader

2 · High-quality CPR, airway & access

  • Rate: 100-120 compressions/min
  • Depth: 2-2.4 inches (5-6 cm) in adults
  • Allow full chest recoil between compressions
  • Minimize interruptions — all pauses <10 sec for rhythm checks and shocks
  • Rotate the compressor every 2 minutes (or sooner if fatigued)
Waveform capnography is your CPR-quality meter
Attach ETCO₂ immediately after the airway is secured. >20 mmHg = adequate CPR quality. A sudden jump >10 mmHg = likely ROSC — check a pulse.[1]

BVM first — adequate oxygenation without interrupting compressions; defer intubation early. With an advanced airway: continuous compressions (no 30:2), ventilate every 6 seconds (10 breaths/min), and avoid hyperventilation. A supraglottic airway (iGel, King LT) is an acceptable alternative to an ETT.[4]

Large-bore peripheral IV first; if it fails, external jugular. Place an IO within 60 sec if there is no IV — proximal tibia preferred in adults. IO drug delivery equals IV in arrest, so do not delay drugs for a central line.[5]

3 · Rhythm routing — what to do now

At 2 minutes, pause compressions briefly (<10 sec) to read the rhythm. Shockable = VF (chaotic, no organized QRS) or pulseless VT (wide, regular, fast). Non-shockable = PEA (organized rhythm, no pulse) or asystole (flatline — confirm in 2 leads). Set the toggles above to route the recommendation.

Torsades / polymorphic VT
Defibrillate, then give Magnesium 2 g IV. Do NOT give amiodarone — it prolongs the QT and worsens Torsades. Correct K⁺ and Mg²⁺; stop any QT-prolonging drugs.

Epinephrine — the backbone

POCUS discipline
Scan only during the scheduled <10-second pulse check — never interrupt compressions. RUSH targets: cardiac activity, tamponade, RV dilation (PE), absent lung sliding (tension PTX), flat IVC (hypovolemia). No cardiac activity after adequate resuscitation is a strong futility signal.[2]

7 · Termination vs ROSC

Termination of resuscitation (TOR)
ETCO₂ <10 mmHg after 20 min of high-quality CPR, with no reversible cause and no cardiac activity on POCUS, supports stopping. Document the rationale and time of death.[1]
  1. 12-lead ECG immediately — STEMI? new arrhythmia?
  2. Avoid hypoxia — titrate FiO₂ to SpO₂ 92-98% (do not hyperoxygenate)
  3. Avoid hypotension — SBP >90, MAP >65; vasopressors as needed
  4. Waveform capnography — confirm ETT placement
  5. Targeted temperature management per protocol
  6. Check and correct K⁺, Mg²⁺, Ca²⁺ — then ICU with continuous monitoring
