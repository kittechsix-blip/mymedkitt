Cardiac Arrest: Scene to ROSC or Termination

Adults in cardiac arrest in the ED or resuscitation bay. Excludes pediatric arrest, drowning/hypothermic arrest (needs prolonged/modified resuscitation), and detailed post-ICU management — see the linked rhythm-specific sub-protocols (VF/pVT, PEA, bradycardic arrest, Torsades, post-ROSC) for branch detail.

⚠ Confirm arrest in ≤10 seconds — if unsure, start CPR. It is better to perform compressions on a patient with a pulse than to withhold them from one without.
⚠ Do NOT give amiodarone for polymorphic VT / Torsades de Pointes — it prolongs the QT and worsens TdP. Give magnesium instead.
⚠ Do NOT give high-dose epinephrine — Class 3: No Benefit. Standard dose is 1 mg IV/IO every 3-5 minutes.
⚠ POCUS only during scheduled pulse checks, <10 seconds — never interrupt chest compressions to look.
⚠ TOR criteria are guidelines, not mandates. Clinical judgment supersedes — consider prolonging resuscitation for hypothermia, toxin ingestion, young patients, or suspected PE.

1 · Recognize & confirm

Signs precluding resuscitation — if any are present, do NOT initiate CPR:

  • Decapitation
  • Hemicorporectomy (transection of the torso)
  • Dependent lividity (non-blanching discoloration in dependent areas)
  • Rigor mortis
  • Decomposition
  • Valid DNAR/POLST — verify document, confirm identity
  • Obviously non-survivable traumatic injury

Large-bore peripheral IV first; IO within 60 seconds if no IV — tibial plateau preferred in adults. IO delivery is equivalent to IV in cardiac arrest.[4][7]

BVM first — adequate oxygenation without interrupting compressions. Defer intubation early; CPR quality is the priority. Once an advanced airway is placed: continuous compressions, ventilate every 6 sec (10/min), avoid hyperventilation. Supraglottic airway is an acceptable alternative to ETT.[4][5]

Rhythm check — every 2 minutes, <10 sec
Shockable: VF (chaotic, no organized QRS) or pulseless VT (wide, regular, fast). Non-shockable: PEA (organized rhythm, no pulse) or asystole (flatline — confirm in 2 leads).[4]

3 · Universal principles & epinephrine

VF / pulseless VT — shock now
Defibrillate immediately, minimize interruptions. Epinephrine after the 2nd shock, then q3-5min. Amiodarone/lidocaine after the 3rd shock. Full protocol: VF/VT Arrest.[4]
Torsades de Pointes — magnesium, NOT amiodarone
Polymorphic VT with prolonged QTc. Defibrillate, then magnesium sulfate 2 g IV over 1-2 min. Do NOT give amiodarone — it prolongs the QT and worsens TdP.[4][12]
Non-shockable — PEA / asystole
PEA: POCUS at the first pulse check — look for cardiac activity, tamponade, RV dilation, absent lung sliding.[2][8] Asystole: confirm in 2 leads, check connections, consider transcutaneous pacing if any organized activity.[4][5]

6 · ROSC or termination

  1. 12-lead ECG immediately — STEMI? new arrhythmia?
  2. Avoid hypoxia — titrate FiO2 to SpO2 92-98% (do not hyperoxygenate)
  3. Avoid hypotension — SBP >90, MAP >65; vasopressors as needed
  4. Waveform capnography — confirm ETT placement, monitor ventilation
  5. Targeted temperature management — initiate per protocol
  6. Check and correct K+, Mg2+, Ca2+ — then ICU with continuous monitoring
RuleCriteria
BLS terminationNot witnessed by EMS + no bystander CPR + no AED shock + no ROSC before transport
ALS terminationBLS criteria + no shocks by EMS + no ROSC before transport
Guidelines, not mandates
ETCO2 <10 mmHg after 20 min of high-quality CPR + no reversible cause treated + no cardiac activity on POCUS = near-zero survival. Clinical judgment supersedes — consider prolonging resuscitation for hypothermia, toxin ingestion, young patients, or PE.[1][10]
  • Document time of arrest and time CPR initiated
  • Document all interventions: shocks, medications (doses + times), airway management
  • Document ETCO2 values throughout resuscitation
  • Document POCUS findings at each pulse check
  • Document reversible causes considered and treated
  • Document reasoning for termination and time of death
  • Family notification — in person, with empathy
  • Organ/tissue donation consideration — contact OPO
  • Team debrief and staff wellness check

5 · One-glance pathway

Obvious signs of death? — Decapitation, hemicorporectomy, dependent lividity, rigor mortis, decomposition, valid DNAR/POLST, or obviously non-survivable injury.

Do NOT initiate CPR — Document findings and time of death = time of discovery. Notify family, consider organ/tissue donation.

Confirm arrest ≤10 sec → CPR — No pulse (carotid), no breathing or only agonal gasps. If unsure — start CPR.

CPR: 100-120/min, 5-6 cm, IO ≤60 s — Full chest recoil, rotate compressor q2min, minimize interruptions <10 sec. Attach waveform ETCO2 after airway secured.

BVM first — defer intubation — Continuous compressions once an advanced airway is placed — ventilate every 6 sec (10/min). Avoid hyperventilation.

Rhythm check (q2min, <10 s) — Shockable: VF or pulseless VT. Non-shockable: PEA or asystole (confirm 2 leads).

Non-shockable — POCUS at pulse check — PEA: look for tamponade, RV dilation, absent lung sliding. Asystole: confirm 2 leads, consider pacing.

Shockable — which pattern? — VF, pulseless VT, or polymorphic VT / Torsades — Torsades needs magnesium, not amiodarone.

VF / pVT — shock, epi after 2nd shock — Defibrillate immediately. Epinephrine after 2nd shock; amiodarone/lidocaine after 3rd shock.

Torsades — Mg 2g, NO amiodarone — Polymorphic VT + long QT. Amiodarone prolongs QT and worsens Torsades — give magnesium instead.
