Resuscitative Thoracotomy: Indication, Sequence, Control

Adults with selected traumatic arrest or profound peri-arrest traumatic shock in the ED/resuscitation bay, where a trauma team is assembled to perform emergency thoracotomy. Excludes blunt arrest without signs of life, prolonged downtime, devastating injury, or no response after HOTT — thoracotomy is not indicated in those patterns. Not a pediatric-specific protocol.

⚠ Proceed only for selected traumatic arrest or profound peri-arrest shock where thoracotomy can correct a survivable physiology — not a universal traumatic-arrest intervention. Avoid nonbeneficial thoracotomy when the patient is outside survivable selection criteria (blunt arrest without signs of life, prolonged downtime, devastating injury, no response after HOTT).
⚠ Make a large, decisive left anterolateral incision in the 4th/5th intercostal space — an inadequate incision wastes time. Extend to clamshell immediately if exposure is poor or right-chest/mediastinal access is needed.
⚠ Open the pericardium anterior to the phrenic nerve, never through it — avoid injuring the phrenic nerve or myocardium while opening and inspecting.
⚠ Never tie sutures through a coronary artery when controlling a cardiac wound — use digital pressure, a Foley balloon, a skin stapler, or pledgeted horizontal mattress sutures instead.
⚠ Cross-clamp the descending thoracic aorta above the diaphragm, avoiding the esophagus — this is a bridge to definitive hemorrhage control, not definitive care itself; announce clamp time and move to the OR.

Rapid procedural reminder for selected traumatic arrest. Core sequence: left anterolateral thoracotomy → enter pleura, spread ribs → open pericardium anterior to phrenic nerve → relieve tamponade/control cardiac wound → open cardiac massage → cross-clamp descending thoracic aorta when indicated → move to OR.

2 · Setup, Position, Incision

  • Operator at left chest
  • Assistant for exposure/suction
  • Airway/ventilation lead
  • Blood/MTP lead
  • Procedure nurse/tech for instruments
  • Trauma surgery/OR activation
  • Large scalpel, heavy scissors, forceps, clamps
  • Finochietto/rib spreader if available
  • Suction x2 if possible
  • Vascular clamp for aorta
  • Satinsky/vascular clamps, needle driver, cardiac sutures/stapler/Foley
  • Chest tubes and dressings
PPE and sharps discipline
Use full PPE and sharps discipline.
  1. Supine; left arm abducted if feasible — do not delay if not feasible
  2. Prep wide: neck to groin, bilateral chest, upper abdomen
  3. Landmark: left 4th or 5th intercostal space, usually inframammary fold/nipple line region
  4. Incision from sternum toward posterior axillary line, entering over the superior rib edge
  5. Team ready to extend to clamshell if exposure is inadequate or right-sided injury is suspected
  1. Make a large left anterolateral incision in the 4th/5th intercostal space
  2. Cut skin/subcutaneous tissue/muscle decisively — inadequate incision slows exposure
  3. Enter pleura over the superior rib edge
  4. Sweep finger to clear adhesions and confirm intrathoracic entry
  5. Insert rib spreader if available and open gradually
  6. Evacuate hemothorax and pack/suction as needed
  7. Extend across sternum into clamshell if exposure is poor or right chest/mediastinal access is needed

3 · Open Pericardium

  1. Identify phrenic nerve running along the lateral pericardium
  2. Grasp/tent pericardium anterior to the phrenic nerve
  3. Make a small opening with scissors or scalpel
  4. Extend longitudinally from apex toward aortic root, staying anterior to phrenic nerve
  5. Evacuate clot and deliver heart only as needed for inspection/control
  6. Avoid injuring the phrenic nerve or myocardium

4 · What to do now — cardiac inspection

Cardiac wound present — control it
Temporary options: direct digital pressure; Foley catheter through the wound, balloon inflated gently, traction applied; skin stapler for small linear ventricular wounds if appropriate; horizontal mattress sutures with pledgets if available. Avoid tying sutures through coronary arteries. Continue blood resuscitation and prepare immediate OR transfer.
No cardiac wound / empty heart — aortic clamp indications
Likely exsanguination or a noncardiac source. Indications:
- Exsanguinating subdiaphragmatic hemorrhage
- Empty heart despite blood resuscitation
- Need to prioritize coronary/cerebral perfusion during open resuscitation

Technique: retract the left lung superiorly/anteriorly, identify the descending thoracic aorta just anterior/lateral to spine and above diaphragm, open pleura bluntly, place a vascular clamp avoiding the esophagus, and announce clamp time. Cross-clamping is a bridge, not definitive care.
Massive hilar bleeding / air embolism — temporary hilar control
For massive unilateral pulmonary hemorrhage or suspected air embolism: manual compression of the hilum, or clamp the pulmonary hilum en bloc if experienced and exposure is adequate. Right mainstem intubation may improve left chest exposure when appropriate. This is temporizing. Definitive repair requires OR. The path continues to aortic control assessment.

5 · Cardiac Massage & Response

  • Use two hands when possible
  • Compress from apex toward base, avoiding ventricular twisting
  • Allow complete filling between compressions
  • Coordinate with blood resuscitation and definitive repair
  • Defibrillate internal paddles if available for VF/VT; external pads may still be used per local practice
If the heart is empty, massage alone will not restore output — prioritize blood and hemorrhage control.
ROSC — immediate OR handoff
Recognized by: Perfusing rhythm, improving ETCO2, palpable central pressure/pulse.

Trauma surgeon assumes definitive operative plan. Maintain airway, oxygenation, normocapnia, warmth. Continue balanced blood products and calcium. Keep aortic clamp time visible. Control open chest bleeding and contamination. Expedite OR transfer — CT is rarely appropriate before definitive hemorrhage control.

Document indication, arrest time, CPR duration, incision time, pericardial findings, cardiac wounds, aortic clamp time, products given, and response.
No response — confirm before stopping
No response after reversible causes addressed.

Before stopping, confirm: HOTT causes addressed; pericardium opened if tamponade suspected; cardiac wound/hemorrhage control attempted if present; blood resuscitation underway; aortic clamp considered if indicated; rhythm/cardiac motion reassessed. Terminate when thoracotomy does not reveal or correct a survivable cause and there is no physiologic response — follow local policy and trauma team consensus.

Critical pitfalls5 items
Do NOT perform thoracotomy outside survivable selection criteria — blunt arrest without signs of life, prolonged downtime, devastating injury, or no response after HOTT is a futile pattern.

6 · Procedure algorithm
