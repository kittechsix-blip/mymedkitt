Emergency Contraception — ED Management

Adults and adolescents of reproductive potential in the ED/outpatient setting after unprotected intercourse or contraceptive failure, within the EC window (oral methods ≤120 hours; copper IUD up to 5 days post-ovulation). Not effective if pregnancy is already established, though not harmful to an existing pregnancy. Not intended as routine ongoing contraception.

⚠ Copper IUD is the MOST effective EC (>99%) — offer to all eligible patients
⚠ Ulipristal (ella) maintains efficacy 72–120h — preferred if >72h since intercourse or weight >165 lbs
⚠ Levonorgestrel (Plan B) efficacy drops significantly after 72h and with higher body weight
⚠ EC will NOT harm an existing pregnancy — safe to give if pregnancy status is unknown
⚠ NO absolute medical contraindications to EC exist (ACOG/CDC)
⚠ Enzyme inducers (phenytoin, rifampin, etc.) reduce oral EC efficacy — double the levonorgestrel dose or use copper IUD
⚠ Ulipristal is CONTRAINDICATED with CYP3A4 enzyme inducers — use copper IUD or double-dose levonorgestrel instead
⚠ After ulipristal: hold hormonal contraception for 5 days (barrier method only) — competitive progesterone-receptor binding
⚠ EC is legal in all 50 states, including Texas — Plan B is OTC, ella is Rx, copper IUD is standard of care; FDA-labeled non-abortifacient (Dec 2022)

Overview

Emergency contraception (EC) prevents pregnancy after unprotected intercourse or contraceptive failure. Ranked by efficacy: copper IUD (>99%) > ulipristal (ella) (85–95%) > levonorgestrel (Plan B) (75–89%) > Yuzpe method (56–86%, backup only). Time since intercourse is critical for every method except the copper IUD.

Mechanism — not an abortifacient
EC works by preventing or delaying ovulation — it does NOT disrupt implantation. FDA-labeled non-abortifacient since the Dec 2022 label update. Safe, effective, and legal in all 50 states.

Pregnancy testing is NOT required before EC if timing is appropriate. It is safe to give EC when pregnancy status is unknown — EC will simply be ineffective if a pregnancy already exists, and will not harm it. Test if LMP was >4 weeks ago, if there were multiple unprotected events, or if IUD insertion is being considered.

▶ What to do now

>120 hours — copper IUD only
Oral EC is NOT effective beyond 120 hours. Copper IUD may still work if placed within 5 days of ovulation (not intercourse) — determine ovulation timing from LMP/cycle if possible; if uncertain, IUD can still be placed if the pregnancy test is negative.
Copper IUD selected
Most effective EC option (>99%), unaffected by weight, timing within the window, or enzyme-inducing medications. Also provides up to 10 years of ongoing contraception.
Ulipristal (ella) selected
30 mg PO ×1. Maintains efficacy through 120 hours. Hold hormonal contraception for 5 days afterward (barrier method only).
Levonorgestrel (Plan B) selected
1.5 mg PO ×1 standard dose (3 mg if weight ≥165 lb or on an enzyme inducer — see the computed dose below). OTC, no age restriction. Efficacy declines after 72h and with weight ≥165 lb — reconsider ulipristal or copper IUD if either applies.
Weight / drug-interaction check
Weight affects oral EC efficacy — levonorgestrel more than ulipristal; the copper IUD is unaffected by either. Route by weight band or check for enzyme-inducing medications below.

Copper IUD

Copper IUD (ParaGard)
Single insertion — effective as EC, then up to 10 years of ongoing contraception
Intrauterine
Pregnancy rate 0.1% (>99% effective). Unaffected by weight, timing within the window, or enzyme-inducing medications.
  • Current pregnancy
  • Current purulent cervicitis or PID
  • Unexplained vaginal bleeding (until evaluated)
  • Cervical or endometrial cancer
  • Uterine cavity distortion (fibroids, anomaly)
  • Wilson disease (copper overload)

NOT contraindications: nulliparity, prior ectopic pregnancy, STI risk factors, breastfeeding.

  1. Pregnancy test (if not already done) and bimanual exam for uterine position
  2. STI testing if high-risk (can insert before results return)
  3. NSAIDs for pain — ibuprofen 400–600 mg
  4. Standard IUD insertion technique; confirm and document string length
  5. Counsel: cramping/spotting expected; heavier, longer periods for 3–6 months; string check in 4–6 weeks; can remain in place up to 10 years

If unable to insert in the ED: refer urgently (OB/GYN clinic next day, family planning clinic, or trained primary care). Give oral EC NOW as a bridge — ulipristal preferred if >72h since intercourse, otherwise levonorgestrel. IUD can still be placed within 5 days of intercourse. Patient should abstain or use a barrier method until placement.

Ulipristal blocks progesterone receptors — concurrent hormonal contraception reduces its efficacy. Use a barrier method only for 5 days, then start the hormonal method plus backup for 7 more days. Repeat the dose if vomiting occurs within 3 hours.

Weight & drug interactions

WeightFirst ChoiceSecond Choice
<165 lbsAny method—
165-195 lbsCu-IUD or UPALNG (reduced efficacy)
>195 lbsCu-IUDUPA (better than LNG)

LNG efficacy is reduced above 165 lbs (~75 kg); both LNG and UPA are reduced above 195 lbs (~89 kg). The copper IUD is unaffected by weight at any threshold. LNG is still better than nothing at any weight — do NOT withhold EC based on weight.

  • Phenytoin (Dilantin), carbamazepine (Tegretol), phenobarbital, oxcarbazepine, topiramate (>200 mg/day)
  • Rifampin (most potent), rifabutin
  • Efavirenz, nevirapine, some protease inhibitors
  • St. John's Wort, griseofulvin
Enzyme inducers — management
Ulipristal: contraindicated — do not use. Levonorgestrel: double the dose to 3 mg. Copper IUD: unaffected — preferred option. This effect persists for 4 weeks after stopping the inducer.

PO
Use ONLY if levonorgestrel, ulipristal, and copper IUD are all unavailable. Give antiemetic 1 hour before (ondansetron 4 mg or meclizine 25 mg) — nausea 54%, vomiting 16%. Effective within 72h; some efficacy to 120h.

Efficacy 56–86% — inferior to progestin-only methods. Example combined-OC regimens (12h apart, per package insert): Levora/Nordette 4 pills ×2; Alesse/Levlite 5 pills ×2.

Sexual assault considerationsStandard of care + Catholic/Ascension hospital protocol

Offer EC to all sexual assault survivors, regardless of stated desire for pregnancy — patient may accept or decline. In Texas, hospitals must provide information about EC but are NOT required to dispense it (patient may go to a pharmacy; Plan B is OTC). Document the offer and the patient's decision.

Catholic/Ascension hospitals
Require serum LH testing before giving Plan B — Plan B is given only if LH is outside 19.18-103.03 mIU/mL. Ulipristal and copper IUD are NOT permitted under this protocol.

Disposition & follow-up

Provide EC immediately. Expected effects: nausea, headache, fatigue (usually mild); menstrual changes or spotting. Return precautions: period >7 days late → pregnancy test; severe abdominal pain → evaluate for ectopic; heavy bleeding → evaluate.

  • After LNG: start hormonal contraception immediately + backup ×7 days
  • After UPA: wait 5 days, then start hormonal contraception + backup ×7 days
  • After Cu-IUD: already protected — no additional method needed
  • Follow up with PCP or OB/GYN for contraception counseling
  • If IUD placed: string check in 4–6 weeks
  • STI testing if indicated
