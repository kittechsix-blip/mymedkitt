Pediatric Appendicitis: PAS, Image Wisely, Treat, Dispo

Children ~4-15 years old in the ED with suspected acute appendicitis. PAS is validated for ages 4-15 and poorly validated under 5. Excludes adults and confirmed non-appendiceal surgical abdomen.

⚠ Preop antibiotics within 1 hour of diagnosis — gram-negative + anaerobic (± enterococcal) coverage for every confirmed/strongly suspected case. Do not delay for OR scheduling.
⚠ Minimize ionizing radiation — graded compression ultrasound first, MRI if equivocal, CT only as a last resort. In pregnant adolescents, use US/MRI and avoid CT entirely.
⚠ Children <5 years old: atypical presentation (diffuse pain, diarrhea, refusal to walk, irritability) and >80% perforation rate at presentation — maintain a low threshold to image and re-examine frequently.
⚠ PAS does not replace clinical judgment. A low PAS (≤3) does not rule out appendicitis — the rate is low (~2%) but not zero, and PAS is poorly validated under age 5.
⚠ Normal WBC does NOT rule out appendicitis (15-20% have a normal WBC), and pyuria on UA does NOT rule out appendicitis (an inflamed appendix can irritate the adjacent ureter).
⚠ Non-operative (antibiotic-only) management carries ~30% recurrence at 1 year and is NOT appropriate for complicated appendicitis, an appendicolith on imaging, severe symptoms, or immunocompromised patients — requires pediatric surgery discussion and informed consent.

Retrocecal appendix: pain more lateral, may radiate to flank; less anterior tenderness; psoas sign more pronounced; US may miss it — consider MRI/CT if high suspicion.
Pelvic appendix: pain low/suprapubic; may have urinary symptoms and pyuria mimicking UTI; tenesmus or diarrhea.

3 · Management algorithm

Suspected Appendicitis — Lifetime incidence ~7%; perforation 30-40% overall, >80% in <5yo. Classic: periumbilical→RLQ migration, vomit AFTER pain. Atypical <5yo: diffuse pain, diarrhea, refusal to walk.

Focused History & Exam — Migration, anorexia, vomit-after-pain, fever, RLQ tenderness, pain with cough/hop (Markle sign). Rectal exam not routine.

Calculate PAS (10-point) — Samuel 2002: RLQ tenderness 2pt, pain w/ cough/percussion/hop 2pt, migration 1pt, anorexia 1pt, N/V 1pt, fever≥38°C 1pt, WBC≥10,000 1pt, PMN>7,500 1pt. Sensitivity ~88%, specificity ~50%.

PAS Risk Stratification — ≤3 low (~2%), 4-6 intermediate (~30%), ≥7 high (~80%). Not validated <5yo; does not replace clinical judgment.

Laboratory Workup — CBC, CRP, UA, bHCG (post-menarchal). Normal WBC does NOT rule out appendicitis (15-20% have normal WBC). Pyuria does NOT rule out appendicitis.

Imaging Strategy — US First — US first (sens 85-95%, spec 90-95%; ≥6-7mm non-compressible appendix). MRI if equivocal. CT only as last resort (>95% sens/spec). Avoid CT in pregnant adolescents.

Imaging Result — Positive → antibiotics. Equivocal/not visualized → serial exams/MRI/observe. Negative → discharge.

Preop Antibiotics — Within 1 hour: cefoxitin, OR ceftriaxone+metronidazole, OR amp-sulbactam (uncomplicated); pip-tazo or meropenem (complicated); cipro+metronidazole (true PCN allergy). NOM option exists for select uncomplicated cases (~30% recurrence).

Equivocal Imaging Pathway — Serial exams (4-6h) + re-trend labs > MRI > CT (last resort) > admit for observation. Shared-decision discharge only if low PAS + reliable family.

Disposition — OR (confirmed, surgery accepting) vs admit (equivocal/NOM/perforated awaiting OR) vs discharge (low PAS + negative/non-diagnostic imaging + reliable family).

OR — Appendectomy — Laparoscopic appendectomy is the gold standard. Preop: IV access, NPO, IVF, antibiotics within 1h, T&S if complicated, ondansetron 0.15 mg/kg IV (max 8 mg).

Admit for Observation — IV abx, NPO, IVF, q4h abdominal exams, repeat imaging in 12-24h. Escalate to OR/PICU for worsening peritoneal signs, new fever/tachycardia, instability, or imaging progression.

Discharge — Rapid Recheck — PAS ≤3 + negative/non-diagnostic imaging, tolerating PO, reliable family. Ibuprofen 10 mg/kg PO q6h (max 600mg) and/or acetaminophen 15 mg/kg PO q4-6h (max 1g). Recheck in 12-24h; explicit return precautions.
