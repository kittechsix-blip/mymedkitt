Acute Ischemic Stroke: Time, Deficit, Reperfusion

Adults ≥18 with suspected acute ischemic stroke in the ED. Rule out hemorrhage with non-contrast CT before thrombolytics. Excludes intracerebral hemorrhage, pediatrics, and pregnancy — each needs a modified pathway.

⚠ Establish last-known-well time immediately — it defines every reperfusion window. Use time last seen normal, NOT time symptoms were discovered.
⚠ Fingerstick glucose is the ONLY required pre-tPA lab — do not delay thrombolysis for coags unless the patient is anticoagulated or has known coagulopathy.
⚠ BP must be <185/110 mmHg before IVT — give labetalol 10–20 mg IV bolus (first-line) or nicardipine 5 mg/hr; if BP cannot be controlled, thrombolysis is contraindicated.
⚠ Tenecteplase 0.25 mg/kg is a single IV bolus — preferred over the alteplase 60-minute infusion; after either agent hold ALL antithrombotics × 24h.
⚠ Post-thrombolysis: BP <180/105 × 24h, neuro checks q15min × 2h, no aspirin/heparin/anticoagulant × 24h, repeat non-contrast CT at 24h before starting antiplatelets.
⚠ EVT is NOT excluded by thrombolysis contraindications — get a CTA for large-vessel occlusion in every disabling deficit regardless of tPA eligibility.

1 · First 10 minutes

Ischemic until proven otherwise
A focal deficit is ischemic stroke until a non-contrast CT head rules out hemorrhage. You do not yet know ischemic vs hemorrhagic — if intracerebral hemorrhage is suspected, image first, before any thrombolytic.[13]
  1. Establish last known well time — the clock that sets every window.
  2. Fingerstick glucose — the only pre-tPA lab; hypoglycemia mimics stroke.
  3. Score the deficit with NIHSS; use the syndrome pattern (aphasia, neglect, field cut, posterior signs) when the number understates it.
  4. Two large-bore IVs and activate the stroke team.
  5. Non-contrast CT head to exclude hemorrhage, then CTA for vessel imaging.
Time is brain
For wake-up or unknown onset, treat the clock as time last seen normal. MRI DWI–FLAIR mismatch can still identify candidates within the 4.5h window.[2]

4.5–9h disabling → extended IVT (perfusion-selected)

9–24h disabling → EVT window only

>24h → beyond reperfusion
No IVT or EVT. Start antiplatelet, complete etiology workup, begin secondary prevention, and admit for neuro monitoring and rehab evaluation.

BP gate before you push tPA
Target <185/110 mmHg before IVT. Labetalol 10–20 mg IV bolus (first-line) or Nicardipine 5 mg/hr IV infusion (second-line). If BP will not stay below 185/110, thrombolysis is contraindicated.[1]

6 · Secondary prevention

Minor stroke / high-risk TIA → DAPT × 21 days
Load aspirin 325 mg + clopidogrel 300 mg, then aspirin 81 mg + clopidogrel 75 mg daily × 21 days, then single agent. CYP2C19 poor metabolizer → ticagrelor 180 mg load + aspirin 300 mg, then ticagrelor 90 mg BID through day 90 total + aspirin 81 mg through day 21 only — aspirin stops at day 21, ticagrelor continues alone to day 90 (CHANCE-2 protocol). POINT: DAPT cut 90-day stroke 6.5%→5.0% (NNT 67).[3][12]
Low-risk minor stroke → single antiplatelet
Aspirin 160–325 mg within 24–48h of onset, then 81 mg daily. Complete workup: telemetry ≥24h, echo, lipid panel → high-intensity statin, HbA1c.[11]
Cardioembolic (AF) → anticoagulate, don't bridge
DOAC preferred over warfarin (unless mechanical valve / moderate-severe mitral stenosis): apixaban 5 mg BID or rivaroxaban 20 mg daily. Start timing by severity — TIA 1–3d, minor 4–7d, moderate 7–14d, severe ≥14d. Do NOT bridge with heparin.[11]
Large-artery atherosclerosis
Aspirin 325 mg + high-intensity statin (atorvastatin 40–80 mg), LDL <70. Symptomatic carotid 70–99% → CEA within 2 weeks (Class I); 50–69% reasonable (Class IIa). Intracranial 70–99% → DAPT × 90 days.[11]

Critical pitfalls5 items
Do NOT delay thrombolysis for labs beyond fingerstick glucose unless the patient is anticoagulated or coagulopathic.
