Chest Tube: Pneumothorax, Hemothorax & Empyema Management

Adults with pneumothorax, hemothorax, or empyema/pleural infection being evaluated for or undergoing chest tube thoracostomy, in the ED, trauma bay, or ICU. Excludes pediatric patients and non-thoracic drainage procedures.

⚠ Tension pneumothorax is a clinical diagnosis — do NOT delay for imaging. Needle decompression at the 4th-5th intercostal space, anterior axillary line has a higher success rate than the traditional 2nd ICS midclavicular site.
⚠ NEVER use trocars for chest tube insertion — unacceptable rate of visceral injury. All tubes must be placed by blunt dissection.
⚠ Traumatic pneumothorax <35 mm on CT: observation is safe in stable patients not on positive pressure ventilation.
⚠ Insert over the SUPERIOR edge of the rib to avoid the neurovascular bundle — anatomic studies show the bundle can still run aberrantly within the interspace.
⚠ Do NOT strip or milk chest tubes — this generates -300 to -400 cmH2O of dangerous negative pressure with no evidence of benefit.
⚠ Massive hemothorax (>1500 mL initial output OR >200 mL/hr for 2-4 hours) is an indication for emergent thoracotomy.
⚠ Pigtail catheters (10-14 Fr) are equivalent to large-bore tubes for simple pneumothorax — less pain, fewer complications.
⚠ Primary spontaneous pneumothorax: conservative management is non-inferior to intervention (Brown et al, NEJM 2020) — 98.5% resolution at 8 weeks without intervention.
⚠ MAC spin test for kinking: rotate the tube 180° clockwise and release — if it spins back to its previous position, it is kinked.
⚠ Do NOT clamp a functioning chest tube without explicit attending approval — clamping with an ongoing air leak risks tension pneumothorax.

1 · Overview & Indication

Chest tubes were once an automatic step in EM and critical care. Evidence now supports conservative management in many scenarios previously considered automatic indications: traumatic PTX <35 mm on CT in stable, non-ventilated patients [3]; primary spontaneous PTX — conservative management non-inferior to intervention, 98.5% resolution at 8 weeks (Brown et al, NEJM 2020) [1]; selective observation of occult PTX on ventilator (OPTICC trial) [4]; pigtail catheters comparable to large-bore for hemothorax (PCAT trial) [5] and empyema [7].

Classify the primary indication first — pneumothorax, hemothorax, or empyema — then the pneumothorax sub-type if applicable.

Hemothorax
Chest tube indicated. Large-bore 28-36 Fr (28 Fr minimum) is standard for traumatic hemothorax to ensure adequate drainage of blood and clot. The PCAT trial showed pigtail catheters may be comparable in select cases, but large-bore remains standard.
Empyema / Pleural Infection
Small-bore tubes (10-14 Fr) have outcomes similar to large-bore for pleural infection drainage (Rahman et al). BTS Pleural Disease Guideline 2010 supports image-guided small-bore catheter as first-line. If loculated: consider intrapleural fibrinolytics (tPA + DNase) or VATS.

2 · What to do now

Tension pneumothorax — emergent decompression
Clinical diagnosis — do NOT delay for imaging. Needle decompression: 14-16 gauge angiocatheter, 4th-5th intercostal space, anterior axillary line (preferred — higher success than 2nd ICS midclavicular). Finger thoracostomy (same site as chest tube) is more reliable. Proceed immediately to definitive chest tube placement.
Traumatic PTX — observation
Small traumatic pneumothorax (<35 mm on CT), stable, not on PPV. Admit for observation. Repeat imaging at 6 hours and prior to discharge. Supplemental O2 may accelerate reabsorption. Convert to chest tube if progression, worsening symptoms, or new PPV requirement.
Traumatic PTX — proceed to tube
≥35 mm, symptomatic, or hemopneumothorax — proceed to definitive chest tube placement.
Occult PTX on ventilator — observation
Selective observation of small occult PTX on ventilator, supported by OPTICC trial evidence. Close ICU monitoring, serial imaging, low threshold for chest tube if any progression, avoid PEEP increases if possible.
PTX on ventilator — proceed to tube
Large, progressing, or high ventilator pressures — proceed to definitive chest tube placement.
PSP — conservative management
Small PSP with minimal symptoms. Observe 3-6 hours with repeat CXR. Supplemental O2 accelerates reabsorption (~1.25%/day → ~4%/day with high-flow O2). Discharge with 2-4 week follow-up if stable. No air travel or diving until confirmed resolution. Smoking cessation counseling (strongest modifiable risk factor). Recurrence ~30% at 1 year.
PSP — needle aspiration
Moderate or symptomatic PSP. Needle aspiration first-line per BTS/ERS guidelines: 2nd ICS midclavicular line (or safe triangle), 16-18ga catheter + 3-way stopcock + 60 mL syringe, aspirate until resistance or 2.5 L, post-aspiration CXR.
Aspiration successful
Lung re-expanded on post-aspiration CXR. Observe 4-6 hours, repeat CXR, discharge with 2-4 week follow-up. Recurrence ~30% at 1 year. After 2nd ipsilateral episode, strongly recommend VATS pleurodesis.
Failed aspiration / large or recurrent PSP — proceed to tube
Persistent pneumothorax after aspiration, >2.5 L aspirated without resolution, or large/recurrent PSP — proceed to definitive chest tube placement.
SSP — observation with caution
Very small (<1 cm) SSP, minimal symptoms. Observation may be appropriate but requires close inpatient monitoring given limited pulmonary reserve. Admit with supplemental O2, serial CXR at 6 and 24 hours, low threshold for chest tube if any worsening, pulmonology consult.
SSP — proceed to tube
≥1 cm or symptomatic SSP — BTS recommends chest tube for all SSP >1 cm or symptomatic given limited pulmonary reserve. Proceed to definitive chest tube placement.
Good position — proceed to drainage management
Tube position confirmed, lung re-expanding on post-insertion CXR. Tube resting location does NOT matter — function, not trajectory, dictates reintervention. Proceed to drainage system management.
Malposition
Never advance a tube that has already been placed — infection risk. Pull back under sterile technique if too deep; place a second tube if too shallow. Repeat CXR after any adjustment. Consult thoracic surgery.
Massive hemothorax — surgical emergency
Criteria: >1500 mL initial OR >200 mL/hr for 2-4 hours. Activate massive transfusion protocol. Emergent thoracic surgery consultation. Continue chest tube drainage. Autotransfusion of hemothorax blood is safe and effective in trauma.

6 · Chest tube insertion technique

Triangle of safety: pectoralis major anteriorly, latissimus dorsi posteriorly, 5th ICS inferiorly, axilla superiorly. Insertion site: 4th or 5th intercostal space, mid-axillary line. Insert over the SUPERIOR edge of the rib — the neurovascular bundle classically runs along the inferior edge but can be variable. Avoid below the 6th ICS, posterior to the mid-axillary line, or too medial.

Mid-Arm Point (MAP) technique: an alternative landmark — with the arm adducted, the point on the chest wall level with the mid-arm identifies a safe insertion site within the triangle of safety [9].

Anticoagulation: chest tube insertion is probably safe in anticoagulated patients [10].

Water seal is sufficient for most cases, initial management, CT transport, or patient transfer. Reserve suction for failure of lung re-expansion on water seal or persistent large air leak. When used: low-pressure suction, typically -20 cmH2O.

Water-seal trial
All four criteria must be met before removal: lung fully re-expanded, no air leak, fluid output <100 mL/day, patient clinically stable. A normal CXR obtained 3 hours after placing the tube to water seal effectively excludes development of a clinically significant pneumothorax. Timing of removal (end-expiration vs. end-inspiration) does not affect recurrence.
  1. Pre-medicate with analgesia 5-15 minutes before removal
  2. Remove dressing, cut the securing suture (leave the stay suture intact)
  3. Instruct the patient to take a deep breath and bear down (Valsalva) during removal
  4. Pull the tube out in one smooth, swift motion; immediately apply petroleum gauze + occlusive dressing
  5. Close the stay suture; obtain CXR 1-2 hours post-removal

8 · Disposition & follow-up

  • Confirm lung remains expanded on post-removal CXR
  • Wound care: keep dressing clean and dry, remove in 48 hours
  • Avoid heavy lifting or straining for 1-2 weeks
  • No air travel until confirmed resolution (minimum 2 weeks post)
  • No scuba diving until pulmonology clearance
  • Smoking cessation counseling
  • Outpatient follow-up CXR in 2-4 weeks; pulmonology referral for SSP, recurrent PSP, or pleurodesis discussion

Clindamycin: 900 mg IV once pre-procedure, then q8h for 24h IV — For PCN/cephalosporin allergy. Alternative: vancomycin 15 mg/kg IV.

Removal criterion

Lung fully re-expanded on CXR

No air leak (no bubbling in water seal)

Fluid output <100 mL/day

Patient clinically stable

All four criteria must be met before removal: lung fully re-expanded, no air leak, fluid output <100 mL/day, patient clinically stable. A normal CXR obtained 3 hours after placing the tube to water seal effectively excludes development of a clinically significant pneumothorax. Timing of removal (end-expiration vs. end-inspiration) does not affect recurrence.

  • Pre-medicate with analgesia 5-15 minutes before removal
  • Remove dressing, cut the securing suture (leave the stay suture intact)
  • Instruct the patient to take a deep breath and bear down (Valsalva) during removal
  • Pull the tube out in one smooth, swift motion; immediately apply petroleum gauze + occlusive dressing
  • Close the stay suture; obtain CXR 1-2 hours post-removal

Morphine: 0.1 mg/kg IV (typical adult: 4-8 mg) IV — Administer 10-15 minutes before tube removal.

8 · Disposition & follow-up

  • Confirm lung remains expanded on post-removal CXR
  • Wound care: keep dressing clean and dry, remove in 48 hours
  • Avoid heavy lifting or straining for 1-2 weeks
  • No air travel until confirmed resolution (minimum 2 weeks post)
  • No scuba diving until pulmonology clearance
  • Smoking cessation counseling
  • Outpatient follow-up CXR in 2-4 weeks; pulmonology referral for SSP, recurrent PSP, or pleurodesis discussion
