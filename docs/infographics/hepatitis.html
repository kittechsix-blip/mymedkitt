Elevated Liver Enzymes: Classify the Pattern, Don't Miss Acute Liver Failure

Adults ≥18 with elevated liver chemistries in the ED or inpatient setting. Excludes pediatric-specific dosing, pregnancy-specific liver disease (HELLP, AFLP), and decompensated cirrhosis management beyond the acute-liver-failure screen — ALF by definition requires no prior cirrhosis.

⚠ Screen for Acute Liver Failure before any leisurely workup. ALF = INR ≥1.5 + hepatic encephalopathy + illness <26 weeks, without prior cirrhosis. Any encephalopathy with coagulopathy is treated as ALF until proven otherwise — check mental status, INR/coags, ammonia, glucose, lactate, and trend the bilirubin.
⚠ Give NAC for acetaminophen-associated ALF, and it is reasonable in early non-APAP ALF too. Watch for cerebral edema (grade III/IV encephalopathy) — elevate the head and treat raised ICP. Correct coagulopathy only for active bleeding or a procedure — don't mask the INR trend.
⚠ Anaphylactoid reactions to NAC (flushing, urticaria, bronchospasm) are most common during Bag 1 — slow or pause the infusion and treat with antihistamines. Do NOT permanently stop NAC.
⚠ Transfer early to a transplant center — do NOT wait for King's College Criteria to be fully met. Acetaminophen ALF: list for transplant if arterial pH <7.3, OR all three of INR >6.5 + creatinine >300 µmol/L (>3.4 mg/dL) + grade III/IV encephalopathy. Non-acetaminophen ALF: INR >6.5 alone, OR any 3 of: unfavorable etiology, age <10 or >40, jaundice-to-encephalopathy >7 days, INR >3.5, bilirubin >300 µmol/L (>17.5 mg/dL). Criteria have high specificity (~90%) but modest sensitivity.

King's College Criteria — non-acetaminophen ALF

3 · Hepatocellular injury (R >5)

Alcohol-associated: AST:ALT >2:1, both usually <300–400 IU/L (rarely >500), GGT elevated. If jaundiced/sick, assess severity (Maddrey discriminant function ≥32 or MELD) before considering corticosteroids.

Suspect viral: send HAV IgM, HBsAg/anti-HBc/anti-HBs, HCV Ab + HCV RNA, and HEV IgM (HDV only co/super-infects with HBV).

Interpreting viral serology (HBV/HCV quick map)6 rules
  • HBsAg+ / anti-HBc IgM+ → acute hepatitis B
  • HBsAg+ / anti-HBc IgG+ (>6 months) → chronic hepatitis B
  • anti-HBs+ only → vaccinated/immune
  • anti-HBs+ and anti-HBc+ → resolved infection
  • anti-HBc+ alone → window period or occult infection
  • HCV Ab+ with HCV RNA+ = active; Ab+ / RNA– = cleared or treated
Ischemic (shock) liver
ALT/AST in the thousands, peaking fast and falling ~50%/day after a hemodynamic insult (shock, arrest, severe hypoxia, RV failure). LDH high, early INR rise. Treat the hemodynamic cause — transaminases normalize as perfusion is restored.

Drug-induced liver injury: review all meds, OTCs, herbals/supplements — including Amanita mushroom ingestion — and acetaminophen dose/timeline. Hy's law: hepatocellular DILI + bilirubin >2× ULN + no other cause → ~10% mortality risk. Stop the offending agent. For APAP toxicity give N-Acetylcysteine.

Metabolic / autoimmune / inherited / vascular / obstructive6 causes
  • MASLD/MASH (formerly NAFLD) — most common chronic cause; mild ALT↑, metabolic syndrome; diagnosis of exclusion + imaging
  • Autoimmune hepatitis — young/middle-aged, ↑IgG, ANA/ASMA positive; can flare to ALF
  • Wilson disease — age <40, low ceruloplasmin, Coombs-negative hemolysis, low ALP-to-bilirubin ratio
  • Hemochromatosis — high ferritin + transferrin saturation >45%
  • Budd-Chiari / venous outflow obstruction — hepatic vein or IVC thrombosis; don't forget the vascular cause
  • Acute biliary obstruction (passing stone) — can transiently spike transaminases into a hepatocellular pattern before ALP rises and the picture turns cholestatic

4 · Cholestatic injury (R <2)

First confirm the ALP is hepatobiliary, not bone/placental: check GGT (or ALP isoenzymes). GGT elevated → hepatobiliary; GGT normal → extrahepatic ALP source.

Isolated ALP, normal GGT
A high ALP with a normal GGT points away from the liver: bone (Paget, fracture, mets, vitamin D deficiency), growth in children/adolescents, or placenta in pregnancy. Liver workup not indicated.

Extrahepatic/obstructive: image the ducts — RUQ ultrasound first. If dilated ducts or stones, escalate to MRCP/ERCP. Causes: choledocholithiasis, benign/malignant stricture, pancreatic/cholangiocarcinoma. Ascending cholangitis (fever + jaundice + RUQ pain ± shock/AMS) is an emergency — see the Gallbladder consult for biliary emergencies.

Intrahepatic cholestasis causes4 causes
  • PBC — middle-aged women, anti-mitochondrial antibody (AMA), pruritus
  • PSC — associated with IBD; MRCP shows beading; ↑cholangiocarcinoma risk
  • Drug-induced cholestasis — amoxicillin-clavulanate, anabolic steroids, etc.
  • Infiltrative — sarcoidosis, lymphoma, amyloid, granulomatous disease

5 · Mixed pattern (R 2–5)

Mixed pattern — trend the labs
Both transaminases and ALP elevated. Most often DILI or an evolving/resolving viral hepatitis. Manage by the dominant clinical features and recheck the trend — the pattern often declares itself over time.

6 · Targeted management & disposition

Match therapy to the cause: acetaminophen toxicity → NAC. Autoimmune hepatitis → corticosteroids, GI/hepatology. Severe alcohol-associated hepatitis (Maddrey discriminant function ≥32) → consider corticosteroids after sepsis/GI-bleed excluded. DILI → stop the offending agent, supportive care. Obstruction → biliary decompression (ERCP). Encephalopathy → lactulose (titrate to 2–3 soft stools/day) ± rifaximin, and reassess for ALF.

Disposition
Admit: ALF features, severe symptomatic hepatitis, intractable vomiting/dehydration, cholangitis/obstruction needing intervention, or uncertain rapidly rising LFTs. Discharge with follow-up: mild stable transaminitis, reliable patient, clear outpatient plan and repeat labs arranged.

8 · Do NOT

Critical pitfalls

**Do NOT skip the ALF screen** to chase the pattern differential first — coagulopathy + encephalopathy without known cirrhosis is time-critical and must be excluded up front.

**Do NOT permanently stop NAC for an anaphylactoid reaction** — slow or pause the infusion and treat with antihistamines instead; the reaction is histamine-mediated, not a true drug allergy.

**Do NOT correct coagulopathy in ALF** except for active bleeding or a procedure — pre-emptive correction masks the INR trend that drives the transplant decision.

**Do NOT wait for King's College Criteria to be fully met** before contacting a transplant center — sensitivity is only modest; transfer early.

**Do NOT read a high ALP with a normal GGT as liver disease** — pursue the non-hepatic source (bone, growth, placenta) instead of an unnecessary liver workup.
