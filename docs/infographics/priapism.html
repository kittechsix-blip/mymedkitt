Priapism: Classify, Block, Aspirate, Phenylephrine

Adult ED patients with priapism (erection >4 hours, unrelated to sexual stimulation). Covers ischemic (low-flow) vs non-ischemic (high-flow) classification, sickle cell disease and post-ICI variants, dorsal penile nerve block, corporal aspiration, and intracavernosal phenylephrine/epinephrine dosing. Surgical shunting and prosthesis detail is summarized at the level ED hands off to urology; full technique-level surgical detail is in the linked myMedKitt consult. Excludes non-urologic causes of prolonged erection.

⚠ Ischemic priapism is a UROLOGIC EMERGENCY — compartment syndrome of the penis. Smooth muscle necrosis begins at 4–6 hours; delayed treatment >24 hours carries a 30–70% erectile dysfunction rate.
⚠ Dorsal penile nerve block: 10 mL total 1% lidocaine WITHOUT epinephrine — the penis is end-artery territory; epinephrine risks ischemic necrosis. Avoid the 12 o'clock (dorsal neurovascular bundle) and 6 o'clock (urethra) positions for both the block and aspiration needle.
⚠ Phenylephrine 200 mcg (2 mL of 100 mcg/mL) intracavernosal every 5 minutes, up to 5 doses TOTAL — not per side, 1 mg max. Hold the next dose if SBP > 160 or HR > 110; check BP/HR every 5 minutes between injections. Contraindicated in uncontrolled hypertension or MAO inhibitor use.
⚠ >36–48 hours: smooth muscle necrosis likely advanced — still attempt aspiration + phenylephrine first, but do not delay surgical consultation if unsuccessful. AUA/SMSNA 2022 recommends early penile prosthesis over delayed placement.
⚠ Sickle cell priapism <4 hours: try supportive care first (IV fluids, analgesia, warm packs, O2) — do NOT use RBC transfusion to treat priapism.

1 · Assessment & classification

Priapism = an erection lasting more than 4 hours, unrelated to sexual stimulation. Two types: ischemic (low-flow) — a urologic emergency — and non-ischemic (high-flow) — not an emergency. Obtain duration, pain level, trauma history, PDE5-inhibitor/intracavernosal-injection (ICI) use, sickle cell disease status, and medications (antipsychotics, trazodone, anticoagulants).

FeatureIschemic (low-flow)Non-ischemic (high-flow)
PainPainfulPainless or mild discomfort
RigidityFully rigidPartially erect (not fully rigid)
Trauma historyNonePresent (straddle injury, kick, fall)
Aspirate colorDark, deoxygenatedBright red, oxygenated
MechanismVeno-occlusive — compartment syndrome of the penisArterial fistula — unregulated inflow
Emergency?YES — urologic emergencyNO — not an emergency

If unsure, obtain a corporal blood gas or color Doppler ultrasound.

4 · Classification & blood gas detail

Ischemic priapism — emergency
Ischemic priapism = compartment syndrome of the penis. Smooth muscle necrosis begins at 4–6 hours. Delayed treatment >24 hours → 30–70% erectile dysfunction rate. Time-sensitive: proceed to assess duration and etiology now.
ParameterIschemic patternNon-ischemic pattern
pO₂< 30 mmHg> 90 mmHg
pCO₂> 60 mmHg< 40 mmHg
pH< 7.257.35–7.45

Exact cutoffs are not standardized across studies, but the pattern of hypoxia + hypercarbia + acidosis reliably identifies ischemic priapism. Color Doppler ultrasound is a complementary noninvasive option.

Non-ischemic priapism
Not a urologic emergency — usually from perineal trauma causing an arterial fistula, partially tumescent, painless. 62% resolve spontaneously. If persistent, refer for selective arterial embolization (85–100% success); erectile function preserved in 80–100% of cases. Urology follow-up in 1–2 weeks.

5 · Sickle cell priapism

~40% of males with sickle cell disease experience priapism; SCD priapism has specific initial management steps.

  1. IV hydration (normal saline bolus)
  2. Analgesia — ketorolac, opioids PRN
  3. Warm shower or warm packs
  4. Supplemental O₂
  5. Do NOT use RBC transfusion to treat priapism in SCD patients
SCD ≥4 hours — same protocol as non-SCD ischemic
Requires the same procedural treatment as non-SCD ischemic priapism. Continue IV hydration and analgesia; obtain hematology consult for crisis management. Do NOT use RBC transfusion to treat priapism.
SCD — resolved with supportive care
Discharge with hematology follow-up. Discuss recurrence prevention: hydroxyurea, GnRH agonists, or PDE5-inhibitor prophylaxis. Return immediately for any erection lasting >4 hours.

  2. Attempt ejaculation
  3. Exercise — walking, climbing stairs
  4. Try briefly (<30–60 min) — do NOT let this delay treatment if fully rigid and painful
Post-ICI — resolved

Prolonged (>36–48h)

7 · Penile block & corporal aspiration

Lidocaine 1% (without epinephrine)
10 mL total (5 mL per side)
Dorsal penile nerve block — inject at 10 o'clock and 2 o'clock, under Buck's fascia
NEVER use epinephrine — the penis is end-artery territory; risk of ischemic necrosis. Avoid the 12 o'clock (dorsal neurovascular bundle) and 6 o'clock (urethra) positions.
  1. Clean the base of the penis with antiseptic
  2. 27-gauge needle at 10 o'clock, advance under Buck's fascia (feel a slight pop), aspirate, inject 5 mL of 1% lidocaine (no epi)
  3. Repeat at 2 o'clock with 5 mL
  4. Wait 5–10 minutes for full anesthetic effect; test with pinprick
  5. 18–21 gauge butterfly or angiocath at 2 or 10 o'clock into corpus cavernosum — single side is sufficient, the corpora communicate
  6. Aspirate dark blood until it turns bright red (typically 20–30 mL per cycle)
  7. Irrigate with 20–30 mL normal saline, re-aspirate, repeat cycles until aspirate is bright red

Aspiration + irrigation alone succeeds in about 36% of cases. If still rigid, proceed to intracavernosal phenylephrine.

Aspiration successful
Observe 30–60 minutes to confirm sustained resolution. Discharge with urology follow-up in 1–2 days. Return immediately for recurrence >4 hours.

8 · Intracavernosal phenylephrine

Phenylephrine is the first-line sympathomimetic (AUA/SMSNA 2022) — a pure alpha-1 agonist with no intrinsic inotropy and no increase in heart rate. 74% success alone; combined with aspiration, 70–100% success. Onset 1 minute, duration 10–20 minutes.

Surgical shunt — successful | Post-op per urology | PDE5i trial at 4–6 weeks for erectile rehabilitation

Surgical shunt — failed | Urgent urology consultation | Proximal shunt if not yet attempted, then immediate prosthesis

Immediate penile prosthesis | Post-op per urology team | Definitive treatment for refractory prolonged ischemic priapism

Other pitfalls

Do NOT let conservative measures (ice, ejaculation, exercise) delay treatment if the penis is fully rigid and painful — proceed directly to phenylephrine.

Do NOT use RBC transfusion to treat priapism in sickle cell disease patients.

Do NOT use epinephrine-containing local anesthetic for the penile block — the penis is end-artery territory; risk of ischemic necrosis.

Do NOT dose phenylephrine or epinephrine 'per side' — the corpora communicate freely; the dose count is the TOTAL, not per side.

Do NOT delay surgical consultation once refractory after 1 hour of aspiration + phenylephrine — early escalation and early prosthesis (when indicated) improve outcomes.
