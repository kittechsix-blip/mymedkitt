Thrombolytic Protocols: Indication, Dose, Contraindication, and Bleeding Plan

ED patients being evaluated for systemic thrombolysis — acute ischemic stroke, STEMI without timely PCI access, or massive/submassive PE (including PE arrest). Contraindication screening is indication-specific. Does not detail pediatric dosing, catheter-directed/mechanical PE therapies, or reversal-agent dosing beyond the bleeding-plan overview — those are specialist-directed (PERT/IR/cardiothoracic surgery/hematology) and not itemized in this consult.

⚠ Thrombolytics are high-risk, time-sensitive medications — the indication determines the drug, dose, contraindication framework, anticoagulation plan, and monitoring; confirm indication before selecting a drug or dose.
⚠ Universal contraindication screen before any thrombolytic leaves pharmacy: any intracranial hemorrhage history or current hemorrhage on imaging, known structural cerebral vascular lesion or malignant intracranial neoplasm, ischemic stroke within 3 months (except the current acute stroke pathway), suspected aortic dissection, active internal bleeding or bleeding diathesis, significant recent closed-head/facial trauma or intracranial/spinal surgery, or severe uncontrolled hypertension despite treatment. Massive PE arrest may justify thrombolysis despite relative contraindications when death is otherwise imminent.
⚠ Stroke thrombolysis requires BP <185/110 mmHg before treatment and <180/105 mmHg for 24 hr after — plus noncontrast CT excluding hemorrhage and a disabling deficit within the treatment window.
⚠ No antiplatelet or anticoagulant for 24 hr after stroke thrombolysis unless specialist-directed; repeat brain imaging at 24 hr or sooner for deterioration, and treat severe headache, vomiting, new hypertension, or neurologic worsening as possible symptomatic ICH.
⚠ STEMI lytics require symptom onset generally ≤12 hr, PCI unavailable within guideline time targets (commonly FMC-to-device >120 min), and no contraindications.
⚠ Reassess reperfusion 60–90 min after STEMI lytic — ST resolution <50%, persistent chest pain, hemodynamic/electrical instability, or worsening heart failure all trigger urgent rescue PCI.
⚠ Massive PE with shock is a systemic thrombolysis indication (alteplase 100 mg IV over 2 hr) unless bleeding risk clearly outweighs imminent death risk; hold UFH during the infusion.
⚠ PE arrest may warrant alteplase 50 mg IV push with continued high-quality CPR — evidence is limited but mortality without reperfusion is high.
⚠ After any PE lysis: ICU monitoring, reassess shock/oxygenation/RV function/bleeding, and have a clear anticoagulation-restart plan before disposition.
⚠ Major bleeding after thrombolysis: stop the thrombolytic and all anticoagulants immediately, activate the massive-transfusion/critical-bleeding pathway if unstable, and replace fibrinogen with cryoprecipitate or fibrinogen concentrate — antifibrinolytics (TXA, aminocaproic acid) only with specialist input.
⚠ Do not send a post-thrombolytic patient to an unmonitored setting — disposition requires an explicit BP target, anticoagulant/antiplatelet hold-or-restart plan, and documented bleeding watch points.

1 · Indication & Universal Screen

Systemic thrombolytics are high-risk, time-sensitive medications. The indication determines the drug, dose, contraindication framework, anticoagulation plan, and monitoring. Common ED use cases: acute ischemic stroke, STEMI when timely PCI is unavailable, massive PE or PE arrest/peri-arrest, and selected catheter-directed or reduced-dose PE strategies with specialist input.

  • Confirm indication before selecting thrombolytic dose.
  • Screen contraindications using indication-specific framework.
  • Control stroke BP before and after thrombolysis.
  • Use systemic lysis for massive PE shock unless bleeding risk dominates.
  • Have a bleeding/fibrinogen replacement plan before administration.

2 · Acute Ischemic Stroke

Core requirements: a disabling neurologic deficit within the protocol window, noncontrast CT excluding hemorrhage, BP <185/110 mmHg before treatment and <180/105 mmHg for 24 hr after, and a glucose/stroke-mimic screen addressed without delaying eligible treatment.

0.25 mg/kg IV bolus (max 25 mg)

IV: 10% as bolus over 1 min, remaining 90% infused over 60 min
= computed total dose at current weight.
No antiplatelet or anticoagulant for 24 hr
No antiplatelet or anticoagulant for 24 hr after IV thrombolysis unless specialist-directed.[1]

After IV stroke thrombolysis: neuro checks and BP monitoring per stroke protocol, maintain BP <180/105 mmHg, no antithrombotics for 24 hr unless specialist-directed, repeat brain imaging at 24 hr or sooner for deterioration, and treat severe headache, vomiting, hypertension, or neurologic worsening as possible symptomatic ICH. Do not place nonessential invasive lines for 24 hr.

5 · Bleeding Plan & Disposition

For serious bleeding after thrombolytic therapy: stop the thrombolytic and anticoagulants, activate the massive-transfusion/critical-bleeding pathway if unstable, check CBC, PT/INR, aPTT, fibrinogen, type/cross, replace fibrinogen with cryoprecipitate or fibrinogen concentrate per protocol, use antifibrinolytic therapy (TXA or aminocaproic acid) with specialist input, and repeat neuroimaging for neurologic deterioration after stroke lytic. Management is indication- and institution-specific; involve pharmacy, hematology, stroke/cardiology/PERT as appropriate.

  2. Drug, exact dose, start/stop time
  3. Contraindications reviewed and key risks accepted
  4. BP target and monitoring frequency
  5. Anticoagulant/antiplatelet hold or restart plan
  6. Bleeding watch points and reversal plan
  7. Destination: stroke unit/ICU/cath lab/PERT-capable ICU
Do not send a post-lytic patient to an unmonitored setting
