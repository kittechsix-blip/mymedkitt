PEA Arrest: PREM/PRES, QRS Width, Reversible Causes

Adults in pulseless electrical activity (organized rhythm, no palpable pulse) in the ED or resuscitation bay with bedside POCUS/RUSH capability. Excludes VF/pulseless VT (shockable rhythms) and pediatric arrest.

⚠ Pseudo-PEA (PREM — cardiac motion + signs of perfusion present): STOP chest compressions and treat as shock — do NOT give full-dose epinephrine 1 mg; CPR during preserved contractility may worsen output.
⚠ True PEA (PRES — cardiac standstill): very poor prognosis — check for fine VF (increase gain, confirm lead placement) before treating as PEA; defibrillate if fine VF is found.
⚠ Wide complex PEA: give empiric NaHCO3 1-2 mEq/kg IV push + calcium (gluconate 1 g IV or chloride 1 g IV central) immediately — before the cause is confirmed.
⚠ Hyperkalemia in arrest: give calcium FIRST for membrane stabilization, then insulin/D50 and albuterol to shift potassium — calcium alone does not lower serum K+.
⚠ TCA / sodium-channel blocker toxicity: target serum pH 7.45-7.55 with NaHCO3; do NOT use Class IA/IC antiarrhythmics or physostigmine in arrest.
⚠ Cardiac tamponade: do NOT delay pericardiocentesis for a formal echo or the OR — this is a bedside procedure in arrest; even 20-30 mL aspiration can restore output.
⚠ Tension pneumothorax: do NOT wait for a chest X-ray — needle decompression is a clinical/POCUS diagnosis in arrest; consider bilateral decompression in trauma.
⚠ Massive PE (RV dilation + McConnell sign): give alteplase 50 mg IV push and continue CPR 60-90 minutes after the lytic — do not stop compressions early; thrombolytics are NOT contraindicated by ongoing CPR.

1 · Recognition & POCUS

Pulseless Electrical Activity (PEA): an organized rhythm on the monitor with no palpable pulse. PEA is not a single diagnosis — it is a presentation with a treatable cause.

Immediate actions: begin high-quality CPR, attach ETCO₂ immediately (target >20 mmHg), obtain IV/IO access, and perform a RUSH exam <10 sec at the first pulse check to identify a reversible cause.

  • Pump — cardiac motion? RV dilation (RV > LV)? Pericardial effusion with RV diastolic collapse?
  • Lungs — absent lung sliding → tension pneumothorax?
  • Tank — IVC collapsed (hypovolemia) or plethoric (obstruction)?
  • Pipes — abdominal aorta for AAA?

POCUS pulse check at the femoral or carotid artery (<5 sec) is more accurate than manual palpation. PREM = Pulseless Rhythm with Echocardiographic Motion (pseudo-PEA). PRES = Pulseless Rhythm with Echocardiographic Standstill (true PEA).

3 · Management algorithm

PEA Recognition + POCUS — Organized rhythm, no pulse. Begin CPR, ETCO2 >20 mmHg, IV/IO, RUSH exam <10 sec at first pulse check.

Cardiac Activity on POCUS? — POCUS pulse check <5 sec. PREM = motion present (pseudo-PEA). PRES = standstill (true PEA).

PREM: Assess Perfusion — Weingart chain: POCUS pulse, arterial line DBP >30-40, SpO2 waveform, ETCO2 >30-40.

PRES: True PEA — Standstill — No wall motion — poor prognosis. Check fine VF. Epi 1 mg IV/IO q3-5min, standard ACLS.

Pseudo-PEA: Treat as Shock — STOP compressions. Norepi 10-50 mcg/min or push-dose epi 5-20 mcg; vasopressin 20 IU bolus bridge. No 1mg epi.

QRS Width? — Wide (>120ms) = Na-channel blockade pattern. Narrow (≤120ms) = mechanical/RV problem. First-move guide only (Bergman 2016).

Wide Complex — Empiric Tx — NaHCO3 1-2 mEq/kg IV push + calcium gluconate 1g IV immediately; lipid emulsion if TCA suspected.

Narrow Complex — Fluids First — Myocardium intact — mechanical/RV obstruction. Wide-open IV fluids first-line. Calcium/bicarb unlikely to help.

Wide-Complex Cause? — Hyperkalemia, TCA/Na-channel blocker, or severe acidosis/no clear cause.

Narrow-Complex POCUS Finding? — Tamponade, tension PTX, massive PE, or hypovolemia — each has a specific bedside intervention.

Hyperkalemia — Stabilize + Shift K+ — Calcium first (gluconate 1g or chloride 1g central), then insulin 10u+D50, then albuterol. NaHCO3 if acidotic.

TCA / Na-Channel Blocker Tx — NaHCO3 1-2 mEq/kg, target pH 7.45-7.55. Lipid emulsion 1.5 mL/kg if refractory. Avoid class IA/IC, physostigmine.

Tamponade — Pericardiocentesis — Emergent subxiphoid pericardiocentesis, US-guided. 20-30 mL aspiration can restore output. Don't delay for formal echo.

Tension PTX — Needle Decompression — 2nd ICS MCL or 4th/5th ICS AAL, 14g angiocath ≥5cm. Don't wait for CXR. Follow with chest tube.

Massive PE — Alteplase — Alteplase 50 mg IV push (half systemic dose). Continue CPR 60-90 min after lytic. McConnell sign = RV akinesis + apical sparing.

Hypovolemia — Volume Resuscitation — Rapid 1-2L crystalloid bolus. Hemorrhagic: MTP+TXA. Sepsis: antibiotics. Anaphylaxis: epi IM. Adrenal crisis: hydrocortisone.

CPR Quality + Epinephrine — Rate 100-120/min, depth 2-2.4in. ETCO2 >20 target. Epi 1mg IV/IO q3-5min, limit ≤3 doses (PARAMEDIC-2).

Rhythm/Pulse Check: ROSC? — At 2-min check: ROSC (pulse, rising ETCO2), continue CPR/reassess causes, or consider TOR.

ROSC Achieved — 12-lead ECG, MAP ≥65, avoid hyperoxia (SpO2 92-96%), avoid hypotension/hyperthermia, identify cause.

TOR / Code Called — TOR criteria: no ROSC, no POCUS activity, ETCO2 <10 at 20min, no reversible cause. Document and debrief.
