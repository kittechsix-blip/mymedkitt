Amniotic Fluid Embolism (AFE): Recognize, Resuscitate, Deliver

Peripartum patients (labor, cesarean, D&E, or within 30 min postpartum) with sudden cardiopulmonary collapse. Clinical diagnosis of exclusion — treat empirically while ruling out PE, anaphylaxis, sepsis, MI, eclampsia, hemorrhagic shock, peripartum cardiomyopathy, air embolism, and local anesthetic toxicity. Not for stable peripartum hemorrhage without collapse (see Postpartum Hemorrhage) or non-arrest obstetric emergencies.

⚠ AFE classic triad: hypoxia + hypotension + coagulopathy in labor or ≤30 min postpartum. Mortality 20–60%. Clinical diagnosis of exclusion — treat empirically, do not wait for confirmation.
⚠ Activate the full team simultaneously: OB, anesthesia, critical care, NICU, blood bank (MTP), perfusion/ECMO. Do NOT transport to the OR — bring the OR to the patient.
⚠ Cardiac arrest: standard hand position, standard defibrillation joules — pregnancy-modified ACLS does NOT change compression technique.
⚠ Manual left uterine displacement (two-handed push-to-left) is mandatory if fundus ≥ umbilicus. Do NOT delay chest compressions to achieve LUD.
⚠ 4-Minute Rule: if no ROSC by 4 minutes of arrest and ≥23 weeks gestation, start resuscitative hysterotomy at the bedside — deliver by minute 5. Primary goal is maternal survival.
⚠ Resuscitative hysterotomy requires no anesthesia and no OR transport — start with a scalpel at the bedside, no waiting for formal setup.
⚠ Activate MTP at diagnosis — do not wait for labs. Target fibrinogen ≥150–200 mg/dL (higher than the trauma threshold).
⚠ Call the ECMO team DURING resuscitation, not after — VA-ECMO survival is 60–80% vs ~30% without it in refractory AFE shock or arrest.

Recognition & Immediate Activation

AFE is a clinical diagnosis of exclusion. Classic triad: hypoxia + hypotension + coagulopathy in labor, cesarean, D&E, or within 30 min postpartum. Mortality 20–60%. Neurologically intact survival depends on minutes, not hours. [1][2][3]

Prodrome in ~30%: agitation, sense of doom, restlessness, shivering, paresthesias, metallic taste — precedes collapse by seconds to minutes. [5]

Differential diagnosis — rule out treatable mimicsPE, anaphylaxis, sepsis, MI, eclampsia, hemorrhage, PPCM, air/LAST

Before committing to AFE, rule out treatable mimics: [1][6]

• Pulmonary embolism — bedside echo shows RV strain without the fibrinolytic DIC pattern
• Anaphylaxis — urticaria, bronchospasm, clear trigger; responds to epinephrine alone
• Septic shock — fever, leukocytosis, source; slower onset
• Myocardial infarction — ECG changes, troponin; rare peripartum but possible
• Eclampsia — seizure, proteinuria, HTN (AFE is typically hypotensive)
• Hemorrhagic shock — identifiable bleeding source, responsive to volume; DIC is late
• Peripartum cardiomyopathy — subacute onset, LV dysfunction, no DIC
• Air embolism / local anesthetic toxicity — context-specific (C-section, epidural placement)

What To Do Right Now

Cardiac arrest — CPR + LUD, prep hysterotomy
Begin high-quality CPR immediately. Standard hand position, standard defibrillation joules. Add two-handed LUD without delaying compressions. Prepare for resuscitative hysterotomy at 4 minutes if no ROSC and ≥23 wk gestation.
Shock without arrest — intubate, resuscitate
Check pulse; if present, proceed directly to airway management (immediate intubation, 100% FiO2) and access/monitoring — hysterotomy is not indicated without arrest unless deterioration occurs.
Start resuscitative hysterotomy NOW
ROSC not achieved by 4 minutes of arrest — begin bedside resuscitative hysterotomy, deliver by minute 5. No anesthesia, no OR transport. Continue CPR + LUD throughout.
Hysterotomy not indicated
Fundus <20 wk / not palpable — proceed with standard adult ACLS. No hysterotomy.
Active hemorrhage — TXA + MTP
Give TXA 1 g IV over 10 min now (repeat once at 30 min if bleeding continues). Confirm MTP 1:1:1 running, fibrinogen target ≥150–200 mg/dL.
No bleeding yet — trend labs
Monitor fibrinogen, platelets, PT/PTT every 30–60 min. MTP remains activated at diagnosis regardless — do not wait for bleeding to start products.
ROSC achieved — transition to ICU

Refractory — optimize every domain, activate ECMO
Rotate compressors q2min, confirm LUD, perform hysterotomy now if not yet done and ≥20 wk, cannulate for VA-ECMO during ongoing CPR. Extended CPR can still yield neurologically intact survival in AFE — do not terminate without senior OB/EM/CCM consensus.

Perimortem Hysterotomy — 4-Minute Rule

4-Minute Rule (AHA/SMFM): if ROSC not achieved by 4 minutes of arrest, start resuscitative hysterotomy at minute 4 — deliver by minute 5. The primary goal is maternal survival, not fetal rescue. [7][8][9]

  1. Continue CPR + LUD throughout the procedure
  2. Midline vertical skin incision — xiphoid to pubis
  3. Vertical uterine incision — avoid placenta if visible
  4. Deliver fetus — clamp and cut cord, hand to neonatal team
  5. Deliver placenta — manual extraction, uterine massage
  6. Pack uterus — do not attempt definitive closure during arrest
  7. Close abdomen only after ROSC or on transport to OR

Einav 2015: maternal survival reported up to 15 min after arrest, neonatal survival up to 30 min. If ROSC not achieved, hysterotomy at any time is still reasonable.

A-O-K Pharmacologic Adjuncts

A-O-K protocol (Rezai 2017) — blocks three pulmonary vasoconstriction/platelet pathways simultaneously. Case-report evidence; not standard of care but a reasonable adjunct to ACLS. Give all three together, IV push within 1 minute of recognition. These are adjuncts — do not substitute for ACLS, MTP, pressors, or hysterotomy.

Hemodynamic Support — RV-First Strategy

AFE shock = acute RV failure from pulmonary HTN. Fluid overload worsens RV distension and septal shift. Vasopressors are first-line; inotropes support RV failure with preserved SBP; pulmonary vasodilators do not drop SBP.

Norepinephrine
0.05–0.5 mcg/kg/min IV infusion
First choice — alpha > beta activity supports RV coronary perfusion without excessive pulmonary vasoconstriction. Titrate to MAP ≥65.
Vasopressin
0.04 units/min IV (fixed, non-titratable)
Spares pulmonary vasculature, synergistic with norepinephrine — add early rather than escalating norepinephrine to high doses.
Epinephrine
0.05–0.5 mcg/kg/min IV infusion
If arrest or mixed shock. Dose per AFE consult (no AFE-specific myMedKitt drug-store entry — see source conflicts).
Milrinone
0.25–0.75 mcg/kg/min IV infusion — NO loading dose
Reduces PVR while augmenting RV contractility. Never bolus — drops SBP. Pair with norepinephrine or vasopressin.
Dobutamine
2.5–10 mcg/kg/min IV infusion
For RV failure with preserved SBP. Dose per AFE consult (no AFE-specific myMedKitt drug-store entry — see source conflicts).

Pulmonary vasodilators (selective — will not drop SBP): inhaled nitric oxide 20–40 ppm (max 80); inhaled epoprostenol (Flolan) 50 ng/kg/min nebulized; inhaled milrinone 5 mg nebulized.

Limit crystalloid
Crystalloid 500 mL bolus MAX during initial resuscitation — after that, blood products preferred. CVP >12 or RV dilation on bedside echo = STOP fluids.

MTP / DIC Management

Activate MTP at diagnosis — do not wait for labs. AFE coagulopathy is often fibrinolytic plus consumptive. Standard MTP pack PRBC : FFP : Platelets = 1:1:1. Fibrinogen target ≥150–200 mg/dL — the obstetric threshold is higher than trauma.

Cryoprecipitate
10 units (two 5-unit pools)
Raises fibrinogen ~70–100 mg/dL. Repeat q30–60 min guided by TEG/ROTEM or fibrinogen level. Fibrinogen concentrate 2–4 g IV is faster and volume-sparing if available.
Platelets
Keep >50 ×10⁹/L (>75 if ongoing bleeding)
1 apheresis unit per MTP cooler, maintains 1:1:1 ratio.

TEG/ROTEM-guided resuscitation: FIBTEM A5 <12 mm → fibrinogen; EXTEM CT prolonged → FFP or PCC; LY30 >3% → TXA.

TXA + Antifibrinolytics

Tranexamic Acid (TXA)
1 g IV over 10 min
Repeat 1 g IV over 10 min at 30 minutes if bleeding continues. Must be given <3 hours from onset of hemorrhage — effect diminishes after.

WOMAN trial (Lancet 2017): TXA within 3 h of postpartum hemorrhage onset reduces death from bleeding. NNT 267. No increase in thromboembolic events.

Avoid rFVIIa
Leighton 2011 systematic review showed increased thrombotic death in AFE patients who received rFVIIa. Reserve only for refractory bleeding AFTER maximal standard MTP has failed and surgical bleeding is controlled.
Oxytocin
20 units in 1 L NS at 250 mL/hr IV infusion
NEVER IV push — profound hypotension can re-arrest a post-ROSC AFE patient. Add methylergonovine 0.2 mg IM (if no HTN/preeclampsia) or carboprost 250 mcg IM (if no asthma) if atony persists.

Surgical/procedural hemostasis: Bakri balloon or uterine packing for uterine atony. Proceed to hysterectomy if refractory — do not delay for a dying patient.

ECMO / Mechanical Circulatory Support

Call the ECMO team DURING the resuscitation, not after. Case series show survival 60–80% with VA-ECMO vs ~30% without.

Indications: prolonged CPR (>10 min) without ROSC; refractory RV failure despite pressors + inotropes + pulmonary vasodilators; refractory hypoxemia (PaO₂/FiO₂ <100); reasonable candidate (no pre-existing irreversible multi-organ failure). Alternatives if unavailable: Impella RP (percutaneous RV assist), RVAD, IABP (limited utility in RV-predominant shock). Peripheral VA-ECMO (femoral artery + vein) is fastest.

  1. CPR quality — rotate compressors q2 min, ETCO₂ >20 mmHg target, mechanical CPR device if available
  2. Confirm LUD ongoing — two-handed push-to-left
  3. Hysterotomy now if not yet performed and ≥20 wk — relieves aortocaval compression
  4. ECMO cannulation — continue CPR during cannulation; ROSC after ECMO start is acceptable
  5. Continue MTP 1:1:1 + cryoprecipitate + TXA
  6. Push-dose epinephrine/phenylephrine between ACLS rounds if infusions not tolerated

Termination criteria: senior OB + EM + CCM consensus; ETCO₂ <10 mmHg after 30+ min of optimized CPR; no reversible cause remaining. Document thoroughly.

Post-Resuscitation Care

  1. Transfer to ICU immediately after initial stabilization
  2. Targeted temperature management 32–36°C × 24 h if comatose post-ROSC
  3. Continuous EEG if persistent unresponsiveness — subclinical seizures common
  4. Lung-protective ventilation (TV 6 mL/kg IBW, plateau <30, PEEP titrated)
  5. Trend fibrinogen, platelets, TEG, lactate q1–2 h until stable
  6. Urine output >0.5 mL/kg/hr target

Watch for delayed complications: AKI, ARDS, ischemic hepatopathy, Sheehan syndrome (pituitary necrosis — monitor cortisol/prolactin/TSH), cardiomyopathy (follow-up echo 2–4 weeks), delayed DIC (keep MTP products nearby 24 h). Maternal PTSD is common — psychiatry consult before discharge.

  1. Definitive airway secured, appropriate ventilator settings
  2. Hemodynamics stable on documented pressor regimen
  3. MTP products given — documented ratios, fibrinogen >150, platelets >50
  4. TXA dosed ×1 (± repeat), hemostasis achieved
  5. Uterus firm or packed; Foley in place; bladder not distended
  6. Neonatal team has infant — APGAR documented, disposition clear
  7. Imaging post-stabilization: CXR (ETT position, ARDS), bedside echo (RV/LV function)
  8. ICU bed, ECMO status if on support, continuous EEG if comatose
  9. Code team debrief scheduled
  10. Family updated — both mother and infant status
  11. Report case to AFE Foundation registry
