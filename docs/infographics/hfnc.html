High-Flow Nasal Cannula (HFNC) Management

HFNC management for hypoxemic respiratory failure (PaO2/FiO2 <300), post-extubation support, pre-oxygenation/apneic oxygenation for RSI, CHF/pulmonary edema (non-SCAPE), and immunocompromised patients with acute respiratory failure. Includes special-population reference for COPD, obesity, pediatric patients, and DNR/comfort-focused care. Not a substitute for NIV in severe hypercapnic respiratory failure (COPD, pH <7.25) or SCAPE.

⚠ SCAPE (Sympathetic Crashing Acute Pulmonary Edema): BiPAP remains first-line, not HFNC — transition to BiPAP if not improving within 15-30 minutes
⚠ Do NOT use HFNC as a substitute for NIV in severe hypercapnic respiratory failure (pH <7.25) — NIV preferred for COPD with significant hypercapnia
⚠ Delayed intubation after HFNC failure is associated with worse outcomes — do NOT delay for a prolonged HFNC trial once failure criteria are met
⚠ If a patient needs a nonrebreather mask, they likely need HFNC or a higher level of care
⚠ Pediatric ROX index is not well validated — use clinical judgment rather than the adult ROX thresholds in children

HFNC overview

High-Flow Nasal Cannula (HFNC) delivers warmed, humidified gas at flow rates up to 60-70 L/min via nasal prongs — near 100% FiO2 at max settings, vs an NRB's ~60-70%. A standard NRB delivers only 10-15 L/min, insufficient for patients with elevated minute ventilation; room air entrainment drops actual FiO2 to 60-70%.

  • High FiO2 delivery — near 100% at max settings
  • PEEP effect — 3-5 cm H2O at 50-60 L/min (mouth closed)
  • Dead space washout — reduces anatomic dead space, improves CO2 clearance
  • Reduced work of breathing — even if SpO2 is adequate, higher flow reduces WOB
  • Patient comfort — allows eating, talking, prolonged use without nasal ulceration
Abandon the nonrebreather
If a patient needs an NRB, they likely need HFNC or a higher level of care — the NRB's low flow rate cannot meet high minute ventilation demand.

▶ What to do now

Hypoxemic respiratory failure
FLORALI trial: HFNC reduced intubation vs standard O2/NIV in the PaO2/FiO2 ≤200 subgroup, with lower 90-day mortality. HFNC is arguably front-line therapy for parenchymal lung disease (pneumonia, ARDS).
Post-extubation support
HFNC preferred over standard O2 for most post-extubation patients; non-inferior to NIV for reintubation in high-risk patients. Consider NIV instead for hypercapnic respiratory failure (COPD).
Pre-oxygenation / apneic oxygenation
Leave nasal cannula at 15-60 L/min through laryngoscopy for apneic oxygenation; evidence for lowest SpO2 benefit is mixed (FELLOW trial), but sicker patients and multiple attempts may benefit.
CHF / pulmonary edema
BiPAP remains first-line for SCAPE. Consider HFNC only for mild-moderate edema, BiPAP intolerance, post-diuresis maintenance, or as a bridge while setting up BiPAP. Transition to BiPAP if not improving in 15-30 minutes.
Immunocompromised patient
HFNC reduces intubation rate vs standard O2/NIV in immunocompromised patients with ARF in meta-analyses. Close monitoring is essential — delayed intubation is associated with worse outcomes.
ROX ≥4.88 — low risk
Continue current settings; reassess ROX every 4-6 hours. Wean FiO2 first, then flow, in 10 L/min increments; trial standard NC at 6 L/min when tolerating flow 20 L/min and FiO2 50%.
ROX 3.85-4.88 — gray zone
Only 11% of patients fall here at 12h, and 33% of them are ultimately intubated. Optimize settings (flow to 60 L/min, fit, positioning), address reversible causes, and reassess in 1-2 hours.
ROX <3.85 — high risk, escalate
High risk of HFNC failure. Do NOT delay escalation for a prolonged HFNC trial — prolonged trials before intubation are associated with worse outcomes.
NIV trial
Appropriate if protecting airway with no hemodynamic instability. Reassess within 1-2 hours; if no improvement, proceed to intubation without delay.
Proceed to intubation
Required for altered mental status, inability to protect the airway, refractory hypoxemia, or hemodynamic instability. Continue HFNC during preparation for apneic oxygenation.

Aerosol generation risk

Flow is titrated first against work of breathing (RR, accessory muscle use) — dead space washout reduces ventilatory demand even if SpO2 is already adequate. Increase flow for RR >25, accessory muscle use, dyspnea, or speaking in short sentences. FiO2 is titrated against SpO2 (92-96%, avoid hyperoxia).

  1. Wean FiO2 first — target SpO2 92-96%
  2. Then wean flow — decrease by 10 L/min increments
  3. Ready for standard NC when flow 20 L/min + FiO2 50%
  4. Trial standard NC at 6 L/min
  5. If tolerated 2-4 hours, continue standard O2 or room air

Escalation criteria & options

  • RR >35 despite maximal HFNC
  • SpO2 <88% on HFNC 60 L/min, FiO2 100%
  • Accessory muscle use (sternocleidomastoid recruitment)
  • Paradoxical abdominal breathing
  • Altered mental status — confusion, agitation, somnolence
  • Inability to speak in sentences
  • Hemodynamic instability requiring vasopressors
  • Copious secretions patient cannot clear
Risk of delayed intubation
Prolonged HFNC trial before intubation is associated with worse outcomes. HFNC success/failure is usually apparent within 24-48 hours — if no improvement by then, strongly consider escalation.

ERS guidelines: HFNC suggested over NIV for acute hypoxemic respiratory failure (weak recommendation); NIV suggested over HFNC for acute hypercapnic respiratory failure (COPD). If failure is due to hypoxemia alone with the airway protected, trial NIV. If AMS or hemodynamic instability is present, intubate.

  • BiPAP IPAP 12-16 cm H2O, EPAP 8-10 cm H2O
  • FiO2 100% initially, titrate down
  • CPAP (CHF): start 8-10 cm H2O, titrate up to 15-20 cm H2O
  • Reassess within 1-2 hours
  • Contraindications: AMS (GCS <10), hemodynamic instability, copious secretions, facial trauma, high aspiration risk
  1. Continue HFNC during preparation (apneic oxygenation benefit)
  2. Head-up positioning 20-30 degrees
  3. Preoxygenate 3-5 minutes at max HFNC settings
  4. Have vasopressors ready (push-dose epinephrine or norepinephrine)

Induction agent selection: Ketamine preserves hemodynamics; Etomidate is hemodynamically neutral; Propofol causes hypotension — use with caution. Drug-store calculator anchor doses used below: ketamine 1.5 mg/kg, etomidate 0.3 mg/kg, propofol 2 mg/kg (RSI induction entries).

COPDNIV remains first-line for severe hypercapnia; HFNC as an alternative for mild-moderate exacerbations

Reduced FRC and increased atelectasis (worse supine) raise oxygen consumption and difficult-airway risk. HFNC's PEEP effect helps recruit atelectatic lung and is better tolerated than an NIV mask on an obese face.

  • Reverse Trendelenburg or sitting upright essential — 30-45 degree head elevation minimum

  • Lower threshold for NIV trial due to atelectasis component
  • Early intubation if needed — difficult airway anticipated

HFNC management algorithm

HFNC: Initial Assessment — indication? — Select the clinical indication: hypoxemic respiratory failure, post-extubation, pre-oxygenation/apneic oxygenation, CHF/pulmonary edema, or immunocompromised ARF.

Hypoxemic respiratory failure — FLORALI trial: reduced intubation in PaO2/FiO2 ≤200 subgroup, lower 90-day mortality vs standard O2/NIV.

Post-extubation support — Non-inferior to NIV for reintubation in high-risk patients; start 50-60 L/min immediately post-extubation.

Pre-oxygenation / apneic oxygenation — 60 L/min flow during RSI maintains FiO2 and washes out nitrogen; continue through laryngoscopy.

CHF / pulmonary edema — BiPAP first-line for SCAPE; HFNC only for mild-moderate edema, BiPAP intolerance, or bridge to BiPAP.

Immunocompromised patient — Reduced intubation rate vs standard O2/NIV in meta-analyses; close monitoring essential.

Device setup: flow 30-40 L/min, FiO2 50-60%, temp 37C — Titrate flow against work of breathing (up to 60-70 L/min); titrate FiO2 against SpO2 92-96% (88-92% in chronic hypercapnic COPD).

Titrate flow (WOB) then FiO2 (SpO2 target) — Increase flow for RR >25, accessory muscle use, or dyspnea. Wean-ready at flow 20 L/min + FiO2 50% + RR <25.

HFNC vs NRB vs NIV comparison — HFNC provides 3-5 cm H2O PEEP at 60 L/min flow with dead-space washout, unlike standard NC or NRB.

Monitor ROX index: (SpO2/FiO2)/RR — ROX ≥4.88 = low risk of failure (~80% PPV for success). ROX <3.85 = high risk. Calculate at 2, 6, 12h.

Current ROX index? — Failure thresholds: <2.85 at 2h, <3.47 at 6h, <3.85 at 12h. Success: ≥4.88 at any timepoint (~80% PPV).

ROX ≥4.88: continue & wean — Wean FiO2 first, then flow by 10 L/min; trial standard NC at 6 L/min when flow 20 L/min + FiO2 50% tolerated.

ROX 3.85-4.88: gray zone, optimize & reassess — Only 11% of patients at 12h; 33% of them intubated. Optimize settings and reversible factors; reassess in 1-2h.

ROX <3.85: HFNC failure criteria met — RR >35, SpO2 <88% on max HFNC, accessory muscle use, AMS, hemodynamic instability. Do NOT delay escalation.

Escalation pathway? — NIV suggested over HFNC for hypercapnic failure (COPD); HFNC suggested over NIV for hypoxemic failure (ERS guidelines).

Trial NIV/BiPAP — IPAP 12-16, EPAP 8-10 cm H2O. Reassess in 1-2h; if no improvement, proceed to intubation without delay.

Proceed to intubation — Continue HFNC during preparation (apneic oxygenation). Ketamine, etomidate, or propofol for induction per hemodynamics.

Disposition — ICU for high FiO2/flow requirements or ROX <4.88; step-down for stable/improving patients; rarely discharged on HFNC.

This summary consolidates key points from across the consult; it is not itself a decision-branch step.

  • Starting flow 30-60 L/min (start 40, titrate to comfort); FiO2 to minimum needed for SpO2 92-96%
  • ROX index q2h: (SpO2/FiO2)/RR — ROX <3.85 at 2-6h predicts HFNC failure
  • Escalate to NIV/intubation if ROX declining, RR >30, work of breathing worsening, or pH <7.25
  • Best evidence for hypoxemic respiratory failure (COVID, pneumonia, ARDS)
  • Avoid in pure hypercarbic respiratory failure (COPD, obesity hypoventilation) — use NIV instead
  • Monitor for aerosol generation risk — airborne precautions for COVID/TB
  • Wean by decreasing FiO2 first, then flow (wean to 30 L/min before transitioning to NC)
  • Early intubation for impending respiratory arrest — do NOT delay for a "HFNC trial"
  • ROX index >4.88 at 12h predicts HFNC success
