Brugada Syndrome — Recognition, Risk Stratification & Storm Management

Emergency department recognition, risk stratification, and acute management of suspected or known Brugada syndrome — ECG pattern recognition and phenocopy exclusion, Shanghai score risk stratification, cardiac arrest and electrical storm management, fever and drug-exposure complications, and ED disposition. Complements, and does not replace, formal EP consultation for ICD, ablation, and genetic-testing decisions.

⚠ Cardiac arrest / VF in suspected Brugada: standard ACLS. AVOID amiodarone, procainamide, and flecainide — these may worsen the Brugada pattern. If VF is refractory, isoproterenol may suppress it. ICD is Class I for essentially all VF/VT arrest survivors.
⚠ Electrical storm (≥3 VF/VT episodes per day) is an emergency: first-line is isoproterenol. Do NOT use beta-blockers or lidocaine (ineffective) or amiodarone, flecainide, or propafenone (may worsen the pattern).
⚠ Fever unmasks the Type 1 pattern 20× more frequently and carries a 40% recurrence rate with subsequent fevers — treat aggressively with antipyretics (do not wait for high fever) plus external cooling if refractory.
⚠ Exposure to a contraindicated drug: discontinue immediately, place on continuous cardiac monitoring with serial 12-lead ECGs, and monitor for a duration matched to the drug's half-life (ajmaline minutes; procainamide 3-4h; flecainide 20h; TCAs 24+h).
⚠ Rule out Brugada phenocopies (hyperkalemia, PE, RV infarct, hypothermia, lead misplacement) before diagnosing true Brugada syndrome — the ECG pattern resolves once the underlying condition is corrected.

1 · Overview

Brugada syndrome (BrS) is an inherited sodium channelopathy causing distinctive ECG patterns and ventricular fibrillation risk. Male predominance 8:1; mean age of arrhythmic events ~40 years. Risk is highest after cardiac arrest or arrhythmic syncope; asymptomatic patients have lower but nonzero risk.

  • Fever (most common trigger)
  • Sodium channel blocking drugs
  • Vagal stimulation, large meals
  • Cocaine use

2 · What to do now

Cardiac Arrest / VF
Standard ACLS. AVOID Amiodarone, procainamide, and flecainide — may worsen the Brugada pattern (the standard ACLS arrest dose, 300 mg IV/IO push, is contraindicated here). If VF is refractory, isoproterenol may suppress it. Post-ROSC: 12-lead ECG, continuous monitoring, check temperature. ICD Class I for essentially all VF/VT arrest survivors.
Syncope Evaluation
High-risk (arrhythmic) features: onset at rest/sleep, no prodrome, palpitations, brief duration. Type 1 ECG + arrhythmic syncope is a high-risk combination — ICD discussion (Class IIa), admit for monitoring, EP consult.
Phenocopy — Treat Underlying Cause
Correct the underlying condition and repeat the ECG. If it resolves: no long-term Brugada management needed, no ICD, no drug restrictions. If the pattern persists after correction: true BrS may have been unmasked — proceed with Brugada evaluation and EP consult.
High Risk — ICD Indicated
ICD Class I: cardiac arrest/VF/sustained-VT survivors, documented spontaneous sustained VT. ICD Class IIa: spontaneous Type 1 + arrhythmic syncope, or spontaneous Type 1 + ≥2 multiparametric risk factors. Admit to telemetry, EP consult.
Intermediate Risk — Shared Decision
Discuss ICD implantation vs quinidine (88% effective suppressing VF induction) vs close surveillance. Factors favoring ICD: spontaneous Type 1 (not drug-induced), strong family history of SCD, syncope of any type, QRS fragmentation.
Low Risk — Observation
Annual event rate ~0.5%. Lifestyle modification, aggressive fever treatment (antipyretics immediately), drug avoidance list, avoid cocaine/cannabis/excess alcohol, EP follow-up for ongoing risk reassessment.
Fever — Treat Aggressively
Fever unmasks the Type 1 pattern 20× more frequently, with a 40% recurrence rate on subsequent fevers. Acetaminophen 1000 mg PO/IV q6h and Ibuprofen 400-600 mg PO q6h — do not wait for high fever. External cooling if refractory. Admit if febrile without an ICD.
1-2 ICD Shocks
Continuous monitoring, 12-lead ECG, check temperature, review medications, magnet available at bedside. Await device interrogation to confirm appropriate vs inappropriate therapy. Admit for observation if appropriate.
Electrical Storm — Emergency
Electrical storm = ≥3 VF/VT episodes/day. First-line isoproterenol. NEVER beta-blockers, amiodarone, lidocaine, or propafenone. Add quinidine once stable. If refractory: catheter ablation, urgent EP consult.
Contraindicated Drug Exposure
Discontinue the offending agent, continuous cardiac monitoring, serial 12-lead ECGs. Monitoring duration depends on drug half-life. If VF/VT develops: isoproterenol protocol, ACLS avoiding amiodarone.

Lead Placement Tip
Use high precordial leads — place V1/V2 in the 2nd or 3rd intercostal space to increase sensitivity for detecting the pattern.
  • Metabolic: hyperkalemia (most common), hyponatremia, hypocalcemia, hypothermia, acidosis
  • Structural: pulmonary embolism, anterior MI / RV infarct, myocarditis, pericarditis
  • Mechanical: tension pneumothorax, mediastinal mass
  • Technical: lead misplacement (V1/V2 too high)

If unclear whether a reversible cause exists: BMP (K+, Na+, Ca++, glucose), magnesium, ABG/VBG (acidosis), troponin (MI), D-dimer if PE suspected, CXR (pneumothorax, mediastinal mass), echo (RV strain, pericardial effusion), and temperature check. If negative, proceed with Shanghai score assessment.

6 · Risk Stratification & ICD

Annual event rates: asymptomatic ~0.5%; prior syncope ~1.9%; prior arrest much higher. High risk: prior cardiac arrest (highest), documented spontaneous sustained VT, arrhythmic syncope + spontaneous Type 1 ECG. Intermediate: spontaneous Type 1 ECG, family history of SCD, QRS fragmentation, inducible VF on EPS (controversial). Low: drug-induced only pattern, asymptomatic, no family history.

3 · Management Algorithm

Suspected or Known Brugada Syndrome — Inherited sodium channelopathy. Type 1 coved ST elevation ≥2mm V1-V3 is the only diagnostic ECG pattern. Male predominance 8:1, mean age of events ~40 years. Triggers: fever (most common), Na-channel blocking drugs, vagal stimulation/large meals, cocaine.

How did the patient present? — Cardiac arrest/VF, syncope/near-syncope, an incidental ECG finding, or known Brugada with an acute complication.

Cardiac Arrest / VF — Standard ACLS. AVOID amiodarone, procainamide, flecainide — may worsen Brugada pattern. Isoproterenol for refractory VF. Post-ROSC: 12-lead ECG, continuous monitoring, check temperature. ICD Class I for essentially all survivors.

Syncope Evaluation — High-risk (arrhythmic): rest/sleep onset, no prodrome, palpitations, brief. Type 1 ECG + arrhythmic syncope = high-risk combination, ICD discussion (Class IIa), admit for monitoring, EP consult.

ECG Pattern Recognition — Type 1 (coved, ≥2mm V1-V3) is the ONLY diagnostic pattern. Type 2 (saddleback, ≥0.5mm) is suggestive only, needs provocation test. Type 3 is a normal variant. Use high precordial leads (V1/V2 in 2nd/3rd ICS) to increase sensitivity.

Known Brugada — Complication Type? — Patient with known Brugada syndrome presenting with an acute complication: fever, ICD shocks, electrical storm, or a medication concern.

Reversible Cause (Phenocopy) Found? — Check for hyperkalemia (most common), hyponatremia/hypocalcemia, hypothermia, acidosis, PE, anterior MI/RV infarct, myocarditis/pericarditis, tension pneumothorax, mediastinal mass, or lead misplacement.

Fever Management — Temperature-sensitive SCN5A channels. Aggressive antipyresis (do not wait for high fever) plus external cooling if refractory, continuous cardiac monitoring, treat underlying cause. Admit if febrile without ICD.

ICD Shocks — How Many in 24h? — Assess number of shocks, circumstances (fever, medication, activity), symptoms before shocks. Device interrogation needed to confirm appropriate vs inappropriate therapy.

Drug Exposure Management — Discontinue offending agent, continuous cardiac monitoring, serial 12-lead ECGs. Monitoring duration depends on drug half-life. If VF/VT develops: isoproterenol protocol, ACLS avoiding amiodarone.

Phenocopy — Treat Underlying Cause — Correct the underlying condition, repeat ECG. If pattern resolves: no long-term Brugada management, no ICD, no drug restrictions. If pattern persists after correction: may be true BrS unmasked by stressor — proceed with Brugada evaluation, EP consult.

Phenocopy Workup — BMP (K+, Na+, Ca++, glucose), magnesium, ABG/VBG (acidosis), troponin (MI), D-dimer if PE suspected, CXR (pneumothorax, mediastinal mass), echo (RV strain, pericardial effusion), temperature check.

Isolated ICD Shocks (1-2) — Continuous monitoring, 12-lead ECG, check temperature, review medications, magnet at bedside. Device interrogation to confirm appropriate vs inappropriate. Admit for observation if appropriate.

Electrical Storm Protocol — Isoproterenol first-line (bolus + infusion, titrate to 20% HR increase). Add quinidine when stable. NEVER beta-blockers, amiodarone, lidocaine, or propafenone. If refractory: catheter ablation, urgent EP consult.

10 · Pitfalls

Do NOT — 8 critical pitfalls

  • Do NOT diagnose Brugada from a Type 2 or Type 3 pattern alone — only Type 1 (coved, ≥2mm) is diagnostic.
  • Do NOT skip the phenocopy work-up — hyperkalemia, PE, RV infarct, and hypothermia all mimic the pattern and resolve with treatment of the underlying cause.

  • Do NOT give amiodarone, procainamide, or flecainide during cardiac arrest or electrical storm in Brugada — these agents may worsen the pattern.
  • Do NOT use beta-blockers or lidocaine for electrical storm — both are ineffective in Brugada VF storm.
  • Do NOT discharge a febrile Brugada patient without an ICD.
  • Do NOT withhold ICD discussion from a patient with spontaneous Type 1 ECG plus arrhythmic syncope (Class IIa).
  • Do NOT forget family screening (first-degree relatives) before discharge.
