Vaginal Bleeding Hub: Sick Check, 9 Lanes, Bundle, Disposition

Reproductive-age through postmenopausal ED patients with vaginal bleeding of any etiology — pregnancy-related, postpartum, coagulopathy-associated, adolescent, trauma/assault-associated, or stable nonpregnant AUB. This is a triage hub: definitive management lives in the linked myMedKitt consults (Massive Transfusion, First Trimester Emergencies, Pregnancy Trauma, Anticoagulation Reversal, DFSA/SANE Workup, Abnormal Uterine Bleeding). Excludes definitive OB/GYN surgical management, outpatient gynecologic-oncology workup, and pre-menarchal pediatric bleeding.

⚠ 5 do-not-miss diagnoses: ruptured ectopic pregnancy, hemorrhagic shock from any source, postpartum hemorrhage/retained products, late-pregnancy bleeding (placenta previa/abruption), and coagulopathy or gynecologic malignancy.
⚠ Choose the most dangerous lane first. Hemorrhage, pregnancy-related bleeding, late-pregnancy bleeding, postpartum/procedure bleeding, coagulopathy, and trauma/assault are each screened before treating bleeding as routine AUB.
⚠ Unstable bleeding = hemorrhage first, diagnosis second. Two large-bore IVs or IO, type and cross, bedside US when skilled, OB/GYN early, TXA only when postpartum hemorrhage or institutional hemorrhage protocol supports it, blood products over crystalloid-heavy resuscitation. Do not delay resuscitation for complete diagnostic sorting.
⚠ A pregnancy of unknown location is not a benign diagnosis. Bleeding with a positive pregnancy test requires ectopic-safe documentation and explicit follow-up before discharge is considered.
⚠ Do NOT perform a digital cervical exam at ≥20 weeks pregnant bleeding until placenta previa is excluded. Maternal stabilization, fetal assessment when viable, and OB/OR readiness come first.
⚠ Postpartum or post-procedure bleeding needs early OB/GYN involvement. Think uterine atony, retained products, laceration, uterine rupture/perforation, endometritis/sepsis, coagulopathy, or secondary PPH.
⚠ Major vaginal bleeding on anticoagulants is major bleeding until proven otherwise. DOAC/warfarin/heparin/antiplatelet exposure, last dose, and a medication-specific reversal pathway must be identified.
⚠ Use trauma-informed care and forensic preservation when assault is possible. Consent-based exam only, SANE/forensic pathway, mandatory reporting rules for minors/vulnerable adults, and surgical/OB help for deep laceration or uncontrolled bleeding.
⚠ Admit/consult/transfer for: hemorrhagic shock, ongoing heavy bleeding, severe symptomatic anemia, ectopic concern, pregnancy ≥20 weeks bleeding, postpartum hemorrhage, retained products with infection/hemorrhage, major anticoagulant/coagulopathy bleeding, unstable adolescent bleeding, or inability to ensure follow-up.

1 · Sick check first

5 do-not-miss diagnoses
Ruptured ectopic pregnancy — positive β-hCG + bleeding/shock. Hemorrhagic shock from any source. Postpartum hemorrhage / retained products. Late-pregnancy bleeding (placenta previa/abruption) — no digital/speculum exam until previa excluded. Coagulopathy or gynecologic malignancy.
  • Vitals, shock index, mental status, perfusion, syncope/presyncope
  • Estimate bleeding: pads/hour, clots, soaked clothing/bedding, ongoing active bleeding
  • Pregnancy test for reproductive potential — do not rely on reported contraception, tubal ligation, lactation, or age alone
  • LMP, gestational age if pregnant, postpartum status, fertility treatment, prior ectopic, anticoagulants, bleeding disorder history
  • Pain, shoulder pain, fever, discharge, trauma/assault, tissue passage, postmenopausal status
  • Pelvic exam when it changes care and patient consents; inspect for non-uterine sources

The ED job: separate hemorrhage, pregnancy-related emergencies, postpartum/late-pregnancy bleeding, coagulopathy, malignancy-risk bleeding, and stable AUB. Pregnancy test is mandatory in reproductive-age vaginal bleeding — ectopic can present with bleeding and minimal pain.

Hemorrhage / unstable bleeding
Open Massive Transfusion when hemorrhagic shock is possible. Two large-bore IVs or IO, type and cross, CBC/coags/fibrinogen if severe or obstetric, pregnancy test, bedside US when skilled, pelvic exam/speculum if it will identify a controllable source, OB/GYN early, TXA when postpartum hemorrhage or institutional hemorrhage protocol supports it, and blood products for shock rather than crystalloid-heavy resuscitation. Do not delay resuscitation for complete diagnostic sorting. Treat unstable vaginal bleeding as hemorrhage first, diagnosis second.
Tranexamic Acid (TXA) — only if postpartum hemorrhage or institutional protocol supports it
1 g IV over 10 minutes (infuse at 1 mL/min)
Repeat once 30 min later or if bleeding restarts within 24h. Must be given within 3h of delivery/bleeding for benefit.
Early pregnancy bleeding / ectopic / miscarriage
Open First Trimester Emergencies. Core workup: quantitative beta-hCG, transvaginal ultrasound, CBC, Rh status, type and screen when moderate bleeding or pain, OB/GYN for unstable, ectopic suspicion, concerning free fluid, or pregnancy of unknown location with high-risk features. Critical rule: a pregnancy of unknown location is not a benign diagnosis. Ectopic precautions and follow-up must be explicit if discharge is considered.
Bleeding ≥20 weeks pregnant
Open Pregnancy Trauma when trauma is involved, and involve OB urgently. Do not perform digital cervical exam until placenta previa is excluded. Priorities: maternal stabilization, fetal assessment when viable, Rh status, type and cross, CBC/coags/fibrinogen if significant bleeding, ultrasound for placental location, OB/OR readiness. Consider placenta previa, placental abruption, vasa previa, uterine rupture, preterm labor, cervical lesions, and trauma.
Postpartum / post-procedure bleeding
Recent delivery, miscarriage management, abortion, D&C, IUD insertion/removal, or gynecologic procedure changes the risk. Think: uterine atony, retained products, laceration, uterine rupture/perforation, endometritis/sepsis, coagulopathy, secondary postpartum hemorrhage. Actions: resuscitate, uterine massage if postpartum atony suspected, OB/GYN early, type and cross, CBC/coags/fibrinogen when significant, pelvic ultrasound for retained products, CT if procedural/surgical injury is plausible, antibiotics if infection/sepsis is present.
Adolescent heavy menstrual bleeding
ACOG recommends considering an underlying bleeding disorder in adolescents with heavy menstrual bleeding, especially anemia, flooding, bleeding through products quickly, easy bruising, epistaxis, surgical/dental bleeding, or family history. ED priorities: pregnancy test, CBC/ferritin when appropriate, hemodynamics, bleeding severity, medication safety, and gynecology/hematology involvement for severe anemia, unstable bleeding, suspected bleeding disorder, or need for high-dose hormonal therapy.
Anticoagulant / coagulopathy bleeding
Identify DOAC/warfarin/heparin/antiplatelet exposure, last dose, renal/liver disease, thrombocytopenia, cancer therapy, inherited bleeding disorder, and anemia symptoms. Open Anticoagulation Reversal when bleeding is major, unstable, or reversal is being considered. Workup: CBC, type and screen, pregnancy test, PT/INR/aPTT when relevant, fibrinogen in severe obstetric bleeding, renal/liver function, medication-specific reversal pathway. Major vaginal bleeding on anticoagulants is major bleeding until proven otherwise.
Postmenopausal bleeding / malignancy risk
Postmenopausal bleeding requires outpatient gynecology evaluation even if ED bleeding has stopped. The ED role is to stabilize, exclude severe anemia/hemorrhage, identify obvious non-uterine sources, and arrange timely follow-up. Red flags for urgent escalation: heavy ongoing bleeding, symptomatic anemia, pelvic mass, anticoagulation, infection, pain, or unreliable follow-up. Do not label postmenopausal bleeding benign without gynecology follow-up.
Trauma / foreign body / assault
Open DFSA / SANE Workup when sexual assault or drug-facilitated assault is possible. Actions: trauma-informed consent, stabilize bleeding, inspect for laceration/foreign body only with consent and appropriate support, forensic/SANE pathway, STI/pregnancy prophylaxis when indicated, mandatory reporting rules for minors/vulnerable adults, surgical/OB/GYN help for deep laceration, expanding hematoma, or uncontrolled bleeding.
Stable nonpregnant abnormal uterine bleeding
Open Abnormal Uterine Bleeding. Stable AUB priorities: pregnancy excluded, anemia assessed, bleeding severity estimated, contraindications to estrogen/progestin/TXA considered, malignancy risk and follow-up arranged. ACOG frames acute AUB treatment around hemodynamic status, medical therapy when stable, and procedural/surgical management when unstable or refractory.

10 mg/kg IV (max 600 mg/dose) q8h
IV (oral alternative: 1.3 g PO TID × 5 days)
Reduces menstrual blood loss 30–55%. Can be used alone if hormonal therapy is contraindicated, or as adjunct.

3 · Common ED bundle → reassess

Default ED bundle (applies after any lane's immediate action): pregnancy test, vitals trend, orthostatics only if safe. Rh status when pregnant or pregnancy status uncertain by local protocol.

  • CBC; type and screen/cross when moderate/heavy bleeding, pregnancy, anemia symptoms, or instability
  • Coags/fibrinogen when severe bleeding, anticoagulants, liver disease, postpartum/obstetric bleeding, or suspected coagulopathy
  • Pelvic exam/speculum when it changes care and patient consents: source, laceration, products/tissue, cervix, active bleeding
  • Ultrasound by pregnancy status and suspected source
  • Reassess bleeding rate, vitals, pain, syncope, Hgb trend when needed, and response to therapy

5 · Disposition

Admit / consult / transfer
Admit, transfer, or urgently consult for hemorrhagic shock, ongoing heavy bleeding, severe symptomatic anemia, ectopic concern, pregnancy ≥20 weeks bleeding, postpartum hemorrhage, suspected retained products with infection/hemorrhage, major anticoagulant/coagulopathy bleeding, unstable adolescent bleeding, or inability to ensure follow-up.
Urgent follow-up / observation
Use observation or urgent follow-up for pregnancy of unknown location, improving but still significant bleeding, anemia without instability, new heavy menstrual bleeding needing medication initiation, postmenopausal bleeding, or coagulopathy medication adjustment. Give exact repeat beta-hCG/US timing when early pregnancy is unresolved.
Discharge checklist
Discharge only when vitals are stable, bleeding is controlled, pregnancy status and ectopic risk have a written plan, Hgb risk is acceptable, pain is controlled, no late-pregnancy/postpartum/coagulopathy danger remains, and follow-up is realistic. Return now for: soaking pads rapidly, syncope, worsening pain, shoulder pain, dizziness, fever, pregnancy symptoms with bleeding, passing large clots, shortness of breath, chest pain, or inability to obtain follow-up.
