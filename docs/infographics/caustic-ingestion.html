Caustic Ingestion — ED Management

Adults ≥18 in the ED with suspected or confirmed caustic (acid/alkali) ingestion. Excludes pre-hospital field management and pediatric-specific dosing (adult doses shown throughout).

⚠ Do NOT induce emesis, give activated charcoal, attempt pH neutralization, place a blind NG tube, or use dilution therapy — all worsen caustic injury.
⚠ Up to 50% of adults with large caustic ingestions require intubation. Airway compromise (stridor, drooling, edema, respiratory distress) = emergent fiberoptic/video laryngoscopy with surgical airway backup at bedside. Avoid blind intubation attempts, LMA, and bougies.
⚠ Absence of oropharyngeal burns does NOT exclude significant esophageal or gastric injury — up to 15% of patients with significant GI injury have no visible oral burns.
⚠ Confirmed or strongly suspected perforation (free air, mediastinitis, peritonitis, hemodynamic instability) bypasses endoscopy — go directly to emergent surgery.
⚠ Perform EGD within 12-24 hours. AVOID endoscopy on days 5-15 post-ingestion — tissue is most friable and perforation risk is highest.
⚠ Grade 3a-3b (necrotic) injury carries high mortality — emergent surgical consultation required; grade 3b usually requires surgery.
⚠ Do NOT place a blind NG tube or blind nasojejunal tube through the injured esophagus — a feeding tube must be placed under fluoroscopy, not blind.
⚠ Mandatory psychiatric evaluation before discharge for ALL intentional ingestions, regardless of injury severity — 1:1 observation if actively suicidal.

1 · Initial assessment

Establish the 5 Ws: Who (age, weight), What (substance, concentration, pH, form), When (time of ingestion), Where (intentional vs accidental), Why (suicide attempt vs unintentional exposure). Time-critical airway and GI pathway follows.

Do NOT
Do NOT induce emesis, give activated charcoal, attempt pH neutralization, place a blind NG tube, or dilute with water/milk (controversial, limited benefit) — all can worsen injury or cause harm.

2 · Acid vs alkali mechanism

Alkalis (pH >11): liquefactive necrosis via saponification — deep penetrating injury, more esophageal involvement, higher perforation risk. Common: drain cleaners (NaOH), oven cleaners, industrial bleach, ammonia, button batteries.

Acids (pH <3): coagulative necrosis with protective eschar — more gastric injury, may cause "skip lesions." Common: toilet bowl cleaners (HCl), rust removers, pool chemicals (H2SO4).

Alkali injury patternLiquefactive necrosis — stricture and perforation risk
Liquefactive necrosis dissolves tissue proteins via saponification, penetrating deeply without forming a protective barrier. Esophageal injury predominates (slower transit); transmural perforation is more common. Delayed stricture formation in 30% of grade 2b+ injuries. Titrable Acid/Alkali Reserve (TAR) is a better predictor of injury than pH alone.
Acid injury patternCoagulative necrosis — HF causes systemic hypocalcemia
Coagulative necrosis denatures proteins into a protective eschar limiting tissue penetration. Gastric injury is more common (rapid esophageal transit, pyloric pooling); may create esophageal "skip lesions." Hydrofluoric acid (HF) is uniquely dangerous — penetrates deeply, binds calcium/magnesium causing severe hypocalcemia and arrhythmias. Treat with calcium gluconate.

3 · Airway management

Red flags requiring urgent airway evaluation: oropharyngeal edema, stridor or voice changes, hypersalivation/drooling with difficulty clearing, respiratory distress, visible posterior pharyngeal burns. Up to 50% of adults with large caustic ingestions require intubation.

Do NOT
Do NOT attempt blind intubation, use a laryngeal mask airway (LMA), or use a bougie (perforation risk).
  1. Fiberoptic or video laryngoscopy preferred; consider awake fiberoptic intubation for suspected significant ingestion
  2. Surgical airway backup ready (cricothyrotomy kit at bedside) — avoid blind attempts and bougie use
  3. Premedicate: topical lidocaine 4% + glycopyrrolate 0.2 mg IV; avoid deep sedation that eliminates protective reflexes
  4. Post-intubation: large-bore IV access ×2; vasopressors if hypotensive; CXR; avoid NG tube
  5. If surgical airway required: cricothyrotomy (tracheostomy may be difficult due to tissue damage)

4 · Symptom assessment

Symptoms suggesting significant injury: odynophagia, dysphagia, retrosternal/abdominal pain, vomiting (especially hematemesis), refusal to swallow, hypersalivation. Absence of oropharyngeal burns does NOT exclude esophageal/gastric injury — up to 15% of patients with significant GI injury have no visible oral burns.

5 · Labs and imaging

  • CBC with differential
  • BMP (renal function, electrolytes)
  • LFTs
  • Coagulation studies (PT/INR, PTT, fibrinogen)
  • Type and screen
  • VBG or ABG with lactate
  • CRP (elevated suggests transmural injury)
  • Lipase (if abdominal pain)
Predictive of transmural necrosis/perforation
Leukocytosis, thrombocytopenia, elevated CRP, metabolic acidosis, renal dysfunction, hepatic dysfunction.

Chest/abdominal X-ray: evaluate for pneumomediastinum, pneumoperitoneum, pleural effusion — free air suggests perforation, a surgical emergency.

Contrasted CT chest/abdomen: specificity ~95% for transmural necrosis; sensitivity ~80% — does NOT replace endoscopy. Findings of concern: loss of mucosal enhancement, wall thinning/discontinuity, periesophageal fat stranding, free air or fluid.

Endoscopy timing
Perform EGD within 12-24 hours for all intentional ingestions, symptomatic patients, large-volume ingestions, or high-concentration substances. Acceptable up to 48-96 hours with gentle technique. AVOID days 5-15 — tissue is most friable, highest perforation risk. Goals: grade injury severity (Zargar), guide treatment/disposition, predict stricture risk.

6 · Perforation — emergent surgery

Confirmed or strongly suspected perforation bypasses endoscopy — proceed directly to OR. Indications: pneumomediastinum/pneumoperitoneum, peritoneal signs, hemodynamic instability despite resuscitation, persistent lactic acidosis, CT evidence of transmural necrosis. Surgical options: esophagectomy with cervical esophagostomy, gastrectomy (partial/total), mediastinal drainage. Supportive care: NPO, aggressive fluid resuscitation, ICU admission.

Intentional ingestion
