Anticoagulant Reversal: Identify the Agent, Match the Antidote

Adults ≥18 in the ED or inpatient setting on an anticoagulant or antiplatelet with active bleeding, supratherapeutic levels, or a pre-procedure need. Excludes pediatrics and pregnancy — each needs a modified protocol. Weight-based doses assume normal-to-moderate renal function.

⚠ Act fast — for ICH on warfarin give PCC immediately without waiting for INR; identify agent and last dose timing
⚠ Warfarin reversal: 10 mg IV vitamin K + 4-factor PCC 25-50 units/kg (based on INR)
⚠ Apixaban/rivaroxaban: 4-factor PCC 50 units/kg or andexanet alfa 400-800 mg IV
⚠ Dabigatran: idarucizumab 5 g IV (2 vials of 2.5 g) - specific reversal agent
⚠ Heparin: protamine 1 mg per 100 units heparin (max 50 mg, infuse slowly)
⚠ Enoxaparin: protamine 1 mg per 1 mg enoxaparin (within 8h: 100% dose, 8-12h: 50% dose)

1 · Before you reverse — four questions

Frame the reversal
How coagulopathic? Assess every drug affecting coagulation plus labs — critically ill patients often carry several coagulopathies at once. Why anticoagulated? AF or DVT tolerates a short interruption; a mechanical mitral valve does not. How important is reversal? Life-threatening bleed → aggressive; minor bleed → local measures; minor procedure → often unnecessary. Cirrhosis or DIC? The INR may not reflect true hemostasis — use viscoelastic testing (TEG) to decide.[1][2][16]

Life-threatening or intracranial bleed — act now
Give reversal immediately. For warfarin, start 4-Factor PCC + 10 mg IV vitamin K without waiting for the INR result. For a massive bleed on an unknown agent, activate MTP (1:1:1) and give empiric 4-factor PCC 25–50 units/kg while you identify the drug.[1][15]
No life-threatening bleed — match intensity to scenario
Do not over-treat. Minor bleeding is usually hold the dose + local measures; supratherapeutic levels without bleeding use the tiered pathway in section 4. Reserve factor concentrates for true major or intracranial hemorrhage.[2][16]

Supratherapeutic DOAC — hold and watch the kidneys
Hold the dose; elimination is usually sufficient. DOAC + new renal failure is the trap — the patient can be severely supratherapeutic with near-normal labs. Dabigatran is 80% renally cleared and accumulates fastest in AKI. A normal INR argues against a significant Xa level but does not exclude it.[4]
Pre-procedure — do not over-reverse
We overestimate bleeding risk. SIR 2019 lists CVC, thoracentesis, and paracentesis as low risk — technique matters more than the INR. Do not chase INR <1.7 with FFP; it is impossible and dangerous (volume overload, TRALI).[8][9]

5 · Monitor, resume, and avoid pitfalls

  • Warfarin: INR at 30 min post-PCC, then 6h and 24h — watch for rebound as PCC wears off (vitamin K prevents it).
  • Dabigatran: PTT/TT baseline, 2–4h post-idarucizumab, then q12h; rebound >12h from adipose redistribution.
  • Xa inhibitors: INR after PCC then q6h; anti-Xa level if available.
  • UFH: PTT at 10–15 min, 2h, then q4h × 24h for heparin rebound.
  • Enoxaparin: anti-Xa at 10–15 min, 2h, then q4h × 24h.
Resume anticoagulation when safe
The original indication has not gone away — failure to restart carries its own mortality risk. ICH: restart in 4–8 weeks for high-risk indications (mechanical valve, recurrent DVT/PE). GI bleed: often 1–2 weeks once hemostasis is secure. Mechanical valve: shortest possible interruption. Involve hematology for complex cases.[1]
Classic pitfalls to avoid4 items
PCC or FFP alone for warfarin → INR rebounds when they wear off (6–8h). Always give vitamin K simultaneously.[2][16]
Vitamin K subcutaneously → erratic absorption; IM → hematoma. Always IV for emergent reversal.[16]
FFP to push INR below 1.7 → impossible (FFP holds factors at normal plasma concentration). Futile and dangerous — volume overload, TRALI.[8]
DOAC + new renal failure → silently supratherapeutic with no screaming INR. Dabigatran half-life doubles when GFR <30.[3][4]
