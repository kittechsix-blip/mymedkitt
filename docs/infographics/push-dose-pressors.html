Push-Dose Pressors: Bridge the Gap Safely

Adults with a pulse and immediate, transient hypotension in the ED / peri-intubation / procedural setting, as a 5–15 minute bridge to a continuous infusion. Excludes pulseless arrest (use ACLS arrest dosing), and is not definitive shock therapy.

⚠ NEVER give the 1 mg cardiac-arrest amp to a patient with a pulse. A full cardiac amp (1 mg = 1000 mcg) to a perfusing patient causes malignant HTN, VT/VF, ICH, and death — a 61-fold dosing error vs the IV push dose. ALWAYS dilute, ALWAYS label.
⚠ Push-dose pressors are a BRIDGE, not definitive therapy. They buy 5–15 minutes while a continuous infusion is mixed and started. Prepare the infusion simultaneously.
⚠ Epinephrine is the default. Weingart now recommends push-dose epinephrine over phenylephrine for most situations (combined inotropy + vasoconstriction).
⚠ Dilute correctly: 1 mL cardiac epi (1:10,000) + 9 mL NS = 10 mcg/mL — the same concentration as lidocaine with epi, safe peripherally. Do NOT reconstitute from a pre-filled saline flush.
⚠ Refractory anaphylaxis uses IV epi 10–20 mcg/min — NOT the arrest dose. Persistent symptoms after 2–3 IM epi doses; mix 1 mg in 100 mL NS = 10 mcg/mL and titrate.
⚠ If shock persists despite adequate push-dose + fluids: maximize the pressor, add vasopressin, stress-dose hydrocortisone, methylene blue, and consider VA-ECMO.

1 · Is this a push-dose moment?

What push-dose pressors are
Vasopressors given as a small IV bolus for immediate blood-pressure support, bridging to a continuous infusion when a drip cannot be running within 5–8 minutes.[1][2]

Hypotension complicates ~40% of emergency intubations in the critically ill and independently raises mortality — draw the pressor up before induction.[4][7]

The 1 mg error
These are not cardiac-arrest doses. Giving the 1 mg cardiac amp to a patient with a pulse is a 61-fold overdose that causes malignant hypertension, VT/VF, ICH, and death. ALWAYS dilute, ALWAYS label.[5]

2 · Pick your agent

Toggle the two pivotal states below — the box that lights up is your agent. Bradycardia overrides everything (phenylephrine is never appropriate when the heart is slow).

Give EPINEPHRINE — phenylephrine is contraindicated
Symptomatic bradycardia needs β-1 chronotropy and inotropy. Push-dose epinephrine 5-20 mcg IV q1-5 min. A pure α-agonist (phenylephrine) would worsen the bradycardia via reflex vagal tone.[1][2]
Reasonable to use PHENYLEPHRINE
Rapid AF / tachyarrhythmia with preserved cardiac output is the one clear win for phenylephrine: 50-200 mcg IV q3-5 min gives α-vasoconstriction without adding to the heart rate.[1][3]
Give EPINEPHRINE — the default inopressor
For most bedside scenarios epinephrine has won: combined α + β effects raise both cardiac output and perfusion pressure. Push-dose epinephrine 5-20 mcg IV q1-5 min.[1][6]

Why epinephrine is the default (evidence)response rates

Push-dose epinephrine: 71.8% of patients achieved a >25% SBP increase, and a history of CHF predicted an even better response (inotropic benefit in low-output states).[11]

Push-dose phenylephrine response rate is lower at 55.9%, with no inotropy and a risk of reflex bradycardia — reserve it for the tachyarrhythmia niche.[11]

3 · Mix it right

Epinephrine — EMCrit dilution
1 mL cardiac amp (1:10,000) + 9 mL NS
= 10 mcg/mL
Cardiac amp is 100 mcg/mL (1:10,000) — draw 1 mL into a 10 mL syringe pre-filled with 9 mL NS. Result is the same concentration as lidocaine with epi, safe peripherally. LABEL the syringe.
Phenylephrine — EMCrit dilution
1 mL (10 mg) + 100 mL NS
= 100 mcg/mL
Higher error potential than epi (more manipulation) — use a pharmacy pre-mixed 100 mcg/mL syringe when available.
Norepinephrine — push-dose premix
16 mcg/mL premix (or 1 mg in 100 mL = 10 mcg/mL)
peripheral OK <6 h
Emerging, primarily anesthesia settings; less bradycardia than phenylephrine and maintains CO better.
Do NOT reconstitute from a flush
The FDA prohibits using pre-filled saline flush syringes to reconstitute drugs. Draw from a proper NS source, mix well, and label with drug + concentration before the first dose.[1][2]

6 · Poor response & escalation

Fill the tank first
Pressors fail in hypovolemia. POCUS a collapsing IVC or a positive passive-leg-raise → 500-1000 mL crystalloid bolus and re-check. Patients who got >30 mL/kg before phenylephrine needed fewer, lower pressor doses.[9][4]

Phenylephrine not working? That usually means low cardiac output — switch to epinephrine (71.8% vs 55.9% response). Reassess for hemorrhage, acidosis, tension PTX, and tamponade.[1][11]

Bridge to the drip
Push-dose is a 5-15 minute bridge. Start the continuous infusion the moment it is mixed, run it low, wait 5-10 min, and wean the pushes as it takes effect. Target MAP 65 mmHg.[9]
Refractory shock escalation ladder5 steps
  1. Maximize the push-dose pressor (more frequent dosing).
  2. Start a continuous norepinephrine infusion (peripheral OK <6 h).
  3. Add vasopressin 0.03-0.04 units/min (non-titratable).
  4. Stress-dose hydrocortisone 50 mg IV q6h (or 200 mg/day).
  5. Methylene blue 1-2 mg/kg IV for NO-mediated vasoplegia; consider VA-ECMO.
Before climbing the ladder, re-confirm the diagnosis: rule out PE, cardiac tamponade, tension pneumothorax, and adrenal crisis.

6 · Poor response & escalation

Pressors fail in hypovolemia. POCUS a collapsing IVC or a positive passive-leg-raise → **500-1000 mL** crystalloid bolus and re-check. Patients who got >30 mL/kg before phenylephrine needed fewer, lower pressor doses.{{ref9}}{{ref4}}

**Phenylephrine not working?** That usually means low cardiac output — switch to epinephrine (71.8% vs 55.9% response). Reassess for hemorrhage, acidosis, tension PTX, and tamponade.{{ref1}}{{ref11}}

Push-dose is a 5-15 minute bridge. Start the continuous infusion the moment it is mixed, run it low, wait 5-10 min, and wean the pushes as it takes effect. Target MAP **65 mmHg**.{{ref9}}

Refractory shock escalation ladder

  • Maximize the push-dose pressor (more frequent dosing).
  • Start a continuous norepinephrine infusion (peripheral OK <6 h).
  • Add vasopressin 0.03-0.04 units/min (non-titratable).
  • Stress-dose hydrocortisone 50 mg IV q6h (or 200 mg/day).
  • Methylene blue 1-2 mg/kg IV for NO-mediated vasoplegia; consider VA-ECMO.

Before climbing the ladder, re-confirm the diagnosis: rule out PE, cardiac tamponade, tension pneumothorax, and adrenal crisis.
