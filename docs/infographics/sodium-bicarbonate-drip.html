Sodium Bicarbonate Drip Protocol

Adults in the ED/ICU requiring a CONTINUOUS sodium bicarbonate infusion (not a single reflex ampule). Covers severe metabolic acidemia/AKI, hyperkalemia with metabolic acidosis, salicylate toxicity, and TCA/sodium-channel blocker toxicity. Excludes routine cardiac arrest/CPR, isolated respiratory acidosis, routine DKA, routine lactic acidosis without severe acidemia/AKI, and metabolic alkalosis.

⚠ Indication-specific only — do NOT use bicarbonate drip as routine treatment for cardiac arrest, isolated lactic acidosis without severe acidemia/AKI, routine DKA, or primary respiratory acidosis.
⚠ Severe metabolic acidemia/AKI: best-supported ICU phenotype is pH ≤7.20 with low bicarbonate and moderate-severe AKI, uremic/bicarbonate-loss acidosis, or severe acidemia causing vasopressor resistance/cardiac dysfunction. Bicarbonate is a bridge, not source control — plan dialysis when refractory or due to renal failure/toxin requiring clearance.
⚠ Hyperkalemia + metabolic acidosis: give calcium, insulin/dextrose + albuterol, and arrange dialysis/diuresis/potassium binders FIRST for ECG changes or severe K+ — do NOT rely on bicarbonate alone for life-threatening hyperkalemia.
⚠ Salicylate toxicity: call poison center/toxicology and nephrology early; add potassium chloride 20-40 mEq/L (hypokalemia blocks urine alkalinization); target serum pH 7.45-7.55 (avoid >7.60) and urine pH 7.5-8.0; do NOT use acetazolamide; escalate to hemodialysis for severe poisoning or alkalinization failure.
⚠ TCA/sodium-channel blocker toxicity: bolus FIRST (1-2 mEq/kg, repeat q3-5min until QRS narrows) before the infusion; target serum pH 7.50-7.55 and QRS <100 ms; avoid class IA/IC antiarrhythmics; treat seizures with benzodiazepines.
⚠ Do NOT start a bicarbonate drip for routine cardiac arrest/CPR without hyperkalemia or sodium-channel toxicity, isolated respiratory acidosis, routine lactic acidosis, routine DKA (ADA/EASD 2024 consensus: consider only if pH <7.0, and even then no mortality benefit shown), or metabolic alkalosis.
⚠ Monitoring is mandatory throughout: continuous ECG for hyperkalemia, TCA/sodium-channel toxicity, severe acidemia, or rapid shifts; VBG/ABG q2-4h (q1-2h if unstable); ionized calcium q4-6h. Bicarbonate generates CO2 — ensure the patient can increase minute ventilation or is mechanically ventilated appropriately; inadequate ventilation worsens intracellular/respiratory acidosis.

1 · Indication

This protocol is for a continuous sodium bicarbonate infusion, not reflex ampules during routine resuscitation. Common indications: severe metabolic acidemia (pH ≤7.20 with moderate-severe AKI or bicarbonate loss), hyperkalemia with metabolic acidosis, salicylate toxicity, and TCA/sodium-channel blocker toxicity after bolus therapy.

The target and infusion rate depend on whether the goal is pH correction, potassium shift, urine alkalinization, or sodium-channel rescue — select the indication above.

2 · What to do now

Severe metabolic acidemia / AKI
Best-supported ICU phenotype: pH ≤7.20 with low bicarbonate and moderate-severe AKI, uremic/bicarbonate-loss acidosis, or severe acidemia causing vasopressor resistance/cardiac dysfunction. Sodium bicarbonate 150 mEq in 1 L D5W/sterile water at 100-250 mL/hr, or as resuscitative fluid if volume depleted. Target pH >7.20 initially, then >7.30 (BICAR-ICU-style). Treat the cause and plan dialysis if refractory or renal failure/toxin requires clearance.
Hyperkalemia + metabolic acidosis
Do FIRST for ECG changes/severe K+: calcium for membrane stabilization, insulin/dextrose + albuterol for rapid intracellular shift, dialysis/diuresis/potassium binders for removal. Sodium bicarbonate as resuscitative fluid: 150 mEq in 1 L D5W/sterile water at 500-1000 mL/hr if hypovolemic/acidotic, slower if euvolemic/overload risk. Once serum bicarbonate is >24 and more volume is needed, transition to balanced crystalloid. Do NOT rely on bicarbonate alone for life-threatening hyperkalemia.
Salicylate toxicity
Goal is serum AND urine alkalinization, not a bicarbonate number. Call poison center/toxicology and nephrology early. Give Sodium bicarbonate 1-2 mEq/kg IV bolus if severe toxicity, acidemia, altered mental status, or before unavoidable intubation, then infuse 150 mEq in 1 L D5W at 150-250 mL/hr. Add Potassium chloride IV 20-40 mEq/L unless contraindicated — hypokalemia blocks urine alkalinization. Targets: serum pH 7.45-7.55 (avoid >7.60), urine pH 7.5-8.0, urine output 2-3 mL/kg/hr. Do NOT use acetazolamide. Escalate to hemodialysis for severe poisoning or alkalinization failure.
TCA / sodium-channel blocker
Sodium bicarbonate 1-2 mEq/kg IV bolus, repeat q3-5 min until QRS narrows, hypotension improves, or pH target reached. Target serum pH 7.50-7.55 and QRS <100 ms. Then infusion: 150 mEq in 1 L D5W at 150-250 mL/hr while QRS/hemodynamics require alkalinization. Avoid class IA/IC antiarrhythmics; treat seizures with benzodiazepines; involve poison center/toxicology.
Unclear or routine arrest/lactic/DKA — hold the drip
Hold bicarbonate drip and treat the underlying process unless a clear indication/target exists. See the Section 8 do-not table below.

5 · Monitoring

  • VBG/ABG q2-4 hr during active titration; q1-2 hr if unstable or target-dependent tox case
  • BMP q2-4 hr: sodium, potassium, bicarbonate, chloride, creatinine
  • Ionized calcium q4-6 hr or sooner if symptomatic/hypocalcemia risk
  • Volume status, pulmonary edema, urine output
  • Continuous ECG for hyperkalemia, TCA/sodium-channel toxicity, severe acidemia, or rapid shifts

Ventilation requirement
Bicarbonate generates CO2. Ensure the patient can increase minute ventilation or is mechanically ventilated appropriately — inadequate ventilation worsens intracellular/respiratory acidosis.

6 · Complications

  • Hypernatremia and hyperosmolality
  • Hypokalemia from intracellular shift
  • Lower ionized calcium with tetany, seizures, hypotension, or reduced contractility
  • Volume overload / pulmonary edema
  • Metabolic alkalosis
  • Increased CO2 load with worsening respiratory acidosis if ventilation is inadequate
  • Delayed dialysis when bicarbonate is used beyond its role as a bridge
Stop / slow the drip
Stop or slow the drip when targets are reached, adverse effects develop, or definitive therapy has taken over.

7 · Disposition / Handoff

Most bicarbonate drip patients need ICU, stepdown, or close monitored care. Continue only with a clear endpoint, monitoring schedule, and definitive treatment plan.

  1. Indication and target: pH, urine pH, QRS, potassium, or bridge to dialysis
  2. Recipe and current rate
  3. Total bicarbonate/sodium administered
  4. Latest pH, Na, K, ionized Ca, creatinine, urine output
  5. Ventilation status and CO2 clearance
  6. Stop criteria and next lab time
  7. Toxicology/nephrology/poison center recommendations when applicable

3 · Management algorithm

Bicarbonate Drip — Scope — Continuous infusion, not reflex ampules. 4 core indications; avoid routine use without a severe-acidemia/target/tox indication.

Why start a bicarbonate drip? — 5-way indication: metabolic/AKI, hyperkalemia, salicylate, TCA/Na-channel, or unclear/routine.

Severe Metabolic Acidemia / AKI — pH ≤7.20 + AKI/bicarbonate-loss. 150 mEq/1L D5W at 100-250 mL/hr. Target pH >7.20 then >7.30.

Hyperkalemia + Acidosis — Calcium/insulin-dextrose/albuterol/dialysis first. Bicarb as resuscitative fluid 500-1000 mL/hr if hypovolemic/acidotic.

Salicylate Toxicity — Bolus 1-2 mEq/kg if severe. Infusion 150-250 mL/hr + KCl 20-40 mEq/L. Target serum pH 7.45-7.55, urine pH 7.5-8.0.

TCA / Sodium-Channel Blocker — Bolus 1-2 mEq/kg q3-5min until QRS narrows. Target pH 7.50-7.55, QRS <100ms. Then infusion 150-250 mL/hr.

Not Routine — Hold Drip — Do not use for routine arrest, isolated respiratory acidosis, routine lactic acidosis, routine DKA, or metabolic alkalosis.
