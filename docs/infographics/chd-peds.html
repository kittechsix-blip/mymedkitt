Congenital Heart Disease in the Neonate: Blue Baby vs Shocked Baby

Neonates and young infants (birth to ~6 months, ~0.5-15 kg) in the ED/NICU with suspected critical congenital heart disease — cyanosis unresponsive to O2, or shock/poor perfusion as the ductus arteriosus closes. Excludes older children with known repaired CHD presenting for unrelated complaints and acquired (non-congenital) pediatric cardiomyopathy.

⚠ Tet spell (hypercyanotic episode): knee-chest position first, calm the infant, 100% O2, then Morphine 0.1-0.2 mg/kg IV/IM. If refractory: Phenylephrine 5-20 mcg/kg IV bolus, then Propranolol 0.1 mg/kg slow IV push, then Ketamine 1-2 mg/kg IV. Avoid agitation, crying, and volume depletion.
⚠ Transposition of the Great Arteries is DUCTAL-DEPENDENT — start PGE1 0.05-0.1 mcg/kg/min immediately and get emergent cardiology consult for balloon atrial septostomy (BAS); do not wait for echo confirmation.
⚠ Coarctation of the aorta: weak/absent femoral pulses + arm-to-leg BP gradient >20 mmHg. Start PGE1 0.05-0.1 mcg/kg/min to reopen the ductus. This is cardiogenic, not hypovolemic, shock — use fluids and inotropes (Dopamine or Epinephrine) cautiously.
⚠ Hypoplastic left heart syndrome (HLHS) is DUCTAL-DEPENDENT for all systemic perfusion. Start PGE1 immediately. AVOID high FiO2 — target SpO2 75-85% (NOT normal); high O2 steals blood from the systemic circulation. Allow mild permissive hypercapnia.
⚠ PGE1 0.05-0.1 mcg/kg/min continuous infusion. Be ready for apnea (12% incidence) — have intubation equipment ready before starting. Continuous infusion only, never a bolus. Do not wait for echo to start if clinical suspicion is high.
⚠ Do NOT use high FiO2 in ductal-dependent lesions — worsens cyanosis paradoxically; target SpO2 75-85% in HLHS and similar lesions; room air or minimal O2 is often better.
⚠ Do NOT give excessive fluid resuscitation — these are cardiogenic, not hypovolemic, shocks; use small boluses (10 mL/kg) judiciously; large boluses may worsen pulmonary edema.
⚠ Do NOT wait for echo to start PGE1 — if clinical suspicion is high, start PGE1 empirically; echo confirms but should not delay treatment.
⚠ Do NOT miss coarctation — always check femoral pulses and compare arm/leg BP in a sick neonate; coarctation can mimic sepsis (gray, acidotic, poor perfusion).
⚠ Do NOT hyperventilate — blowing off CO2 decreases pulmonary vascular resistance; in HLHS this steals systemic blood flow; allow mild permissive hypercapnia.
⚠ Do NOT treat a sick neonate as sepsis only — start antibiotics AND PGE1 if uncertain; check pulses and 4-extremity sats.

1 · Recognition

Congenital Heart Disease (CHD) affects ~1% of live births. Critical CHD presents in the first weeks of life as the ductus arteriosus closes.

Key assessment: cyanosis that does NOT improve with supplemental O2, poor perfusion/weak pulses/shock, murmur (may be absent in critical lesions), hepatomegaly, respiratory distress.

4-extremity assessment: obtain simultaneous pre-ductal (right arm) and post-ductal (either foot) saturations, plus 4-extremity blood pressure. Weak/absent femoral pulses = coarctation until proven otherwise.

FindingInterpretation
Pre-ductal sat > post-ductal by >3%Right-to-left shunt through PDA (ductal-dependent systemic lesion)
Post-ductal sat > pre-ductalTGA with coarctation (rare)
Both pre- and post-ductal sats <90%Cyanotic heart disease
Arm BP > leg BP by >20 mmHgCoarctation of the aorta
Femoral pulses weak/absentCoarctation until proven otherwise

Hyperoxia test distinguishes cardiac from pulmonary cyanosis: baseline ABG on room air (or current FiO2) → 100% FiO2 for 10 minutes → repeat ABG.

<100 mmHgCARDIAC cause (fixed shunt) — does not respond to O2

Hyperoxia test suggests a PULMONARY cause. Differential: persistent pulmonary hypertension of the newborn (PPHN), respiratory distress syndrome, meconium aspiration, pneumonia/sepsis, congenital diaphragmatic hernia, airway obstruction. Still obtain echo if clinical suspicion for CHD remains high, a murmur is present, or 4-extremity sats/BP are abnormal.

5 · Shocked baby detail — left-sided obstruction

Cardiogenic shock in the neonate: pale/gray/mottled (not blue), poor perfusion, weak pulses, tachycardia/tachypnea, hepatomegaly, metabolic acidosis. May appear septic. Check femoral pulses — weak/absent = coarctation until proven otherwise.

Coarctation of the aorta: narrowing typically just distal to the left subclavian artery. May be asymptomatic while the ductus is open; shock when ductus closes (days 1-14). Weak/absent femoral pulses (pathognomonic). Associated: bicuspid aortic valve (50%), VSD, Turner syndrome (screen females).

Hypoplastic Left Heart Syndrome: underdeveloped LV, mitral valve, aortic valve, ascending aorta. 6 · PGE1, pitfalls & stabilization

Prostaglandin E1 (Alprostadil) — the duct drug. Keeps the ductus arteriosus patent; life-saving in ductal-dependent lesions. Indications: suspected ductal-dependent CHD, cyanotic neonate failing the hyperoxia test, shocked neonate with suspected CHD — do NOT wait for echo. Effect within 15-30 minutes. Central line preferred; do NOT bolus.

7 · Disposition

  • Call cardiology immediately for any neonate with suspected CHD, unexplained cyanosis, cardiac shock, abnormal echo, or PGE1 started
  • Provide: age/weight/gestational age, presenting symptoms and timeline, current vitals including 4-extremity sats, response to interventions (O2, PGE1), CXR/ECG findings, echo findings if available
  • Request urgent bedside echo if available; do NOT delay PGE1 for it
  • Most critical CHD requires a surgical center — stabilize before transport
  • Airway secured (if on PGE1, low threshold to intubate)
  • IV access (two lines if possible, one dedicated to PGE1)
  • PGE1 running with adequate supply for transport, plus backup PGE1 infusion prepared
  • Intubation equipment available
  • Blood glucose checked and corrected
  • Temperature maintained
  • Neonatal/pediatric critical care transport team, PGE1/apnea-capable, intubation-capable
  • Destination: pediatric cardiac surgery center, NICU/PICU with cardiology coverage

Dopamine: 5-20 mcg/kg/min IV infusion — For cardiogenic shock. Use cautiously with coarctation. Alternative: Epinephrine.

Epinephrine: 0.01-0.1 mcg/kg/min IV infusion — Alternative to dopamine for cardiogenic shock in coarctation.

Hypoplastic Left Heart Syndrome: underdeveloped LV, mitral valve, aortic valve, ascending aorta. Systemic circulation entirely dependent on the PDA — the RV pumps to both pulmonary AND systemic circulations via the PDA. May look well initially, then rapid deterioration as the ductus closes. Single S2 (no aortic component).

Prostaglandin E1 (Alprostadil): 0.05-0.1 mcg/kg/min continuous IV — Critical for systemic perfusion — start immediately.

%: Too low 0-75; Target in HLHS (NOT normal) 75-85; Too high — steals systemic blood flow 85-100

6 · PGE1, pitfalls & stabilization

Prostaglandin E1 (Alprostadil) — the duct drug. Keeps the ductus arteriosus patent; life-saving in ductal-dependent lesions. Indications: suspected ductal-dependent CHD, cyanotic neonate failing the hyperoxia test, shocked neonate with suspected CHD — do NOT wait for echo. Effect within 15-30 minutes. Central line preferred; do NOT bolus.

Prostaglandin E1 (Alprostadil): 0.05-0.1 mcg/kg/min continuous IV (initial) — Titrate to effect (improved sats, perfusion, lactate).

Prostaglandin E1 (Alprostadil): 0.01-0.05 mcg/kg/min continuous IV (maintenance) — Once ductus open and stable — can often wean to this range.

PGE1 side-effect management detail

Side effect | Management note

Apnea (12%) | Most significant — usually within the first hour; have intubation equipment ready

Fever | Common, typically low-grade; may mimic sepsis; usually benign, monitor

Hypotension | From vasodilation; support with fluids, consider inotropes, may need to decrease infusion rate

Flushing/erythema | Cutaneous vasodilation; benign, no treatment needed

Bradycardia | Less common; monitor, decrease dose if significant

Jitteriness/seizure-like activity | Usually benign, not true seizures; EEG typically normal

Long-term effects (prolonged use, weeks) | Cortical hyperostosis (bone thickening), gastric outlet obstruction

Pitfall | Why it matters

High FiO2 in ductal-dependent lesions | Constricts the ductus, may worsen cyanosis paradoxically; target SpO2 75-85% in HLHS

Excessive fluid resuscitation | This is cardiogenic, not hypovolemic, shock — large boluses may worsen pulmonary edema; use 10 mL/kg boluses

Waiting for echo to start PGE1 | If clinical suspicion is high, start PGE1 — echo should confirm, not delay treatment

Missing coarctation | Always check femoral pulses and compare arm/leg BP — can mimic sepsis

Hyperventilation | Decreases PVR; in HLHS this steals systemic blood flow — allow permissive hypercapnia

Treating CHD as sepsis only | Give antibiotics AND PGE1 if uncertain; check pulses and 4-extremity sats

  • Airway — low threshold to intubate if on PGE1 (apnea risk); ketamine preferred for induction (maintains SVR); avoid excessive positive pressure
  • Breathing — SpO2 target by lesion: cyanotic/HLHS accept 75-85%; avoid high FiO2 unless clear benefit; avoid hyperventilation
  • Circulation — IV/IO access immediately; start PGE1 if ductal-dependent suspected; fluid boluses 10 mL/kg (not 20); inotropes dopamine or epinephrine; correct acidosis cautiously
  • Disability — check glucose (hypoglycemia common); avoid hyperthermia; monitor neurologic status
  • Exposure — maintain normothermia; full exam for associated anomalies

Ketamine: 1-2 mg/kg IV — Preferred induction agent — maintains SVR and HR, preserves respiratory drive longer.

Fentanyl: 1-2 mcg/kg IV — Alternative induction agent if ketamine contraindicated — less hemodynamic effect.

Rocuronium: 1 mg/kg IV — Paralytic.

Succinylcholine: 2 mg/kg IV — Paralytic — if rapid onset needed.

Avoid: propofol (significant hypotension), high-dose opioids without preparation, prolonged laryngoscopy (vagal). Ventilation: avoid excessive positive pressure, target normal (not hyper-) ventilation, FiO2 as low as tolerated.

7 · Disposition

  • Call cardiology immediately for any neonate with suspected CHD, unexplained cyanosis, cardiac shock, abnormal echo, or PGE1 started
  • Provide: age/weight/gestational age, presenting symptoms and timeline, current vitals including 4-extremity sats, response to interventions (O2, PGE1), CXR/ECG findings, echo findings if available
  • Request urgent bedside echo if available; do NOT delay PGE1 for it
  • Most critical CHD requires a surgical center — stabilize before transport
  • Airway secured (if on PGE1, low threshold to intubate)
  • IV access (two lines if possible, one dedicated to PGE1)
  • PGE1 running with adequate supply for transport, plus backup PGE1 infusion prepared
  • Intubation equipment available
  • Blood glucose checked and corrected
  • Temperature maintained
  • Neonatal/pediatric critical care transport team, PGE1/apnea-capable, intubation-capable
  • Destination: pediatric cardiac surgery center, NICU/PICU with cardiology coverage

ECMO in neonatal CHD | Detail

Indications | Refractory cardiogenic shock despite maximal medical therapy; bridge to surgery; post-op cardiac failure; refractory arrhythmias with hemodynamic compromise

Mode | Venoarterial (VA) ECMO for cardiac support — peripheral or central cannulation

Relative contraindications | Extreme prematurity, lethal chromosomal abnormalities, irreversible organ damage, prolonged cardiac arrest with poor neurologic prognosis

Transport | Some centers offer mobile ECMO; consider early transfer before ECMO is needed
