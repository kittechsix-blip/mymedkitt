Adult Constipation: Impaction, Ileus & Ogilvie Syndrome

Adults in the ED with constipation, suspected fecal impaction, postoperative ileus, or acute colonic pseudo-obstruction (ACPO/Ogilvie syndrome). Excludes pediatric constipation and confirmed mechanical bowel obstruction (that pathway is urgent surgical workup, not this algorithm).

⚠ Red flags (bilious vomiting, complete obstipation, rigid/peritoneal abdomen, coffee-bean sign, pain out of proportion, fever+tachycardia with colonic dilation) → immediate abdominal imaging (XR or CT) + surgical consult BEFORE categorizing the constipation type.
⚠ ACPO/Ogilvie perforation risk: cecal diameter >12 cm OR duration >6 days = high risk; cecal perforation mortality is 40-50%.
⚠ Neostigmine contraindications: mechanical bowel obstruction (must be ruled out first), recent MI or severe cardiac disease, active bronchospasm, pregnancy, renal failure (relative, Cr >3 mg/dL). Pre-treatment requires continuous cardiac monitoring, IV access, and atropine 0.5 mg at the bedside.
⚠ During/after neostigmine: give atropine 0.5 mg IV for HR <60 with symptoms or asymptomatic HR <40. Watch for the "Killer Bees" — bradycardia (most common), bronchospasm, bronchorrhea.
⚠ Sodium phosphate (Fleet) enema: single dose only (118 mL) — avoid in elderly, renal impairment, heart failure, or dehydration; risk of fatal hyperphosphatemia and hypocalcemia. Tap water or normal saline enema (500-1000 mL) is safer.

Fecal impaction — soften, disimpact, cleanse

3 · Management algorithm

Adult Constipation Spectrum — 15-20% prevalence. Spectrum: simple constipation → impaction → ileus → Ogilvie. Rome IV: <3 BMs/week.

Key History Elements — Last BM, alarm symptoms, post-surgical status, constipating medications (opioids #1).

Red Flags Present? — Obstruction, perforation, volvulus, ischemia, toxic megacolon.

Urgent Evaluation — XR or CT. NPO, IV fluids, NG if vomiting. Surgical consult if obstruction/perforation.

Categorize the Problem — Impaction = rectal mass. POI = post-op, diffuse dilation. ACPO = massive colonic dilation.

Fecal Impaction Assessment — Risk: elderly, opioids, immobility. Paradoxical diarrhea common. Can cause delirium in elderly.

Disimpaction Technique & Aftercare — Pre-medicate (acetaminophen/ketorolac, +/- benzo or pudendal block if severe), position left lateral decubitus with knees to chest. Soften with mineral oil enema (120-150 mL) → manual disimpaction (scissoring/hook, stop if vagal symptoms) → cleansing enema (500-1000 mL, avoid phosphate in elderly/renal disease). If above reach: whole-bowel irrigation (GoLYTELY via NG) or GI consult for colonoscopic decompression.

Postoperative Ileus — Expected 3-5 days post-colonic surgery. Opioids major contributor. Rule out mechanical SBO.

Ileus Management & Escalation — NPO, NG, correct K+/Mg2+, minimize opioids, early ambulation, gum chewing, alvimopan for select cases (methylnaltrexone: limited POI evidence, more established for opioid-induced constipation). If >5-7 days: repeat CT, check infection, consult GI/surgery.

ACPO / Ogilvie Assessment & Imaging — Massive colonic dilation without obstruction. Cecum >12 cm or >6 days = high perforation risk (mortality 40-50%). Measure cecal diameter on XR/CT — haustral markings preserved (vs. toxic megacolon). CT: no transition point. Water-soluble contrast enema is an alternative if CT unavailable/equivocal.

ACPO Initial Management — Conservative Rx: NPO, NG, rectal tube, correct K+/Mg2+, stop offending meds. 80% respond.

ACPO Monitoring — Serial XR q12-24h, exam q4-6h, daily lytes. Expect improvement in 24-72h.

Neostigmine Administration — 2 mg IV over 5 FULL MINUTES. 91% response. Contraindicated in mechanical obstruction, recent MI, bronchospasm, pregnancy, renal failure (Cr>3). Atropine 0.5 mg if HR<60 symptomatic or HR<40. Watch Killer Bees: bradycardia, bronchospasm, bronchorrhea. Max 3 doses.

If Neostigmine Fails — Colonoscopic decompression (70-80% success). Surgery mortality ~25% — avoid if possible. PEG prevents recurrence (33% without vs ~0% with).

Simple Constipation Treatment — PEG is first-line. Mag citrate for rapid effect. Avoid docusate alone — no better than placebo.

Maintenance Therapy — PEG 17 g daily long-term. Rx options: linaclotide, prucalopride if OTC fails. Fiber + fluids.

Disposition — Discharge: simple constipation treated. Admit: ACPO, ileus, refractory impaction, unstable.

Discharge Home — PEG daily, fiber/fluids, PCP follow-up. Return if no BM ×3 days, severe pain, vomiting.

Admit — NPO, NG, rectal tube, serial KUB, correct lytes, ambulate, GI/surgery consult as needed.
