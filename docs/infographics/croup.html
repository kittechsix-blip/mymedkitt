Croup: One Steroid for All, Epinephrine for Stridor at Rest

Children 6 months–6 years in the ED with viral croup (laryngotracheobronchitis). Excludes suspected bacterial tracheitis, epiglottitis, retropharyngeal abscess, and airway foreign body — each needs a different pathway.

⚠ Give dexamethasone 0.6 mg/kg PO (max 16 mg) to EVERY severity — a single dose reduces return visits by ~50%. No child with croup should leave without a steroid.
⚠ Epinephrine relief is temporary (onset minutes, duration 1–2 h). Observe a minimum of 2 hours after the last nebulized epinephrine dose for rebound stridor before any discharge.
⚠ If intubation is needed, use an ETT 0.5–1.0 size SMALLER than age-predicted (subglottic narrowing) and call anesthesia/ENT for airway backup.
⚠ Keep the child calm — agitation worsens airway obstruction. Use blow-by oxygen, leave the child on the caregiver's lap, and minimize interventions in severe disease.

1 · Recognize croup

Subglottic inflammation from parainfluenza
Barking, seal-like cough + inspiratory stridor + hoarseness, usually after a 1–3 day URI prodrome with low-grade fever. The parainfluenza virus inflames the subglottic airway — the narrowest point in a young child.[1]

Affects 3% of children aged 6 months to 3 years, peaking at 1–2 years. Most cases are mild and self-limited — only 1–8% require hospitalization and <3% of admitted patients require intubation.[1]

Before you commit to the croup pathway
Toxic appearance, drooling, high fever, no barking cough, or failure to improve with steroid + epinephrine → rule out bacterial tracheitis, epiglottitis, retropharyngeal abscess, or airway foreign body.[3]

2 · Classify severity

The Westley Croup Score grades stridor, retractions, air entry, cyanosis, and consciousness. The pivotal clinical question is stridor at rest vs. stridor only with agitation — it sets the threshold for nebulized epinephrine.[5]

Mild, but cannot take oral — steroid by another route
No stridor at rest → no epinephrine needed. Give steroid by a non-oral route: Dexamethasone IM or Budesonide 2 mg nebulized.[5] Then observe 1–2 hours for improvement.
Mild — a single oral steroid is the whole treatment
No stridor at rest → no epinephrine. Give one dose of Dexamethasone 0.6 mg/kg PO (max 16 mg), observe 1–2 hours, and discharge with return precautions.[2]

5 · Observe & disposition

  • Post-dexamethasone: observe 1–2 h. Most children improve within 2–3 h; the ~36 h half-life gives sustained benefit from one dose.
  • Post-epinephrine: observe a minimum of 2 hours after the last dose — symptoms can rebound as the effect wears off.
  • Repeat epinephrine at the same dose restarts the 2-hour clock; needing >2 doses is a strong indicator for admission.
  • Discharge when there is no stridor at rest for ≥2 h after the last epinephrine, the child tolerates fluids, and a reliable caregiver has access to emergency care.
