algorithm

IIH (Pseudotumor Cerebri): Ocular POCUS, Confirm, Treat by Severity

Adults with suspected idiopathic intracranial hypertension (IIH / pseudotumor cerebri) in the ED. Excludes patients with an already-identified secondary cause (mass, CVST, medication-induced) — those follow the underlying etiology's own pathway once found. Pediatric opening-pressure threshold (>28 cm H₂O) is noted only where the source explicitly gives it; this pathway otherwise models adult criteria.

⚠ Vision loss is the major morbidity of IIH — it can be irreversible if untreated.
⚠ Exclude secondary causes before diagnosing IIH: cerebral venous sinus thrombosis (CVST), intracranial mass, meningitis/encephalitis, and hydrocephalus. Red-flag risk factors: pregnancy/postpartum, oral contraceptives, hypercoagulable state, recent head trauma, infection (otitis/mastoiditis). Medications that raise ICP: vitamin A/retinoids, tetracyclines, growth hormone, lithium, corticosteroid withdrawal.
⚠ Neuroimaging (MRI with MRV, or CT/CTV) is required BEFORE lumbar puncture — to rule out mass and CVST.
⚠ Absence of papilledema does NOT rule out IIH — "IIH without papilledema" (IIHWOP) is recognized; ~5% of IIH patients lack papilledema.
⚠ If CSF is abnormal, it is NOT IIH — evaluate for meningitis, carcinomatosis, or an inflammatory process instead.
⚠ Acetazolamide contraindications: sulfonamide allergy (cross-reactivity possible), severe hepatic/renal disease, uncorrected hypokalemia, adrenal insufficiency.
⚠ Fulminant IIH is an emergency — rapid visual decline, PMD <-7 dB, visual acuity loss, or high-grade papilledema (Frisén 4-5) requires emergent ophthalmology + neurosurgery consult, IV acetazolamide, therapeutic LP, and admission.

2 · Ocular POCUS — ONSD & fundoscopy

ACEP core ocular ultrasound application
Ocular POCUS is a core emergency ultrasound competency per ACEP 2023 guidelines. Two key findings: Optic Nerve Sheath Diameter (ONSD) — precedes papilledema by hours to days — and Optic Disc Elevation (ODE) — ≥0.6 mm elevation is significant. Advantages: rapid (2-3 min), non-invasive, can be done before CT/LP.[4]
  1. High-frequency linear probe (7.5-12 MHz); patient supine, eyes closed, looking straight ahead.
  2. Place probe gently on the closed eyelid (transverse), generous gel.
  3. Identify the optic nerve as a hypoechoic stripe behind the globe.
  4. Measure 3 mm posterior to the globe, inner edge to inner edge of the sheath.
  5. Repeat in both eyes and take the average — unilateral findings may be artifact.

Diagnostic accuracy of ONSD >5 mm: sensitivity 88-100%, specificity 63-95%. ONSD dilates before papilledema develops on fundoscopy.[5]

Frisén GradeFindings
0Normal disc
1C-shaped halo, temporal blurring preserved
2Circumferential halo, no obscuration
3Partial obscuration of vessels at disc
4Total vessel obscuration at disc
5Dome-shaped protrusion, obliterated cup

Papilledema is present if ANY of: blurred disc margins, elevated optic disc, obscured vessels at the disc margin, venous engorgement, loss of spontaneous venous pulsations, or peripapillary hemorrhages.[1][8]

Absence of papilledema does not rule out IIH
"IIH without papilledema" (IIHWOP) is recognized — ONSD elevation may precede papilledema and ~5% of IIH patients lack papilledema.[1][2]

If ONSD <5 mm: ONSD sensitivity is not perfect (prior optic nerve pathology, or chronic elevated ICP, can produce a normal reading) — still consider IIH if classic symptoms persist (positional headache, pulsatile tinnitus, transient visual obscurations, typical demographics). Alternative diagnoses to consider: migraine, tension-type headache, medication-overuse headache, cervicogenic headache.[5][6]

Ophthalmology consult (when fundus cannot be adequately visualized, or papilledema findings are uncertain): dilated fundoscopic exam, formal Frisén grading, Humphrey visual-field perimetry (perimetric mean deviation guides treatment intensity), optic-nerve photography, OCT if available. Timeline: severe vision loss → same-day; moderate concerns → 24-48 h; mild concerns → within 1 week.[1][9]

Probable IIH: meets criteria 1-4 but OP 20-25 cm H₂O. IIH without papilledema (IIHWOP): meets criteria 2-5, no papilledema but has CN VI palsy, plus additional supporting MRI features — empty sella, optic nerve sheath distension, or transverse sinus stenosis.[1][2][3]

Empty sella70%
Optic nerve sheath distension45%

MRI + MRV is preferred over CT/CTV; these IIH-associated findings SUPPORT but do not CONFIRM the diagnosis — LP is still required. If MRI unavailable, CT head without contrast + CTV is adequate to rule out mass/CVST but is less sensitive for IIH-associated findings.[1][3]

LP technique: lateral decubitus position (most accurate opening pressure), legs extended (flexion falsely elevates OP). Measure opening pressure before removing fluid. CSF analysis (must be normal for IIH): protein, glucose, cell count, Gram stain/culture.[1][2]

Therapeutic LP: remove 20-30 mL CSF — diagnostic and therapeutic, provides temporary symptom relief. If CSF is abnormal, it is NOT IIH — consider meningitis, carcinomatosis, or another inflammatory process.[1][2]

Not IIH: elevated protein → inflammatory, infectious, or neoplastic process; pleocytosis → meningitis or carcinomatosis; low glucose → bacterial/fungal meningitis or carcinomatosis. If OP is normal with papilledema, consider pseudopapilledema (optic disc drusen) or a recent LP that transiently normalized pressure — repeat after a recovery period. Other alternatives: CVST, chronic meningitis, leptomeningeal carcinomatosis, sarcoidosis.[1][2]

Mild — acetazolamide + weight loss
Start Acetazolamide 500 mg PO BID (1 g/day), titrate to 2-4 g/day as tolerated (IIHTT mean dose 2.5 g/day at 6 months, max 4 g/day). Weight loss target 5-10% body weight + low-sodium diet. Expected side effects: paresthesias, fatigue, dysgeusia, GI upset — check BMP for hypokalemia. Headache: topiramate 25-100 mg BID is an alternative (also promotes weight loss); avoid long-term NSAIDs.[9][10][11]
Moderate — higher-dose acetazolamide + serial LPs
Acetazolamide 500 mg PO QID (2 g/day) or 1 g BID, titrate rapidly to 3-4 g/day; consider IV acetazolamide 500 mg if symptoms are severe. Serial therapeutic LPs (remove 20-30 mL) q1-3 days buys time for medications to work. Dietary consult; bariatric surgery referral if BMI >40. Close follow-up: visual fields 1-2 weeks, ophthalmology within 1 week. If no improvement in 2-4 weeks — escalate to surgery (ONSF or CSF shunting).[1][9][10]
Severe / Fulminant — emergent surgical pathway
Fulminant IIH is an emergency: emergent ophthalmology and neurosurgery consults, IV acetazolamide 500 mg, therapeutic LP remove 30-40 mL CSF, and admit. Surgical options: Optic nerve sheath fenestration (ONSF) for vision-predominant/rapid loss (~90% stabilization/improvement); VP/LP shunt for headache-predominant or failed ONSF; venous sinus stenting for transverse sinus stenosis with a pressure gradient.[1][9][12]

5 · Disposition

Discharge (mild): stable, tolerating oral meds, follow-up arranged. Follow-up: ophthalmology 1-2 weeks (visual fields, fundus exam), neurology 2-4 weeks, PCP 1-2 weeks (BMP, weight). Return immediately for vision changes, severe headache unrelieved by medication, nausea/vomiting, or weakness/numbness.[1][9][10]

Set follow-up reliability above — admit if rapid follow-up cannot be arranged, patient needs serial LPs, is not tolerating oral medication, or has social factors limiting compliance; discharge only if stable after therapeutic LP with confirmed ophthalmology follow-up in 3-5 days.

Admit — moderate, follow-up not reliable
Admit for observation and treatment: acetazolamide 500 mg PO QID (2 g/day) titrated as tolerated, daily visual acuity/pupil checks, therapeutic LP q1-2 days PRN, daily BMP, dietary consult, ophthalmology consult for visual fields, neurology co-management. Discharge when visual function is stable/improving, tolerating ≥2 g/day PO, and follow-up is confirmed.[1][9]
Discharge — moderate, close follow-up confirmed
Discharge with very close follow-up: acetazolamide 500 mg PO TID-QID (1.5-2 g/day), potassium 20-40 mEq PO daily. CRITICAL follow-up: ophthalmology within 3-5 days (visual fields mandatory), neurology within 1 week, PCP within 1 week. Document patient understanding of severity and return precautions; low threshold to return for ANY vision change, severe unrelieved headache, vomiting, or confusion.[1][9][10]

Admit (severe/fulminant): neuro checks q4h (visual acuity, pupil exam), acetazolamide IV 500 mg q6h (or PO if tolerating), therapeutic LP if not recently done, strict I/Os, daily weights, low-sodium diet, urgent ophthalmology and neurosurgery consults. Surgical planning: ONSF if vision-predominant, VP/LP shunt if headache-predominant, venous stenting if gradient >8 mmHg on venogram.[1][9][12]

7 · Do NOT

Do NOT diagnose IIH before excluding CVST, mass, meningitis, or hydrocephalus — MRV (or CTV) is mandatory, especially with CVST risk factors.

Do NOT perform LP before neuroimaging — MRI+MRV (or CT/CTV) is required first to rule out mass and CVST.
