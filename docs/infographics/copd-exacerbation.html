1 · Recognize & set the oxygen target

AECOPD = acute worsening + the Anthonisen triad
An acute worsening of respiratory symptoms needing added therapy. The cardinal (Anthonisen) symptoms are: (1) increased dyspnea, (2) increased sputum volume, (3) increased sputum purulence. Most triggers are infectious — viral 50–70%, bacterial 30–50%.[4]
The one number that changes everything
Target SpO2 88-92% (NOT higher - avoid hyperoxia). Over-oxygenating a CO2-retainer worsens V/Q mismatch and drives hypercapnia — it can precipitate respiratory failure. Titrate FiO2 down to the band; do not chase 100%.[1]
  1. IV access, cardiac monitor, continuous SpO2.
  2. Titrate O2 to SpO2 88-92% — low-flow NC or venturi.
  3. Start scheduled nebulized bronchodilators (below).
  4. Labs: CBC, BMP, VBG/ABG, procalcitonin; CXR to rule out pneumonia, CHF, pneumothorax.

5 · NIV, sedation & the airway

Why BiPAP first, not intubation
In hypercapnic AECOPD, NIV reduces intubation (RR 0.41, NNT 4) and mortality (RR 0.52, NNT 10). Start it for pH <7.35 / PaCO2 >45, RR >30, or severe work of breathing.[6] If BiPAP is contraindicated or not tolerated, HFNC (flow 30–60 L/min, FiO2 to SpO2 88–92%) is the GOLD 2026-endorsed salvage/comfort option.[11]
  1. Start iPAP 10 / ePAP 5 cm H2O; FiO2 to SpO2 88–92%.
  2. Increase iPAP by 2–3 cm every 5–10 min toward 18–20 / ePAP 8.
  3. Confirm tidal volume >300–400 mL and falling RR; reassess pH at 2h.
