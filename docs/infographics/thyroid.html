Thyroid Emergencies: Two Lethal Extremes

Adults in the ED with a suspected thyroid emergency — decompensated hypothyroidism ("myxedema coma") or thyroid storm. Both are clinical diagnoses; do NOT delay treatment for lab confirmation. Excludes routine outpatient thyroid management. Subclinical/incidental lab findings are triaged separately.

⚠ Clinical diagnosis — do NOT delay treatment for labs. Lab values reflect the chronic state, not acute severity. Decompensated hypothyroidism mortality 25-50%; thyroid storm mortality 8-25%. Sepsis is the #1 precipitant for BOTH. [9]
⚠ Give steroids BEFORE T4 in decompensated hypothyroidism — concomitant adrenal insufficiency in 5-10%; thyroid hormone without cortisol can precipitate adrenal crisis. [1]
⚠ Passive rewarming ONLY — active rewarming causes vasodilation and cardiovascular collapse. Anticipate a difficult airway (macroglossia, goiter). [3]
⚠ ECHO FIRST in thyroid storm — before ANY beta-blocker. Up to 38% develop thyrotoxic cardiomyopathy/cardiogenic shock; a beta-blocker in decompensated HF can cause arrest. [2]
⚠ Iodine MUST wait ≥1 hour after the thionamide. Given too early, iodine becomes substrate for MORE hormone synthesis and worsens the storm. [2]
⚠ Acetaminophen for fever — AVOID aspirin and NSAIDs. Salicylates/NSAIDs displace T4 from binding globulin, raising free active hormone and worsening thyrotoxicosis. [9]

2 · Decompensated hypothyroidism — treat in sequence

"Myxedema coma" is a misnomer — most patients are neither comatose nor myxedematous. It is hypothyroidism causing organ failure, the brain failing first: AMS ~90%, hypothermia 70-90%, bradycardia ~70%, plus CO₂ narcosis, hyponatremia (~50%), and hypoglycemia. [3][9]

Airway + rewarming first
Physiologically and anatomically difficult airway (macroglossia, goiter, pharyngeal myxedema) — video laryngoscopy first-line, fiberoptic backup. Passive rewarming ONLY; active rewarming precipitates cardiovascular collapse. [3]
STEP 1 — steroids BEFORE thyroid hormone
Give Hydrocortisone 100 mg IV, then 50 mg IV q8h, BEFORE any T4. Concomitant adrenal insufficiency in 5-10%; T4 accelerates cortisol metabolism and can trigger adrenal crisis. Draw a random cortisol first if it will not delay care. [1][5]

T3 is the active form with faster onset but higher risk. Reserve for critically ill patients needing hemodynamic/ventilatory support; not mandatory. Liothyronine 5-20 mcg IV load, then 2.5-10 mcg IV q8h. High-dose T3 (>75 mcg/day) is associated with increased mortality — stop at 48h or if T3 normalizes. [3][5]

ICU-level care. Passive rewarm to normothermia; glucose q2-4h; sodium q4-6h (fluid-restrict if Na <130). AVOID sedatives, opioids, amiodarone, and diuretics. Follow clinical parameters — TSH/fT4 lag by days. All patients admit. [3]

3 · Thyroid storm — block the cascade in order

Thyroid storm = thyrotoxicosis + end-organ dysfunction. No validated diagnostic test — the Burch-Wartofsky Score frames the assessment but is NOT diagnostic (≥45 highly suggestive, 25-44 impending). Triggers: known hyperthyroid + acute deterioration, new AFib/cardiomyopathy, delirium + fever + tachycardia, temp >40°C, or a septic-appearing patient with no source. [4][7]

STEP 1 — sepsis workup + ECHO first
Storm mimics sepsis and sepsis triggers storm — do a full workup (cultures, lactate, LFTs, coags, CK, calcium) and start empiric antibiotics on any concern. Get an ECHO before any beta-blocker — up to 38% develop thyrotoxic cardiomyopathy. [2][9]
  1. Steroids — Hydrocortisone 300 mg IV load, then 100 mg IV q8h. Blocks peripheral T4→T3 conversion AND covers possible adrenal insufficiency. [2]
  2. Thionamide (block synthesis) — methimazole or PTU (see §5). Must be on board BEFORE iodine. [2]
  3. Iodine (block release) — SSKI 5 drops (250 mg) PO q6h, given ≥1 hour AFTER the thionamide. Earlier, iodine fuels new synthesis. [2]
  4. Cholestyramine (block recirculation) — Cholestyramine 4 g PO q6h; binds gut hormone, cuts T4 an extra 20-30%. Separate other oral drugs by ≥1-2 h. [2][11]

STEP 6 — hyperthermia. Actively cool for temp >40°C (blankets, ice to axillae/groin). Antipyretic of choice is acetaminophen — adults 650-1000 mg PO/IV q6h (max 4 g/day). Pediatric weight-based dose is 15 mg/kg (max 1000 mg) — enter weight above. Never aspirin/NSAIDs. [2][9]

—Acetaminophen pediatric dose (15 mg/kg, max 1000 mg)
STEP 7 — agitationolanzapine preferred

Agitation worsens hyperthermia. IBCC prefers Olanzapine 5-10 mg IM/PO — sedation without respiratory depression, possible antithyroid effect. Avoid haloperidol (QT risk elevated in thyrotoxicosis) and ketamine (sympathomimetic). [2]

5 · Thionamide choice — toggle pregnancy

Thionamides inhibit thyroid peroxidase to block new synthesis — no effect on pre-formed hormone. Give BEFORE iodine. Toggle Pregnant — 1st trimester above to select the agent. [2][6]

Standard → Methimazole
IBCC-preferred outside the 1st trimester — longer-acting, lower hepatotoxicity/agranulocytosis risk. Methimazole 40 mg PO load, then 20 mg PO q6h. [2]
1st-trimester pregnancy → PTU
Methimazole is teratogenic in the 1st trimester — use PTU 500-1000 mg PO load, then 250 mg PO q4h. PTU also blocks peripheral T4→T3 conversion (useful acutely) but carries higher hepatotoxicity, so it is reserved for pregnancy or methimazole intolerance. [6][10]

8 · Disposition & do-NOT pitfalls

All decompensated hypothyroidism and all thyroid storm patients require admission — ICU vs monitored floor. Follow clinical parameters (mental status, temperature, hemodynamics); TSH/fT4 changes lag by days. Refractory storm (no improvement at 24-48h): therapeutic plasma exchange, then emergency thyroidectomy; lithium 300 mg PO q8h if not already used. [2][3][8]
