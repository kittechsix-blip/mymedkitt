Thyroid Emergencies: Steroids-First Sequencing

Adults ≥18 in the ED with clinical suspicion of decompensated hypothyroidism (myxedema coma) or thyroid storm — both are clinical diagnoses treated empirically before lab confirmation. Excludes pediatrics, pregnancy (thionamide choice differs), mild/subclinical thyroid lab abnormalities, and refractory storm requiring plasmapheresis/thyroidectomy — each needs a modified protocol.

⚠ Steroids BEFORE levothyroxine in decompensated hypothyroidism. Concurrent adrenal insufficiency is present in 5-10% of patients — thyroid hormone accelerates cortisol metabolism and can precipitate adrenal crisis if T4 is given first.
⚠ ECHO before any beta-blocker in thyroid storm. Up to 38% of thyroid storm patients develop cardiogenic shock — a beta-blocker in decompensated systolic heart failure can cause cardiac arrest.
⚠ Iodine must wait ≥1 hour after the thionamide dose. Given first, iodine can be used as substrate for new hormone synthesis and worsen thyrotoxicosis.
⚠ Passive rewarming ONLY in decompensated hypothyroidism. Active rewarming (heating blankets, forced air) causes peripheral vasodilation and can precipitate cardiovascular collapse.

2 · Decompensated hypothyroidism — steroids first

Airway is difficult (macroglossia, goiter) — supportive care runs simultaneously with hormone therapy, but hormone sequencing matters: steroids before T4, always.[1]

Concomitant adrenal insufficiency in 5-10% of patients. T4 without cortisol can precipitate adrenal crisis. Draw random cortisol first if practical — but do not delay treatment.[3]

Hydrocortisone: 100 mg IV bolus, then 50 mg IV q8h IV — Give BEFORE levothyroxine. Taper over 2-3 days once stable and cortisol results available.

Levothyroxine: 200-400 mcg IV load IV — Oral absorption is unreliable in decompensated hypothyroidism. Maintenance: 50-100 mcg IV daily or 1.2 mcg/kg/day IV — transition to PO once GI function restored.

Levothyroxine IV maintenance: 84 IV daily — 1.2 mcg/kg/day, capped at 100 mcg/day. Enter weight above.

3 · Thyroid storm — sequential blockade

Full sepsis workup always — thyroid storm mimics sepsis and sepsis can trigger it. Treat in this order: block synthesis → block release → block recirculation, then address rate control last.[2]

Hydrocortisone: 300 mg IV load, then 100 mg IV q8h IV — IBCC protocol (preferred). Dual benefit: blocks peripheral T4→T3 conversion and treats potential concurrent adrenal insufficiency. Continue 3-5 days, then rapid taper.

Methimazole: 40 mg PO load, then 20 mg PO q6h PO (or NG/rectal) — IBCC-preferred — safer long-term profile, no IV formulation. PTU (500-1000 mg PO load, then 250 mg q4h) is the alternative and is mandatory in 1st-trimester pregnancy. Must be given BEFORE iodine.

Give ≥1 hour after the thionamide dose. Without thionamide on board, iodine can fuel new hormone synthesis and worsen thyrotoxicosis.[2]

SSKI (potassium iodide): 5 drops (250 mg) PO q6h PO — Up to 10 days (suppressive effect wears off via Wolff-Chaikoff escape).

Up to 38% of thyroid storm patients develop cardiogenic shock. A beta-blocker in decompensated heart failure can cause cardiac arrest — confirm EF first.[2]

Esmolol: 35 IV load over 1 min, then 50-100 mcg/kg/min infusion — 250-500 mcg/kg IV load, then 50-100 mcg/kg/min. Preferred over propranolol — ultra-short acting, immediately titratable, stop instantly if hypotension. ONLY with confirmed preserved EF. Enter weight above.

Manage tachycardia by treating the storm. Target permissive HR <130 rather than aggressive rate control.

Digoxin: 0.25-0.5 mg IV IV — No negative inotropy, but may need higher doses in thyrotoxicosis (increased Vd, renal clearance) and response may be blunted.

All thyroid storm requires ICU admission. Clinical improvement expected in 24-72h with aggressive multimodal therapy.

5 · Do NOT

Critical pitfalls

Do NOT give levothyroxine before steroids in decompensated hypothyroidism — can precipitate adrenal crisis.

Do NOT give a beta-blocker without an echo in thyroid storm — up to 38% have cardiogenic shock.

Do NOT give iodine before the thionamide — it fuels new hormone synthesis and worsens thyrotoxicosis.

Do NOT actively rewarm decompensated hypothyroidism (heating blankets, forced air) — passive rewarming only.

Do NOT give aspirin or NSAIDs for thyroid storm fever — they displace T4 from binding protein and can worsen thyrotoxicosis.

Do NOT use haloperidol for agitation in thyroid storm — elevated QT-prolongation risk; olanzapine is preferred.
