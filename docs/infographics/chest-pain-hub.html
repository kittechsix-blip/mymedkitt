Chest Pain Hub — Sick Check, Time-Critical Exclusions, Disposition

ED triage hub for adult chest pain. Routes to the time-critical exclusion (STEMI/OMI, NSTE-ACS, aortic dissection, PE, pericarditis/myocarditis, esophageal rupture, pneumothorax) or, if none is hit, through a rescue bundle, reassessment, imaging, and disposition. This is a router — it hands off to the dedicated deep-dive consult for each diagnosis, not a substitute for it.

⚠ Do NOT give heparin, thrombolytics, or routine ACS antithrombotics until aortic dissection is reasonably excluded when the story fits.
⚠ Tension pneumothorax — hypotension, severe respiratory distress, tracheal shift, or peri-arrest — decompress immediately; do not wait for imaging.
⚠ Wellens syndrome with pain resolved is NOT reassuring. Do not stress test — it needs urgent cardiology/cath planning.
⚠ Post-procedure or ventilated patient with sudden chest pain/hypotension is tension pneumothorax until proven otherwise.
⚠ Do not discharge severe post-emesis chest pain as reflux — do not place a blind NG tube or wait for the classic Mackler triad in suspected Boerhaave.
⚠ Diffuse ST elevation is not automatically benign pericarditis — hypotension, elevated JVP, electrical alternans, or RV diastolic collapse is tamponade posture.
⚠ Do not order broad CT just because pain is hard to label — image the diagnosis you are willing to act on.

5 do not miss
1. Acute coronary syndrome (STEMI/NSTEMI) — serial ECG + troponin; watch for posterior, RV, de Winter, Wellens, new LBBB.
2. Aortic dissection — tearing/migrating pain, pulse or BP differential, neuro deficit; do NOT anticoagulate until excluded.
3. Pulmonary embolism — pleuritic pain, dyspnea, hypoxia, RV strain on POCUS/ECG.
4. Tension pneumothorax — unilateral absent breath sounds + shock → decompress, do not wait for imaging.
5. Esophageal rupture (Boerhaave) — severe pain after vomiting, subcutaneous emphysema, sepsis.

  • Monitor, IV access, defib pads if unstable, bedside glucose if altered.

▶ Which time-critical exclusion fits?

STEMI / OMI pattern
Open the STEMI Management consult. Next 5 minutes: cath activation if criteria or OMI-equivalent pattern; aspirin if not already given; heparin and P2Y12 per local protocol; defib pads if unstable; treat ventricular dysrhythmia or cardiogenic shock in parallel. Do not miss: posterior MI (posterior leads), right-sided/RV MI (avoid nitrate-induced crash), de Winter, hyperacute T waves, new LBBB with convincing ischemic story.
NSTE-ACS / high-risk ischemia
Open the NSTEMI Management or Wellens Syndrome consult. Next 5 minutes: repeat ECG during pain, high-sensitivity troponin pathway, aspirin, anticoagulation if ACS remains likely and dissection is not suspected, pain control, cardiology if dynamic ECG or high-risk story. Pitfall: Wellens with pain resolved is not reassuring — do not stress test; needs urgent cardiology/cath planning.
Aortic dissection posture
Open the Aortic Dissection consult. Next 5 minutes: bilateral arm BP, pulse/neuro check, CTA chest/abdomen/pelvis if stable enough, bedside echo if crashing, arterial line if available, esmolol first to target HR about 60 before vasodilator. Then SBP usually 100-120 if perfusing. Stop point: do not give heparin, thrombolytics, or routine ACS antithrombotics until dissection is reasonably excluded when the story fits.
Pulmonary embolism posture
Open the PE Treatment consult. Next 5 minutes: oxygen, monitor, bedside echo for RV strain if unstable, anticoagulation if PE likely and bleeding risk acceptable, CT pulmonary angiography when stable, lower-extremity DVT ultrasound when CT is delayed or contraindicated. Massive PE clues: hypotension, syncope, RV dilation/strain, obstructive shock — consider PERT, systemic thrombolysis, catheter intervention, or ECMO pathway per local resources.
Pericarditis / myocarditis
Open the Pericarditis or Myocarditis consult. Next 5 minutes: ECG, troponin, inflammatory markers if helpful, bedside echo for effusion/tamponade or LV dysfunction, cardiology if troponin elevation with LV dysfunction, arrhythmia, syncope, or heart failure. Pitfall: diffuse STE does not automatically mean benign pericarditis — hypotension, elevated JVP, electrical alternans, or RV diastolic collapse is tamponade posture.
Vomiting + toxic chest pain — esophageal rupture
No standalone deep-dive consult yet — treat as time-critical surgical disease. Next 5 minutes: NPO, broad-spectrum IV antibiotics, antiemetics/analgesia, early thoracic surgery or general surgery, CT chest with water-soluble contrast when stable. Do not: place blind NG tube, discharge as reflux after severe post-emesis chest pain, or wait for classic Mackler triad.
Pneumothorax / tension physiology
Open the Pneumothorax consult. Next 5 minutes: if hypotension, severe respiratory distress, tracheal shift, or peri-arrest — decompress immediately, then tube thoracostomy. If stable: lung ultrasound and CXR guide size/location and observation vs drainage. Pitfall: post-procedure or ventilated patient with sudden chest pain/hypotension is tension pneumothorax until proven otherwise.

Management algorithm

This is the high-yield triage spine. Each exclusion routes to its own deep-dive consult (STEMI, NSTEMI, aortic dissection, PE treatment, pneumothorax, pericarditis, myocarditis) for full management.

Rescue bundle & reassessment

  • Repeat ECG if symptoms continue or change.
  • Serial high-sensitivity troponin pathway.
  • CXR for pneumothorax, pneumonia, widened mediastinum, edema.
  • POCUS if unstable, hypoxic, suspected tamponade/RV strain/pulmonary edema.
  • Aspirin if ACS possible and no contraindication.
  • Nitrates only if BP adequate and no RV infarct/PDE5 issue/severe AS concern.
  • Analgesia and antiemetic without masking serial exams.

Reassess at 15-30 minutes: pain trajectory, vitals, repeat ECG, response to nitro/analgesia, new dyspnea/syncope/neuro deficit. Deterioration returns to time-critical exclusions; stable patients move to imaging or disposition by risk.

Disposition

Admit / ICU / procedure lane
Admit or transfer for STEMI/OMI, NSTEMI/high-risk ACS, dissection, massive/submassive PE needing intervention, myocarditis with HF/arrhythmia/syncope, tamponade, pneumothorax requiring drainage, esophageal rupture concern, persistent hypoxia, unstable vitals, or unsafe follow-up. Use the deep-dive consult and destination service early.
Observation lane
Appropriate when the first pass is not diagnostic but risk is not low: persistent or recurrent pain, intermediate HEART/EDACS or clinician concern, equivocal ECG/troponin, need for serial ECG/troponin, or pending targeted imaging. Serial ECG/troponin and diagnosis-directed imaging before discharge.
Discharge checklist
Discharge only when serial ECG/troponin strategy is reassuring, symptoms controlled, vitals stable, no concerning alternate life-threat, patient understands return precautions, and follow-up is realistic. Return precautions: recurrent/worsening pain, dyspnea, syncope, weakness/numbness, fever, hemoptysis, persistent vomiting, or new severe back/tearing pain.

Disposition

Admit or transfer for STEMI/OMI, NSTEMI/high-risk ACS, dissection, massive/submassive PE needing intervention, myocarditis with HF/arrhythmia/syncope, tamponade, pneumothorax requiring drainage, esophageal rupture concern, persistent hypoxia, unstable vitals, or unsafe follow-up. Appropriate when the first pass is not diagnostic but risk is not low: persistent or recurrent pain, intermediate HEART/EDACS or clinician concern, equivocal ECG/troponin, need for serial ECG/troponin, or pending targeted imaging. Serial ECG/troponin and diagnosis-directed imaging before discharge.

Discharge only when serial ECG/troponin strategy is reassuring, symptoms controlled, vitals stable, no concerning alternate life-threat, patient understands return precautions, and follow-up is realistic. Return precautions: recurrent/worsening pain, dyspnea, syncope, weakness/numbness, fever, hemoptysis, persistent vomiting, or new severe back/tearing pain.
