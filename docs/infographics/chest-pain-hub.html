Chest Pain Hub: Which Diagnosis Kills First

Adults ≥18 with acute chest pain in the ED. A triage-and-exclusion hub, not a single-disease protocol — excludes pediatrics, pregnancy, and trauma-primary presentations, which follow dedicated pathways.

⚠ ECG reviewed within 10 minutes for any acute chest pain — repeat it during pain and after any change.
⚠ Do NOT give heparin, thrombolytics, or routine ACS antithrombotics until aortic dissection is reasonably excluded when the story fits (tearing/migrating pain, pulse or BP differential, neuro deficit).
⚠ Shock + chest pain = bedside echo/POCUS and resuscitation in parallel — do not wait for CT.
⚠ Tension pneumothorax (unilateral absent breath sounds + shock) → decompress immediately, do not wait for imaging.

4 · What to do now — per lane

Each lane below is the consult's "next 5 minutes." Tap the matching box in the pathway above to focus a single lane.

STEMI / OMI
Next 5 minutes: cath activation if criteria or OMI-equivalent pattern; aspirin if not already given; heparin and P2Y12 per local protocol; defib pads if unstable; treat ventricular dysrhythmia or cardiogenic shock in parallel. Do not miss: posterior MI (posterior leads), right-sided/RV MI (avoid nitrate-induced crash), de Winter, hyperacute T waves, new LBBB with a convincing ischemic story. Open STEMI Management.
NSTE-ACS / high-risk ischemia
Next 5 minutes: repeat ECG during pain, high-sensitivity troponin pathway, aspirin, anticoagulation if ACS remains likely and dissection is not suspected, pain control, cardiology if dynamic ECG or high-risk story. Pitfall: Wellens with pain resolved is not reassuring — do not stress test; it needs urgent cardiology/cath planning. Open NSTEMI Management or Wellens Syndrome.
Aortic dissection
Next 5 minutes: bilateral arm BP, pulse/neuro check, CTA chest/abdomen/pelvis if stable enough, bedside echo if crashing, arterial line if available. Esmolol first to target HR about 60 before vasodilator, then SBP usually 100-120 if perfusing. Stop point: do NOT give heparin, thrombolytics, or routine ACS antithrombotics until dissection is reasonably excluded when the story fits. Open Aortic Dissection.
—Beats above the HR-60 rate-control target
Pulmonary embolism
Next 5 minutes: oxygen, monitor, bedside echo for RV strain if unstable, anticoagulation if PE likely and bleeding risk acceptable, CT pulmonary angiography when stable, lower-extremity DVT ultrasound when CT is delayed or contraindicated. Massive PE clues: hypotension, syncope, RV dilation/strain, obstructive shock — escalate to PERT, systemic thrombolysis, catheter intervention, or ECMO pathway per local resources. Open PE Treatment.
Pericarditis / myocarditis
Next 5 minutes: ECG, troponin, inflammatory markers if helpful, bedside echo for effusion/tamponade or LV dysfunction, cardiology if troponin elevation with LV dysfunction, arrhythmia, syncope, or heart failure. Pitfall: diffuse STE does not automatically mean benign pericarditis — hypotension, elevated JVP, electrical alternans, or RV diastolic collapse = tamponade posture. Open Pericarditis or Myocarditis.
Pneumothorax / tension physiology
Next 5 minutes: if hypotension, severe respiratory distress, tracheal shift, or peri-arrest — decompress immediately, then tube thoracostomy. If stable — lung ultrasound and CXR guide size/location and observation vs drainage. Pitfall: a post-procedure or ventilated patient with sudden chest pain/hypotension is tension pneumothorax until proven otherwise. Open Pneumothorax.
Esophageal rupture (Boerhaave)
Treat as time-critical surgical disease. Next 5 minutes: NPO, broad-spectrum IV antibiotics, antiemetics/analgesia, early thoracic or general surgery, CT chest with water-soluble contrast when stable. Do not: place a blind NG tube, discharge as reflux after severe post-emesis chest pain, or wait for the classic Mackler triad.

5 · Rescue bundle + reassess

Default bundle when no single killer has declared itself:

  • Repeat ECG if symptoms continue or change.
  • Serial high-sensitivity troponin pathway.
  • CXR for pneumothorax, pneumonia, widened mediastinum, edema.
  • POCUS if unstable, hypoxic, or tamponade/RV strain/pulmonary edema is suspected.
  • Aspirin if ACS is possible and no contraindication.
  • Nitrates only if BP is adequate and no RV infarct, PDE5 use, or severe AS concern.
  • Analgesia and antiemetic without masking serial exams.
Reassess at 15-30 minutes
Recheck pain trajectory, vitals, repeat ECG, and response to nitro/analgesia. Any deterioration or new ECG/troponin/neuro/respiratory finding sends the patient back to the exclusions — reassessment should change the lane if the patient evolves.

7 · Disposition

Disposition follows risk and trajectory, not pain score alone.

Admit / ICU / procedure lane
Admit or transfer for STEMI/OMI, NSTEMI/high-risk ACS, dissection, massive/submassive PE needing intervention, myocarditis with HF/arrhythmia/syncope, tamponade, pneumothorax requiring drainage, esophageal rupture concern, persistent hypoxia, unstable vitals, or unsafe follow-up.
Observation lane
Observation fits when the first pass is not diagnostic but risk is not low: persistent or recurrent pain, intermediate HEART/EDACS or clinician concern, equivocal ECG/troponin, need for serial ECG/troponin, or pending targeted imaging.
Discharge — all must be true
Discharge only when the serial ECG/troponin strategy is reassuring, symptoms are controlled, vitals are stable, no concerning alternate life-threat remains, the patient understands return precautions, and follow-up is realistic.
  • Recurrent or worsening chest pain
  • Dyspnea
  • Syncope
  • Weakness or numbness
  • Fever
  • Hemoptysis
  • Persistent vomiting
  • New severe back or tearing pain

8 · Do NOT

Critical pitfalls6 items
Do NOT anticoagulate before excluding dissection when the story fits — heparin/thrombolytics can be fatal in a dissection.[3]
Do NOT wait for imaging in tension pneumothorax — decompress on exam findings.[9]
Do NOT discharge severe post-emesis chest pain as reflux — that is a missed Boerhaave.[7]
Do NOT give nitrates in RV infarct, PDE5 use, or severe AS concern.[1]
Do NOT call diffuse STE benign pericarditis without excluding tamponade (hypotension, high JVP, electrical alternans, RV diastolic collapse).[5]
Do NOT stress test Wellens with resolved pain — it needs urgent cardiology/cath planning.[2]
