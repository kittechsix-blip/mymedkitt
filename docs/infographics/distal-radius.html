Distal Radius Fracture Reduction — Obtain & Maintain / TRAMP

Adults and children presenting to the ED with a distal radius fracture requiring assessment for closed reduction. Excludes fractures already accepted for primary operative management on presentation. Pediatric acceptable-angulation thresholds differ from adult and are called out separately.

⚠ Open fracture, grossly unstable/comminuted fracture, intra-articular step-off >2 mm, radiocarpal subluxation, DRUJ instability, or 2 failed closed-reduction attempts = SURGICAL CONSULT, not repeated closed reduction.
⚠ Hematoma block max dose 4.5 mg/kg lidocaine without epinephrine — fracture hematoma is contiguous with marrow space, so systemic absorption is rapid, similar to IO administration.
⚠ Bier block: maintain tourniquet a minimum of 20 min before release to prevent systemic local-anesthetic toxicity; have lipid emulsion available.
⚠ Open fractures: irrigate, splint, give antibiotics within 1 hour of injury if possible, update tetanus, and document a baseline median nerve exam before consult.
⚠ After reduction: any of increasing pain, numbness/tingling, inability to move fingers, or fingers turning blue/white/cold could indicate compartment syndrome — a surgical emergency.
⚠ Pediatric fractures remodel ONLY in the plane of joint motion — rotation/malrotation does NOT remodel and must be corrected. Delayed pediatric reduction increases growth-plate injury risk.

Obtain & Maintain — is reduction needed?

"Obtain & Maintain" — the two questions that guide every fracture decision:

1. OBTAIN — is the current position acceptable? If yes → immobilize and follow up. If no → reduce.

2. MAINTAIN — will the fracture shift? If stable → fiberglass OK. If unstable → plaster cast with proper molding.

Evaluate the fracture on AP and lateral radiographs against the acceptable-position criteria.

CriterionThreshold
Radial heightMaintained
Radial inclinationradial inclination >15°
Volar tiltNeutral or positive
Dorsal angulationNo significant dorsal angulation
Intra-articular step-offstep-off >2 mm is unacceptable

Buckle (torus) fractures: removable wrist splint 3–4 weeks, no follow-up x-ray needed — these do not displace.

Minimally displaced, stable: short arm splint or cast; fiberglass is acceptable (no molding needed). Keep thumb, fingers, and elbow FREE. Ortho follow-up 5–7 days.

Analgesia selection

Hematoma block is the preferred technique for most ED reductions — faster discharge (1.5 hours vs 4.6 hours), no NPO requirement, no monitoring team needed. Procedural sedation remains appropriate for complex reductions or patient anxiety. Regional anesthesia offers superior and longer-lasting analgesia but requires more setup time.

  1. Identify the fracture site on the dorsal aspect of the wrist
  2. Prep skin with chlorhexidine
  3. Insert a 20-gauge needle at the dorsal fracture line
  4. Aspirate — dark blood confirms placement in the fracture hematoma
  5. Inject 5–10 mL of 1% plain lidocaine
  6. For combined radius + ulna fractures: second injection into the ulnar hematoma
  7. Wait 5–10 minutes for full anesthetic effect

TRAMP reduction

T — Traction. Disimpact the fracture first. Patient seated, arm pronated. Apply longitudinal traction (waterski position); make small circles to free the distal fragment. Do NOT try to reduce yet — just get the fragments apart. Finger traps on the thumb, index, and middle fingers with 5–10 lbs countertraction at the elbow. If you skip traction, the fragments are locked together and reduction will fail.

R — Reduction. Exaggerate the deformity first to unlock the dorsal periosteal hinge, then push the distal fragment palmar with your thumbs while ulnar deviating to restore radial inclination. Ulnar deviation is critical — without it the radius shortens relative to the ulna and the reduction is lost. If both bones are fractured, reduce the radius first; the ulna often reduces with it.

A — Apply. Plaster, not fiberglass, when molding matters — fiberglass sets too rigid too fast to mold. 10 layers, 6-inch width, measured on the uninjured side. Cut a thumb hole before applying. Minimal padding at the fracture site (2 layers of Webril only) — excess padding creates a gap that lets the fracture shift inside the splint.

M — Mold. Use flat hands (thenar eminences), never fingertips. Create an oval cross-section, not a circle — "straight cast → crooked bone, crooked cast → straight bone." Apply 3-point pressure: dorsal at the fracture, volar proximal and distal. Hold the mold — do NOT move your hands while the plaster sets (~3–5 minutes). Do NOT squeeze — broad flat pressure only.

P — Position. Wrist at 10° flexion, ulnar deviation. Elbow, MCP joints, and thumb must remain FREE — a short arm cast, stopping at the distal palmar crease, with full thumb ROM. For highly unstable fractures, a sugar-tong splint (elbow-to-palm with forearm loop) prevents pronation/supination for additional stability.

Post-reduction assessment & disposition

Obtain post-reduction AP and lateral films. 2nd metacarpal line should be parallel to the radial shaft on AP view; cast index <0.80 (AP width ÷ lateral width of cast at the fracture) confirms an oval, adequately-molded cast. If the cast looks perfectly round on x-ray, the mold is inadequate and the fracture will re-displace.

Re-attempt the full TRAMP sequence
Remove the current cast/splint completely, top up analgesia, and re-run the full sequence from Traction — do not skip steps. If 2 attempts fail, consult orthopedics for operative management; repeated manipulation increases soft tissue swelling and complication risk.
  • Elevation — keep hand above heart for 48 hours to minimize swelling
  • Ice — 20 min on / 20 min off over cast padding
  • Finger exercises — wiggle fingers frequently to prevent stiffness
  • Neurovascular checks — ensure fingers remain pink, warm, with normal sensation
Return immediately if
Increasing pain not relieved by elevation and medication; numbness, tingling, or inability to move fingers; fingers turn blue, white, or cold; cast feels too tight or causes pressure sores. Any of these could indicate compartment syndrome — a surgical emergency.

Ortho clinic in 5–7 days for repeat films; 1-week x-ray checks for re-displacement (especially the first 2 weeks). Cast duration typically 4–6 weeks for adults, 3–4 weeks for children.
