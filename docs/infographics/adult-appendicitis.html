algorithm

Adult Appendicitis: Score It, Image It, Call Surgery

Adults ≥18 with suspected acute appendicitis in the ED. Excludes patients <18 yo (use the dedicated pediatric appendicitis pathway — PAS scoring, US-first algorithm) and known prior complete appendectomy (stump appendicitis after partial appendectomy is rare but real).

⚠ Anchor on the classic sequence — anorexia → periumbilical/epigastric pain → migration to RLQ → vomiting AFTER pain onset, low-grade fever. Perforation risk climbs sharply with delay: ~20% at 36 h of symptoms, >65% at 48 h. Missed appendicitis is a leading driver of EM malpractice payouts.
⚠ Analgesia does NOT mask the exam — this myth is debunked. Do not withhold analgesia while awaiting surgical evaluation.
⚠ Lower the threshold to image in elderly (≥60, mortality ~5-10% vs <1% in young adults, up to 50% lack classic migration), pregnant (appendix migrates cephalad; fetal loss climbs from 1-5% uncomplicated to 20-35% perforated), and immunocompromised patients (blunted inflammatory response, can present in septic shock from missed perforation).
⚠ Pregnancy imaging order: graded compression US first (all trimesters) → MRI without gadolinium if non-diagnostic → CT only if both unavailable and clinical concern persists. Do NOT delay surgical consult while imaging completes — delayed surgery is more dangerous to the fetus than the appendectomy itself.
⚠ Give preop antibiotics within 1 h of the decision to operate.
⚠ Discharge is appropriate ONLY if ALL of: low AAS, improving after analgesia/fluids, PO tolerated, no peritoneal signs, WBC/CRP trending down or normal, reliable patient who can return, shared decision-making documented, and a 12-24 h recheck BOOKED (not just recommended). Do NOT discharge: unclear/atypical pain pattern, any peritoneal sign, rising WBC/CRP, cannot return reliably, pregnancy, elderly with vague pain, or immunocompromise.
⚠ Antibiotics-alone (CODA/NOTA) is NOT first-line in most US centers and is contraindicated with suspected perforation, abscess, appendicolith, immunocompromise, or inability to return for follow-up — shared decision-making required.

1 · Recognize & risk-stratify

The classic story
Anorexia ("would they normally eat right now?") → periumbilical/epigastric dull pain migrating to RLQ over 6-24 h → nausea/vomiting AFTER pain onset → low-grade fever → RLQ tenderness ± guarding. Lifetime incidence ~7%, peak age 10-30 but presents at every age.[1]
Perforation risk climbs with delay
Perforation rate: ~20% at 36 h of symptoms, >65% at 48 h. Missed appendicitis is a leading driver of EM malpractice payouts.[1][2]

Markle (heel-drop/cough) sign is the single most reliable physical exam finding. Also check Rovsing (palpate LLQ → RLQ pain), psoas (retrocecal appendix), obturator (pelvic appendix). Pelvic exam in reproductive-age females; testicular exam in males (torsion can refer to RLQ). Vitals trend matters more than a single snapshot — tachycardia + rising temp + falling BP after a fluid bolus = perforation/sepsis until proven otherwise.[4][6]

Atypical populations — lower threshold to image
Elderly ≥60: up to 50% lack classic migration; mortality ~5-10% (vs <1% young adults) — image early.[3] Pregnancy: appendix migrates cephalad/laterally; 3rd-trimester pain often RUQ; fetal loss 1-5% uncomplicated → 20-35% perforated.[7] Immunocompromised/DM: blunted response, minimal pain, no fever, normal WBC — can present in septic shock.[8] Patients <18 yo use the dedicated pediatric pathway (PAS scoring, not AAS/Alvarado).

Workup pearls (labs, adjuncts)3 pitfalls
Normal WBC does NOT rule out appendicitis — up to 20% have a normal WBC, especially early presentations or the elderly.
Sterile pyuria is common in appendicitis due to bladder irritation — do not attribute RLQ pain to UTI alone on urinalysis.

CBC + CRP + CMP + lipase + UA + β-hCG (any reproductive-age female) + lactate if sepsis concern. Combined WBC + CRP + PMN elevation has higher specificity than any single marker; a normal CRP + normal WBC + low AAS is the strongest single rule-out combination. β-hCG positive does NOT exclude concurrent appendicitis.

2 · What to do now

Set the AAS/Alvarado risk band above (and imaging profile, if intermediate) — the single correct next step highlights below and on the algorithm.

High probability — surgical consult now
AAS ≥16 (or Alvarado 9-10 with classic picture) → surgical consult BEFORE imaging completes; imaging confirms but does not gate the call. Parallel orders: IV access ×2, NS 1-2 L bolus, CBC/CMP/lipase/lactate/UA (β-hCG if female), type & screen, NPO, antiemetic, analgesia (do NOT withhold — does not mask the exam), antibiotics within 1 h of the decision to operate.[1][2]
Intermediate — imaging decides
AAS 11-15 (Alvarado 5-8) — the modality matters more than the order. Select the imaging profile above.
Pregnant — US → MRI (no gad) → CT
Graded compression US first-line, all trimesters (sensitivity ~70-85% when visualized; non-visualization 30-50% in 2nd-3rd trimester — non-diagnostic ≠ negative). MRI without gadolinium next if non-diagnostic (sensitivity ~94%, specificity ~97%; T2 SSFSE + DWI protocol; safe all trimesters). CT only if MRI unavailable and concern persists (single-pass low-dose ~2-3 mGy fetal dose, below the 50 mGy teratogenic threshold; iodinated contrast FDA Category B). Do NOT delay surgical consult while imaging completes.
Young, lean, <40 — US first
Graded compression US reasonable first-line: sensitivity ~70-85% when appendix visualized, specificity ~95%; non-visualization ~20-40% in adults. Non-diagnostic US is NOT a negative US — if suspicion remains, proceed to CT.
Obese, ≥40, or equivocal — CT first
CT abdomen/pelvis with IV contrast is gold standard: sensitivity ~95-98%, specificity ~95%. Oral contrast NOT required. If confirmed → surgical consult + antibiotics. If complicated (perforation/abscess/phlegmon) → surgical consult + IR consult if abscess ≥3 cm.
<18 yo — open the pediatric pathway
Different scoring (Pediatric Appendicitis Score, Samuel 2002) and a US-first imaging algorithm. Pediatric Appendicitis.
Low probability — reassess after analgesia + fluids
Reassess at 1-2 h after analgesia, 1 L NS, antiemetic. Improving + benign exam + reliable patient → discharge with strict 12-24 h recheck. Unchanged equivocal → proceed to imaging strategy. Worsening or new peritoneal signs → escalate to imaging + surgical consult now; do NOT discharge.

3 · Antibiotics + surgical decision

Preop antibiotics within 1 h of the decision to operate. Set true penicillin anaphylaxis and complicated above to see the right regimen.

Uncomplicated — no perforation, no abscess
Cefoxitin 2 g IV OR Cefazolin 2 g IV + Metronidazole 500 mg IV OR Ceftriaxone 2 g IV + Metronidazole 500 mg IV. Pip-tazo not required for uncomplicated disease.
Complicated — perforated, gangrenous, or abscess

5 · Disposition

OR for appendectomy
Confirm NPO, antibiotics given within 1 h (documented), type & screen sent, reverse anticoagulation if warfarin with INR >1.5 (PCC/FFP). Laparoscopic appendectomy is standard; open reserved for diffuse peritonitis, instability, or unavailable laparoscopic expertise. Pregnant patients: laparoscopic safe in all trimesters with lower abdominal entry + lower insufflation pressures.
Admit — complicated disease
Abscess/phlegmon — IR drainage if ≥3 cm or non-operative management with interval appendectomy in 4-8 weeks. Diffuse peritonitis needing stabilization pre-OR, sepsis resuscitation, or CODA antibiotics-alone (IV antibiotics 24-48 h then transition PO if tolerating).
ED observation / serial exams
Equivocal exam + AAS 11-15, non-diagnostic imaging, reliable patient. Serial exam q2-4h, repeat WBC/CRP at 4-6 h (both trending up = appendicitis until proven otherwise). Escalate if exam evolves; discharge with 24 h recheck if stable/improving at 6-12 h.
  1. PO challenge completed
  2. Final exam documented (soft, mild or no tenderness)
  3. Vitals normal
  4. Written return precautions: worsening/migrating pain, fever ≥38°C, persistent vomiting, rigidity, inability to walk normally, black/bloody stool, severe unresponsive pain
  5. 12-24 h ED or PCP recheck BOOKED (not just recommended)
  6. Surgical follow-up phone number provided
  7. NSAIDs discouraged (mask perforation signs); acetaminophen acceptable

Discharge recheck window: 12-24 h. A negative ED visit today does not rule out appendicitis tomorrow — the recheck is the safety net.
