Fever Without a Source — Infants 0–6 Months

Infants 0–6 months, ED, rectal T ≥38°C (100.4°F) with no source identified on history and exam. Excludes preterm <37wk gestation, immunocompromised, indwelling devices (VP shunt, central line), known immune deficiency, recent hospitalization (<7 days), and current/recent (<48h) antibiotics — these are off-pathway and need individualized workup.

⚠ Do NOT give antipyretics before LP — may mask fever and delay diagnosis.
⚠ 0–21 days + bronchiolitis/RSV/viral syndrome still requires full sepsis workup including LP — RSV-positive neonate IBI risk is not negligible: bacteremia 1.1%, meningitis 0.8%, UTI 5.2%.
⚠ HSV risk factors (vesicles, seizures, lethargy, hepatosplenomegaly, thrombocytopenia, transaminitis, CSF pleocytosis, maternal HSV history) → start empiric Acyclovir 20 mg/kg IV q8h immediately; do not wait for PCR results.
⚠ Ceftriaxone is CONTRAINDICATED in neonates receiving calcium-containing IV fluids (risk of precipitation) — use Cefepime instead.
⚠ CSF pleocytosis (WBC >15 cells/mm³ for 0–28 days; WBC >9 cells/mm³ for 29–60 days), a positive Gram stain, or a traumatic/uninterpretable tap → treat as bacterial meningitis and escalate to meningitic dosing.
⚠ ALL 0–21 day neonates are admitted regardless of clinical appearance or lab results.
⚠ Toxic/ill-appearing infants of ANY age require full sepsis workup and empiric antibiotics regardless of age — this pathway does not apply.

Disposition (0–21 days)

ALL 0–21 day neonates are admitted regardless of clinical appearance or lab results. Continue empiric antibiotics pending cultures. Other age bands (22 days – 6 months) — summary

22–28 and 29–60 days: labs + procalcitonin (± CRP), inflammatory markers drive the LP decision. Abnormal inflammatory markers: T >38.5°C (101.3°F), Procalcitonin >0.5 ng/mL, CRP >2.0 mg/dL, ANC >4,000 cells/mm³ — any single abnormal value defines higher risk and LP is recommended; if all normal, LP is a shared decision. The CSF pleocytosis threshold is age-dependent: WBC >15 cells/mm³ (0–28 days) vs the lower WBC >9 cells/mm³ (29–60 days). UTI risk remains 5–7% even with a confirmed viral syndrome — always obtain a catheterized UA.

2–6 months: assess for focal source, defined viral syndrome (still screen for UTI), or fever without an apparent source (FWAS). FWAS labs include PCV vaccination status — incomplete PCV (<2 doses) with an abnormal WBC count raises risk and may warrant empiric Ceftriaxone.

Management algorithm

Fever Without a Source — Age Group — Rectal T ≥38°C (100.4°F), infant 0–6 months, no identifiable source. Do NOT give antipyretics before LP.

0–21d: focal / viral / well-appearing? — Screen for focal infection, hypothermia, ill-appearance, or viral syndrome before applying the FWS pathway.

22–28 days: labs + PCT → risk stratification — CBC, blood culture, CMP, procalcitonin, catheterized UA. Inflammatory markers (T>38.5, PCT>0.5, CRP>2.0, ANC>4000) drive the LP decision; LP is shared decision-making if all normal. See summary below.

29–60 days: labs → risk stratification — Same inflammatory-marker logic as 22–28 days, but the CSF pleocytosis threshold is lower: WBC >9 cells/mm³ (vs >15). See summary below.

2–6 months: source assessment → PCV status → UTI screen — Focal infection, defined viral syndrome (UTI screen still required), FWAS (labs + PCV status), or toxic/ill-appearing. See summary below.

Off-Pathway — Alternate Management — Preterm <37wk, immunocompromised, indwelling devices, immune deficiency, recent hospitalization (<7d), current/recent antibiotics (<48h), focal infection, hypothermia (<36°C), or ill-appearing.

Febrile neonate with viral syndrome — still full workup — IBI risk in RSV-positive neonates: bacteremia 1.1%, meningitis 0.8%, UTI 5.2%. Viral syndrome does NOT exclude serious bacterial infection at this age.

Order full sepsis workup (incl. LP) — CBC w/diff, blood culture, CMP, catheterized UA, LP (cell count, glucose/protein, Gram stain, culture, meningitis/encephalitis PCR, hold tube #4 for HSV PCR).

Neonatal Viral Syndrome — ADMIT — All 0–21 day neonates with fever + viral syndrome are admitted. Empiric antibiotics by age + supportive viral-illness care. Monitor for apnea, especially <4 weeks.

HSV risk factors present? — Vesicles, seizures, lethargy/poor feeding, hepatosplenomegaly, thrombocytopenia/transaminitis, CSF pleocytosis, maternal HSV history.

Add Acyclovir + HSV workup — Acyclovir 20 mg/kg IV q8h. HSV PCR blood + CSF (held tube #4), surface cultures, vesicle fluid, AST/ALT. Minimum 5 doses or until PCR negative.

Start empiric antibiotics by age — 0–7 days: Ampicillin 50 mg/kg IV q8h + Gentamicin 4 mg/kg IV q24h. 8–21 days: Ceftriaxone 50 mg/kg IV q24h (max 2 g) — Cefepime if contraindicated.

CSF pleocytosis? — Abnormal (0–28 days): WBC >15 cells/mm³, positive Gram stain, or traumatic/uninterpretable tap — treat as abnormal.

Meningitis protocol — escalate dosing — 0–7 days: Ampicillin 100 mg/kg IV q8h + Cefepime 50 mg/kg IV q12h (discontinue Gentamicin). 8–21 days: Ceftriaxone 50 mg/kg IV q12h + Ampicillin 75 mg/kg IV q6h.

Admit — Await Cultures — ALL 0–21 day neonates are admitted regardless of clinical appearance or lab results. Continue empiric antibiotics; blood cultures finalize 24–36h, CSF cultures 48–72h.

Empiric antibiotics by age (0–21 days)

Ampicillin: 50 mg/kg IV q8h IV — 0–7 days, paired with Gentamicin for GBS/Listeria synergy.

Gentamicin: 4 mg/kg IV q24h IV — 0–7 days, synergy with Ampicillin. Transition to Ceftriaxone/Cefepime regimen after day 7.

Ceftriaxone: 50 mg/kg IV q24h (max 2 g) IV — 8–21 days. Contraindicated with calcium-containing IV fluids — use Cefepime instead.

Cefepime (alternative): 50 mg/kg IV q12h IV — Use instead of Ceftriaxone if contraindicated (calcium-containing IV fluids).

Acyclovir: 20 mg/kg IV q8h IV — HSV risk factors present. Minimum 5 doses or until HSV PCR negative. Ensure adequate hydration (crystalline nephropathy risk).

Meningitis escalation (0–21 days, CSF abnormal)

Ampicillin (meningitic): 100 mg/kg IV q8h (meningitic) IV — 0–7 days. Discontinue Gentamicin once escalated.

Cefepime (added): 50 mg/kg IV q12h IV — 0–7 days meningitis escalation, added to meningitic-dose Ampicillin.

Ceftriaxone (meningitic): 50 mg/kg IV q12h (meningitic dose) IV — 8–21 days meningitic maintenance dosing (max 2 g/dose).

Ampicillin (meningitic, added): 75 mg/kg IV q6h (meningitic) IV — 8–21 days, added to meningitic Ceftriaxone.
