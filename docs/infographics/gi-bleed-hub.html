GI Bleed Hub: Sort Sick, Then Source

Adults in the ED with suspected acute GI bleeding (upper or lower). This is a triage hub, not a treatment playbook — sort sick vs not-sick, commit to one phenotype, then open the linked deep-dive consult. Pediatric GI bleed follows a separate age-based differential (see the Pediatric branch).

MASSIVE HEMORRHAGE — do this first
Shock index >1.4. Activate the Massive Transfusion Protocol: 1:1:1 RBC:plasma:platelets, replace calcium, permissive hypotension (SBP 90–100) until source control. Give TXA selectively — HALT-IT showed no GI-bleed mortality benefit and more VTE.[10]

4 · Five do-not-miss

1 · Hemorrhagic shock / massive bleed
Shock index >1, ongoing hematemesis or exsanguination → activate the MTP. Permissive hypotension until source control.
2 · Variceal hemorrhage
Cirrhosis stigmata → octreotide + ceftriaxone 1 g IV + early endoscopy. High mortality; treat empirically before confirmation.[5]
3 · Aorto-enteric fistula
Prior aortic graft (any era) + GI bleed = AEF until excluded. The herald bleed precedes exsanguination by minutes-to-days → CTA (arterial + delayed) + vascular surgery NOW.[12]
4 · Brisk upper bleed masquerading as hematochezia
10-15% of hematochezia in unstable patients is upper. Unstable + hematochezia → place an NG and/or urgent EGD before colonoscopy.[3]
5 · Anticoagulant / antiplatelet-driven bleed
Identify the agent, reverse where indicated — bleeding patient + therapeutic anticoagulant = reverse. Reversal is independent of the source workup.[8]
Also on the list
Mesenteric ischemia — CTA before colonoscopy; scoping ischemic bowel can perforate it.[14] IBD flare — hold anti-motility agents (toxic megacolon risk); C. diff coinfection in up to 5% of severe flares.[16]

6 · Initial bundle & reassess

  1. Two large-bore IVs (16 g+), NPO
  2. Type and cross 2–4 units (2–6 if shock)
  3. Initial fluid bolus 500–1000 mL LR — avoid over-resuscitation, which dilutes coagulation factors
  4. Permissive hypotension (SBP 90–100) until hemostasis
  5. Send CBC, BMP/CMP, INR/PTT, fibrinogen, lactate, LFTs, type & cross
The first Hgb lies
A baseline hemoglobin can under-estimate an active bleed before hemodilution. Trend it — do not be reassured by one normal value in a patient who is still bleeding.

Reassess at 30–60 minutes: vitals trend, mental status, NG aspirate, repeat rectal exam, stool log, and returning labs. If bleeding is ongoing or vitals drop → escalate to the massive pathway. If a clear source pattern emerges → move to imaging.
