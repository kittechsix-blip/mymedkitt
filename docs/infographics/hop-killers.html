HOP Killers: The Physiologically Difficult Airway

Adults undergoing high-risk/critical intubation in the ED or ICU. Screen for 6 peri-intubation killers BEFORE inducing. Excludes routine/low-risk RSI and pediatrics (adult weight-based doses only).

⚠ Screen every critical intubation for 6 killers BEFORE induction: SBP <100 (hypotension), SpO2 <93% on O2 (hypoxemia), pH <7.2 (severe acidosis), JVD + hypotension (RV failure), K+ >5.5 mEq/L (metabolic), TBI/herniation signs (elevated ICP). Many critically ill patients have MULTIPLE killers — address all.
⚠ Hyperkalemia or a hyperkalemia-prone state (burns >24h, crush injury >24h, denervation/spinal cord injury, prolonged immobility >1 week, muscular dystrophy) — NEVER give succinylcholine; use rocuronium.
⚠ RV failure: avoid high PEEP, hyperinflation, hypoxia, hypercarbia, and acidosis (all raise PVR); avoid propofol (drops SVR); avoid fluid boluses (worsens RV distension).
⚠ Severe acidosis with pH <7.1 — intubation may be fatal; post-intubation ventilator must match the patient's pre-intubation compensatory minute ventilation. Do NOT start the vent at a standard RR of 12.
⚠ Elevated ICP — CPP = MAP − ICP; avoid hypotension and hypoxia at all costs. Avoid routine hyperventilation unless there are active herniation signs (pupil changes, posturing, Cushing's triad).
⚠ Hypotension plus induction agents carries a cardiac arrest rate of 12% vs 3% in normotensive intubations (p < 0.002) — resuscitate and have pressors running BEFORE induction. NEVER give code-dose epinephrine (1 mg) to a patient with a pulse.

**Do NOT** start the post-intubation ventilator at a standard RR of 12 in severe metabolic acidosis — match the patient's own pre-intubation compensatory rate or risk lethal acute-on-chronic acidosis.
