Multiple Sclerosis in the ED: Relapse, Rule-Outs, and Rescue

Adults with known or suspected MS presenting to the ED with new/worsening neurological symptoms. Excludes pediatric MS, definitive new-onset diagnosis (requires neurology + MRI, not made in the ED), and non-MS demyelinating disease already confirmed (isolated NMOSD/ADEM management differs).

⚠ Check UA, CBC, and CXR in ALL MS patients with new/worsening symptoms — UTI is the #1 cause of pseudoexacerbation. Steroids are NOT indicated until the trigger is treated and deficits are reassessed.
⚠ NEVER give oral prednisone ALONE for optic neuritis — the ONTT trial showed oral-only dosing DOUBLES the recurrence risk. Use IV steroids first, or not at all.
⚠ Get MRI BEFORE steroids whenever there is concern for cord compression or an alternative diagnosis — for cord/transverse-myelitis symptoms, MRI spine is EMERGENT and must exclude compression before treating.
⚠ Baclofen withdrawal (abrupt discontinuation or intrathecal pump failure) is LIFE-THREATENING — hyperthermia, rebound rigidity, altered mental status, seizures, and rhabdomyolysis. Restore baclofen immediately and admit to the ICU; do not treat with benzodiazepines alone.
⚠ PML risk is highest with natalizumab. New PROGRESSIVE deficits over weeks (not a typical relapse pattern) require holding steroids, urgent MRI, and CSF JCV PCR.
⚠ NMOSD and MOG-antibody disease require DIFFERENT treatment than MS — interferon-beta and fingolimod can WORSEN NMOSD. Confirm AQP4/MOG antibody status before starting or continuing a DMT.
⚠ Respiratory failure can occur with brainstem or high cervical lesions — maintain a low threshold for intubation and ICU admission.
⚠ Postpartum MS patients are HIGH-RISK for severe relapse, especially in the first 3 months after delivery.

5 · Complications

UTI is the #1 cause of pseudoexacerbation (neurogenic bladder, chronic catheterization, DMT immunosuppression). Check UA + culture in ALL MS patients with symptom worsening; only attribute to true relapse if deficits persist 24-48h after infection is treated. If ALC <500 (from lymphopenic DMTs): increased infection risk, lower threshold for broad-spectrum antibiotics, consider opportunistic infections, contact prescribing neurologist.[5][14]

Baclofen withdrawal — EMERGENCY
Caused by abrupt discontinuation, intrathecal pump malfunction/catheter issue, empty reservoir, or medication error. Features: severe rebound spasticity, hyperthermia (can be >40°C), altered mental status, seizures, rhabdomyolysis, multi-organ failure. Mimics sepsis, meningitis, NMS, malignant hyperthermia. Restore baclofen IMMEDIATELY; add benzodiazepines as adjunct (not monotherapy); ICU admission; if intrathecal pump — urgent neurosurgery consult.[15]

6 · DMT issues & special populations

PML — hold steroids if suspected
Fatal opportunistic brain infection from JC virus reactivation. Presentation is progressive over weeks (not like typical relapse): cognitive decline, confusion, visual field defects, ataxia, hemiparesis, seizures. Do NOT give steroids (can worsen PML); urgent MRI brain; LP for JCV PCR; neurology consult STAT; stop the DMT.[17]
DMTPML risk
Natalizumab1/100 to 1/1000 (highest)
Fingolimod~1/18,000
Dimethyl fumarate~1/50,000 (if prolonged lymphopenia)
OcrelizumabRare case reports

Fingolimod first-dose bradycardia: S1P receptor modulation causes transient bradycardia/AV block. First-dose observation: HR/BP monitoring hourly × 6 hours, ECG at baseline and 6 hours; extend if HR <45, new AV block, or QTc prolongation. Treat symptomatic bradycardia with atropine; usually resolves within 24 hours. Can recur if restarted after >14 days off therapy.[18]

Infusion reactions (natalizumab, ocrelizumab, alemtuzumab): flushing, pruritus, urticaria, throat tightness, hypotension/hypertension. Mild — slow/pause infusion + diphenhydramine; moderate — stop infusion + diphenhydramine + methylprednisolone; anaphylaxis — stop immediately, epinephrine 0.3-0.5 mg IM, IV fluids, airway management, do NOT rechallenge.

Rebound after stopping fingolimod or natalizumab: can trigger severe, sometimes tumefactive relapses, usually 2-6 months after discontinuation (fingolimod 4-16 weeks; natalizumab 3-6 months). Ask about recent DMT changes in severe relapses — these patients need aggressive treatment and neurology consult; may need PLEX.

Do NOT start steroids before MRI when cord compression or an alternative diagnosis is a concern.

Do NOT abruptly stop baclofen or ignore an intrathecal pump alarm — withdrawal is life-threatening.

Do NOT give steroids if PML is suspected — can worsen the infection; get urgent MRI + JCV PCR instead.

Do NOT start interferon-beta or fingolimod before excluding NMOSD — these DMTs can worsen NMOSD.

Do NOT ignore respiratory symptoms in brainstem/high cervical disease — keep a low threshold for intubation.

Do NOT reassure postpartum patients that relapse risk is low — the first 3 months postpartum carry the highest rebound risk.
