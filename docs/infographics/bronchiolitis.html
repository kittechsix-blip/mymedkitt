algorithm

Bronchiolitis: Suction, Score, Support — Not Salbutamol

Infants age >28 days and <24 months with first-episode bronchiolitis (increased work of breathing, cough, feeding difficulty, ± fever). Excludes chronic lung disease/CF, congenital heart disease, immunodeficiency, neuromuscular disease, artificial/abnormal airway, recurrent wheezing (>3 episodes), and respiratory failure requiring mechanical ventilation — those need individualized management, not this pathway.

⚠ This pathway is for previously healthy infants 28 days–24 months with first-episode bronchiolitis. Comorbidities (chronic lung disease/CF, CHD, immunodeficiency, neuromuscular disease, artificial airway, recurrent wheeze, respiratory failure) require individualized management, not this pathway.
⚠ CXR, viral testing, albuterol, corticosteroids, and antibiotics have NO role in standard bronchiolitis — do not order them by default.
⚠ Nasal suction must be gentle and superficial — do NOT deep-suction beyond the nasopharynx.
⚠ Target SpO₂ ≥90% awake / ≥88% asleep — do not over-oxygenate.
⚠ Critical care consult triggers: worsening after 60 min on HFNC, need for positive pressure ventilation, witnessed apnea, FiO₂ >50%, flow above the recommended maximum, or severe dehydration/shock.

4 · HFNC — live weight-based flow

Enter weight above. HFNC starting flow — <7 kg: 4 LPM · ≥7 kg: 6 LPM. Start FiO₂ at 21%, titrate to maintain SpO₂ ≥90%.[2][3]

—Starting HFNC flow
—Max flow: 2 LPM/kg for ≤7 kg, 14 LPM for >7 kg
  • Patient must be watched for at least 30 minutes in the ED after starting HFNC, with continuous pulse oximetry.
  • Feeding: NPO for ~1 hour to assess response.
  • Assess BAS every 15 minutes after HFNC initiation.
  • If BAS ≥9: suction first, then reassess — if still ≥9 after suction, contact the primary team.
HFNC maintenance
Continuous pulse oximetry, BAS Q4h, suction PRN. Mild BAS → resume PO (first feed observed by staff); moderate–severe BAS → consider NG feeds. Must be stable for ≥4 hours before weaning.[3]
Escalation triggers
If not at max flow, increase by 2 LPM and reassess in 15 min. Critical care consult triggers: worsening after 60 minutes on HFNC, severe respiratory distress, FiO₂ >50%, flow above the recommended maximum, apnea, or severe dehydration/shock.[2][3]
  • Confirm stable for ≥4 hours on current HFNC settings before attempting to wean.
  • If not yet stable, continue maintenance phase and reassess in 4 hours.
  • If stable ≥4 hours, begin weaning and admit for continued monitoring.

5 · Disposition (live)

Toggle severity, response, BAS response on HFNC, and stable ≥4h above — exactly one disposition below is the live recommendation.[1][2]

ED discharge
ALL must be met: SpO₂ ≥90% on room air. Respirations <60/min and/or minimal WOB, tolerating oral feeding, parents comfortable with home care and suction technique. Follow up with pediatrician in 1–2 days.[1][2]
Admit — acute care unit (stable on O2)
Requires ongoing supplemental O2/NC but not escalating. BAS Q4h, nasal suction PRN, continuous or intermittent pulse oximetry. Inpatient discharge requires SpO₂ ≥90% on room air for ≥2 continuous hours (not a single reading), RR <60, tolerating PO, and parents demonstrating suction technique.[1][2][3]
Admit — acute care unit (post-HFNC wean)
Stable ≥4 hours on HFNC maintenance — begin weaning, admit for continued monitoring, BAS Q4h, feeding reassessment.[1][2][3]
Admit — high acuity
Worsening despite increasing HFNC flow rates, or significant comorbidities/BAS 4–12. Continuous monitoring, reassess Q15min, lower threshold for PICU transfer.[1][2]
PICU / critical care consult
Worsening after 60 minutes of HFNC, need for positive pressure ventilation, witnessed apnea, flow above maximum, FiO₂ >50%, or severe dehydration/shock. Prepare for possible intubation, ensure IV access.[1][2][3]
Excluded from standard pathway
Comorbidities present — individualized management required, not this pathway.[1]

7 · Do NOT

Critical pitfalls

Do NOT order CXR, viral testing, albuterol, steroids, or antibiotics by default — none have a role in standard bronchiolitis.[1][2][5][7]

Do NOT deep-suction beyond the nasopharynx — use gentle, superficial nasal aspiration only.[1][2]

Do NOT over-oxygenate — target SpO₂ ≥90% awake / ≥88% asleep, not 100%.[1][2]

Do NOT treat a single SpO₂ reading as discharge-ready — inpatient discharge requires SpO₂ ≥90% on room air for ≥2 continuous hours.[1][2]

Do NOT normalize a BAS ≥9 on HFNC without suctioning first — suction, then reassess before escalating further.[3]

Do NOT delay critical care consult past 60 minutes of worsening on HFNC, FiO₂ >50%, apnea, or flow above the recommended maximum.[2][3]
