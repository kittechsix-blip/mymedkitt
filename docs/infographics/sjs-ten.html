SJS/TEN: Stop the Drug, Classify by %BSA, Score, Support

Adults and adolescents presenting to the ED with a suspected drug-induced severe cutaneous adverse reaction on the SJS/TEN spectrum. Also differentiates erythema multiforme (HSV/Mycoplasma-driven, distinct entity, NOT on the spectrum) and MIRM/RIME (Mycoplasma/respiratory-pathogen-driven mucositis, distinct entity, no culprit drug). Excludes staphylococcal scalded skin syndrome and thermal/chemical burns.

⚠ STOP the culprit drug immediately — delay correlates with mortality. Skin pain out of proportion to the visible rash, often preceding it, is the earliest clue; early SJS/TEN is routinely mistaken for a viral exanthem or erythema multiforme. ≥2 mucosal sites plus painful eyes point away from a benign viral exanthem — get ophthalmology early.
⚠ Review ALL drugs started in the prior 4–8 weeks (typical latency 4–28 days; anticonvulsants up to ~8 weeks). Highest-risk: allopurinol, carbamazepine, lamotrigine, phenytoin, phenobarbital, sulfonamide antibiotics (TMP-SMX), sulfasalazine, nevirapine, oxicam NSAIDs, abacavir.
⚠ This is acute skin failure, managed like a burn but with LESS fluid — 2 mL/kg × %BSA (≈⅔ Parkland), titrated to urine output 0.5–1 mL/kg/hr. Over-resuscitation causes pulmonary/intestinal edema.
⚠ Do NOT use silver sulfadiazine — it is itself a sulfonamide. NO prophylactic antibiotics (sepsis is the leading killer, but prophylaxis breeds resistance — use surveillance cultures instead). Urgent ophthalmology — ocular disease is the leading cause of long-term morbidity.
⚠ Score SCORTEN on day 1 AND day 3 — a single early score underestimates risk for scores ≥3 and never justifies under-treating.

1 · Recognize & stop the drug

Drug-induced dermatologic emergency

Prodrome (1–3 days before rash): fever (often >39°C), malaise, sore throat, cough, conjunctivitis, myalgias. Skin: dusky/purpuric macules and flat atypical targets beginning truncally, coalescing → detachment, maximal by ~4 days, painful/tender. Nikolsky sign (lateral shear peels epidermis) and Asboe-Hansen sign (pressure spreads a blister) are positive. Mucosa: ≥2 sites in most cases — eyes, oral/lips, genital, pharynx/airway, GI.[3][8]

Erythema multiforme — not SJS/TEN
Not on the SJS/TEN spectrum. HSV is the dominant trigger (also Mycoplasma) — not drugs. Typical target lesions: 3 concentric zones, <3 cm, acral (dorsal hands/feet), Nikolsky-negative, self-limiting over 1–2 weeks. 4 · Severity — SCORTEN

Calculate SCORTEN — 7 variables, 1 point each: age >40, malignancy, HR >120, BSA detachment >10%, BUN >28 mg/dL, glucose >252 mg/dL, bicarbonate <20 mEq/L. Mortality bands: 0–1 ≈3% · 2 ≈12% · 3 ≈35% · 4 ≈58% · ≥5 ≈90%. Score on day 1 AND day 3 — a single early score underestimates risk for scores ≥3 and never justifies under-treating.[2][10]

7 · Disposition

Multidisciplinary, threshold-driven disposition
Any >10% BSA epidermal loss = ICU/specialist unit (acute skin failure: thermoregulatory dysfunction, insensible loss, hemodynamic instability, sepsis risk). Burn-center transfer for TEN (>30% BSA) and/or deterioration — extension of detachment, wound conversion, local sepsis, delayed healing. Engage dermatology (lead + adjuvant decision), urgent ophthalmology, urology/gynecology (GU mucosa), burn surgery, ICU/critical care, ± pulmonology for airway.[9]
