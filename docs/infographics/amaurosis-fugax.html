Amaurosis Fugax: Screen for GCA, Confirm the Stroke Territory, Fix the Source

Adults presenting to the ED with transient or persistent monocular vision loss (TMVL). Excludes established permanent monocular blindness with no acute change, pediatric patients, and primary ophthalmologic emergencies (globe rupture, acute angle-closure glaucoma) once those are excluded on exam.

⚠ TMVL = retinal TIA equivalent — stroke risk 5-10% at 1 year, highest in the first 7 days.
⚠ Screen ALL patients ≥50 for GCA — order ESR + CRP STAT. GCA can cause permanent bilateral blindness within hours if untreated; ~4% of GCA patients have normal inflammatory markers.
⚠ Do NOT wait for temporal artery biopsy — start steroids empirically the moment vision-threatening GCA is suspected: methylprednisolone 1 g IV daily × 3 days, or prednisone 1 mg/kg/day (max 80 mg) if IV unavailable.
⚠ Persistent vision loss >1 hour = possible CRAO (eye stroke). If <4.5 hours from onset, consider IV tPA per stroke protocol — activate stroke code.
⚠ Carotid imaging is mandatory in every TMVL patient — CEA is indicated for symptomatic stenosis ≥50%.
⚠ Start DAPT (aspirin + clopidogrel) within 24 hours for high-risk TIA/minor stroke; MRI-DWI is preferred over CT — 20-30% of TMVL patients have silent acute infarcts.

1 · Recognize TMVL

Amaurosis fugax = "eye TIA"
Classic presentation: painless monocular vision loss, a "curtain" or "shade" descending over the visual field, duration seconds to minutes (usually <15 min), with complete spontaneous recovery. TMVL is a stroke equivalent: 5-10% stroke risk at 1 year, highest in the first 7 days, and 20-30% have silent infarcts on MRI-DWI.[1][6][11]

Assess vision status first: is vision loss still present, or has it resolved? Persistent vision loss (>1 hour) raises concern for CRAO (eye stroke) — a time-critical diagnosis. Resolved vision loss is classic amaurosis fugax.[1][10]

Persistent >1h — possible CRAO (eye stroke)
Classic fundoscopic findings: pale edematous retina, cherry-red spot at the macula, box-car segmentation of retinal vessels, retinal whitening.

Time-critical treatment: <4.5 hours — Alteplase (tPA) per stroke protocol, activate stroke code. <24 hours — emergent ophthalmology + stroke consult. >24 hours — workup still needed, no acute reperfusion option.[1][6][10]

Cover testResolves when affected eye coveredPersists with either eye covered
AnatomyRetina, optic nerve, ophthalmic arteryChiasm, optic tract, occipital cortex
ImpliesCarotid/ophthalmic artery diseaseVertebrobasilar or posterior circulation

Ask: "When you covered one eye at a time, which eye couldn't see?" If the patient is unsure, assume monocular and proceed with the full workup.[11][12]

Binocular / hemianopia — posterior circulation
Binocular transient vision loss suggests vertebrobasilar TIA or occipital cortex ischemia — look for homonymous hemianopia and associated vertigo, diplopia, ataxia, or dysarthria. Workup: MRI brain with DWI, MRA/CTA head and neck (including vertebral arteries), echocardiogram, telemetry for AF. Proceed with stroke/TIA workup — this is NOT amaurosis fugax.[6][11]

5 · Carotid & other etiologies

StenosisSignificance
<50%Medical management
50-69%CEA beneficial if symptomatic (NNT ~22)
70-99%CEA strongly indicated if symptomatic (NNT ~6)
100%Occluded — no intervention, medical management

Symptomatic = ipsilateral TIA/stroke within 6 months. Best CEA outcomes if performed within 2 weeks of the event (after 48 hours, before 14 days), and the patient must be neurologically stable.[9]

≥70% stenosis — CEA strongly indicated
Symptomatic carotid stenosis ≥70%: CEA NNT ~6, should be performed within 2 weeks at a center with <3% perioperative complication rate. Urgent vascular surgery consult. CAS may be considered instead if: high surgical risk, radiation-induced stenosis, prior CEA with restenosis, or lesion above C2. While awaiting CEA: start antiplatelet therapy, high-intensity statin, BP control, smoking cessation.[9]
50-69% stenosis — CEA may be beneficial
Symptomatic carotid stenosis 50-69%: CEA NNT ~22 — less benefit than ≥70%. Consider age, sex, comorbidities, and the center's surgical complication rate; discuss with vascular surgery. If not pursuing CEA: aggressive medical management — DAPT (aspirin + clopidogrel x21 days), high-intensity statin, risk-factor modification.[9]
<50% or imaging pending — consider other etiologies
Cardioembolic: atrial fibrillation, PFO, valvular disease, LV thrombus, recent MI. Other vascular: carotid dissection (trauma, neck manipulation), ophthalmic artery stenosis, hyperviscosity syndromes. Echo and telemetry are essential for cardiac source workup.[11][12]

7 · Disposition

  • ABCD² score ≥3 AND presenting within 72 hours
  • Unable to complete workup within 24 hours as outpatient
  • High-grade carotid stenosis requiring CEA
  • Atrial fibrillation requiring anticoagulation initiation
  • Persistent symptoms or fluctuating deficits
  • Unable to ensure medication compliance
  • GCA requiring IV steroids
  • ABCD² score 0-2
  • Workup can be completed within 24-48 hours
  • Reliable follow-up assured
  • Medications started in ED
Admit — high risk
Telemetry x24-48h minimum, neuro checks q4h, BP goal <130/80. Complete MRI-DWI, MRA/CTA head+neck, echo (TTE, consider TEE if high suspicion), extended cardiac monitoring if AF not found. Consult neurology; vascular surgery if high-grade stenosis; ophthalmology if persistent visual symptoms; cardiology if AF or cardiac source. Continue DAPT (if non-cardioembolic) + high-intensity statin.[6]
Discharge — expedited outpatient workup
Rx: aspirin 325 mg x1 then 81 mg daily; clopidogrel 300 mg x1 then 75 mg daily x21 days (if ABCD²≥4); high-intensity statin. Expedited outpatient: carotid Doppler + MRI brain within 24-48h, echocardiogram within 1 week, extended cardiac monitoring (Zio/Holter). Follow-up: stroke clinic/neurology within 1 week, PCP within 1-2 weeks, ophthalmology if persistent visual symptoms.[3][6]
Return precautions
Return immediately if: vision loss recurs; weakness, numbness, or facial droop; speech difficulty; severe headache; any new neurological symptoms.[3][6]
