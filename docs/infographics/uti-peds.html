Pediatric UTI — Neonatal, Febrile Infant & First Febrile UTI Management

Pediatric patients >7 days old presenting with suspected urinary tract infection, spanning neonates (<48 weeks postmenstrual age) through toilet-trained children (>24 months). Excludes immunocompromised patients, indwelling urinary catheter, known complex urologic anatomy (unless first presentation), and infants <7 days of life — these are managed off-pathway with specialist consultation.

⚠ Catheter or suprapubic aspirate (SPA) specimen is REQUIRED for urine culture — bag specimens are NOT acceptable for culture (false-positive rate up to 85%).
⚠ Screen ALL febrile uncircumcised males for UTI — uncircumcised males under 12 months have 4–8× higher UTI risk than circumcised males.
⚠ Neonatal UTI (<48 wks PMA): do NOT repeat urine culture for test of cure unless the organism is Candida or ESBL-producing.
⚠ VCUG is only indicated after a first febrile UTI if the renal/bladder ultrasound (RBUS) is abnormal or specific VCUG criteria are met — do not obtain VCUG reflexively.
⚠ Prophylactic antibiotics are NOT routinely recommended after a first febrile UTI — reserve for VUR grade III or higher (RIVUR trial).
⚠ Ceftriaxone pre-discharge dose for pediatric UTI is 75 mg/kg IV/IM (max 2 g) — do not confuse with the 50 mg/kg ceftriaxone dose used for the separate Pediatric Fever/Neonatal Sepsis indication.
⚠ Neonatal complicated and uncomplicated UTI use the SAME antibiotic regimen (cefazolin IV → cephalexin PO) but DIFFERENT total duration — 7 days uncomplicated vs 10 days complicated. Do not shorten the complicated course.
⚠ Grade IV or V VUR on VCUG carries increased risk of future complications — ensure urology follow-up.

1 · Initial assessment & UTI definition

Pediatric UTI is defined as >50,000 CFU/mL in a catheter-obtained (or suprapubic aspirate) specimen, OR ≥100,000 CFU/mL in a clean-catch specimen, with pyuria on urinalysis. E. coli accounts for ~80% of pediatric UTIs.

Exclusions — manage off-pathway
Immunocompromised patients, indwelling urinary catheter, known complex urologic anatomy (unless first presentation), and age <7 days of life are managed off-pathway with specialist consultation.

▶ What to do now

Neonatal UTI (<48 wk PMA, >7 days old)
Confirm diagnosis (>50,000 CFU/mL catheter specimen). Exclude Candida infection, concomitant bacteremia/meningitis, >48 wks PMA, and <7 days of age — these route off-pathway. Do NOT repeat urine culture for test of cure unless Candida or ESBL. Classify as complicated vs uncomplicated below to determine total duration.
Febrile infant 2–24 months
Risk-screen for UTI likelihood >1%. Screen ALL uncircumcised febrile males — uncircumcised males under 12 months have 4–8× higher UTI risk than circumcised males. If screening criteria are met, obtain a catheter or SPA urinalysis — bag specimens are NOT acceptable for culture.
>24 months / toilet-trained
Screen for urinary symptoms, prior UTI with fever ≥2 days, or prolonged fever ≥5 days without a source. Collect a clean-catch UA with micro; if positive, obtain a catheter or clean-catch specimen for culture — bag specimens are NOT acceptable for culture.
Uncomplicated neonatal UTI — 7 days
Cefazolin 50 mg/kg/day IV divided q8h, transition to PO cephalexin once term by PMA and sensitivities are back. Total duration: 7 days.
Complicated neonatal UTI — 10 days
Same cefazolin → cephalexin regimen as uncomplicated, but total duration: 10 days for functional/anatomic abnormality, indwelling catheter, recent instrumentation, recent antibiotics, or immunosuppression.
Outpatient — ED discharge antibiotics
First-line cephalexin 50 mg/kg/day PO divided TID-QID. Single-dose ceftriaxone if intolerant, compliance concern, or age <6 months. Duration 7 days (≥6 months) or 10 days (<6 months).
Admit — inpatient IV antibiotics
First-line cefazolin 50 mg/kg/day IV divided q8h. Transition to PO after 24-48 hours of clinical improvement. Total duration: 7 days (IV + PO).
RBUS indicated
Order renal/bladder ultrasound — outpatient >48h of antibiotics, inpatient after 36h (no less than 24h).
VCUG criteria met
Obtain VCUG. Urology consult if abnormal. Grade IV or V VUR carries increased risk of future complications.

Specimen requirements
Catheter or suprapubic aspirate (SPA) is required for culture — bag specimens are NOT acceptable for culture (false-positive rate up to 85%). If a bag sample was tested, another specimen must be obtained for culture.

Positive UA: leukocyte esterase OR nitrites OR microscopic WBC/bacteria. Nitrites have high specificity (~98%) but low sensitivity (~53%) — a negative nitrite does NOT rule out UTI; leukocyte esterase is more sensitive.

5 · Antibiotic management — outpatient & inpatient

Cephalexin: 50 mg/kg/day PO divided TID–QID — First-line. Max 1 g/dose.

Amoxicillin-Clavulanate: 20–40 mg/kg/day PO divided BID — Alternative outpatient agent. Max 875 mg/dose.

Ciprofloxacin: 20 mg/kg/day PO divided BID — Only for IgE-mediated allergy to penicillins AND cephalosporins. Max 750 mg/dose.

Ceftriaxone: 75 mg/kg IV or IM single dose prior to discharge — For medication intolerance, compliance concern, or age <6 months. Max 2 g/dose.

TMP-SMX should be used with caution — only 71% susceptible among E. coli isolates (~29% resistance). Cephalexin susceptibility remains >90%, supporting first-line use.

Total duration: <6 months: IV + PO = 10 days. ≥6 months: IV + PO = 7 days.
