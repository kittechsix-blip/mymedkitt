Calcium Channel Blocker Overdose: Phenotype-Directed Rescue

Adults with suspected or confirmed calcium channel blocker (CCB) overdose in the ED/ICU. Excludes isolated pediatric ingestions, combined beta-blocker/CCB co-ingestion (manage as mixed cardiotoxic overdose), and chronic therapeutic CCB adverse effects without overdose.

⚠ HIET is NOT DKA dosing. Insulin 1 unit/kg bolus, then 1 unit/kg/hr infusion titrated up to 10 units/kg/hr. Maintain glucose 150-250 mg/dL and replete K⁺ to 4.0-4.5 mEq/L — do NOT stop insulin even if glucose <100 mg/dL, give more dextrose instead.
⚠ Vasopressors need HIGH doses. Norepinephrine 0.1 mcg/kg/min titrated up to 0.5-3 mcg/kg/min — far above septic-shock dosing — because CCB toxicity causes profound receptor desensitization and catecholamine resistance.
⚠ Calcium chloride requires a central line (peripheral extravasation causes tissue necrosis); calcium gluconate can be given peripherally. Both are temporizing only — repeat q15-20 min PRN or run a continuous infusion.
⚠ Whole bowel irrigation (GoLYTELY 1.5-2 L/hr) is strongly recommended for extended-release CCB ingestions unless contraindicated by ileus, obstruction, or hemodynamic instability precluding positioning.
⚠ Atropine and transcutaneous pacing are usually ineffective — CCB toxicity is at the calcium channel, not vagal. Try atropine briefly but do not delay calcium, glucagon, and HIET.
⚠ Hemodialysis does NOT remove CCBs — highly protein-bound with a large volume of distribution. VA-ECMO is the definitive bridge; CCB toxicity is reversible with full recovery possible — contact the ECMO team EARLY, not at arrest.

1 · Recognize the class & phenotype

Classic triad
Hypotension + bradycardia + hyperglycemia — CCBs block pancreatic insulin release. Mental status is often preserved until late, unlike beta-blocker toxicity.[1][3]

Non-dihydropyridineCardiac SA/AV node + myocardiumCardiogenic — bradycardia, poor contractilityVerapamil — most cardiotoxic CCB, most overdose deaths
Extended-release formulations
Verapamil SR/ER, diltiazem CD/XR/LA, nifedipine XL (amlodipine is inherently long-acting) delay toxicity onset up to 12-24 hours and prolong it 48-72+ hours. Whole bowel irrigation (GoLYTELY 1.5-2 L/hr) is strongly recommended unless contraindicated by ileus, obstruction, or hemodynamic instability precluding positioning. All extended-release ingestions need a minimum 24-48 hour ICU monitoring period regardless of initial presentation.[1][3]

Shock phenotype determines treatment. Vasodilatory (warm extremities, normal/high CO, low SVR) → emphasize vasopressors. Cardiogenic (cool extremities, low CO, bradycardia) → emphasize HIET. Mixed → both. Assess clinically when the specific agent is unknown.[1][2]

3 · High-dose insulin euglycemia therapy (HIET)

A completely different mechanism
HIET does not overcome channel blockade directly — it shifts the shocked heart to glucose metabolism, restoring contractility independent of calcium channels. Onset 15-60 minutes; some patients take longer — do not abandon therapy early.[4]

Why such high doses?
Typical septic shock: NE 0.1-0.5 mcg/kg/min. CCB toxicity may need NE 1-3+ mcg/kg/min due to catecholamine resistance. Multi-agent approach: norepinephrine + vasopressin ± epinephrine — do not abandon vasopressors if initial doses fail.[2]

Critical pitfalls

Do NOT rely on atropine or pacing alone. Toxicity is at the calcium channel, not vagal — escalate to calcium/glucagon/HIET without delay.

Do NOT delay whole bowel irrigation in extended-release ingestions unless truly contraindicated (ileus, obstruction, hemodynamic instability precluding positioning).

Do NOT dose HIET like DKA. This is 1 unit/kg bolus then up to 10 units/kg/hr — far above DKA dosing. Do not abandon it early; onset is 15-60 minutes.

Do NOT stop insulin for a low glucose during HIET — give more dextrose instead. Never simply hold the insulin.

Do NOT rely on hemodialysis to remove CCBs — highly protein-bound with a large volume of distribution, not meaningfully dialyzable.

Do NOT discharge an extended-release or verapamil ingestion early. Minimum 24-48h monitoring even if initially stable — delayed deterioration is common.

Do NOT wait for arrest to call the ECMO team. CCB toxicity is reversible — contact early for refractory shock. Poison Control: 1-800-222-1222.
