Pediatric Trauma — Initial ED Management

Children (neonate–18yr) presenting to the ED after blunt or penetrating trauma. Excludes adult-only trauma protocols (permissive hypotension targets, adult MTP ratios differ). Assumes weight-based dosing throughout — always confirm weight via Broselow tape or age-based formula before treating.

⚠ Hemodynamically unstable: 20 mL/kg isotonic crystalloid bolus, may repeat x2. If unstable after 40 mL/kg crystalloid → blood products. TXA 15 mg/kg (max 1 g) IV over 10 min within 3 hours of injury, then 2 mg/kg/hr infusion. Empiric calcium gluconate 0.5 mL/kg (citrate chelates calcium during transfusion).
⚠ GCS <=8 = INTUBATE. Herniation signs (blown pupil, Cushing triad, posturing, acute GCS drop >=2): 3% hypertonic saline 3-5 mL/kg IV over 10-20 min (preferred if hypotensive) or mannitol 0.5-1 g/kg IV (AVOID if hypotensive). Permissive hypotension is NOT recommended for pediatric TBI.
⚠ Compartment syndrome is a CLINICAL diagnosis — do not wait for pressure measurement. Pain out of proportion and pain with passive stretch are the most reliable early signs. Remove all circumferential dressings/casts immediately; fasciotomy within 6 hours to prevent irreversible damage.
⚠ Non-accidental trauma: healthcare providers are mandated reporters. If you suspect abuse, you MUST report to CPS — you do not need certainty. Do NOT discharge to a suspected abuser without CPS clearance.

Initial assessment & weight-based dosing

Children are NOT small adults. Anatomic and physiologic differences change injury patterns, presentation, and management. Airway: larger occiput causes neck flexion, smaller airway diameter, anterior/cephalad larynx. Breathing: higher metabolic rate = faster desaturation; compliant chest wall = pulmonary contusion WITHOUT rib fractures. Circulation: higher physiologic reserve = BP maintained until >25-30% blood loss — tachycardia is the early sign, hypotension is LATE. Disability: fontanelles/open sutures can mask elevated ICP in infants. Exposure: large body-surface-area = rapid heat loss.

Weight estimation is critical — all pediatric resuscitation is weight-based. Broselow tape is the gold standard. If unavailable: infants 0-12mo = (age in months + 9) / 2 kg; children 1-5yr = (age x 2) + 8 kg; children 6-12yr = (age x 3) + 7 kg. Cuffed ETT size = (age/4) + 3.5. Fluid bolus 20 mL/kg isotonic crystalloid; blood products 10-20 mL/kg.

Hemorrhage control & transfusion

Pediatric blood volume: 70-80 mL/kg (higher in neonates ~85-90 mL/kg). A 10 kg child has only ~700-800 mL total blood volume — what looks like "minor" bleeding can be catastrophic. Children compensate well: BP drops LATE (Class III-IV shock). Tachycardia and delayed capillary refill are the early signs.

Permissive hypotension — NOT for pediatric TBI
Less well-established in children than adults. NOT recommended for pediatric TBI — maintain adequate cerebral perfusion. May be considered in non-TBI penetrating trauma only (target SBP 70 + 2 x age mmHg). Generally target normal BP for age in blunt trauma and TBI.

Head trauma

TBI is the leading cause of death in pediatric trauma. Larger head-to-body ratio, thinner calvarium, less myelination, and higher water content increase vulnerability. Assume cervical spine injury until cleared. Maintain high suspicion for abusive head trauma in infants <1yr, subdural hematomas without significant mechanism, or retinal hemorrhages.

Thoracoabdominal trauma

Compliant chest wall = pulmonary contusion WITHOUT rib fractures; rib fractures in children indicate a HIGH-ENERGY mechanism. Solid organs (liver, spleen) are relatively larger and less protected; bladder is intra-abdominal. Seatbelt sign = ecchymosis across the abdomen, associated with hollow viscus injury and Chance (lumbar spine) fracture. Handlebar injury = direct epigastric blow, associated with pancreatic/duodenal injury. FAST is less sensitive in children (~50-75%) — a negative FAST does NOT rule out solid organ injury.

Orthopedic trauma & compartment syndrome

The physis (growth plate) is the weakest point in pediatric bone — thicker periosteum gives excellent remodeling potential, but physeal injuries risk growth disturbance. Unique pediatric patterns: greenstick (incomplete fracture), torus/buckle (compression, no cortical break), plastic deformation (bowing without a discrete fracture line). Always weigh non-accidental trauma in non-ambulatory children or fractures in multiple stages of healing.

  • Open fractures
  • Fractures with neurovascular compromise
  • Salter-Harris III, IV, V
  • Supracondylar humerus fractures
  • Femur fractures
  • Pelvic fractures

Compartment syndrome — clinical diagnosis
Do not wait for pressure measurement. Pain out of proportion (especially with passive stretch) is the most reliable early sign. Classic 6 Ps (late signs, do not wait for all): pain, pressure, paresthesias, pulselessness, pallor, paralysis. Pediatric "3 As": anxiety, agitation, increasing analgesic requirement. Remove all circumferential dressings/casts immediately; keep the limb at heart level (not elevated); emergent orthopedic consultation; fasciotomy within 6 hours to prevent irreversible damage. If measured, delta P <30 (within 30 mmHg of diastolic) indicates compartment syndrome.
  • Supracondylar humerus fractures (forearm compartments)
  • Tibial fractures (leg compartments)
  • Crush injuries
  • Circumferential burns
  • Tight casts/splints

Disposition & non-accidental trauma

  • GCS <14 or declining GCS
  • Intubated or requiring airway intervention
  • Multisystem trauma
  • Solid organ injury grade III or higher
  • Pelvic fracture with hemodynamic instability
  • Spinal cord injury or unstable spine fracture
  • Penetrating trauma to head, neck, torso
  • Significant burns (>10% TBSA, face/hands/feet/perineum, inhalation)
  • Crush injury or amputation proximal to wrist/ankle
  1. Secure airway if any concern before transport
  2. Two large-bore IVs or IO access
  3. C-spine immobilized
  4. Chest tubes placed if indicated (do not clamp)
  5. Foley catheter if abdominal/pelvic trauma
  6. All imaging sent with patient
  7. Direct physician-to-physician report to receiving team

Admit to ICU if GCS <14, hemodynamically unstable or required significant resuscitation, solid organ injury grade III+, chest trauma requiring chest tube, intubated, or multiple injuries. Admit to floor if stable with isolated injury or low-grade solid organ injury for observation. DVT prophylaxis if immobilized >48h.

Discharge when hemodynamically stable, pain controlled with oral medications, no concerning exam findings, reliable caregiver understands return precautions, and follow-up is arranged. Provide written instructions in the family's preferred language when possible.

Moderate TBI (GCS 9-13) — Avoid hypoxia (SpO2>94%) and hypotension. HOB 30 degrees. CT head STAT. Neurosurgery consult for EDH (any size), SDH >5mm/midline shift, contusion, depressed skull fracture, or pneumocephalus. Admit minimum 24h, serial neuro checks q1h initially.

Severe TBI (GCS <=8): INTUBATE — GCS<=8 = INTUBATE. RSI with c-spine inline stabilization; avoid hypoxia/hypotension. Normoventilation PaCO2 35-40. Herniation signs: 3% saline 3-5 mL/kg or mannitol 0.5-1 g/kg (avoid if hypotensive). Seizure prophylaxis: levetiracetam 20 mg/kg or phenytoin 20 mg/kg. Target CPP >40-50 mmHg.

Solid Organ Injury: non-operative management — Non-operative management standard for most pediatric liver/spleen injuries (APSA guidelines by grade). Serial abdominal exams, serial Hgb q6h initially. Surgery only for instability, transfusion >40 mL/kg/24h, peritonitis, or ongoing hemorrhage. Contrast blush may need angioembolization.

Unstable Abdominal Trauma: OR — MTP activation; pediatric surgery STAT; prepare for emergent laparotomy. Positive FAST + unstable → OR for damage control laparotomy. Do NOT delay surgery for CT in unstable patients.

Chest Trauma — Rib fracture = high-energy mechanism; first rib fracture = major vascular injury until proven otherwise. Tension pneumothorax: needle decompression then chest tube. Massive hemothorax: chest tube, thoracotomy if >15 mL/kg initial output or >2-4 mL/kg/hr ongoing.

Compartment Syndrome Check — CLINICAL diagnosis — do not wait for pressure measurement. Pain out of proportion and pain with passive stretch are the most reliable early signs; '3 As' in children: anxiety, agitation, increasing analgesic requirement. Remove all circumferential dressings/casts immediately. Fasciotomy within 6 hours. Delta P <30 confirms diagnosis if measured.

Disposition planning — Transfer to pediatric trauma center if GCS<14/declining, intubated, multisystem trauma, solid organ injury grade III+, unstable pelvic fracture, spinal cord injury, penetrating torso trauma, significant burns, or inadequate local facilities.

Trauma Center Transfer — Stabilize before transfer — secure airway if any concern, two large-bore IVs/IO, C-spine immobilized, chest tubes not clamped, all imaging sent with patient. Direct physician-to-physician report to receiving team.

Admit to Local Hospital — ICU if GCS<14, hemodynamically unstable/required significant resuscitation, solid organ injury grade III+, chest tube, intubated, or multiple injuries. Floor if stable isolated injury. Serial exams and DVT prophylaxis if immobilized >48h.

Discharge with Follow-up — Hemodynamically stable, pain controlled orally, no concerning exam findings, reliable caregiver, follow-up arranged. Written injury-specific return precautions in family's preferred language.

NAT: Mandatory CPS Report — TEN-4-FACESp: torso/ear/neck bruising in <4yr or any non-mobile age; frenulum/angle-of-jaw/cheeks/eyelids injury; subconjunctival hemorrhage in pre-mobile infants. Skeletal survey mandatory <2yr. Healthcare providers are mandated reporters — report to CPS if you suspect abuse; you do not need certainty. Do NOT discharge to a suspected abuser without CPS clearance.

This is the high-yield spine across all six modules. See the [full protocol](#/tree/peds-trauma) for complete secondary-survey detail and every disposition pathway.

Hemorrhage control & transfusion

**Pediatric blood volume: 70-80 mL/kg** (higher in neonates ~85-90 mL/kg). A 10 kg child has only ~700-800 mL total blood volume — what looks like "minor" bleeding can be catastrophic. Children compensate well: BP drops LATE (Class III-IV shock). Tachycardia and delayed capillary refill are the early signs.

Class | Blood Loss | Heart Rate | BP | Cap Refill | Mental Status

I | <15% | Normal-mild increase | Normal | Normal | Anxious

II | 15-30% | Tachycardia | Normal | Delayed (2-3s) | Irritable/Confused

III | 30-40% | Marked tachycardia | Decreased | Delayed (>3s) | Lethargic

IV | >40% | Severe tachycardia then bradycardia | Severely decreased | Absent | Unresponsive

**Transfusion trigger in trauma: Hgb <7-8 g/dL** (or ongoing hemorrhage regardless of Hgb). pRBCs 10-15 mL/kg (expect ~1 g/dL rise per 3 mL/kg); FFP 10-15 mL/kg; platelets 5-10 mL/kg; cryoprecipitate 1-2 units/10 kg (fibrinogen <150). MTP ratio 1:1:1 (pRBC:FFP:platelets by volume).

Tranexamic Acid (TXA): 15 mg/kg (max 1g) IV over 10 min, then 2 mg/kg/hr infusion x8h or until bleeding controlled IV — Give within 3 hours of injury — most benefit if given within 1 hour. Not a drug-store-matched pediatric trauma indication (see conflict note); value taken directly from the consult.

TXA infusion rate: (computed)

Calcium Gluconate (10%): 0.5 mL/kg IV, empiric during massive transfusion IV — Citrate in transfused blood chelates ionized calcium. Not a drug-store-matched indication (existing entries are fixed-dose adult hyperkalemia/hypocalcemia/hypermagnesemia regimens); value taken directly from the consult.

Less well-established in children than adults. **NOT recommended for pediatric TBI** — maintain adequate cerebral perfusion. May be considered in non-TBI penetrating trauma only (target SBP 70 + 2 x age mmHg). Generally target normal BP for age in blunt trauma and TBI.

Head trauma

TBI is the leading cause of death in pediatric trauma. Larger head-to-body ratio, thinner calvarium, less myelination, and higher water content increase vulnerability. Assume cervical spine injury until cleared. Maintain high suspicion for abusive head trauma in infants <1yr, subdural hematomas without significant mechanism, or retinal hemorrhages.

Severity | GCS | Management

Mild | 14-15 | PECARN-guided imaging decision

Moderate | 9-13 | CT indicated, observation vs admission

Severe | 3-8 (GCS <=8) | Intubate, CT, ICU, neurosurgery

  • GCS <=8 or rapidly declining GCS
  • Unequal pupils or pupil unreactive to light
  • Cushing triad (hypertension, bradycardia, irregular respirations)
  • Open skull fracture
  • Signs of herniation

PECARN Head CT Decision Rule (full criteria)

  • GCS <15
  • Palpable skull fracture
  • Altered mental status (agitation, somnolence, repetitive questioning, slow response)
  • Loss of consciousness >=5 seconds
  • Severe mechanism (MVC with ejection/death/rollover, pedestrian/cyclist vs vehicle, fall >3 feet, head struck by high-impact object)
  • Acting abnormally per parent
  • GCS <15
  • Signs of basilar skull fracture (Battle sign, raccoon eyes, hemotympanum, CSF otorrhea/rhinorrhea)
  • Altered mental status
  • Loss of consciousness
  • Vomiting
  • Severe mechanism (MVC with ejection/death/rollover, pedestrian/cyclist vs vehicle, fall >5 feet, head struck by high-impact object)
  • Severe headache

If NO criteria present: <0.05% risk of clinically important TBI — observation over CT is reasonable.

  • Worsening headache not relieved by acetaminophen/ibuprofen
  • Repeated vomiting (>2 episodes)
  • Increasing confusion or difficulty arousing
  • Seizures
  • Clear fluid from nose or ears
  • Weakness or numbness
  • Unequal pupils
  • Unusual behavior

**GCS <=8 = INTUBATE.** RSI with c-spine inline stabilization, video laryngoscopy preferred, avoid hypoxia/hypotension during intubation. Target ETCO2 35-40 mmHg. HOB 30 degrees, head midline, normoventilation PaCO2 35-40, avoid hyperthermia.

3% Hypertonic Saline: 3-5 mL/kg IV over 10-20 min, may repeat PRN for herniation IV — Preferred over mannitol in hypotensive patients. Can give via peripheral IV.

Mannitol: 0.5-1 g/kg IV over 15-20 min, may repeat q4-6h IV — AVOID if hypotensive — hypertonic saline preferred in that setting. Monitor serum osmolality, keep <320.

Levetiracetam: 20 mg/kg IV (max 3000 mg) loading dose over 15 min IV — Preferred seizure-prophylaxis agent — fewer drug interactions and side effects than phenytoin.

The consult lists phenytoin 20 mg/kg as an alternative to levetiracetam for seizure prophylaxis. myMedKitt's drug reference does not carry a plain "phenytoin" entry — only fosphenytoin, dosed in phenytoin-equivalents (20 mg PE/kg, max 1500 mg PE), a different unit convention and cap than the consult's stated phenytoin dose. This dose is presented as text only, not linked to a calculator.

  • Blown pupil (unilateral dilated, unreactive)
  • Cushing triad
  • Posturing (decorticate or decerebrate)
  • Acute GCS drop >=2 points

Thoracoabdominal trauma

Compliant chest wall = pulmonary contusion WITHOUT rib fractures; rib fractures in children indicate a HIGH-ENERGY mechanism. Solid organs (liver, spleen) are relatively larger and less protected; bladder is intra-abdominal. Seatbelt sign = ecchymosis across the abdomen, associated with hollow viscus injury and Chance (lumbar spine) fracture. Handlebar injury = direct epigastric blow, associated with pancreatic/duodenal injury. FAST is less sensitive in children (~50-75%) — a negative FAST does NOT rule out solid organ injury.

Grade | ICU Days | Hospital Days | Activity Restriction

I | 0 | 2 | 3 weeks

II | 0 | 3 | 4 weeks

III | 0 | 4 | 5 weeks

IV | 1 | 5 | 6 weeks

Indications for surgery: hemodynamic instability despite resuscitation, transfusion requirement **>40 mL/kg** in 24h, peritonitis (hollow viscus injury), or ongoing hemorrhage. Contrast blush on CT may indicate active extravasation — angioembolization if available.

Massive Transfusion Protocol: pRBC:FFP:Platelets 1:1:1 by volume, start with 10-20 mL/kg of each product IV — TXA 15 mg/kg load, then 2 mg/kg/hr. Do NOT delay surgery for CT in unstable patients.

Age Group | Chest Tube Size

Infant | 10-12 Fr

Toddler | 16-20 Fr

School age | 20-28 Fr

Adolescent | 28-32 Fr

Massive hemothorax: chest tube, then thoracotomy if **>15 mL/kg initial output** or >2-4 mL/kg/hr ongoing. First rib fracture = major vascular injury until proven otherwise.

Orthopedic trauma & compartment syndrome

The physis (growth plate) is the weakest point in pediatric bone — thicker periosteum gives excellent remodeling potential, but physeal injuries risk growth disturbance. Unique pediatric patterns: greenstick (incomplete fracture), torus/buckle (compression, no cortical break), plastic deformation (bowing without a discrete fracture line). Always weigh non-accidental trauma in non-ambulatory children or fractures in multiple stages of healing.

  • Open fractures
  • Fractures with neurovascular compromise
  • Salter-Harris III, IV, V
  • Supracondylar humerus fractures
  • Femur fractures
  • Pelvic fractures

Salter-Harris Classification (SALTR)

Type | Description | Mnemonic | Prognosis

I | Through physis only | Slipped | Good

II | Through physis + metaphysis | Above | Good

III | Through physis + epiphysis (intra-articular) | Lower | Guarded

IV | Through metaphysis + physis + epiphysis | Through | Poor

V | Crush injury to physis | cRush | Poor

**Do not wait for pressure measurement.** Pain out of proportion (especially with passive stretch) is the most reliable early sign. Classic 6 Ps (late signs, do not wait for all): pain, pressure, paresthesias, pulselessness, pallor, paralysis. Pediatric "3 As": anxiety, agitation, increasing analgesic requirement. Remove all circumferential dressings/casts immediately; keep the limb at heart level (not elevated); emergent orthopedic consultation; fasciotomy within 6 hours to prevent irreversible damage. If measured, **delta P <30** (within 30 mmHg of diastolic) indicates compartment syndrome.

  • Supracondylar humerus fractures (forearm compartments)
  • Tibial fractures (leg compartments)
  • Crush injuries
  • Circumferential burns
  • Tight casts/splints

Disposition & non-accidental trauma

  • GCS <14 or declining GCS
  • Intubated or requiring airway intervention
  • Multisystem trauma
  • Solid organ injury grade III or higher
  • Pelvic fracture with hemodynamic instability
  • Spinal cord injury or unstable spine fracture
  • Penetrating trauma to head, neck, torso
  • Significant burns (>10% TBSA, face/hands/feet/perineum, inhalation)
  • Crush injury or amputation proximal to wrist/ankle
  • Secure airway if any concern before transport
  • Two large-bore IVs or IO access
  • C-spine immobilized
  • Chest tubes placed if indicated (do not clamp)
  • Foley catheter if abdominal/pelvic trauma
  • All imaging sent with patient
  • Direct physician-to-physician report to receiving team

Admit to ICU if GCS <14, hemodynamically unstable or required significant resuscitation, solid organ injury grade III+, chest trauma requiring chest tube, intubated, or multiple injuries. Admit to floor if stable with isolated injury or low-grade solid organ injury for observation. DVT prophylaxis if immobilized >48h.

Discharge when hemodynamically stable, pain controlled with oral medications, no concerning exam findings, reliable caregiver understands return precautions, and follow-up is arranged. Provide written instructions in the family's preferred language when possible.

T | Torso bruising

E | Ear bruising

N | Neck bruising (<4yr or any non-mobile age)

F-A-C-E-S | Frenulum, Angle of jaw, Cheeks, Eyelids, Subconjunctival hemorrhage

  • Injury inconsistent with developmental stage
  • Changing or inconsistent history
  • Delay in seeking care
  • Multiple injuries in different stages of healing
  • Patterned injuries (belt marks, bite marks, cigarette burns)
  • Subdural hematomas in infants without significant mechanism
  • Retinal hemorrhages
  • Metaphyseal corner ("bucket handle") fractures

Healthcare providers are mandated reporters. If you suspect abuse, you MUST report — you do not need certainty. Report to Child Protective Services and law enforcement if immediate danger. Skeletal survey mandatory <2 years; head CT mandatory <6 months. Document thoroughly (quotes, photos, all imaging/labs). Do NOT discharge to a suspected abuser without CPS clearance.
