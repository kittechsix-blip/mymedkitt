Wide-Complex Tachycardia: Treat It Like VT

Adults ≥18 in the ED with a wide-complex tachycardia (QRS >120 ms, rate >100 bpm) and a pulse. Excludes pediatrics, pregnancy, and pulseless/arrest VT — pulseless rhythm goes straight to the ACLS arrest protocol.

⚠ Unstable = SBP <90, AMS, ischemia, or shock → immediate cardioversion
⚠ Sodium channel blocker WCT: bicarb 1-2 mEq/kg IV push — NO antiarrhythmics (may cause asystole)
⚠ Hyperkalemic WCT: calcium first (membrane stabilization), then insulin/glucose + albuterol, HD if severe
⚠ WPW+AFib: NO adenosine, CCB, BB, or digoxin (lethal). Treat with cardioversion or procainamide.
⚠ Unstable WCT: synchronized cardioversion 100J → 200J → 360J. Sedate if time. If pulseless, unsync shock.
⚠ Admit ALL VT to ICU/tele. EP consult if EF ≤35%, arrest survivor, recurrent. Echo required.

1 · What a WCT is — and the one rule

Definition
QRS >120 ms + rate >100 bpm. The golden rule: wide + regular = VT until proven otherwise. Hemodynamic stability does NOT differentiate VT from SVT — stable VT is common and does not rule out VT.[1]
Clinical featureProbability it is VT
Baseline WCT80% VT
Age >3585% VT
Structural heart disease>90% VT
History of MI98% VT
Why "treat as VT" is the safe default
VT treatments (procainamide, cardioversion) are safe if the rhythm turns out to be SVT. SVT treatments (adenosine, calcium-channel blockers) can be lethal in VT. When in doubt, treat as VT.[1][2][5]

2 · Set the patient state → what to do now

Flip the toggles above to match your patient. Exactly one "what to do now" box below lights up — precedence is unstable → toxic/metabolic → WPW → polymorphic → default monomorphic VT.

Unstable → cardiovert NOW
Synchronized cardioversion 100J → 200J → 360J (biphasic). Confirm sync mode. Sedate if time (midazolam 2–5 mg, ketamine 1–2 mg/kg, or etomidate 0.3 mg/kg IV). If it deteriorates to pulseless → unsynchronized shock 200J and start ACLS.[2]
Na⁺-channel blocker toxicity → bicarb, NO antiarrhythmics
🛑 Do NOT give amiodarone, procainamide, or lidocaine — all block sodium channels and may cause asystole. Give sodium bicarbonate 1–2 mEq/kg IV push (1.5 mEq/kg calculated below), repeat q3–5 min until QRS narrows; goal serum pH 7.50–7.55. ECG clue: terminal R in aVR >3 mm.[11]
Hyperkalemia → calcium FIRST
Membrane stabilization first: calcium gluconate 3 g IV over 10 min (or CaCl₂ 1 g via central line), repeat in 5 min if no ECG change. Then shift: regular insulin 10 units IV + D50, albuterol 10–20 mg neb. Emergent HD if K >7 or refractory.[3]
WPW + AFib → NO AV-nodal blockers
🛑 Adenosine, diltiazem/verapamil, beta-blockers, and digoxin are contraindicated — blocking the AV node forces conduction down the accessory pathway → VF. Treat with procainamide 20–50 mg/min IV (stable) or synchronized cardioversion 200J (unstable). EP consult for ablation.[2][10]
Polymorphic + long QT = Torsades → magnesium
🛑 Do NOT give amiodarone or procainamide — they prolong QT and worsen Torsades. Give magnesium sulfate 2–4 g IV over 15 min (can push in arrest). If recurrent: overdrive pace to 90–110 bpm. Stop all QT-prolonging meds; K⁺ >4.5, Mg²⁺ >2.0.[3]
Polymorphic + normal QT = ischemia → treat ACS
Polymorphic VT with a normal QT is ischemia until proven otherwise. Activate the cath lab, treat as ACS. Amiodarone is acceptable here (QT not prolonged); beta-blockers help catecholamine-driven VT. Correct K⁺ >4.0, Mg²⁺ >2.0.[8]
Stable monomorphic VT → procainamide
First-line procainamide 20–50 mg/min IV (PROCAMIO: 67% termination vs 38% amiodarone, fewer adverse events). Stop the infusion if QRS widens >50%, hypotension develops, or the arrhythmia terminates. If it fails → synchronized cardioversion.[5]

5 · When you have time: VT vs SVT

High-specificity VT features & morphology algorithms

AV dissociation (P waves marching independent of QRS), fusion beats, and capture beats. Absence of these does not rule out VT.[1][7]

  • QRS >140 ms (RBBB pattern) or >160 ms (LBBB pattern) favors VT
  • Extreme axis deviation ("northwest axis"): positive aVR, negative I and aVF
  • Precordial concordance: all V1–V6 positive, or all negative
  • QRS onset >40 ms to first peak; slurred/notched initial deflection

Algorithm | Uses | Any step positive

Brugada (4-step) | All precordial leads V1–V6 | = VT

Vereckei aVR | Lead aVR only (faster: ~36 s) | = VT

If an algorithm suggests SVT and the rhythm is regular, non-pre-excited, and not polymorphic: Adenosine 6 mg → 12 mg → 12 mg IV rapid push with 20 mL flush, continuous strip running. No effect → still may be VT → proceed with VT treatment. Any doubt → treat as VT.[1][2]

Do NOT — the lethal mistakes

Do NOT let stability talk you out of VT — stable VT is common; 80% VT baseline, 98% with prior MI.

Do NOT give AV-nodal blockers to an irregular WCT — it may be WPW + AFib → VF.

Do NOT give antiarrhythmics in Na⁺-channel blocker toxicity — bicarb only; antiarrhythmics may cause asystole.

Do NOT give amiodarone/procainamide for Torsades — they prolong QT; magnesium is the answer.

Do NOT discharge first-episode VT from the ED — admit all VT, echo, EP consult.
