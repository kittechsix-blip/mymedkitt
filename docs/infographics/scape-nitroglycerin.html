SCAPE High-Dose Nitroglycerin

ED patients presenting with sympathetic crashing acute pulmonary edema (SCAPE) — abrupt respiratory distress, severe hypertension, pulmonary edema, and high catecholamine tone. Routes hypotensive pulmonary edema to the cardiogenic shock pathway and non-SCAPE respiratory/cardiac presentations (COPD/asthma, pneumonia, PE, ACS without severe HTN) to alternative-diagnosis management rather than high-dose nitrate dosing.

⚠ Avoid high-dose nitroglycerin when SBP is low or rapidly falling, RV infarct/preload-dependent state is likely, recent PDE-5 inhibitor exposure (sildenafil/vardenafil within 24 hr, tadalafil within 48 hr), severe aortic stenosis or obstructive HCM with instability, or marked anemia/raised ICP concern
⚠ Hypotensive pulmonary edema is not a high-dose nitrate indication — use the cardiogenic shock pathway (oxygenation/ventilation, bedside echo/ECG, vasopressors/inotropes, early ICU/cardiology)
⚠ Do not delay NIV and nitroglycerin while waiting for diuretic effect — furosemide is not the first-minute treatment for SCAPE
⚠ Nitroprusside is not the usual ED first-line SCAPE medication — it is an ICU hypertensive-emergency tool requiring invasive monitoring and expert oversight

Recognition

SCAPE is sympathetic crashing acute pulmonary edema: abrupt respiratory distress, severe hypertension, pulmonary edema, and high catecholamine tone. ED first-line therapy is noninvasive ventilation plus rapidly titrated high-dose Nitroglycerin. Nitroprusside is not the usual ED first-line pathway.

  • Severe HTN + flash pulmonary edema — diaphoretic, crashing, rales, high work of breathing, SBP often >180
  • Hypotensive pulmonary edema — cardiogenic shock pathway, not high-dose nitrate SCAPE
  • COPD/asthma/pneumonia more likely — treat alternative cause; avoid reflex high-dose nitrate

▶ Not SCAPE

Not SCAPE: shock pathway
If pulmonary edema is accompanied by hypotension, cold shock, RV infarct, or cardiogenic shock, do not use the high-dose nitrate strategy. Use the cardiogenic shock pathway rather than high-dose nitrate SCAPE protocol.
  • Oxygenation/ventilation support
  • Bedside echo and ECG
  • Vasopressors/inotropes as indicated
  • Treat MI, dysrhythmia, mechanical valve/papillary muscle rupture, PE, tamponade, or sepsis as appropriate
  • Early ICU/cardiology involvement
  • COPD/asthma: bronchodilators, steroids, ventilation strategy
  • Pneumonia/ARDS: antibiotics, sepsis/ARDS care
  • PE: PE risk stratification
  • ACS without severe HTN: ACS pathway
Use high-dose SCAPE dosing only for the right phenotype
Nitrates may still be appropriate for ACS/HTN/pulmonary edema, but not high-dose SCAPE dosing unless the hypertensive crashing phenotype is present.

Management algorithm

This is the high-yield spine. See the full protocol and SCAPE Steps for the complete sequence.

NIV first minutes

  1. Sit upright
  2. Apply noninvasive ventilation immediately, typically CPAP/BiPAP with high EPAP/PEEP
  3. Start high-dose Nitroglycerin concurrently; do not wait for diuretics
  4. Obtain ECG, CXR/POCUS, troponin/BNP/BMP as stabilization allows
Rapid response expected
NIV and nitroglycerin should improve work of breathing and BP within minutes when SCAPE is the correct physiology.

High-dose nitroglycerin

Common ED approach for severe hypertensive SCAPE. Hypotension after a bolus is usually transient (short nitro half-life) and responds to observation or a small crystalloid bolus. Use close BP monitoring — an arterial line is helpful but should not delay initial treatment in crashing SCAPE.

Monitoring / response

Expected response: reduced dyspnea and diaphoresis, falling BP without hypotension, improved oxygenation and ventilator synchrony, and decreased rales/work of breathing.

  • If not improving: reconfirm diagnosis
  • Check mask seal/NIV settings
  • Escalate nitrate if BP remains very high and no contraindication
  • Evaluate ACS, valve catastrophe, dysrhythmia, renal failure, PE, aortic syndrome, sepsis
  • Prepare intubation only if NIV/medical therapy fails or mental status/airway protection deteriorates

Diuretics / after stabilization

Furosemide is not the first-minute treatment
Do not delay NIV and nitrates while waiting for diuretic effect. In SCAPE, the immediate problem is often afterload/sympathetic redistribution rather than total body fluid excess alone.
  • Chronic CHF with congestion
  • Renal failure/volume overload
  • Clear volume excess after BP/WOB improve

Nitroprusside?

Nitroprusside is not the usual ED first-line SCAPE medication.

  • Familiar ACS/pulmonary edema medication
  • Rapid titration
  • Venous and arterial dilation at high dose
  • No cyanide/thiocyanate toxicity concern
  • Easier transition as patient stabilizes
Nitroprusside — ICU tool, not routine ED first-line
Nitroprusside may be considered in ICU-level hypertensive emergency with invasive monitoring and expert oversight, but avoid/caution in renal/hepatic failure, raised ICP, pregnancy, and settings where cyanide/thiocyanate toxicity monitoring is not feasible.

Disposition

ICU/stepdown while on high-dose nitrate infusion or NIV. Cardiology/ICU involvement if ACS, valve emergency, persistent respiratory failure, renal failure, or recurrent SCAPE. Wean nitrate only after sustained work-of-breathing and BP improvement. Transition to oral antihypertensives/diuresis after cause is clarified.

Document
Document peak nitroglycerin dose, response time, BP nadir, NIV settings, ECG/troponin findings, and trigger assessment.

Nitroglycerin (while preparing infusion): 0.4 mg q5 min SL — If patient can tolerate, while IV is being prepared.

Nitroglycerin (loading-dose strategy): 600-2000 mcg over ~2 min IV bolus — Front-loaded bolus titrated to initial SBP (current EMCrit/IBCC approach); higher end for SBP >200. OR a brief high-rate infusion of 400-800 mcg/min for ~2.5 min to rapidly break the crisis.

Nitroglycerin (IV push protocol): 400-800 mcg IV every 2-5 min IV push — For severe distress and SBP usually >180.

Nitroglycerin (infusion): 100-200 mcg/min IV infusion — Start dose; titrate rapidly every 3-5 min. Severe SCAPE may require 300-800 mcg/min initially. Reduce once work of breathing improves and SBP approaches 140-160.
