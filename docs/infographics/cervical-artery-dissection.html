Cervical Artery Dissection — Strangulation, Stroke, and Antithrombotic Therapy

Adults presenting after strangulation, neck trauma, or with spontaneous neck pain/headache concerning for carotid or vertebral artery dissection, in the ED. Excludes traumatic aortic/great-vessel injury and isolated blunt cerebrovascular injury protocols in polytrauma (use trauma-specific screening there).

⚠ Ask directly: "Has anyone ever choked you or put hands around your neck?" — 50% have no visible injury on exam. Absence of visible injury does not rule out strangulation.
⚠ Loss of consciousness, incontinence, voice change, dysphagia, neurologic symptoms, or visible neck injury during strangulation are high-risk features requiring immediate CTA head and neck.
⚠ A young patient presenting with stroke should raise suspicion for cervical artery dissection — activate the stroke protocol immediately (CT head, CTA head/neck, emergent neurology consultation).
⚠ tPA thrombolysis is NOT contraindicated in dissection-related stroke — standard eligibility criteria and the 4.5-hour window apply.
⚠ Avoid hypotension in dissection-related stroke — it can worsen ischemia in the setting of arterial stenosis.
⚠ Non-fatal strangulation is a leading predictor of future homicide in intimate partner violence — mandatory safety assessment (safe at home? safe place to go? National DV Hotline 1-800-799-7233) for every strangulation patient, even a "low-risk" presentation with a normal exam.
⚠ Symptoms of cervical artery dissection after strangulation may be delayed 24-72 hours or more — close follow-up and strict return precautions are required even when the initial exam is normal.

1 · Recognition & mechanism

The lesion
Carotid or vertebral artery dissection — an intimal tear with intramural hematoma causing stenosis, occlusion, or pseudoaneurysm. Compression and hyperextension of the neck shear the arterial wall. Strangulation can be fatal with as little as 10 seconds of pressure.[1][2]

Up to 50% of intimate partner violence victims experience strangulation, and symptoms of dissection may be delayed 24-72 hours or more after the event — a normal exam on arrival does not exclude a developing injury.

MechanismNotes
Strangulation / chokingIntimate partner violence, assault
TraumaMVA, sports injury, chiropractic manipulation
SpontaneousConnective tissue disorders, hypertension
Minor traumaCoughing, vomiting, yoga, roller coasters
Screen every strangulation patient
Strangulation is a leading predictor of future homicide in IPV — victims often minimize or deny the event and may present days later with stroke.[2][3]

Even "low-risk" strangulation requires documentation (detailed history, photograph any visible injuries, document negative findings too), IPV safety assessment (screening, safety planning, social work), and return precautions (headache, vision changes, weakness, voice/swallow changes).

5 · Stroke management

Thrombolysis (tPA): NOT contraindicated in dissection-related stroke, standard criteria apply, 4.5-hour window. Thrombectomy: for large-vessel occlusion, Up to 24 hours in selected patients — may be technically challenging at the dissection site.

Avoid hypotension
Hypotension may worsen ischemia in the setting of arterial stenosis. Post-acute care adds antithrombotic therapy, blood-pressure management, and rehab services.

**Do NOT assume a normal exam excludes strangulation injury** — 50% have no visible injury on exam.

**Do NOT delay CTA head and neck** in a patient with loss of consciousness, voice change, dysphagia, or neurologic symptoms during strangulation.

**Do NOT withhold tPA** in a stroke solely because it is dissection-related — standard criteria and the 4.5-hour window apply.

**Do NOT let a single normal CTA reassure you** when clinical suspicion is high — symptoms may be delayed 24-72 hours; consider MRA or repeat imaging.

**Do NOT skip the IPV safety assessment** even in a "low-risk" strangulation presentation — strangulation is a leading predictor of future homicide in IPV.

**Do NOT allow hypotension** in dissection-related stroke — it can worsen ischemia in the setting of arterial stenosis.
