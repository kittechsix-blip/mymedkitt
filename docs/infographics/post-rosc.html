Post-ROSC Care: The First Hours After Cardiac Arrest

Adults ≥18 with sustained return of spontaneous circulation (ROSC) after cardiac arrest, managed in the ED/ICU. Excludes pediatrics, pregnancy, and traumatic arrest — each needs a modified pathway.

⚠ 12-lead ECG immediately. STEMI identification is the #1 priority post-ROSC — STEMI → emergent PCI, do not delay for CT head. TTM can start in the cath lab.[1]
⚠ Secure the airway and confirm targets. Intubate if GCS ≤8, confirm ETT with waveform capnography, target ETCO₂ 35-45, place an arterial line, send labs/CXR.
⚠ Hemodynamic & oxygenation targets: MAP ≥65 mmHg, SpO₂ 94-98% (avoid hyperoxia — PaO₂ >300 is harmful), PaCO₂ 35-45 mmHg (avoid hypocapnia). Titrate FiO₂ DOWN once stable.[2]
⚠ Do NOT prognosticate within 72h post-rewarming. Sedation, hypothermia and paralysis confound the exam — premature withdrawal of care is the leading cause of preventable death after arrest. No single test is sufficient.[8]

3 · Temperature management (TTM)

The pivot: is the patient following commands?
No — comatose (GCS motor ≤3) → prevent fever aggressively. Yes — following commands → standard care, just treat fever.

TTM2 (2021): 33°C vs 37.5°C showed no difference in mortality or neurological outcome.[3] 2023 AHA approach: target normothermia — actively prevent fever >37.8°C × 72h (maintain 32-37.5°C for at least 36h). Cooling to 32-36°C is acceptable but not required.[2]

4 · Seizures & neuroprognostication

⚠ Do NOT prognosticate within 72h post-rewarming
Sedation, hypothermia and neuromuscular blockade confound the exam. Multimodal approach after 72h: absent corneal + pupillary reflexes; EEG burst-suppression / status; bilaterally absent N20 SSEP (most specific); diffuse anoxic injury on CT/MRI; NSE >33 ng/mL at 48-72h. No single test is sufficient.[8]

Post-arrest seizures occur in 20-30% of comatose survivors — put every comatose post-arrest patient on continuous EEG. Clinical seizures may be subtle (eye twitching, nystagmus) or masked by paralysis.[2] Myoclonus ≠ seizure — Lance-Adams (post-hypoxic myoclonus) carries a better prognosis than true status.

First-line treatment is Levetiracetam loading at 20 mg/kg IV (max 3000 mg). Enter weight above for the loading dose:

Refractory: valproate, phenobarbital, or a midazolam / propofol infusion.

5 · ICU bundle & disposition

  • Glucose 140-180 mg/dL — avoid hypoglycemia (<70); treat hyperglycemia with an insulin infusion.
  • Continuous EEG × 24-48h minimum for seizure surveillance.
  • Echo — LV function, wall-motion abnormalities, post-arrest stunning.
  • Ventilator: TV 6 mL/kg IBW, PEEP 5-8, target PaCO₂ 35-45.
  • Labs q4-6h: lactate (trending down = good), troponin, electrolytes, ABG.
  • Foley: strict I/O, target UOP >0.5 mL/kg/hr.
  • DVT prophylaxis: enoxaparin or heparin SQ once hemostasis is confirmed.
Admit to ICU — hand-off checklist
Document code duration, initial rhythm, ETCO₂ during resuscitation, number of shocks, total epi doses, neuro exam at ROSC, and the cooling plan. Communicate: arrest circumstances (witnessed, bystander CPR, initial rhythm), total downtime, interventions + response, current hemodynamics/pressors, TTM plan + temp, and pending workup (cath, EEG, imaging).
