Post-Cardiac Arrest Care: ROSC to ICU

Adults ≥18 with sustained return of spontaneous circulation (ROSC) after cardiac arrest, managed in the ED/ICU. Excludes pediatrics, pregnancy, and traumatic arrest — each needs a modified pathway.

⚠ STEMI + ROSC = emergent PCI. Do not delay for CT head or other workup — activate the cath lab immediately; TTM can be initiated in the cath lab.[7][13]
⚠ Avoid hyperoxia and hypocapnia. Target SpO₂ 94-98% (PaO₂ >300 mmHg is harmful — oxidative injury) and PaCO₂ 35-45 mmHg (hypocapnia causes cerebral vasoconstriction). Titrate FiO₂ DOWN once stable.[1][2]
⚠ Avoid an epinephrine infusion post-ROSC if possible — tachyarrhythmias and increased myocardial oxygen demand; norepinephrine is preferred.[1][5][6]
⚠ Do NOT prognosticate within 72h post-rewarming. Sedation, hypothermia, and neuromuscular blockade confound the exam — premature withdrawal of care is the leading cause of preventable death in post-arrest patients. Multimodal concordance is required; no single test is sufficient.[6][8][9][12]
⚠ TTM2: no mortality/neurologic benefit of cooling to 33°C vs normothermia — comatose patients still require aggressive fever prevention (>37.8°C × 72h, 2023-2025 AHA).[3][2]
⚠ Myoclonus ≠ seizure. Lance-Adams syndrome (post-hypoxic myoclonus) has a better prognosis than true status epilepticus — do not conflate the two.[5][6][8]

1 · Immediate priorities & STEMI

ROSC confirmed. STEMI identification is the #1 priority — obtain a 12-lead ECG immediately.[1][2]

  1. Secure the airway if not already — intubate if GCS ≤8; waveform capnography confirms ETT placement.
  2. Target ETCO₂ 35-45 mmHg to guide ventilation.
  3. 12-lead ECG immediately — STEMI identification is the #1 priority.
  4. Avoid hypoxia (SpO₂ <94%) and hypotension (SBP <90). Arterial line for continuous BP.
  5. Labs: ABG, lactate, troponin, BMP, CBC, coags. Portable CXR (ETT position, pulmonary edema).
STEMI + ROSC → emergent PCI
Do not delay for CT head or other workup. Activate the cath lab immediately — TTM can be initiated in the cath lab. Dual antiplatelet therapy + heparin bolus per ACS/cath-lab protocol. Transfer to a PCI-capable center if unavailable. STEMI management[7][13]
No STEMI — stabilize hemodynamics first
Consider coronary angiography if witnessed arrest with shockable rhythm, high suspicion for cardiac cause, or hemodynamic instability suggesting cardiogenic shock. COACT trial (2019): immediate angiography in OHCA without STEMI did NOT improve survival vs a delayed/selective strategy — timing is less urgent, can wait for clinical stabilization.[7][14]
Indeterminate ECG / new LBBB — cardiology consult
Consider early angiography within 2h if: shockable rhythm arrest, hemodynamically unstable, or high suspicion for ACS. Non-shockable rhythm with stable hemodynamics → angiography can be deferred pending further workup and clinical trajectory.[7][13][14]

3 · Temperature management (TTM)

Comatose — prevent fever aggressively
TTM2 (2021): 33°C vs 37.5°C showed no difference in mortality or neurological outcome.[3] Current approach (2023-2025 AHA): target normothermia — actively prevent fever >37.8°C × 72h (32-37.5°C for at least 36h). Cooling to 32-36°C is acceptable but NOT required. Surface cooling devices, IV cold saline bolus, or intravascular cooling. Monitor core temp continuously — avoid overcooling.[2][4][10]

Following commands — standard care
Following commands = good neurological sign. Monitor temperature and actively treat any fever >37.7°C — no need for aggressive cooling. Continue hemodynamic optimization, work up the arrest cause (echo, labs, serial ECGs), and consult neurology if any concern for seizure activity.[2][6]

7 · ICU bundle & disposition

  • Glucose 140-180 mg/dL — avoid hypoglycemia (<70), treat hyperglycemia with an insulin infusion.
  • Continuous EEG × 24-48h minimum for seizure surveillance.
  • Echo — LV function, wall-motion abnormalities, post-arrest stunning.
  • Ventilator: TV 6 mL/kg IBW, PEEP 5-8, target PaCO₂ 35-45.
  • Labs q4-6h: lactate (trending down = good), troponin, electrolytes, ABG.
  • Foley: strict I/O monitoring, target UOP >0.5 mL/kg/hr.
  • DVT prophylaxis: enoxaparin or heparin SQ once hemostasis confirmed.
Admit to ICU — hand-off checklist
Document code duration, initial rhythm, ETCO₂ during resuscitation, number of shocks, total epi doses, neuro exam at ROSC, and the cooling plan. Communicate: arrest circumstances (witnessed/unwitnessed, bystander CPR, initial rhythm), total downtime, interventions and response, current hemodynamics/pressor support, TTM plan and current temp, and pending workup (cath, EEG, imaging).[1][2][6]
