Hemophilia & von Willebrand Disease: Treat First, Test Second

Adults and children presenting to the ED with known or suspected hemophilia A/B or von Willebrand disease. Excludes routine outpatient prophylaxis dosing, perioperative hematology-managed regimens, and non-hemophilia coagulopathies (e.g., DIC, warfarin/DOAC reversal) beyond the rare-bleeding-disorder triage step.

⚠ Treat FIRST, test later: for any life-threatening or major bleed, give empiric factor replacement or bypassing agents before imaging or definitive diagnosis — do not wait for labs or CT.
⚠ Intracranial hemorrhage: give factor replacement BEFORE imaging — do not delay for CT/MRI. ICH mortality is 20% overall; most patients report only minor trauma and 44% have a normal exam.
⚠ Major trauma: do NOT delay factor replacement for imaging — standard trauma resuscitation takes precedence, then treat as hemophilia with factor to 100%.
⚠ ICH or major trauma WITH inhibitors (or unknown inhibitor status while crashing): factor replacement will NOT work — give rFVIIa or FEIBA bypassing agents. rFVIIa 90 mcg/kg works whether inhibitors are present or not.
⚠ Iliopsoas hemorrhage is a life-threatening bleed that mimics appendicitis — hip flexion contracture in 93% and femoral nerve paresthesia in 57%. Admit with strict bed rest; factor to 80% × 3 days.
⚠ High-titer inhibitor (≥5 BU/mL): factor replacement is ineffective — use bypassing agents. FEIBA carries a hard ceiling of 200 units/kg/day (thrombotic risk).
⚠ Emicizumab (Hemlibra) + FEIBA carries an FDA black-box warning for thrombotic microangiopathy — use rFVIIa preferentially in patients on emicizumab prophylaxis; if FEIBA must be used, limit to <50 units/kg/dose and <100 units/kg/day.
⚠ Antifibrinolytics (aminocaproic acid, tranexamic acid) are CONTRAINDICATED with hematuria — risk of renal/ureteral clot obstruction. Check urinalysis before starting any antifibrinolytic.

1 · Overview & severity

Hemophilia A (factor VIII deficiency) and hemophilia B (factor IX deficiency) are X-linked bleeding disorders. Combined incidence: 1 per 5000 male births.[1] Hemophilia A is 4× more common than B. 30% of hemophilia cases are de novo mutations with no family history.[1]

von Willebrand disease (vWD) is the most common inherited bleeding disorder, affecting up to 1% of the population but only ~10% are symptomatic. Unlike hemophilia, vWD affects both sexes equally.[3]

Golden rule: treat FIRST, test later — give factor replacement or bypassing agents empirically before imaging or definitive diagnosis in any life-threatening or major bleed.

4 · Factor replacement principles

Factor VIII: Dose (units) = (Target% − Baseline%) ÷ 2 × Weight (kg). 1 unit/kg raises factor VIII by ~2%.[2] Half-life 8–12 hours — redose q8–12h.

Factor IX: Dose (units) = (Target% − Baseline%) × Weight (kg). 1 unit/kg raises factor IX by ~1%.[2] Half-life 18–24 hours — redose q18–24h. Note factor IX requires twice the dose per kg as factor VIII for the same percentage rise.

Round dose UP to the nearest whole vial. Check peak factor levels 30–60 min post-infusion for major/life-threatening bleeds — little increase suggests an inhibitor. Recombinant and plasma-derived products are both highly effective (≥90% hemostasis after 1–2 doses); recombinant is preferred. Switching brands in an emergency is acceptable — no clear evidence of increased inhibitor risk.

6 · von Willebrand disease

vWF functions: (1) platelet adhesion to injured vessel wall (GPIb/IX binding), (2) platelet aggregation (GPIIb/IIIa), (3) stabilizes factor VIII in circulation.

Types: Type 1 (80%) — quantitative deficiency, vWF:Ag <30–40%. Type 2A (10%) — can't form HMW multimers. Type 2B — gain-of-function, thrombocytopenia. Type 2M — structural defect. Type 2N — can't bind factor VIII (mimics hemophilia A). Type 3 (<1 in 1M) — undetectable vWF, severe bleeding like hemophilia.

Caution: vWF is an acute-phase reactant — false negatives are common during acute illness; repeat testing outpatient if suspicion remains high.

8 · Do NOT

Do NOT delay factor/bypassing-agent treatment for imaging or diagnosis
In ICH, major trauma, or any life-threatening bleed, give empiric treatment first — imaging and confirmatory labs come after.
Do NOT aspirate a hemarthrotic joint
Arthrocentesis is not recommended for hemophilic joint bleeds — treat with factor replacement instead.
Do NOT give antifibrinolytics with hematuria
Aminocaproic acid and tranexamic acid are contraindicated with hematuria — risk of renal/ureteral clot obstruction. Always check urinalysis first.
Do NOT exceed FEIBA 200 units/kg/day
Thrombotic risk rises sharply above this cumulative daily ceiling — track total units given across all doses in 24h.
Do NOT combine FEIBA with emicizumab unless unavoidable
FDA black-box warning for thrombotic microangiopathy. Use rFVIIa preferentially; if FEIBA is unavoidable, limit to <50 units/kg/dose and <100 units/kg/day.
Do NOT give desmopressin in Type 2B vWD or without a documented prior response
Type 2B: can worsen thrombocytopenia. Any patient: reliance on DDAVP without a documented challenge-test response is unsafe — use factor/vWF concentrate instead.
Do NOT rule out hemophilia for lack of family history
30% of hemophilia cases are de novo mutations with no family history — maintain suspicion in neonates and infants with unusual bleeding.

4 · Factor replacement principles

Factor VIII: Dose (units) = (Target% − Baseline%) ÷ 2 × Weight (kg). 1 unit/kg raises factor VIII by ~2%.[2] Half-life 8–12 hours — redose q8–12h.

Factor IX: Dose (units) = (Target% − Baseline%) × Weight (kg). 1 unit/kg raises factor IX by ~1%.[2] Half-life 18–24 hours — redose q18–24h. Note factor IX requires twice the dose per kg as factor VIII for the same percentage rise.

Round dose UP to the nearest whole vial. Check peak factor levels 30–60 min post-infusion for major/life-threatening bleeds — little increase suggests an inhibitor. Recombinant and plasma-derived products are both highly effective (≥90% hemostasis after 1–2 doses); recombinant is preferred. Switching brands in an emergency is acceptable — no clear evidence of increased inhibitor risk.

Situation | Option

Factor VIII — recombinant | Advate, Kogenate, Xyntha, Eloctate (extended half-life), Jivi, Adynovate

Factor VIII — plasma-derived | Humate-P (also contains vWF), Koate-DVI

Factor IX — recombinant | BeneFIX, Alprolix (extended half-life), Rixubis, Rebinyn, Idelvion

Factor IX — plasma-derived | AlphaNine SD, Mononine

No factor concentrate available (Hemophilia A) | 1 bag per 6 kg body weight (max 10 bags)

No factor concentrate available (Hemophilia B) | Fresh frozen plasma (FFP) — 15 mL/kg initial dose

Bispecific antibody used for hemophilia A prophylaxis only. A patient on emicizumab with an acute bleed still needs factor VIII or bypassing agents — emicizumab itself does nothing acutely.

5 · Special populations

Inhibitors occur in ~33% of patients with hemophilia A and ~6.5% of patients with hemophilia B. Inhibitor titers are not available acutely in the ED — if suspected, check post-infusion factor levels at 30–60 min (little rise suggests inhibitor).

Inhibitor titer management (low-titer vs high-titer)

Low-titer, low-responding inhibitor (<5 BU/mL) can sometimes still be treated with higher-dose factor. If the factor level does not rise adequately, reclassify as high-titer and switch to bypassing agents. Hematology consult mandatory.

Low-titer inhibitor loading dose: (computed)

Factor replacement will NOT work. FEIBA and rFVIIa are equally efficacious (FENOC trial). rFVIIa preferred for factor IX inhibitors (allergic reaction risk to FIX in FEIBA). Administer within 1 hour of bleeding onset when possible.

FEIBA — joint bleed: 50–75 units/kg IV — Repeat q12h as needed. Max 200 units/kg/day.

Recombinant Factor VIIa: 90 mcg/kg q2–3h × 3 doses OR 270 mcg/kg single dose — Preferred for factor IX inhibitors.

Refractory bleeds (10–20% of cases): sequential alternating FEIBA and rFVIIa q6h — reserve for life/limb-threatening bleeds with hematology guidance.

Desmopressin (DDAVP) — mild hemophilia A: 0.3 mcg/kg IV (dilute in 50 mL NS, infuse 20–30 min) OR intranasal 150 mcg (<50 kg) / 300 mcg (≥50 kg) — Use HIGH-CONCENTRATION spray (Stimate 150 mcg/spray). Raises factor VIII 2–6×. Maximum 2–3 doses. Requires prior documented DDAVP response. NOT effective for hemophilia B. Restrict fluids to maintenance × 24h.

Neonatal hemophilia: 30% of hemophilia cases are de novo mutations with no family history — maintain high suspicion for ICH in neonates with unusual bleeding. Circumcision bleeding: 50% of boys with severe hemophilia A have an uncomplicated circumcision, but significant bleeding should prompt screening. Presentation: hematomas (17–40% of affected infants), large soft-tissue bleeds with minimal trauma, slow-resolving bruising. Workup: CBC, PT/PTT, factor VIII and IX levels; consult hematology.

6 · von Willebrand disease

vWF functions: (1) platelet adhesion to injured vessel wall (GPIb/IX binding), (2) platelet aggregation (GPIIb/IIIa), (3) stabilizes factor VIII in circulation.

Types: Type 1 (80%) — quantitative deficiency, vWF:Ag <30–40%. Type 2A (10%) — can't form HMW multimers. Type 2B — gain-of-function, thrombocytopenia. Type 2M — structural defect. Type 2N — can't bind factor VIII (mimics hemophilia A). Type 3 (<1 in 1M) — undetectable vWF, severe bleeding like hemophilia.

Caution: vWF is an acute-phase reactant — false negatives are common during acute illness; repeat testing outpatient if suspicion remains high.

Bleed severity | First-line | Alternative

Minor — documented DDAVP responder | Desmopressin 0.3 mcg/kg IV | vWF concentrate if inadequate response

Minor — non-responder / Type 2B or 3 | vWF concentrate 30–60 RCo units/kg loading | Maintain vWF:RCo + FVIII >50 IU/dL × 3–5 days

Major bleed or surgery | vWF concentrate 40–60 RCo units/kg loading | Maintain >50 IU/dL × at least 7 days; admit

Menorrhagia | Hormonal contraceptives (OCPs) | Add DDAVP and/or antifibrinolytics as adjuncts

Desmopressin (DDAVP) — vWD: 0.3 mcg/kg IV (dilute in 50 mL NS, infuse 20–30 min) OR intranasal 150/300 mcg — Documented prior response REQUIRED. Max 2–3 doses. CONTRAINDICATED in Type 2B (worsens thrombocytopenia).

vWF/Factor VIII Concentrate (Humate-P) — minor bleed: Loading 30–60 RCo units/kg IV — Maintenance 20–40 RCo units/kg q12–48h. Maintain vWF:RCo + FVIII >50 IU/dL × 3–5 days.

vWF/Factor VIII Concentrate (Humate-P) — major bleed/surgery: Loading 40–60 RCo units/kg IV — Maintenance 20–40 RCo units/kg q8–24h. Maintain >50 IU/dL × at least 7 days. Admit — watch for excessive factor VIII accumulation (thrombotic risk).

Desmopressin can worsen thrombocytopenia in Type 2B vWD by releasing abnormal vWF — use vWF concentrate instead.

Menorrhagia (Bleeding >7 days OR >80 mL blood loss per cycle) may be the first or only sign of vWD in adolescent females. First-line: hormonal contraceptives. Adjuncts: desmopressin (if documented responder), or aminocaproic acid/tranexamic acid (same dosing and hematuria contraindication as mucosal bleeding, below). Consider screening family members given autosomal inheritance.

Aminocaproic Acid — menorrhagia: 50–60 mg/kg PO q6h — Max 5 g/dose × 5–7 days during menses. Check urinalysis first — contraindicated with hematuria.

Tranexamic Acid — menorrhagia: 10–15 mg/kg PO q8–12h — 5–7 days during menses. Same hematuria contraindication.

7 · Disposition

Disposition | Criteria

Discharge | Hemarthrosis (post-factor), minor mucosal bleeding (controlled), hematuria (responding to hydration) — bleeding controlled, reliable family, clear hematologist contact, follow-up 24–48h, return precautions given

Admit | ICH (ICU + neurosurgery), iliopsoas (floor, strict bed rest), GI hemorrhage (floor + GI consult), major trauma (per trauma team), muscle bleed with compartment-syndrome concern, inhibitor patients with significant bleeding, unreliable follow-up, or new diagnosis needing workup

Families with no prior experience managing significant bleeds may need inpatient monitoring even for hemarthrosis. Hematology consult for ALL admitted patients; check factor levels q12–24h for severe bleeds.

8 · Do NOT

In ICH, major trauma, or any life-threatening bleed, give empiric treatment first — imaging and confirmatory labs come after.

Arthrocentesis is not recommended for hemophilic joint bleeds — treat with factor replacement instead.

Aminocaproic acid and tranexamic acid are contraindicated with hematuria — risk of renal/ureteral clot obstruction. Always check urinalysis first.

Thrombotic risk rises sharply above this cumulative daily ceiling — track total units given across all doses in 24h.

FDA black-box warning for thrombotic microangiopathy. Use rFVIIa preferentially; if FEIBA is unavoidable, limit to <50 units/kg/dose and <100 units/kg/day.

Type 2B: can worsen thrombocytopenia. Any patient: reliance on DDAVP without a documented challenge-test response is unsafe — use factor/vWF concentrate instead.

30% of hemophilia cases are de novo mutations with no family history — maintain suspicion in neonates and infants with unusual bleeding.
