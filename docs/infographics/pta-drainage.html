Diagnosis

Peritonsillar abscess (PTA) is a collection of pus between the tonsillar capsule and pharyngeal muscles. Symptoms typically develop over 4+ days before an abscess forms.

  • Severe sore throat (odynophagia)
  • "Hot potato" voice (muffled, wet)
  • Trismus (difficulty opening mouth) — 30-60%
  • Uvular deviation (pushed to opposite side)
  • Unilateral tonsillar swelling and exudate
  • Fever
  • Drooling
Abscess vs cellulitis
Cellulitis: no drainable pus, antibiotics alone. Abscess: requires drainage + antibiotics.
Exam componentFindings
Inspect oropharynxAsymmetric tonsillar swelling, soft palate bulging, uvula deviated AWAY from affected side, tonsillar exudate
Assess trismusLimited mouth opening (<3 cm) — may limit examination and procedure access
Palpate (if tolerated)Fluctuance over anterior tonsillar pillar, tenderness
Exam alone is not reliable
Clinical exam alone has sensitivity ~75%, specificity ~50%. Ultrasound improves diagnosis significantly.
  1. Dexamethasone 10 mg IV/IM first — reduces edema and trismus within 30-60 min
  2. Topical anesthesia spray (benzocaine 20% or lidocaine 10%) — reduces reflex guarding
  3. Analgesics: morphine 0.1 mg/kg IV or hydromorphone 1-2 mg IV — reduces pain-induced guarding
  4. Procedural sedation (propofol or ketamine) if still inadequate — reserve for uncooperative patients or children

▶ What to do now

Severe trismus limiting exam
Give dexamethasone 10 mg IV/IM first, then topical anesthesia, analgesics, and procedural sedation if still uncooperative — before proceeding to ultrasound.
Cellulitis — no drainage
No drainable abscess identified. Treat with antibiotics + dexamethasone; observe 4-6 hours; no procedure indicated.
Equivocal ultrasound
Diagnostic needle aspiration is reasonable even with an equivocal ultrasound. Consider CT if deep-space extension is a concern.
Needle aspiration — depth limit
Maximum 8-10 mm insertion depth, targeting the superior pole (70% of abscesses). Carotid artery is 2.5-4 cm posterior/lateral — use Color Doppler first.
Incision & drainage
Incise the anterior tonsillar pillar 1.0-1.5 cm, depth 5-8 mm, then blunt-dissect with a hemostat. Stay anterior — avoid the lateral (carotid) direction. Do NOT suture.
Discharge
Antibiotics 10-14 days + dexamethasone if not already given. ENT follow-up within 24-36 hours mandatory.
Admission
IV antibiotics, ENT consult, airway assessment Q4h. CT if concern for deep-space spread or no improvement in 24h.

Point-of-care ultrasound

91%Intraoral US sensitivity for PTA

Intraoral POCUS is gold standard for ED diagnosis — confirms abscess vs cellulitis, measures size/depth, identifies the carotid artery, and guides real-time aspiration. CT instead if: deep space infection concern, airway compromise, failed ultrasound visualization, or atypical presentation.

  1. Endocavitary probe (preferred) with sterile sheath, or linear hockey-stick probe
  2. Patient upright, head extended; topical anesthetic spray, wait 1-2 minutes
  3. Insert sheathed probe intraorally, scan superior pole (70%), mid-tonsil, inferior pole
  4. Look for hypoechoic/anechoic collection = pus
  5. Use Color Doppler to identify carotid artery — typically 2.5-4 cm deep to mucosal surface
  6. Document abscess size, depth from mucosal surface, and distance to carotid
FindingUltrasound appearanceDisposition
Abscess (drainable)Hypoechoic/anechoic fluid collection, well-defined borders, may have debris/loculationsProceed to drainage
Cellulitis (no drainage)Diffuse edema, hyperechoic "dirty" appearance, no anechoic pocketAntibiotics only

Needle aspirationFirst-line for most patientsLess painful, faster; success rate 85-95%; can be US-guided

  • CT scan with contrast if more definitive diagnosis needed, especially for deep space extension concern
  • Trial of antibiotics + steroids with close follow-up if low clinical suspicion

Peritonsillar cellulitis (no drainage)

  • IV antibiotics if unable to tolerate PO
  • Dexamethasone 10 mg IV/IM single dose if not already given
  • Observation 4-6 hours for improvement
  • IV fluids if dehydrated
  • Consider admission if unable to tolerate PO, severe dehydration, immunocompromised, or no improvement after 4-6h
  • ENT/PCP follow-up within 24-48 hours; repeat ultrasound if worsening

Needle aspiration

  • 18-gauge needle on 10 mL syringe, with needle guard or depth limiter (CRITICAL)
  • Mark syringe at 10 mm from needle tip; maximum insertion depth 8-10 mm
  • Headlamp, Yankauer suction, topical anesthetic (benzocaine 20% or lidocaine 10%)
  • Patient upright, seated, head extended slightly backward, mouth open maximally, basin to spit
  • Topical spray (always): benzocaine 20% or lidocaine 10%, cover tonsil/soft palate/base of tongue, wait 1-2 minutes
  • Local infiltration (optional): lidocaine 1% with epinephrine, 27g needle, 6-10 mL total, aspirate before each injection
  • Dexamethasone 10 mg IV/IM given before or during procedure
Needle depth limit — 8-10 mm
Carotid artery is 2.5-4 cm posterior/lateral to the tonsil. Target the superior pole (70% of abscesses) at a 45° angle, maximum 2-3 passes. Ultrasound-guided is preferred — maintain needle tip visualization throughout.

  • Withdraw needle slowly while observing for bleeding; patient spits, not swallows
  • Ice water gargles x2-3 minutes; observe 30-45 minutes

Incision & drainage

  • Large abscess (>2 cm diameter)
  • Recurrent abscess or prior failed aspiration
  • Multiple loculations suspected
  • Uncooperative patient (with sedation)
  • Patient or provider preference
  1. Incision on anterior tonsillar pillar (mucosa only), curvilinear or vertical, from base of uvula toward last mandibular molar, length 1.0-1.5 cm
  2. Stay on ANTERIOR pillar — avoid lateral (carotid) direction
  3. Depth: superficial through mucosa only, limit to 5-8 mm, scalpel angle 45° directed medially
  4. Blunt dissection: insert closed hemostat, spread gently in 2-3 directions to open cavity and break loculations
  5. Evacuate pus with suction
  6. Do NOT suture — leave open for continued drainage (heals in 3-5 days)
  • Suction remaining pus/blood; patient spits, not swallows
  • Ice water gargles x2-3 minutes; observe 30-45 minutes
  • If brisk bleeding: direct pressure with gauze ± topical epinephrine (1:1000); usually resolves in 10-15 min
  • Persistent bleeding → ENT consultation
