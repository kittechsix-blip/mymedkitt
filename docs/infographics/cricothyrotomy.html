Cricothyrotomy: CICO Decision & Scalpel-Finger-Bougie Technique

Adults with a failed airway (cannot intubate, cannot oxygenate) in the ED or resuscitation bay, or clinicians reviewing/rehearsing the surgical airway technique. Excludes children <10-12 years (use needle cricothyrotomy + transtracheal jet ventilation) and laryngeal fracture/transected airway (needs formal tracheostomy).

Heavy bleeding is expected in up to 50% of cases — IGNORE it during the procedure and control it AFTER the airway is secured; this is a tactile, not visual, procedure.

1 · CICO Decision & Indications

CICO = Can't Intubate, Can't Oxygenate — a FAILED AIRWAY: the patient cannot be intubated AND cannot be oxygenated by any other means. Incidence: 0.06-0.72% prehospital, 0.14-1.4% ED. If CICO, perform a surgical airway now — do not delay.

  • Failed intubation with declining SpO2
  • Failed supraglottic airway (LMA/iGel) rescue
  • Massive facial trauma precluding oral/nasal approach
  • Complete upper airway obstruction (angioedema, tumor, foreign body)
  • Severe trismus/restricted mouth opening
Contraindications
Absolute: Age <10-12 years (CTM too small — use needle cricothyrotomy + transtracheal jet ventilation) and laryngeal fracture/transected airway (needs formal tracheostomy). Relative (proceed with caution): coagulopathy, anterior neck mass, previous neck surgery/radiation, morbid obesity. In a true CICO emergency, the contraindication matters less than hypoxic death.

3 · Anatomy — Locate the Cricothyroid Membrane

The cricothyroid membrane (CTM) sits between the thyroid cartilage (above) and cricoid cartilage (below), average 9 mm height × 30 mm width — just large enough for a 6.0 ETT. No major vessels lie directly over the CTM. Key structures to avoid: superior thyroid artery/vein (superior border of thyroid cartilage), anterior jugular veins (lateral to midline), cricothyroid artery (variable, crosses CTM superiorly).

  1. Stand at patient's side
  2. Grasp larynx with non-dominant hand (thumb + middle finger on each side)
  3. Stabilize between fingers
  4. Use index finger to palpate DOWN from thyroid notch — go from notch DOWN, not chin down (EMCrit method validated superior in an RCT)
Ignore the bleeding
Heavy bleeding is expected and should be ignored while placing the airway. This is a tactile procedure — rely on palpation, not visualization.

4 · Preparation — Equipment & Positioning

  • #10 or #20 blade scalpel — large blade for horizontal incision
  • Bougie (gum elastic bougie) — guides ETT placement
  • 6.0 cuffed ETT — outer diameter 8.2 mm fits through the CTM; have 5.5 as backup for small adults/females
Only 3 items needed
Scalpel, finger, bougie, tube. Also have ready: 10 mL syringe, BVM, ETCO2 detector, suction, tape/tie, gauze. Optional but helpful: tracheal hook (elevates the incision edge), Trousseau dilator. Don't wait for a cric kit if not immediately available.

Standard: supine with neck extended (unless C-spine precautions), shoulder roll, operator standing at patient's side (right-handed: patient's right side; left-handed: patient's left side). C-spine precautions: maintain neutral alignment, may need manual in-line stabilization. Non-dominant hand stabilizes the larynx throughout the procedure. Obese patients may need a "ramped" position (head elevated) and a vertical incision extended further to expose more tissue. Identify the CTM with the laryngeal handshake and mark it with a pen or fingernail before the patient decompensates, if time permits. Ultrasound can localize the CTM when palpation is difficult (obese, edema, distorted anatomy) — DAS 2025 explicitly endorses this.

5 · Technique — Scalpel-Finger-Bougie

Step 1: Vertical skin incision
Stabilize the larynx with the non-dominant hand throughout. Make a 4 cm VERTICAL incision from above the thyroid notch to below the cricoid — vertical is more forgiving than horizontal if landmarks are off, easier to extend if needed, and less likely to injure lateral vessels (you'll make a horizontal incision through the CTM itself). Bleeding is reported in up to 50% of cases — expect it, ignore it; control after the airway is secured.

Bluntly dissect through subcutaneous tissue with a finger until the CTM is palpated — it feels like a soft "trampoline" between the firm thyroid cartilage above and cricoid below, usually only 1-3 cm deep. Common error: cutting too superficially and not reaching the CTM — if you don't feel the membrane, deepen the incision. Maintain laryngeal stabilization throughout; do not release the grip on the larynx.

Step 2: Horizontal CTM incision
With the index finger on the CTM, make a HORIZONTAL stab incision through the full width of the membrane (~2-3 cm), staying in the lower half to avoid the superior thyroid vessels. Feel a "pop" on entry. Rotate the scalpel 90° so the blade faces caudally to keep the incision open, then REMOVE the scalpel — do not leave it in place. Keep a finger in the incision to maintain the tract.

Slide the bougie alongside the finger through the CTM incision, directed caudally, advancing approximately 10-15 cm. A "clicking" sensation over tracheal cartilage rings confirms intratracheal placement. The bougie should meet resistance (hold-up sign) at 15-20 cm (carina/mainstem bronchus), confirming tracheal placement; if the bougie advances freely beyond >25 cm, suspect esophageal placement. Do not force against resistance — if resistance is met early, withdraw and redirect caudally. Once the bougie is in place, keep it steady — do NOT let it advance further or pull it out — and remove the finger from the incision.

Thread the 6.0 cuffed ETT over the bougie, advancing only until the cuff is just past the skin incision — final depth typically 2-4 cm past skin. Do NOT advance further: right mainstem intubation occurs in up to 46% of cricothyrotomies because the ETT is longer than a tracheostomy tube. Remove the bougie while holding the ETT steady, inflate the cuff with 5-10 mL air, attach BVM, and confirm placement immediately.

6 · Confirmation & Post-Procedure Care

Gold standard: waveform capnography (ETCO2) — a sustained waveform confirms tracheal placement (color-change detectors are less reliable in cardiac arrest). Clinical confirmation: bilateral breath sounds, chest rise, condensation in the tube, SpO2 improvement (may be delayed). If NO ETCO2 or chest rise: deflate the cuff, withdraw slightly, and reassess — the tube may be in a false tract or the esophagus; confirm the bougie was actually intratracheal (clicking, hold-up) before re-attempting. Secure the tube, document depth at skin, and obtain a post-procedure chest X-ray to confirm position and rule out pneumothorax/pneumomediastinum.

  1. Tape or suture ETT to skin; mark and document depth at skin level
  2. Control bleeding with direct pressure now that the airway is secure — rarely needs more
  3. Start mechanical ventilation; lower tidal volumes may be needed (smaller tube = higher resistance), monitor airway pressures
  4. Consult ENT or trauma surgery for tracheostomy conversion within 24-72 hours — cricothyrotomy is a bridge, not a definitive airway
  5. ICU admission for close airway monitoring

8 · Alternative Techniques (reference — not wired into the primary CICO pathway)

Needle cricothyrotomy, rapid 4-step, commercial kits3 alternatives
Needle cricothyrotomy + TTJV — pediatrics <10-12 years
14-16G catheter through the CTM, attached to a jet ventilator (50 psi). Cycle: 1 second inspiration, 4 seconds expiration. Temporizing only — does not provide adequate ventilation long-term. Risk: barotrauma, subcutaneous emphysema.

Rapid 4-step technique: similar to scalpel-finger-bougie but streamlined — horizontal incision only (no initial vertical step). Used by some for a faster approach.

Commercial cricothyrotomy kits (Melker, Portex, Cook): Seldinger (wire-guided) technique, may be slower than scalpel-finger-bougie — familiarity with the specific kit is critical. Percutaneous tracheostomy is NOT for emergency CICO — elective ICU procedure requiring bronchoscopy. Recommendation: master the scalpel-finger-bougie technique — minimal equipment, validated across emergency settings.
