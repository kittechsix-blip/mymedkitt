Facial Peripheral Nerve Block — ED Procedure

ED clinicians performing bedside regional anesthesia of the face for laceration repair, abscess I&D, foreign-body removal, and burn/wound care. Covers the ED-relevant trigeminal terminal branches (V1 supraorbital/supratrochlear, V1 infratrochlear, V2 infraorbital, V1/V2 external nasal, V3 mental, V3 inferior alveolar, V3 auriculotemporal) and the external ear ring block. Excludes patients with overlying skin infection/cellulitis at the planned injection site or a known true allergy to the local anesthetic class.

⚠ Absolute contraindication: overlying skin infection/cellulitis at the injection site — do not inject through infected tissue.
⚠ Absolute contraindication: known true allergy to the local anesthetic class.
⚠ Calculate the maximum weight-based LA dose BEFORE drawing up — verify whenever combining a block with wound infiltration.
⚠ ASPIRATE before every injection — the face is densely vascular (angular, infraorbital, superficial temporal, inferior alveolar vessels).
⚠ Deposit anesthetic ADJACENT to the foramen, NEVER into it — intraneural injection causes persistent paresthesia.
⚠ The external ear ring block does NOT cover the concha or external auditory canal (auricular branch of vagus/Arnold) — supplement with separate local infiltration.
⚠ Suspected local anesthetic systemic toxicity (LAST) → stop injecting, O2, treat seizures, 20% lipid emulsion 1.5 mL/kg bolus.

Why regional block for the face

Regional anesthesia of the face numbs a whole sensory territory from ONE distant injection — superior to local infiltration because it (1) avoids distorting the wound margin, critical for cosmetic approximation of the vermilion border, (2) needs far less anesthetic volume, and (3) is less painful than multiple infiltrations. Use for laceration repair, abscess I&D, foreign-body removal, and burn care of the face.

Contraindications + pre-procedure checks
Absolute: overlying skin infection/cellulitis at the injection site; known true allergy to the LA class; patient refusal/inability to cooperate.

Relative: therapeutic anticoagulation (superficial blocks — low bleeding risk, apply pressure); distorted anatomy from prior trauma/surgery at the foramen; grossly contaminated wound better served by infiltration after irrigation.

Confirm before any block: informed consent (transient numbness, failed/partial block, vascular injection, hematoma, nerve irritation); allergies (LA, latex, antiseptic); anticoagulation status; calculate the maximum safe LA dose BEFORE drawing up; aspirate before every injection.
  1. Prep skin with chlorhexidine or alcohol; let dry.
  2. Warn the patient of a brief sting.
  3. Insert needle, ADVANCE toward the target, ASPIRATE.
  4. Inject SLOWLY — never directly INTO a foramen; deposit anesthetic adjacent to it.
  5. Wait 5–10 min and TEST the territory before starting the procedure.

Epinephrine in facial blocks
Epinephrine-containing LA is acceptable for most facial blocks — prolongs duration and reduces bleeding. AVOID near end-arteries (nasal tip in compromised circulation); the classic "no epi in the nose/ear" dogma is largely refuted, but use judgment in vasculopaths.

Individual facial blocks

Supraorbital + Supratrochlear Block (V1). Indication: forehead and anterior scalp lacerations/procedures. Distribution: ipsilateral forehead + anterior scalp. Landmarks: supraorbital foramen/notch on the superior orbital rim at the mid-pupillary line; supratrochlear nerve exits ~1–1.5 cm medial, near the superomedial orbital angle. Technique: raise a 1–2 mL wheal over (not into) the notch, then redirect medially along the rim for the supratrochlear branch. Apply pressure to limit eyelid ecchymosis. Pitfall: injecting too inferiorly causes upper-eyelid swelling — stay on the rim.

~2–3 mL total (1–2 mL over foramen + ~1 mL medially for supratrochlear)
Perilesional, adjacent to supraorbital foramen at mid-pupillary line

Infratrochlear Block (V1). Indication: medial canthal region, bridge/upper-lateral nose, medial lower eyelid. Distribution: medial canthus, root/bridge of nose, medial upper eyelid, lacrimal sac region. Landmark: nerve exits at the superomedial orbital angle, junction of orbital roof and medial wall, just above the medial canthal tendon. Technique: insert at the superomedial rim above the medial canthus, keep needle superficial, aim AWAY from the globe. Pitfall: medial orbital structures and angular vessels — aspirate, inject slowly, apply pressure to avoid hematoma.

Infraorbital Block (V2) — the workhorse facial block. Indication: lower eyelid, medial cheek, lateral nose, and UPPER LIP (preserves the vermilion border). Distribution: ipsilateral lower eyelid, medial cheek, lateral nose, upper lip, and via anterior superior alveolar branches the maxillary incisors/canine. Landmark: infraorbital foramen ~1 cm below the infraorbital rim, mid-pupillary line. Preferred intraoral approach: retract the upper lip, insert in the gingivobuccal sulcus above the 2nd premolar/canine, advance ~1.5–2 cm toward a palpating fingertip over the foramen, inject adjacent to (not into) it. Extraoral: insert ~1 cm below the rim at the mid-pupillary line. Pitfall: never force the needle into the foramen; keep the finger over the foramen during the intraoral approach to protect the eye.

External Nasal / Nasal Tip Block (V1 + V2). Indication: nasal tip, ala, and columella lacerations. Distribution: tip/dorsum via the external nasal branch of the anterior ethmoidal nerve (V1); ala/lateral nose via the infraorbital nerve (V2) — full tip anesthesia often needs BOTH. Landmark: external nasal branch emerges at the junction of the nasal bone and upper lateral cartilage (bony–cartilaginous dorsum). Technique: raise a small wheal at this junction bilaterally; add a bilateral infraorbital block for the ala/lateral nose. Pitfall: the nasal tip has limited collateral flow — keep volumes small; aspirate; the old "never epinephrine on the nose" rule is no longer absolute but stay conservative in vasculopaths.

Landmark: mental foramen below the 2nd mandibular premolar, mid-pupillary line (migrates superiorly in edentulous patients). Inferior Alveolar Nerve Block (V3). Indication: mandibular dental pain/procedures and extensive lower-lip/chin wounds needing a more proximal block than the mental nerve. Distribution: all ipsilateral mandibular teeth, mandibular body, ipsilateral lower lip/chin (via mental branch), and anterior 2/3 tongue + floor of mouth (lingual nerve co-block common). Landmark: mandibular foramen on the medial ramus; entry at the coronoid notch/pterygomandibular raphe, ~1 cm above the occlusal plane. Technique: palpate the coronoid notch, approach from the contralateral premolars, advance ~2–2.5 cm to gently contact bone, withdraw 1 mm, aspirate (inferior alveolar artery/vein run with the nerve). Expect lower-lip numbness as the sign of success. Pitfall: highly vascular — this is a deeper, more technical block; a mental block usually suffices for skin wounds if unfamiliar.

Intraoral, mandibular foramen via coronoid notch, contact bone then withdraw 1 mm

Auriculotemporal Nerve Block (V3). Indication: temple, tragus, anterior/superior auricle, skin anterior to the ear. Distribution: temple skin, anterior/superior external ear (tragus, anterior helix), anterior external auditory canal, TMJ region. Landmark: nerve runs with the superficial temporal artery just anterior to the tragus, crossing the zygomatic root. Technique: palpate the superficial temporal pulse anterior to the tragus, insert just anterior and superior to the tragus, posterior to the artery, and inject a subcutaneous fan. Pitfall: the superficial temporal artery is immediately adjacent — ALWAYS aspirate; intravascular injection risk is real here.

Lidocaine or bupivacaine
2–3 mL subcutaneous fan
Anterior and superior to tragus, posterior to superficial temporal artery

External Ear Ring Block. Indication: lacerations, hematoma I&D (auricular hematoma/cauliflower ear), and procedures of the auricle — anesthetizes the whole auricle except the concha/EAC. Distribution: quadruple supply — great auricular nerve (most of posterior/inferior auricle), auriculotemporal nerve (anterior/superior auricle), lesser occipital (small superoposterior area), auricular branch of vagus/Arnold (concha + EAC — NOT covered, supplement with local infiltration). Technique: insert at the superior ear attachment, inject anteriorly along the pre-tragal sulcus while withdrawing, redirect posteriorly along the posterior auricular sulcus, then repeat from the inferior attachment (lobule) anteriorly and posteriorly, forming a complete ring. Aspirate at each pass. Pitfall: classic teaching avoided epinephrine in the ear — current evidence shows it is generally safe, but stay conservative in vasculopaths and avoid encircling pressure that could compromise the auricle.

Test the block, rescue if partial

Test before proceeding
Wait 5–10 minutes, then test pinprick/sharp sensation across the intended territory and compare to the contralateral side. Fully numb → proceed with the repair. Partial/patchy or no effect after 10 min → supplement or re-block.
Rescue a partial / failed block
Causes of failure: wrong foramen level (recheck mid-pupillary line alignment), anatomic variation, insufficient wait time (bupivacaine can take 8–10 min), too little volume.

Rescue: re-confirm the landmark and re-inject 1–2 mL adjacent to the foramen (aspirate first); add targeted local infiltration at the still-sensate wound edge, small volume, slowly, from within the wound margin; ALWAYS recheck the running total against the maximum LA dose before adding more; if still failing, proceed with full local infiltration or reassess the plan.

Complications, LAST, and documentation

Common/minor: local hematoma or ecchymosis (periorbital blocks, auriculotemporal near the temporal artery) — apply pressure; transient injection pain; failed/partial block; prolonged numbness beyond expected duration (self-limited).

Uncommon but important: intravascular injection (dense facial vasculature — aspirate before EVERY injection); nerve injury/persistent paresthesia (from injecting into a foramen — deposit anesthetic adjacent, never inside, and stop if the patient reports an electric shock); globe injury (periorbital blocks only — keep the needle on the orbital rim, direct away from the globe); infection (rare with antiseptic prep — never inject through infected skin).

Local anesthetic systemic toxicity (LAST)
Feared endpoint of intravascular injection: perioral numbness, tinnitus, metallic taste → seizures, cardiac toxicity. If suspected: stop injecting, give O2, manage the airway, treat seizures (benzodiazepine), and give 20% lipid emulsion 1.5 mL/kg bolus then infusion. Have it available for high-volume blocks (e.g., ear ring block).

Critical actions (all blocks)

No overlying infection at the injection site and no true LA allergy before any block. Calculate the maximum weight-based LA dose before drawing up — especially for high-volume ear ring blocks or when combining with infiltration. ASPIRATE before every injection — the face is densely vascular. Deposit anesthetic ADJACENT to the foramen, never INTO it. For periorbital blocks (supraorbital, infratrochlear), keep the needle on the orbital rim and direct away from the globe. Wait 5–10 min and TEST the territory before starting the procedure. Suspected LAST → stop, O2, treat seizures, give 20% lipid emulsion 1.5 mL/kg.

Procedure map

Facial Nerve Block — Entry — Regional anesthesia numbs a whole sensory territory from one injection — no wound-margin distortion, less volume, less pain than infiltration.

Contraindications + pre-procedure checks — No overlying infection, no true LA allergy, verify consent/allergies/anticoagulation, calculate max LA dose, aspirate before every injection.

Shared technique — all blocks — 25–27 ga needle; lidocaine (fast) or bupivacaine (long); aspirate; inject slowly adjacent to (not into) the foramen; wait 5–10 min.

Which region needs anesthesia? — Select the facial region of the wound to route to the correct trigeminal terminal branch or ear block.

Supraorbital + supratrochlear block (V1) — Forehead/anterior scalp. Foramen at mid-pupillary line on brow ridge; supratrochlear 1 cm medial. ~2–3 mL.

Infratrochlear block (V1) — Medial canthus/nose bridge. Exits at superomedial orbital angle above medial canthus. 1–2 mL, aim away from globe.

Infraorbital block (V2) — Lower eyelid/cheek/upper lip/lateral nose. Foramen 1 cm below rim at mid-pupillary line. Intraoral, finger over foramen, 2–3 mL.

External nasal block (V1+V2) — Nasal tip/ala. External nasal branch at bony-cartilaginous junction (0.5–1 mL each side) ± bilateral infraorbital.

Mental nerve block (V3) — Lower lip/chin. Foramen below 2nd premolar at mid-pupillary line. Intraoral, 1–2 mL, bilateral for midline.

Inferior alveolar block (V3) — Hemi-mandible/teeth + lower lip. Mandibular foramen via coronoid notch, contact bone, withdraw 1 mm, aspirate, 1.5–2 mL. Vascular.

Auriculotemporal block (V3) — Temple/anterior ear. Runs with superficial temporal artery anterior to tragus. Aspirate (artery!), 2–3 mL fan.

External ear ring block — Whole auricle (not concha/canal). Subcutaneous ring, superior + inferior entries, ~6–8 mL, aspirate.

Test the block (pinprick, 5–10 min) — Wait 5–10 min, test pinprick vs contralateral side.

Rescue partial / failed block — Re-block, add local infiltration at the deficient edge, recheck max LA dose before adding more.

Complications + LAST recognition — Hematoma, intravascular injection (aspirate!), nerve injury, globe risk (periorbital), LAST → lipid emulsion.

Procedure note documented — Block type, indication, consent, agent/dose under max, landmarks, aspiration, confirmed effect, disposition.
