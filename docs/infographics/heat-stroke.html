Heat Stroke: Cool First, Then Workup

Adults ≥18 in the ED with suspected heat stroke — core temperature >40°C (104°F) plus CNS dysfunction. Excludes isolated heat exhaustion (normal mentation — shown only as a differential off-ramp, not built out), pediatric weight-based nuances beyond the calculator, and patients with known contraindications to cold-water immersion.

⚠ Cool first, workup second. Core temp >40°C + altered mental status is a true emergency — mortality 10-50% without rapid cooling. Do NOT delay cooling for IV access, labs, imaging, or transport.[1]
⚠ Use rectal or esophageal thermometry. Oral, axillary, and temporal temperatures are UNRELIABLE in heat illness and underestimate core temperature.[1]
⚠ Antipyretics are useless here. Acetaminophen and NSAIDs do not lower temperature — the thermoregulatory setpoint is normal in heat stroke.[1]
⚠ Stop active cooling at 39°C (102.2°F) to prevent overshoot hypothermia (afterdrop); resume cooling if temperature rebounds >39°C.[1]
⚠ Avoid succinylcholine when rhabdomyolysis is suspected — hyperkalemia risk. Use rocuronium for RSI paralysis.[2]
⚠ Every heat stroke patient is admitted. Multi-organ dysfunction — hepatic failure, DIC, AKI — can develop or be delayed 24-72 hours.[2]

2 · Cool first — the only intervention that changes mortality

Cool first, transport second
Target core temp <39°C within 30 minutes. Mortality is near-zero if cooled within 30 min and >50% if cooling is delayed beyond 2 hours. Remove all clothing and start cooling while IV access and monitoring are established in parallel.[1][2]

Endpoint — stop at 39°C
Stop active cooling at 39°C (102.2°F): temperature drifts down further after cooling stops (afterdrop), and overcooling triggers shivering, rebound hyperthermia, and (if overshoot into hypothermia <35°C) coagulopathy and arrhythmias. Dry the patient, cover lightly, recheck rectal temp every 5-15 min. Resume cooling if it rebounds >39°C.[1]

4 · Complications — multi-organ dysfunction

Heat stroke is a systemic inflammatory response — assume multi-organ involvement. Order CBC, CMP, LFTs, coags, lactate, CK, and UA with myoglobin. Repeat CK q6-12h if elevated.[1][2][3]

Crystalloid (NS or LR)
Target UOP 200-300 mL/hr
IV
May need 10-20 L/day in severe cases. POCUS-guided. Early nephrology consult; consider dialysis if CK >40,000 with refractory hyperkalemia, severe metabolic acidosis, volume overload, or anuria.
DIC, hepatic failure & other organ injury4 systems
DIC — thermal endothelial injury plus systemic inflammation. Transfuse only for active bleeding: FFP if INR >2, platelets if <50,000, cryoprecipitate if fibrinogen <100, TXA 1 g IV for significant hemorrhage. Do NOT transfuse prophylactically.[2]
Hepatic failure — can be delayed 24-72 hours; trend LFTs even if initially normal.[2]
AKI — from hypovolemia, myoglobin, and direct thermal injury; monitor creatinine and potassium.[2]
ARDS and cardiac dysfunction — capillary leak, arrhythmias, myocardial injury.[2]

5 · Disposition

ALL heat stroke patients require admission — mild/stable patients to a monitored/floor bed, unstable or organ-dysfunction patients to ICU. Multi-organ dysfunction, especially hepatic failure, can be delayed 24-72 hours.[3]

ICU admission: intubated, vasopressor requirement, severe rhabdomyolysis (CK >40,000), DIC with active bleeding, persistent altered mental status, refractory hyperthermia, or multi-organ failure.[3]
Monitored/step-down bed: cooled to <39°C within 30 minutes, hemodynamically stable off vasopressors, GCS at baseline, CK <10,000, no active seizures. Continue temp monitoring x24h and watch for delayed hepatic failure (24-72h).
