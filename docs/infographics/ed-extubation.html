ED Extubation Protocol

ED patients who are intubated but may no longer need invasive ventilation: short procedure recovery, resolved intoxication, reversed hypercapnic failure, post-seizure recovery, or prolonged ED boarding after ICU-level improvement. Default posture is elective extubation — if the airway may be hard to regain, delay unless the team and backup plan are strong.

⚠ Extubate only in a monitored setting with airway-skilled team and reintubation equipment ready
⚠ Do not extubate if airway protection is inadequate: poor cough, heavy secretions, or persistent depressed mental status
⚠ Use SBT plus airway assessment. Do not rely on RSBI alone.
⚠ Use cuff-leak testing only for patients at high risk of post-extubation stridor
⚠ If post-extubation failure is severe, reintubate early. NIV should not delay definitive airway control.

Overview & Indication

Use this for ED patients who are intubated but may no longer need invasive ventilation. ED extubation starts only after the original indication for intubation has clearly improved.

  • Short procedure recovery
  • Resolved intoxication
  • Reversed hypercapnic failure
  • Post-seizure recovery
  • Prolonged ED boarding after ICU-level improvement
Default posture
Extubation is elective. If the airway may be hard to regain, delay unless the team and backup plan are strong.

Choose the best indication. If there is no clear clinical benefit to extubating in the ED, continue ventilatory support and hand off to ICU.

Do Not Extubate Yet
Continue ventilator support, analgesia/sedation appropriate to the airway plan, and ICU handoff. Reassess when the indication for intubation has resolved.
Goals-of-Care Extubation
Clarify intent: comfort-focused extubation, trial of noninvasive support, or DNI with full medical therapy. Confirm symptom medications, family presence if desired, and what level of re-escalation is allowed.

Hard Stop Screen

Any hard stop should push the plan toward continued ventilation, ICU handoff, or extubation only with specialty backup.

  • Difficult reintubation risk
  • Severe hypoxemia
  • Shock
  • Paralysis
  • Incomplete reversal
Delay Extubation
Keep the tube. Optimize ventilator, sedation, hemodynamics, and oxygenation. If extubation is still desired, do it with ICU/anesthesia/RT support and a reintubation plan.

Airway Protection

Assess mental status, cough strength, secretion burden, gag/swallow when relevant, and ability to follow simple commands. A passed SBT does not compensate for poor airway protection.

Mandatory safety check
Do not extubate if airway protection is inadequate: poor cough, heavy secretions, or persistent depressed mental status.
Airway Protection Fails
Do not extubate for ventilator numbers alone. Continue airway control and reassess after sedation clears, secretions improve, or neurologic status changes.

Spontaneous Breathing Trial

Use a structured SBT when the patient has not already demonstrated spontaneous ventilation. Common approaches: T-piece, CPAP, or low pressure support for 30 minutes. Watch the patient, not just the ventilator. Failure signs: distress, diaphoresis, tachypnea, hypoxemia, rising CO2, arrhythmia, hypotension, or worsening mental status.

Mandatory approach
Use SBT plus airway assessment. Do not rely on RSBI alone.

Did the patient pass a monitored SBT or equivalent spontaneous ventilation assessment?

SBT Failed
Resume ventilator support. Treat the reversible cause: bronchospasm, pulmonary edema, pain/anxiety, metabolic acidosis, fluid overload, pneumonia, or oversedation.

Failure Risk & Stridor Risk

High-risk features include:

  • COPD, CHF, obesity/OSA
  • Age >65
  • Weak cough, secretion burden
  • Prolonged ventilation
  • Failed prior extubation
  • Hypercapnia during SBT
  • High oxygen needs

Cuff leak testing is most useful when risk is high. Do not use it as a universal screen. High-risk features:

  • Traumatic or multiple intubation attempts
  • Airway edema
  • Large tube
  • Prolonged intubation
  • Prior stridor
Selective use
Use cuff-leak testing only for patients at high risk of post-extubation stridor.

If a cuff leak test is used, interpret it as a risk tool. A failed leak test is not a diagnosis, but it should change the plan.

If extubation is not urgent, delay and give systemic corticosteroid prophylaxis. If extubation cannot wait, bring airway-skilled help to bedside and prepare for stridor or reintubation.

Dexamethasone
0.5 mg/kg IV q6h × 4 doses, starting 12-24 hours before planned extubation

Mandatory readiness

Tube Removal

  1. Explain if awake and coach breathing.
  2. Preoxygenate on planned support.
  3. Suction oropharynx and ETT.
  4. Deflate cuff completely.
  5. Remove tube smoothly during inspiration or with gentle positive pressure.
  6. Apply planned oxygen/NIV/HFNC immediately.
  7. Reassess work of breathing, voice, stridor, SpO2, ETCO2 if available, and mental status.

Watch continuously for stridor, fatigue, secretion failure, hypoxemia, hypercapnia, aspiration, or worsening mental status.

Short NIV Bridge
