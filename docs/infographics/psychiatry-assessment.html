Mental Status Exam — Structured Assessment & Risk Stratification

Adults presenting to the ED requiring a documented mental status exam. Structured walkthrough for MSE dictation and suicide/homicide risk stratification. Not intended as a copy/paste template — clinician must verify findings against the actual patient exam before documenting. Excludes formal capacity assessment (separate consult) and pediatric-specific MSE norms.

⚠ Command auditory hallucinations to harm self/others = immediate safety concern — distinguish from non-command or no-harm-content auditory hallucinations, which do not carry the same acuity.
⚠ Document suicidal ideation using standardized tiers: none/denies, passive, active without plan, active with plan, or active with plan and intent — vague documentation (e.g. "denies SI") without ruling out passive ideation is inadequate.
⚠ Active suicidal ideation with plan and intent = HIGH risk requiring admission or a documented safety plan.
⚠ Assess insight and judgment explicitly — poor insight (anosognosia) may require involuntary commitment.
⚠ Quote the patient's exact words for mood (subjective) vs your own observation for affect (objective) — these are not interchangeable and must not be merged in documentation.
⚠ HIGH suicide risk also includes: intent to act, access to lethal means, a recent suicide attempt, hopelessness, impulsivity, and intoxication — any one of these can move a patient into the high-risk tier even without a stated plan.

6 · Insight, Judgment & Suicide Risk Stratification

DomainTiers
InsightGood (understands illness and need for treatment), Fair (partial awareness or inconsistent acknowledgment), Poor/anosognosia (no insight, denies any problem)
JudgmentGood (appropriate decisions, understands consequences), Fair (some impairment), Poor (significantly impaired, unable to make appropriate decisions)
Poor insight and disposition
Assess insight and judgment explicitly — poor insight may require involuntary commitment.[1][2][3][4]
LOW suicide risk
No suicidal ideation, no plan or intent, no history of attempts, protective factors present (family support, reasons for living, future orientation).[1][2][3][4]
MODERATE suicide risk
Passive suicidal ideation, history of prior attempts, or limited protective factors — current stressors without an active plan.[1][2][3][4]
HIGH suicide risk
Active suicidal ideation with plan, intent to act, access to means, recent suicide attempt, command auditory hallucinations to harm self, hopelessness, impulsivity, intoxication. Requires admission or a documented safety plan.[1][2][3][4]

Use the C-SSRS for standardized suicide risk assessment.[3]

Documentation reminder
Verify all findings before submitting documentation. Risk stratification guides disposition and safety planning.[1][2][3][4]

8 · Do NOT

Documentation pitfalls4 items
Do NOT document "denies SI" without screening the full tier ladder — passive ideation, active without plan, active with plan, and active with plan and intent are distinct tiers that change disposition.
Do NOT record a generic "auditory hallucinations" without asking about command content and harm direction — command AH to harm self or others is an immediate safety concern; non-command or no-harm-content AH is not the same acuity.
Do NOT merge mood and affect in documentation — mood is the patient's own words (subjective); affect is your observation (objective).
Do NOT skip insight and judgment assessment — poor insight (anosognosia) may change the disposition to involuntary commitment.
