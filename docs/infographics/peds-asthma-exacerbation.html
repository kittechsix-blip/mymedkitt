Peds Asthma: Score It, Dose It, Don't Intubate

Children 2–17 years in the ED with an acute asthma exacerbation. PRAM is validated 2–17y. Excludes bronchiolitis (<2y, first wheeze), anaphylaxis, foreign-body aspiration, and cardiac wheeze — screen for these mimics first. Enter the child's weight to drive every dose.

⚠ Red flags: silent chest, AMS, cyanosis/SpO2 <90%, bradycardia, paradoxical breathing — labs/CXR only for severe or atypical.
⚠ Continuous albuterol 0.5 mg/kg/hr (10-20 mg/hr) + IV methylpred 1-2 mg/kg + IV MgSO4 25-75 mg/kg over 20min + IM epi if anaphylaxis/failing SABA.
⚠ IV terbutaline 10 mcg/kg load → 0.1-10 mcg/kg/min; heliox 70/30 if FiO2 ≤0.30; NIV BiPAP with KETAMINE sedation (avoid propofol/benzos).
⚠ Induce with KETAMINE 1-2 mg/kg + roc 1.2 mg/kg; AVOID propofol/etomidate; vent LOW rate (10-16), LONG I:E (1:3-1:5), PEEP 0-5, permissive hypercapnia.
⚠ Triage SpO2, work of breathing, mental status; identify silent chest / AMS / cyanosis
⚠ Score with PRAM (0-12) — guides treatment intensity and disposition

1 · Triage & red flags

Peds asthma is the #1 reason for unscheduled ED visits in school-age kids. Air trapping is rapid, decompensation is sudden, and beta-agonist toxicity is real — the whole goal is to avoid intubation.[1][3]

Watch the kid breathe before you touch them
Get SpO2 on room air (the single most important number), HR/RR by age, mental status, work of breathing, ability to speak, color, and the character of wheeze. Tachycardia is early; bradycardia is preterminal.[3][4]
