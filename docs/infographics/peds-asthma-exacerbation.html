Pediatric Acute Asthma: PRAM-Driven Escalation Without Intubating

Pediatric patients (validated PRAM range 2-17 years; younger children by clinical gestalt) presenting to the ED with acute asthma exacerbation. Excludes adults (≥18), bronchiolitis (<2y, no prior wheeze), and other pediatric wheeze mimics that need a different pathway (foreign body aspiration, anaphylaxis, pneumothorax, vocal cord dysfunction).

⚠ Silent chest, altered mental status, cyanosis/SpO₂ <90% on room air, bradycardia, or paradoxical chest/abdominal movement override the PRAM score — any one of these is severe/life-threatening regardless of the numeric total; escalate immediately.
⚠ NIV sedation: ketamine only. Avoid propofol, high-dose benzodiazepines, and opioids in the spontaneously breathing child on BiPAP — they blunt respiratory drive and can precipitate arrest.
⚠ Intubation induction: ketamine + rocuronium. Avoid propofol (no bronchodilation, hypotension) and etomidate (no bronchodilation) as the sole induction strategy.
⚠ Post-intubation hypotension or arrest = air trapping until proven otherwise. Disconnect the ETT and compress the chest firmly for 30-60 seconds to release trapped gas before assuming another cause.
⚠ Continuous albuterol and IV terbutaline cause hypokalemia and lactic acidosis — expected beta-agonist effects, not sepsis; monitor K⁺ every 2-4 hours and replace if low.
⚠ Screen for mimics before committing to the asthma protocol — foreign body aspiration (sudden, unilateral), anaphylaxis (urticaria/hypotension → give IM epinephrine), and bronchiolitis (<2y, no prior wheeze, supportive care only) all masquerade as asthma.

4 · Refractory escalation & the airway

Maxed on inhaled SABA + IV steroid + IV MgSO4 and not improving. Time for systemic beta-agonist, heliox, and an NIV trial before intubating.[3][4][10][11]

IV terbutaline: load 10 mcg/kg IV over 10 min (max 250-500 mcg load), then infuse 0.1-10 mcg/kg/min, titrating q10-15 min to effect under continuous cardiac monitoring. myMedKitt's drug store has no IV/asthma-infusion entry for terbutaline (only subcutaneous dosing) — this dose is sourced to the consult text only, not cross-checked against a drug-store weightCalc.

Heliox (70/30 He:O2)

7 · Mimics & beta-agonist toxicity

Not every wheeze is asthma. Screen these on every severe or atypical presentation.[3][4][13]

MimicDistinguishing features
Foreign body aspirationSudden onset, unilateral wheeze, toddler 1-3y — refractory to SABA
Anaphylaxis with bronchospasmUrticaria, angioedema, hypotension, GI symptoms — treat with IM epi
Bronchiolitis<2 years old, no prior wheeze, viral prodrome — supportive care, not asthma protocol
PneumothoraxSudden chest pain, unilateral decreased breath sounds, post-intubation hypotension
Vocal cord dysfunctionAdolescents, inspiratory/laryngeal wheeze, no SABA response, normal SpO2
Tracheomalacia / vascular ringInfants/toddlers, persistent/stridor-like wheeze, positional, no SABA response
Beta-agonist toxicity recap
Tachycardia, tremor, and agitation are expected. Hypokalemia — replace if <3.5, life-threatening if <2.5. Lactic acidosis (lactate 3-8) is a known beta-2 effect, not sepsis, until proven otherwise.[3][4]
