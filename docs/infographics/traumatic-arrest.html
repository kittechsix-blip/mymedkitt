Traumatic Arrest: HOTT Bundle & Thoracotomy Decision

Adults and pregnant patients (>20 weeks) presenting in traumatic cardiac arrest or peri-arrest shock, ED or prehospital handoff, with trauma surgery/OR backup available for thoracotomy decisions. Excludes primary medical arrest with incidental trauma (routed to standard ACLS) and non-traumatic causes of shock.

⚠ Traumatic arrest is not medical arrest. Correct HOTT causes — hypovolemia/hemorrhage, oxygenation, tension pneumothorax, tamponade — in parallel rather than defaulting to a standard cardiac-arrest drug algorithm.
⚠ Do not delay source control for ACLS tasks. Hemorrhage control, bilateral thoracostomy, and indicated thoracotomy take priority over prolonged airway attempts or serial ACLS steps.
⚠ Blunt thoracotomy is exceptional. Avoid thoracotomy for blunt traumatic arrest without signs of life after HOTT interventions — survival is very low outside a highly selected, witnessed, short-CPR presentation.
⚠ Pregnancy >20 weeks: resuscitate the mother first with manual left uterine displacement; proceed to resuscitative hysterotomy if the uterus is at/above the umbilicus and there is no ROSC by 4 minutes.
⚠ Document before terminating resuscitation: mechanism, signs of life, rhythm, ultrasound findings, procedures performed, and response — involve trauma leadership when available.

1 · Recognize the Scenario

Traumatic arrest ≠ medical arrest
Correct reversible trauma physiology (hemorrhage, oxygenation, tension pneumothorax, tamponade) instead of defaulting to a standard cardiac-arrest drug algorithm. Announce traumatic arrest, assign task leads, and activate trauma surgery, MTP, airway, ultrasound, and OR/IR pathways immediately.[1]

Pulseless traumatic arrest — no central pulse or organized perfusion after trauma. Peri-arrest traumatic shock — profound hypotension, impending arrest, or transient pulses. Primary medical arrest suspected — low-energy trauma after a medical collapse, medical prodrome, no major injury pattern.

  1. Activate trauma team, MTP, OR/IR, and massive hemorrhage resources.
  2. Place a pelvic binder if pelvic injury is possible.
  3. Control external hemorrhage with tourniquet, direct pressure, or hemostatic packing.
  4. Bilateral chest decompression for severe chest trauma or obstructive physiology.
  5. Intubate only if it will not delay hemorrhage control, thoracostomy, or thoracotomy.
  6. Use balanced blood-product resuscitation early.

If arrest appears secondary to medical collapse, run the standard cardiac arrest pathway while screening for occult traumatic causes: bilateral breath sounds, eFAST, hemorrhage/pelvis check, long-bone deformity, temperature and tox/metabolic clues. Return to the traumatic arrest pathway if major injury, hemorrhage, tension pneumothorax, or tamponade is identified.

2 · HOTT Bundle — Run in Parallel

  • H — Hypovolemia/hemorrhage: direct pressure, tourniquets, pelvic binder, MTP, rapid source control.
  • O — Oxygenation/airway: high-flow oxygen, airway control as needed, confirm ventilation with waveform ETCO2.
  • T — Tension pneumothorax: bilateral finger thoracostomies or chest tubes for chest trauma or suspected obstructive physiology.
  • T — Tamponade: eFAST/subxiphoid view if it doesn't delay intervention; resuscitative thoracotomy for selected penetrating arrest or selected blunt arrest with signs of life.
Do not delay source control
Do not delay hemorrhage control, bilateral thoracostomy, or thoracotomy for prolonged ACLS tasks. Assign simultaneous task leads rather than working serially.[1]

6 · Termination & Documentation

  • Injuries incompatible with life.
  • No signs of life and prolonged downtime.
  • Asystole with no cardiac motion after HOTT interventions.
  • Blunt traumatic arrest without signs of life.
  • No response after hemorrhage control, oxygenation, bilateral decompression, and tamponade assessment/intervention.
