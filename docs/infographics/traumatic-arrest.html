Traumatic Arrest: Fix the Physiology, Then Decide Thoracotomy

Adults in the ED or trauma bay in traumatic cardiac arrest or peri-arrest traumatic shock. Screen for and exclude primary medical arrest. Not a pediatric-specific protocol and not for non-trauma resuscitation.

⚠ Traumatic arrest is not medical arrest. Correct reversible trauma physiology immediately — do not default to a standard ACLS algorithm.
⚠ Run HOTT in parallel, not in series: Hypovolemia/hemorrhage, Oxygenation/airway, Tension pneumothorax, Tamponade — assign simultaneous task leads.
⚠ Do not delay hemorrhage control, bilateral chest decompression, or thoracotomy for prolonged ACLS tasks.
⚠ Peri-arrest traumatic shock needs the same source-control urgency as pulseless arrest — treat it as imminent arrest.
⚠ Pregnancy ≥20 weeks: manual left uterine displacement + resuscitate mother first; HOTT indications do not disappear because the patient is pregnant.
⚠ ROSC is a bridge to the OR/IR, not definitive stabilization — the patient still needs source control.

Penetrating arrest with signs of life and a short CPR interval: proceed if local skill, staffing, and surgical backup allow. Goals: relieve tamponade, control cardiac/hilar hemorrhage, open cardiac massage, cross-clamp the descending thoracic aorta when appropriate, bridge to OR for definitive repair. Penetrating thoracic wounds carry the strongest indication.[2]
Penetrating arrest — no signs of life or prolonged CPR
No signs of life or a prolonged CPR interval markedly lowers survival. After HOTT interventions are addressed, move toward termination / withholding unless a correctable cause is found. Reassess if any sign of life returns.[7]
Blunt arrest — narrow, selective thoracotomy
Survival after blunt traumatic arrest is low. Thoracotomy is reasonable only with witnessed arrest, signs of life, short CPR interval (generally <10 minutes), a potentially correctable thoracic cause, and an immediately available trauma surgeon + OR pathway.[3]
Blunt arrest — not a thoracotomy candidate
Blunt traumatic arrest without signs of life after HOTT correction is generally not a thoracotomy candidate. Address reversible causes, then move toward termination / withholding per local policy.[3]

4 · Pregnancy ≥20 weeks

The pregnancy-specific pathway triggers at Pregnancy >20 weeks. Standard traumatic-arrest priorities still apply — thoracic indications do not disappear because the patient is pregnant.[1]

  1. Manual left uterine displacement.
  2. Resuscitate the mother first; continue HOTT simultaneously.
  3. Activate OB, trauma, anesthesia, neonatology.
  4. If the uterus is at/above the umbilicus and there is no ROSC by 4 minutes, proceed to the resuscitative hysterotomy pathway.

6 · Termination / withholding

Factors supporting termination or withholding5 markers
Injuries incompatible with life.
No signs of life and prolonged downtime.
Asystole with no cardiac motion after HOTT interventions.
Blunt traumatic arrest without signs of life.
No response after hemorrhage control, oxygenation, bilateral decompression, and tamponade assessment/intervention.

Follow local policy, involve trauma leadership when available, and document mechanism, signs of life, rhythm, ultrasound findings, and interventions. ROSC is a bridge to OR/IR/source control — not definitive stabilization.[7]
