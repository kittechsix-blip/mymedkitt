Pacemaker & ICD Emergencies

Adults in the ED with a permanent pacemaker, ICD, or CRT device (or who need temporary pacing) presenting with suspected device malfunction, ICD shocks, a need for external defibrillation/cardioversion, a magnet-use question, a paced-rhythm ECG to interpret, or a device pocket/lead complication.

⚠ Magnet on a PACEMAKER = asynchronous pacing (VOO/DOO). Magnet on an ICD = disables SHOCKS only (pacing unchanged) — these are opposite effects, know which device you have.
⚠ Failure to capture + hyperkalemia — ALWAYS check potassium; treat hyperkalemia first.
⚠ Undersensing can pace on the T wave (R-on-T) → VF. Magnet makes pacing asynchronous but does NOT fix undersensing.
⚠ ICD storm (≥3 shocks in 24h): apply magnet, then beta-blocker + amiodarone; this is a medical emergency.
⚠ Safe — and necessary — to defibrillate/cardiovert a patient with a pacemaker or ICD. Do NOT delay defibrillation to find the device; keep pads at least 8 cm from the generator when possible.
⚠ Device interrogation is required within 24 hours after any external shock or cardioversion.
⚠ Leadless pacemakers (Micra) do NOT respond to magnets.

Failure to Pace
Causes: lead fracture (most common hardware cause), lead dislodgment, battery depletion, oversensing, generator failure, loose set screw. Apply a magnet — if pacing is restored, this is oversensing (keep magnet on, arrange interrogation). If pacing is NOT restored, suspect lead fracture, severe battery depletion, or generator failure — proceed to transcutaneous pacing as a bridge.
Failure to Capture — ALWAYS CHECK POTASSIUM
Pacing spikes present WITHOUT a subsequent P wave or QRS. Lead-related (dislodgment, fracture, perforation), threshold-related (hyperkalemia — K+ >7 mEq/L classic cause, acidosis, hypoxia, ischemia, exit block), medication-related (flecainide, propafenone, Class I antiarrhythmics), or device (battery depletion, output too low). Check K+ and treat hyperkalemia aggressively, increase output if reprogrammable, transcutaneous pacing if unstable, magnet may help in some devices.
Undersensing — risk of R-on-T → VF
Pacing spikes occur regardless of intrinsic rhythm — "competition" between device and native rhythm. Causes: sensitivity programmed too low, lead dislodgment/fracture, inadequate intrinsic signal, fibrosis, new bundle branch block. Apply a magnet to convert to asynchronous pacing (consistent, but the danger — pacing when it shouldn't — remains); reprogram sensitivity via interrogation; lead revision if causing hemodynamic issues. Magnet does NOT fix undersensing.

Device inhibited by signals mistaken for cardiac activity: myopotentials (pectoralis), T-wave sensing, EMI, lead fracture noise, cross-talk. Clues: symptoms with arm movement (myopotentials), near equipment (EMI), erratic sensing on interrogation (fracture). Apply magnet — bypasses sensing, restores pacing, and is the definitive treatment until reprogramming (reprogram sensitivity/blanking periods, repair lead if fractured).

Pacemaker-mediated tachycardia (endless-loop tachycardia) occurs only in DDD pacemakers: a PVC or loss of atrial capture conducts retrograde to the atrium, is sensed, triggers ventricular pacing, which conducts retrograde again — a loop at the upper tracking rate (usually 110-130 bpm), ventricular-paced morphology, often starting after a PVC. Apply magnet — converts to asynchronous mode and breaks the loop immediately (diagnostic AND therapeutic); adenosine blocks the AV node as an alternative; reprogramming can extend PVARP or enable PMT-termination algorithms.

Stability & management

  1. Apply magnet — may restore pacing (oversensing, PMT); check magnet rate for battery status
  2. Check potassium — hyperkalemia is a common reversible cause; treat aggressively (calcium, insulin/glucose, bicarb)
  3. Transcutaneous pacing if magnet doesn't help and bradycardic — start 60-80 ppm, increase mA until capture, sedate if conscious
  4. Pharmacologic bridge if needed (see doses below)
  5. Prepare for transvenous pacing if TCP fails or prolonged pacing is needed
  6. STAT cardiology/EP consultation

DDD pacing shows 4 patterns: A-sense/V-sense (native rhythm, no spikes), A-pace/V-sense, A-sense/V-pace, or A-pace/V-pace. Fusion beats (native + paced activation together, intermediate morphology) and pseudofusion (spike on native QRS without contributing to it) are both normal findings, not malfunction.

CriterionPoints
Concordant ST elevation ≥1 mm5
Concordant ST depression ≥1 mm in V1-V33
Discordant STE / S-wave ratio ≥0.25 (Modified/Smith)2
ANY positive criterion = activate cath lab
Normal paced rhythm has discordant ST-T changes. Concordant ST changes are ALWAYS abnormal, and excessive discordant STE (>25% of S wave depth) is abnormal. ≥3 points is highly specific for STEMI, but any single positive criterion warrants cath lab activation.

ICD shock emergencies

  1. Assess stability now — ABCs, vitals, mental status
  2. Count shocks — single vs multiple; ≥3 shocks in 24h = ICD storm
  3. Determine appropriate (correctly detected/treated VT/VF) vs inappropriate (shock when NOT in VT/VF)
  4. Workup: 12-lead ECG, BMP, Mg++, troponin, chest X-ray, device interrogation

  • Heart failure — BNP, chest X-ray, echo if indicated, optimize HF meds

ICD Storm — a medical emergency
≥3 VT/VF episodes requiring ICD therapy in 24 hours. Apply magnet if ongoing shocks; determine appropriate vs inappropriate by interrogating ASAP. For true VT/VF storm: beta-blocker (blunts sympathetic surge, first-line), amiodarone, sedation, and treatment of the underlying cause. Advanced options: overdrive pacing, catheter ablation, left cardiac sympathetic denervation, intubation/sedation if refractory. Admit to ICU.

External defibrillation

YES, you can and SHOULD defibrillate patients with devices
Do NOT delay defibrillation to check for a pacemaker. Safe to defibrillate/cardiovert; some current may travel down the leads. If the ICD is actively shocking, wait 30-60 seconds for the cycle to complete, then apply the AED/defibrillator if still needed — you can shock over the ICD's attempts if necessary. Device interrogation is required within 24 hours after any shock.
PlacementNotes
Anterior-Posterior (preferred)Avoids the generator entirely; current path away from leads
Anterior-Lateral (standard)Keep pads at least 8 cm (3 inches) from the generator; if left-sided device, move the apex pad more lateral/inferior or use A-P

Do NOT place a pad directly over the generator or delay defibrillation to find it. Use standard energy (biphasic 120-200J, monophasic 360J) — no need to reduce energy for a pacemaker. Synchronized cardioversion uses the same pad-placement considerations.

  1. Check for paced rhythm on the monitor, assess capture, watch for failure to pace/capture
  2. Watch for sensing/capture threshold changes and temporary pacing dysfunction (usually resolves); permanent damage is rare but possible
  3. Device interrogation within 24 hours — full assessment of sensing/capture thresholds, reprogram if needed
  4. If pacing malfunction post-shock: transcutaneous pacing as a bridge, urgent interrogation, may need temporary transvenous pacing
  5. Document: number of shocks, energy levels, pad position, post-shock device function

A-P pads preferred, avoiding the generator (≥8 cm away). Typical capture threshold 50-100 mA; set output 1.25x threshold (or 5-10 mA above). In peri-arrest/arrest, start at maximum output for rapid capture, then titrate down. Confirm capture with a wide QRS AND a palpable pulse or POCUS — not just monitor artifact.

  • Check pads: good contact, not dried out, hair not interfering, A-P position
  • Increase output up to maximum (usually 140-200 mA); if still no capture at max → TVP
  • Check connections: pads connected to pacer, correct pacer mode
  • Patient factors that raise thresholds: large body habitus, pericardial effusion, severe metabolic derangement
  • TCP is PAINFUL at effective outputs — sedate with fentanyl, midazolam, ketamine, or propofol
  • Indications: TCP failure/poor capture, prolonged pacing needed (>30 min), overdrive pacing for torsades, more reliable pacing required
  • Access site preference: right IJ (straight path to RV, highest success) > left subclavian (reserve for permanent site) > femoral (higher infection/thrombosis, patient can't sit up)
  • Equipment: 5-6 Fr introducer sheath, balloon-tipped pacing catheter, pacing generator
  • Procedure: place sheath (ultrasound-guided), advance catheter to ~20 cm, inflate balloon and float to RV, confirm position (ECG injury current, POCUS, or fluoro), deflate balloon, test pacing
  • Settings: rate 60-80 ppm, output set at 2-3x threshold

These medications are BRIDGES to pacing, not definitive treatment — prepare for TCP/TVP while infusing. Isoproterenol (pure beta-agonist, 2-10 mcg/min) increases myocardial oxygen demand and is not routinely recommended, though it may be useful in transplant patients.

TCP Troubleshooting — TCP troubleshooting: check pad contact/connections, increase to max mA, try A-P position; TCP is PAINFUL — sedate with fentanyl/midazolam/ketamine.

Transvenous Pacing — TVP: right IJ preferred (straight path to RV), balloon-tipped catheter ~20cm, confirm with injury current/POCUS/fluoro; set output 2-3x threshold.

Pharmacologic Bridge — Atropine 1mg IV (ineffective for Mobitz II/CHB), epinephrine 2-10mcg/min, dopamine 5-20mcg/kg/min — bridges to pacing, not definitive treatment.

Device Complications — Pocket infection: blood cultures, may need complete extraction + IV abx; Twiddler syndrome: leads coiled on CXR; lead fracture at subclavian crush site.

Malfunction types

Type | ECG Finding | Problem

Failure to Pace | No spikes when expected | Device not firing

Failure to Capture | Spikes WITHOUT QRS | Stimulus ineffective

Failure to Sense (undersensing) | Too many spikes (inappropriate pacing) | Not detecting intrinsic activity

Oversensing | Pauses without pacing | Inhibited by non-cardiac signals

  • 12-lead ECG — identify malfunction type
  • Electrolytes — K+, Mg++, Ca++
  • Chest X-ray — lead position, fracture
  • Device interrogation — gold standard

Causes: lead fracture (most common hardware cause), lead dislodgment, battery depletion, oversensing, generator failure, loose set screw. Apply a magnet — if pacing is restored, this is oversensing (keep magnet on, arrange interrogation). If pacing is NOT restored, suspect lead fracture, severe battery depletion, or generator failure — proceed to transcutaneous pacing as a bridge.

Pacing spikes present WITHOUT a subsequent P wave or QRS. Lead-related (dislodgment, fracture, perforation), threshold-related (hyperkalemia — K+ >7 mEq/L classic cause, acidosis, hypoxia, ischemia, exit block), medication-related (flecainide, propafenone, Class I antiarrhythmics), or device (battery depletion, output too low). Check K+ and treat hyperkalemia aggressively, increase output if reprogrammable, transcutaneous pacing if unstable, magnet may help in some devices.

Pacing spikes occur regardless of intrinsic rhythm — "competition" between device and native rhythm. Causes: sensitivity programmed too low, lead dislodgment/fracture, inadequate intrinsic signal, fibrosis, new bundle branch block. Apply a magnet to convert to asynchronous pacing (consistent, but the danger — pacing when it shouldn't — remains); reprogram sensitivity via interrogation; lead revision if causing hemodynamic issues. Magnet does NOT fix undersensing.

Device inhibited by signals mistaken for cardiac activity: myopotentials (pectoralis), T-wave sensing, EMI, lead fracture noise, cross-talk. Clues: symptoms with arm movement (myopotentials), near equipment (EMI), erratic sensing on interrogation (fracture). Apply magnet — bypasses sensing, restores pacing, and is the definitive treatment until reprogramming (reprogram sensitivity/blanking periods, repair lead if fractured).

Pacemaker-mediated tachycardia (endless-loop tachycardia) occurs only in DDD pacemakers: a PVC or loss of atrial capture conducts retrograde to the atrium, is sensed, triggers ventricular pacing, which conducts retrograde again — a loop at the upper tracking rate (usually 110-130 bpm), ventricular-paced morphology, often starting after a PVC. Apply magnet — converts to asynchronous mode and breaks the loop immediately (diagnostic AND therapeutic); adenosine blocks the AV node as an alternative; reprogramming can extend PVARP or enable PMT-termination algorithms.

Stability & management

  • Apply magnet — may restore pacing (oversensing, PMT); check magnet rate for battery status
  • Check potassium — hyperkalemia is a common reversible cause; treat aggressively (calcium, insulin/glucose, bicarb)
  • Transcutaneous pacing if magnet doesn't help and bradycardic — start 60-80 ppm, increase mA until capture, sedate if conscious
  • Pharmacologic bridge if needed (see doses below)
  • Prepare for transvenous pacing if TCP fails or prolonged pacing is needed
  • STAT cardiology/EP consultation

Epinephrine: 2-10 mcg/min IV infusion IV infusion — Chronotropic and inotropic support. Titrate to HR and BP.

Dopamine: 5-20 mcg/kg/min IV infusion IV infusion — Alternative chronotropic support. Higher doses more chronotropic.

  • Complete 12-lead ECG — document malfunction type, compare to prior
  • Labs — BMP (K+, Mg++, Ca++, Cr), troponin if ischemia concern, consider drug levels
  • Chest X-ray — AP and lateral, compare to prior; look for lead position, fracture, pneumothorax
  • Arrange device interrogation — cardiology/EP consult, device company rep if needed
  • Continuous monitoring — telemetry admission, TCP pads on standby
  • Disposition: all confirmed malfunctions need admission and cardiology consultation; may need lead revision or generator replacement

ECG interpretation

Pacing Site | Pattern

RV apex | LBBB + left axis deviation

RV septum | LBBB, axis may be normal

LV (via CS) | RBBB pattern

BiV (CRT) | Narrower than RV alone, variable

DDD pacing shows 4 patterns: A-sense/V-sense (native rhythm, no spikes), A-pace/V-sense, A-sense/V-pace, or A-pace/V-pace. Fusion beats (native + paced activation together, intermediate morphology) and pseudofusion (spike on native QRS without contributing to it) are both normal findings, not malfunction.

Criterion | Points

Concordant ST elevation ≥1 mm | 5

Concordant ST depression ≥1 mm in V1-V3 | 3

Discordant STE / S-wave ratio ≥0.25 (Modified/Smith) | 2

Normal paced rhythm has discordant ST-T changes. Concordant ST changes are ALWAYS abnormal, and excessive discordant STE (>25% of S wave depth) is abnormal. ≥3 points is highly specific for STEMI, but any single positive criterion warrants cath lab activation.

Magnet use

Device | Magnet Effect

PACEMAKER | Switches to asynchronous pacing (VOO/DOO)

ICD | Disables shock therapy ONLY (pacing unchanged)

Pacemaker + magnet: bypasses sensing, paces at the "magnet rate" (indicates battery status) — useful for oversensing, PMT, battery check. ICD + magnet: disables VT/VF detection and shocks only; pacing function is unchanged — useful for inappropriate shocks, ICD storm, end-of-life care, surgery. Apply directly over the generator; tape in place; remove to return to programmed mode.

Manufacturer | Normal Rate | ERI/Low Battery

Medtronic | 85 bpm | 65 bpm

Boston Scientific | 100 bpm | 90 bpm (ERI), ≤85 bpm (EOL)

Abbott (St. Jude) | 100 bpm | 86 bpm

Biotronik | 90 bpm | 80 bpm

ERI = Elective Replacement Indicator (battery low, replace soon); EOL = End of Life (replace urgently). Medtronic devices may have ~1.5 min delay after interrogation before the magnet response. Leadless pacemakers (Micra) do NOT respond to magnets. A low magnet rate suggests battery depletion and urgent generator replacement.

  • PACEMAKER: oversensing, PMT, assess battery status, confirm pacemaker vs ICD
  • ICD: inappropriate shocks, ICD storm, end-of-life care, during surgery (prevent EMI-triggered shocks)
  • Do NOT use magnet for: patient in actual VT/VF who needs ICD therapy, failure to capture (won't fix threshold issues), or as a diagnostic tool when interrogation is readily available
  • Safe to try in most situations; effect is reversible and does NOT permanently reprogram the device

Manufacturer | Behavior

Medtronic | Suspends detection while magnet on

Boston Scientific | Suspends detection while magnet on

Abbott (St. Jude) | Suspends detection while magnet on

Biotronik | May permanently disable if magnet held >8 hours

ICD shock emergencies

  • Assess stability now — ABCs, vitals, mental status
  • Count shocks — single vs multiple; ≥3 shocks in 24h = ICD storm
  • Determine appropriate (correctly detected/treated VT/VF) vs inappropriate (shock when NOT in VT/VF)
  • Workup: 12-lead ECG, BMP, Mg++, troponin, chest X-ray, device interrogation

Appropriate | Inappropriate

Device correctly detected and treated VT/VF | Shock when NOT in VT/VF

Look for: ischemia, HF, electrolytes, drugs | Causes: AFib/RVR, SVT, lead fracture, T-wave oversensing

  • Ischemia — ECG, troponin; consider cath if ACS
  • Heart failure — BNP, chest X-ray, echo if indicated, optimize HF meds
  • Electrolytes — K+, Mg++, Ca++; replete aggressively
  • Medications/drugs — QT-prolonging drugs, digoxin toxicity, stimulants, cocaine
  • Treat underlying cause; consider beta-blocker (reduces VT burden); amiodarone for recurrent VT; admit for monitoring; EP follow-up
  • Apply magnet — disables shock therapy immediately, keep on until reprogramming
  • Rate control if AFib/SVT — beta-blocker or diltiazem to reduce rate below detection threshold
  • Device interrogation — confirms inappropriate shocks, identifies cause, allows reprogramming
  • Disposition: admit for monitoring and EP adjustment

≥3 VT/VF episodes requiring ICD therapy in 24 hours. Apply magnet if ongoing shocks; determine appropriate vs inappropriate by interrogating ASAP. For true VT/VF storm: beta-blocker (blunts sympathetic surge, first-line), amiodarone, sedation, and treatment of the underlying cause. Advanced options: overdrive pacing, catheter ablation, left cardiac sympathetic denervation, intubation/sedation if refractory. Admit to ICU.

Amiodarone: 150 mg IV over 10 min, then 1 mg/min x 6h, then 0.5 mg/min IV — Can repeat 150mg bolus for breakthrough VT. Max 2.2g/24h.

Esmolol: 500 mcg/kg bolus, then 50-300 mcg/kg/min IV — Short-acting beta-blocker. Titrate to HR/BP. Can combine with amiodarone.

  • Stable, single shock: admit for monitoring, arrange interrogation, basic workup (ECG, BMP, troponin, CXR), telemetry
  • Multiple/ongoing shocks: apply magnet to prevent further shocks, assess hemodynamics, if in VT/VF after magnet — external cardioversion/defibrillation, emergent interrogation
  • Contact cardiology/EP consult and device company (24/7 technical support)
  • You can ALWAYS apply a magnet if unsure — if in true VT/VF, you can still externally defibrillate; magnet only disables internal therapy

External defibrillation

Do NOT delay defibrillation to check for a pacemaker. Safe to defibrillate/cardiovert; some current may travel down the leads. If the ICD is actively shocking, wait 30-60 seconds for the cycle to complete, then apply the AED/defibrillator if still needed — you can shock over the ICD's attempts if necessary. Device interrogation is required within 24 hours after any shock.

Placement | Notes

Anterior-Posterior (preferred) | Avoids the generator entirely; current path away from leads

Anterior-Lateral (standard) | Keep pads at least 8 cm (3 inches) from the generator; if left-sided device, move the apex pad more lateral/inferior or use A-P

Do NOT place a pad directly over the generator or delay defibrillation to find it. Use standard energy (biphasic 120-200J, monophasic 360J) — no need to reduce energy for a pacemaker. Synchronized cardioversion uses the same pad-placement considerations.

  • Check for paced rhythm on the monitor, assess capture, watch for failure to pace/capture
  • Watch for sensing/capture threshold changes and temporary pacing dysfunction (usually resolves); permanent damage is rare but possible
  • Device interrogation within 24 hours — full assessment of sensing/capture thresholds, reprogram if needed
  • If pacing malfunction post-shock: transcutaneous pacing as a bridge, urgent interrogation, may need temporary transvenous pacing
  • Document: number of shocks, energy levels, pad position, post-shock device function

Temporary pacing

Method | Speed | Duration | Reliability

Transcutaneous (TCP) | Fastest | Short-term | Variable capture

Transvenous (TVP) | Moderate | Days | Most reliable

Pharmacologic | Fast | Bridge only | Limited efficacy

Parameter | Starting | Adjustment

Rate | 60-80 ppm | Adjust to hemodynamics

Output (mA) | 30-50 mA | Increase by 5-10 mA until capture

A-P pads preferred, avoiding the generator (≥8 cm away). Typical capture threshold 50-100 mA; set output 1.25x threshold (or 5-10 mA above). In peri-arrest/arrest, start at maximum output for rapid capture, then titrate down. Confirm capture with a wide QRS AND a palpable pulse or POCUS — not just monitor artifact.

  • Check pads: good contact, not dried out, hair not interfering, A-P position
  • Increase output up to maximum (usually 140-200 mA); if still no capture at max → TVP
  • Check connections: pads connected to pacer, correct pacer mode
  • Patient factors that raise thresholds: large body habitus, pericardial effusion, severe metabolic derangement
  • TCP is PAINFUL at effective outputs — sedate with fentanyl, midazolam, ketamine, or propofol
  • Indications: TCP failure/poor capture, prolonged pacing needed (>30 min), overdrive pacing for torsades, more reliable pacing required
  • Access site preference: right IJ (straight path to RV, highest success) > left subclavian (reserve for permanent site) > femoral (higher infection/thrombosis, patient can't sit up)
  • Equipment: 5-6 Fr introducer sheath, balloon-tipped pacing catheter, pacing generator
  • Procedure: place sheath (ultrasound-guided), advance catheter to ~20 cm, inflate balloon and float to RV, confirm position (ECG injury current, POCUS, or fluoro), deflate balloon, test pacing
  • Settings: rate 60-80 ppm, output set at 2-3x threshold

These medications are BRIDGES to pacing, not definitive treatment — prepare for TCP/TVP while infusing. Isoproterenol (pure beta-agonist, 2-10 mcg/min) increases myocardial oxygen demand and is not routinely recommended, though it may be useful in transplant patients.

Atropine: 1 mg IV push, repeat q3-5 min, max 3 mg total IV push — First-line for sinus bradycardia. Ineffective in Mobitz II, complete heart block, transplant hearts.

Epinephrine: 2-10 mcg/min IV infusion IV infusion — Chronotropic AND inotropic; titrate to heart rate and blood pressure.

Dopamine: 5-20 mcg/kg/min IV infusion IV infusion — Higher doses more chronotropic. Alternative to epinephrine.

Device complications

Complication | Key Finding | Management

Generator pocket infection | Erythema, warmth, discharge, erosion ± fever/bacteremia | Blood cultures x3; superficial (<30d, no systemic signs): trial oral abx; true pocket infection: complete device extraction + IV abx 10-14d; with endocarditis: extraction + 4-6wk IV abx

Twiddler syndrome | Leads coiled around the generator on X-ray (patient manipulation) | Lead repositioning, secure generator to fascia

Lead fracture | Discontinuity/kinking at clavicle-first rib junction (subclavian crush) on CXR | Lead replacement

Pacemaker syndrome | Fatigue, dyspnea, palpitations, hypotension from loss of AV synchrony (VVI mode) | Upgrade to DDD pacing
