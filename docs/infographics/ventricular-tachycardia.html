Ventricular Tachycardia: Pulse, Stability, Rhythm

Adults with wide-complex tachycardia (QRS >120 ms, rate 150–250 bpm) confirmed or highly suspected to be VT, in the ED/resuscitation bay. Covers pulseless VT (cardiac arrest), unstable VT with a pulse, stable monomorphic VT, and polymorphic VT/Torsades. Excludes pediatrics and VAD-specific arrhythmia management.

⚠ Check pulse FIRST — pulseless VT and VT with a pulse have fundamentally different management protocols.
⚠ Pulseless VT: shock 200J biphasic immediately, then resume CPR right away — do NOT pause to check pulse or rhythm.
⚠ During the pulseless VT CPR cycle: epinephrine 1 mg IV/IO after the 2nd shock, repeated every 3–5 minutes; amiodarone 300 mg IV push after the 3rd shock (lidocaine 1–1.5 mg/kg IV is the alternative); maintain high-quality CPR at 100–120/min throughout.
⚠ In pulseless arrest, if the morphology is polymorphic/Torsades and the QTc is prolonged, do NOT give amiodarone — it prolongs QT and worsens Torsades; give magnesium sulfate instead.
⚠ Unstable VT (SBP <90 mmHg, altered mental status, ischemic chest pain, or acute pulmonary edema) requires immediate synchronized cardioversion — do not delay for pharmacologic therapy.
⚠ During synchronized cardioversion, confirm SYNC mode is engaged (shock must land on the R wave); if the patient deteriorates to pulseless, switch immediately to unsynchronized 200J.
⚠ Polymorphic VT with a prolonged baseline QTc (>480 ms) = Torsades de Pointes — treatment is the OPPOSITE of standard VT: do NOT give amiodarone or procainamide (both prolong QT and worsen Torsades).
⚠ Avoid amiodarone and procainamide if the baseline QTc is already prolonged — risk of inducing Torsades.
⚠ Do NOT combine amiodarone and procainamide in refractory stable VT — additive QT prolongation.

1 · Overview

Ventricular tachycardia is a wide-complex tachycardia (QRS >120 ms), rate usually 150–250 bpm. Key ECG features: AV dissociation (if present, highly specific for VT), concordance across precordial leads, and fusion/capture beats. Check pulse immediately — pulseless VT and VT with a pulse have fundamentally different protocols.

2 · What to do now

Pulseless VT — shock now
Cardiac arrest protocol. Shock 200J biphasic immediately, then resume CPR right away — do NOT pause to check pulse or rhythm.
Unstable VT — cardiovert now
SBP <90 mmHg, altered mental status, ischemic chest pain, or acute pulmonary edema → synchronized cardioversion 100J → 200J → 360J now. Confirm SYNC mode is on; if the patient deteriorates to pulseless, switch immediately to unsynchronized 200J.
Torsades — opposite treatment
Polymorphic VT with prolonged QTc (>480 ms) = Torsades de Pointes. Do NOT give amiodarone or procainamide. Magnesium sulfate 2–4 g IV over 15 min, overdrive pacing 90–110 bpm, stop all QT-prolonging drugs.
Polymorphic VT, normal/unknown QTc — treat as ischemia
Emergent cardiology consult + cath lab activation. Beta-blockers may help; amiodarone acceptable (QT not prolonged). Correct K+ target >4.0 and Mg2+ target >2.0.
Stable monomorphic VT — IV antiarrhythmic
Amiodarone 150 mg IV over 10 min OR procainamide 20–50 mg/min (may be preferred per 2023 AHA — higher termination rate). Avoid both if baseline QTc is already prolonged. Enter weight above to size lidocaine/procainamide doses.

Recurrent VT management
EP consult. ICD evaluation (EF ≤35%, or survived VT arrest). Amiodarone maintenance (200–400 mg PO daily) as bridge to ICD. Catheter ablation for recurrent monomorphic VT. Medication review — discontinue QT-prolonging agents. Beta-blocker therapy reduces VT recurrence in structural heart disease.

ICU with continuous telemetry (minimum 48 hours). Cardiology/EP consult. Echo if not done (assess EF, structural heart disease). ICD evaluation if EF ≤35% or survived VT arrest. Complete medication reconciliation. Electrolyte monitoring q4–6h until stable.
