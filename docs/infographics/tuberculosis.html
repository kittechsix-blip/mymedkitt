Tuberculosis — ED Evaluation, Diagnosis & Treatment

Adults >=18 with suspected or confirmed pulmonary tuberculosis, from ED evaluation through treatment-pathway selection (drug-susceptible, latent, drug-resistant, HIV co-infection). Excludes pediatric TB dosing and pregnancy-specific regimen adjustments.

⚠ Airborne isolation (negative pressure room, N95) required for active cough + TB risk factors, imaging consistent with active TB, known exposure + symptoms, or a positive AFB smear -- before further workup continues.
⚠ A positive NAA (with or without AFB smear positivity) in an at-risk, treatment-naive patient is sufficient for presumptive diagnosis -- do NOT delay treatment awaiting culture.
⚠ Never add a single drug to a failing or relapsing TB regimen -- this promotes further drug resistance.
⚠ MDR-TB (resistant to at least isoniazid + rifampin) requires expert TB consultation -- include at least 4-5 effective drugs, never add drugs one at a time.
⚠ Bedaquiline is contraindicated with rifampin -- rifampin reduces bedaquiline levels by 50%.
⚠ Hold bedaquiline/moxifloxacin-based regimens if QTc >500 ms.
⚠ Never delay TB treatment while awaiting antiretroviral therapy -- start TB treatment immediately regardless of ART timing.
⚠ CNS TB (TB meningitis): delay ART to 8 weeks regardless of CD4 count -- early ART carries high IRIS risk in this group.

Overview

Nearly 2 billion people (~25% of world population) are infected with *M. tuberculosis*. In 2024, an estimated 10.7 million became ill with TB (131/100,000 incidence), and 1.23 million died. Prompt diagnosis facilitates timely treatment and minimizes community transmission.

Suspect pulmonary TB with clinical manifestations AND epidemiologic risk factors:

Symptoms: cough >2-3 weeks, lymphadenopathy, fevers/night sweats, weight loss, hemoptysis.

Epidemiologic factors: prior TB infection/disease, known/possible TB exposure, past or present residence in/travel to a TB-endemic area, incarceration/homelessness/immigration, HIV infection or immunosuppression.

Patients may report no symptoms -- suspicion may be raised by incidental findings.

Specimen collection

Collect 3 sputum specimens at least 8 hours apart (including one early morning specimen) -- first-line. Induced sputum (hypertonic saline 3-5%, 15-20 minutes inhalation) if patient cannot produce; yields comparable to bronchoscopy in many studies. Bronchoscopy/BAL when sputum cannot be obtained or smear-negative with high suspicion -- aerosol-generating, full airborne precautions.

Hepatotoxicity
Definition: AST/ALT >3x ULN with symptoms (nausea, vomiting, abdominal pain, jaundice) OR >5x ULN without symptoms. Management: stop INH, RIF, PZA (continue ethambutol); bridge with moxifloxacin 400 mg PO daily; weekly LFTs until AST/ALT <2x ULN; stepwise rechallenge one drug at a time 1 week apart -- rifampin first, then isoniazid, then pyrazinamide last. If PZA permanently discontinued, extend total duration to 9 months.

Must exclude active TB disease before starting (symptom screen, CXR, baseline LFTs if risk factors).

MDR-TB -- guiding principles
MDR-TB = resistance to at least INH + RIF. Requires expert consultation. Never add a single drug to a failing regimen. Include at least 4-5 effective drugs. WHO Group A (preferred): fluoroquinolones, bedaquiline, linezolid. Group B: clofazimine, cycloserine. Group C: ethambutol, delamanid, pyrazinamide, imipenem, amikacin. Two approaches: abbreviated BPaL/BPaLM (6-9 months) or longer individualized (15-20 months).

ED evaluation & diagnostic algorithm

Tuberculosis: ED Evaluation — ~2 billion people (25% of world population) infected with M. tuberculosis. 10.7 million new cases in 2024 (131/100,000), 1.23 million deaths. Prompt diagnosis reduces transmission.

Assess clinical suspicion — Cough >2-3 weeks, lymphadenopathy, fever/night sweats, weight loss, hemoptysis PLUS epidemiologic risk factors (prior TB, known exposure, endemic travel/residence, incarceration/homelessness/immigration, HIV/immunosuppression). Patients may be asymptomatic.

Isolation criteria met? — Isolate (negative pressure room, N95) if: active cough + risk factors, imaging consistent with active TB, known exposure + symptoms, or AFB smear positive.

Specimen source? — Diagnosis requires isolation of M. tuberculosis from a bodily secretion, fluid, or tissue. 3 sputum specimens at least 8 hours apart (including 1 early morning) is first-line.

Sputum x3, >=8h apart — Expectorated or induced (hypertonic saline 3-5%, 15-20 min inhalation) if unable to produce. Early morning specimen highest yield.

Bronchoscopy / BAL — When sputum cannot be obtained or smear-negative with high suspicion. Aerosol-generating -- full airborne precautions.

Chest radiography — Upper lobe cavitation/infiltrates classic in immunocompetent patients. HIV+/immunocompromised may have lower lobe, non-cavitary, or normal CXR (up to 10-15%). Never treat on CXR alone.

AFB smear + NAA testing — AFB smear: same-day, sensitivity 50-80%. NAA: results in 2-8 hours, sensitivity 95-98% in smear-positive, 60-77% in smear-negative. GeneXpert MTB/RIF also detects rifampin resistance.

AFB/NAA result? — Culture remains the gold standard regardless of smear/NAA result -- send all specimens for mycobacterial culture.

AFB+/NAA-: consider NTM — Most likely non-tuberculous mycobacteria. Repeat NAA on new specimen. Treat empirically if TB risk high. Maintain isolation until excluded.

AFB-/NAA-: not excluded — Smear-negative TB not excluded. If suspicion high: continue isolation, repeat sputum, consider bronchoscopy. Urine LAM antigen if HIV+ with CD4 <200 (sensitivity ~50-60%).

Mycobacterial culture + DST — Gold standard. Solid media (Lowenstein-Jensen): 3-6 weeks. Rapid liquid culture (BACTEC MGIT 960, preferred): 1-3 weeks. Drug susceptibility testing on ALL initial isolates for at least isoniazid and rifampin.

Select treatment pathway — Notify public health (TB is reportable in all US states). Baseline LFTs, CBC, BMP, HIV testing, hepatitis B/C serologies.

Drug-susceptible TB: RIPE regimen — No resistance risk factors, or susceptibility confirmed. Standard ATS/CDC/IDSA regimen.

Latent TB infection — Positive TST/IGRA, no active disease. Treatment reduces reactivation risk up to 90%.

Drug-resistant TB — Risk factors present, resistance detected, or treatment failure. Management should involve TB specialists.

HIV-TB co-infection — TB with known HIV -- special ART timing and drug-interaction considerations.
