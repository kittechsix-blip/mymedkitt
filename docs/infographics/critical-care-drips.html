Vasopressors

SSC 2021/2026 Guidelines for Septic Shock:

First-Line: Norepinephrine
- Alpha-1 dominant + beta-1 moderate
- Fewer arrhythmias than dopamine
- Target MAP ≥65 mmHg (or 60-65 in elderly >65y per SSC 2026)

SSC 2026 Updates:
- Peripheral vasopressors OK to start (don't delay for central access)
- Septic shock + cardiac dysfunction: norepi OR epi as first-line

Second-Line Options:
- Vasopressin 0.04 U/min - catecholamine-sparing, fixed dose
- Epinephrine - if refractory to norepi + vasopressin

Special Situations:
- Phenylephrine - pure alpha, use if arrhythmias on norepi
- Dopamine - only if bradycardic hypotension

Select agent:

FIRST-LINE VASOPRESSOR - SEPTIC SHOCK

Mechanism:
- Alpha-1 dominant (vasoconstriction)
- Beta-1 moderate (inotropy)
- Minimal beta-2 effects

Dosing:
- Start: 2-5 mcg/min (0.01-0.03 mcg/kg/min)
- Titrate: +2-5 mcg/min every 5-10 min
- Target: MAP ≥65 mmHg (or 60-65 mmHg in elderly >65y per SSC 2026)
- No absolute max (refractory shock may need higher)

Administration (SSC 2026):
- Peripheral OK to start - don't delay for central access
- Peripheral acceptable at <20 mcg/min via antecubital or larger vein
- Central line preferred for prolonged/high-dose use
- Typical concentration: 16-128 mcg/mL

Titration Protocol:
- Check BP every 2-3 min during initial titration
- Once stable, check every 5 min
- If MAP >65 on high dose, consider adding vasopressin

Advantages:
- Fewer dysrhythmias vs dopamine
- Better outcomes than dopamine in septic shock

CATECHOLAMINE-SPARING ADJUNCT

Mechanism:
- V1 receptor agonist (vascular smooth muscle)
- Increases SVR without increasing PVR
- NO direct inotropy
- Non-catecholamine - works when catecholamines failing

Dosing:
- FIXED DOSE: 0.04 U/min
- DO NOT TITRATE
- Weight-independent
- If needed: max 0.08-0.1 U/min (monitor for ischemia)

When to Add:
- Patient on high-dose norepinephrine
- Goal: reduce norepinephrine requirement
- Vasodilation is primary problem

Cautions:
- Mesenteric ischemia
- Coronary vasoconstriction
- Digital ischemia (more than catecholamines)
- NO reversal agent for extravasation
- Prefer central line

Key Point: Add to norepi, don't replace

REFRACTORY SHOCK / ANAPHYLAXIS

Mechanism: Dose-dependent receptor effects
- Low dose (1-4 mcg/min): Beta effects dominate (↑CO, ↓SVR)
- Medium (5-10 mcg/min): Mixed alpha/beta
- High (>10 mcg/min): Alpha effects dominate (↑SVR)

Dosing:
- Start: 1-4 mcg/min (0.01-0.04 mcg/kg/min)
- Titrate: +1-2 mcg/min every 20 min
- Typical range: 2-10 mcg/min
- Max: 0.5 mcg/kg/min

Half-life: ~2 minutes (extremely rapid)

Indications:
- Anaphylaxis (after IM epi)
- Cardiac arrest (VF/pVT/asystole)
- Refractory shock on max norepi + vasopressin
- Severe bradycardia with hypotension

Cautions:
- High doses increase myocardial O2 demand
- Worsens post-resuscitation myocardial dysfunction
- Arrhythmogenic at high doses

Epinephrine (Adrenaline)
Start 1-4 mcg/min (0.01-0.04 mcg/kg/min); titrate +1-2 mcg/min q20min; typical 2-10 mcg/min; max 0.5 mcg/kg/min
IV
Dose-dependent alpha/beta effects; half-life ~2 minutes.

PURE ALPHA-1 AGONIST

Mechanism:
- Exclusively alpha-1 adrenergic
- Pure vasoconstriction, no direct inotropy
- NO beta effects

Dosing:
- Start: 100-200 mcg/min
- Maintenance: 40-80 mcg/min (taper once BP controlled)
- Push-dose: 100 mcg boluses PRN

Indications:
- Norepinephrine-induced arrhythmias
- High cardiac output + hypotension (pure distributive)
- Adjunct to vasopressin/inotropes

Cautions:
- Reflex bradycardia - can decrease CO
- Mesenteric/renal/coronary ischemia risk
- Peripheral tissue necrosis (central line preferred)

When to Use:
- Patient tachycardic on norepinephrine
- Arrhythmias limiting norepi dose
- Pure vasodilation without cardiac dysfunction

Phenylephrine (Neosynephrine)
Start 100-200 mcg/min; maintenance 40-80 mcg/min; push-dose 100 mcg boluses PRN
IV
Pure alpha-1 — no beta effects; causes reflex bradycardia; central line preferred.

DOSE-DEPENDENT EFFECTS - NOT FIRST-LINE

SCCM 2021: Recommends AGAINST dopamine as first-line (more arrhythmogenic than norepi)

Mechanism: Dose-dependent receptors
- Low (2-5 mcg/kg/min): DA effects (splanchnic vasodilation)
- Medium (5-10 mcg/kg/min): Beta-1 (↑CO, ↑HR, ↑SVR)
- High (>15 mcg/kg/min): Alpha-1 (pure vasoconstriction)

Dosing:
- Start: 2-5 mcg/kg/min
- Titrate: q5-10 min as needed
- Max: 20 mcg/kg/min

Limited Indications:
- Bradycardic hypotension (best use case)
- When arrhythmias are not a concern

Why Avoid:
- More arrhythmogenic than norepinephrine
- Worse outcomes in septic shock trials
- "Renal-dose dopamine" is a myth - no benefit proven

Inotropes

For Cardiogenic Shock / Low Output States

Dobutamine:
- Beta-1/beta-2 agonist
- ↑Contractility, ↑HR, causes vasodilation
- Risk: hypotension, tachycardia

Milrinone:
- PDE-3 inhibitor ("inodilator")
- ↑Contractility + vasodilation
- Better for RV failure, pulmonary HTN
- Fewer arrhythmias than dobutamine

Key Difference:
- Milrinone: 32.8% significant arrhythmias
- Dobutamine: 62.9% significant arrhythmias

BETA-1/BETA-2 AGONIST INOTROPE

Mechanism:
- Beta-1 dominant (↑contractility, ↑HR, ↑CO)
- Beta-2 secondary (peripheral vasodilation)
- Minimal alpha effects

Dosing:
- Start: 2.5-5 mcg/kg/min
- Titrate: +2.5 mcg/kg/min increments
- Max: 20+ mcg/kg/min (titrate to effect)

Indications:
- Cardiogenic shock (with hypotension)
- Low output heart failure
- When norepinephrine alone insufficient

Cautions:
- Tachycardia and arrhythmias (especially >10 mcg/kg/min)
- Worsens myocardial ischemia in CAD
- Peripheral vasodilation may worsen hypotension
- Often needs norepinephrine alongside

Clinical Pearl:
- If patient becomes MORE hypotensive, switch to milrinone or add vasopressor

PDE-3 INHIBITOR - "INODILATOR"

Mechanism:
- Phosphodiesterase-3 inhibitor
- ↑Inotropy (independent of adrenergic receptors)
- ↑Lusitropy (diastolic function)
- Peripheral vasodilation (↓afterload/PVR)

Dosing:
- NO loading dose (hypotension risk)
- Start: 0.25 mcg/kg/min
- Titrate: +0.125-0.25 mcg/kg/min every 5-15 min
- Range: 0.25-0.75 mcg/kg/min
- Max: 1.13 mcg/kg/min

Indications:
- Cardiogenic shock with elevated SVR
- Acute heart failure with elevated afterload
- RV dysfunction / pulmonary HTN (preferred)
- Septic shock (not first-line)

Advantages vs Dobutamine:
- Fewer arrhythmias (32.8% vs 62.9%)
- Better for pulmonary hypertension
- No increase in myocardial O2 demand

Caution: More hypotension than dobutamine

Hypertensive Emergency

General Principle: Reduce MAP by 20-25% over first hour using short-acting, titratable IV agents.

Condition-Specific Targets:

Aortic Dissection:
- SBP <120, HR <60 ASAP
- Esmolol FIRST, then vasodilator

Acute Ischemic Stroke (tPA candidate):
- SBP <185, DBP <110
- Nicardipine or labetalol

Eclampsia/Preeclampsia:
- SBP <160, DBP <110
- Labetalol or hydralazine

ACS with Hypertension:
- Reduce afterload, preserve coronary flow
- Nicardipine or nitroglycerin

Select clinical scenario:

TARGET: SBP <120 mmHg, HR <60 bpm ASAP

Goal: Reduce dP/dt (aortic shear stress)

STEP 1: BETA-BLOCKER FIRST

Esmolol (preferred - ultra-short acting):
- Load: 500-1000 mcg/kg over 1 min
- Infusion: 50 mcg/kg/min, titrate to 200-300 mcg/kg/min
- Onset: 60 seconds
- Duration: 10-20 min (if problems, wears off fast)

Labetalol (alternative):
- 20 mg IV bolus, then 20-80 mg q10min (max 300mg)
- OR infusion 1-3 mg/min

STEP 2: ADD VASODILATOR IF BP STILL ELEVATED

After HR controlled (<60):
- Nicardipine 5-15 mg/hr OR
- Nitroprusside 0.3-2 mcg/kg/min (with beta-blocker!)

CRITICAL: Never give vasodilator alone (reflex tachycardia worsens dissection)

  1. STEP 1 — beta-blocker first: esmolol load 500-1000 mcg/kg over 1 min, then infusion 50 mcg/kg/min titrated to 200-300 mcg/kg/min (or labetalol 20 mg IV bolus then 20-80 mg q10min, max 300 mg, or infusion 1-3 mg/min).
  2. Target HR <60 bpm and SBP <120 mmHg before adding a vasodilator.
  3. STEP 2 — add a vasodilator only once HR is controlled: nicardipine 5-15 mg/hr OR nitroprusside 0.3-2 mcg/kg/min (with the beta-blocker).
  4. CRITICAL: never give a vasodilator alone — reflex tachycardia worsens the dissection.

Starting:

- pH >7.3

- Give first SQ dose, continue drip 1-2h overlap

- Cannot give insulin if K <2.5 (fatal arrhythmias)

- Vasopressors (septic, distributive, hypovolemic)

- Hypotension and bradycardia (especially with bolus)
- Skip bolus in unstable patients
- Hypertension possible at high rates (biphasic)
- Tachyphylaxis with prolonged use
- Limit to 5 days for maintenance

Clinical Pearl: Start high, titrate down (avoids bolus hypotension)

Dexmedetomidine (Precedex): Optional load 0.5-1 mcg/kg over 10 min (causes hypotension) — better to start infusion at 1-1.4 mcg/kg/hr and titrate down; maintenance range 0.3-1.4 mcg/kg/hr IV — NO respiratory depression; can continue through extubation; skip the bolus in unstable patients; limit to 5 days (tachyphylaxis/delirium risk).

NMDA ANTAGONIST - PRESERVES BP

Mechanism:
- NMDA receptor antagonist
- Maintains catecholamine levels (sympathomimetic)
- Unique preserved hemodynamics

Dosing:
- Low-dose sedation: 0.1-0.3 mg/kg/hr
- Standard range: 1-2 mg/kg/hr
- High-dose/refractory: 1-5 mg/kg/hr
- Onset: <1 min
- Duration: 15-30 min per dose

Advantages:
- PRESERVES/INCREASES BP (unlike propofol/dex)
- Preserves respiratory drive
- Analgesia + sedation (opioid-sparing)
- Less hypotension: 34.6% vs 63.5% (propofol/dex)
- Excellent for hemodynamically unstable/septic patients

Cautions:
- Dissociation/hallucinations
- Increased salivation (may need antisialagogue)
- Avoid in ACS (increased catecholamines)
- ICP may increase (use cautiously in head injury)

Combinations:
- Ketofol: Ketamine + propofol 1:1 (hemodynamic stability)
- Ketamine + Dex: Reduces dissociative effects

Ketamine (Ketalar): Low-dose sedation 0.1-0.3 mg/kg/hr; standard range 1-2 mg/kg/hr; high-dose/refractory 1-5 mg/kg/hr IV — Preserves BP and respiratory drive; less hypotension than propofol/dex (34.6% vs 63.5%); avoid in ACS; use cautiously with elevated ICP.

BENZODIAZEPINE - FALLING OUT OF FAVOR

Dosing:
- Load: 10-50 mcg/kg (optional)
- Infusion: 1-7 mg/hr (2.1-14 mg/hr for 70kg)
- Onset: 1-3 min
- Duration: 15-80 min (highly variable)

Why Avoid as First-Line:
- Metabolite accumulation in renal failure (half-life 7.6-13h in ARF)
- Delayed emergence with prolonged infusions
- Tolerance develops rapidly
- Withdrawal possible with abrupt stop
- Active metabolites prolong effects

Advantages:
- Reversible with flumazenil
- Anxiolytic
- Relatively cheap

Modern Use:
- Reserve for propofol/dex unavailable or contraindicated
- Avoid in acute renal failure
- Avoid for prolonged sedation (>24-48h)

Reversal: Flumazenil 0.2 mg IV q1min (max 3 mg)

Midazolam (Versed): Optional load 10-50 mcg/kg; infusion 1-7 mg/hr IV — Falling out of favor as first-line — metabolite accumulation in renal failure, delayed emergence, rapid tolerance; reversible with flumazenil 0.2 mg IV q1min (max 3 mg).

Analgesia

Analgesia-First Approach

Start with PRN analgesia, reserve infusions for refractory pain.

Agent Selection:

Fentanyl (preferred):
- Fastest onset (<1 min)
- NO active metabolites (renal-safe)
- Most commonly used (83.7% of vented patients)

Hydromorphone:
- Alternative to fentanyl
- No active metabolites
- Slower onset (10-15 min)

Morphine:
- AVOID in renal failure (active metabolites)
- AVOID continuous infusions
- Histamine release → hypotension

Equianalgesic:
100 mcg fentanyl = 10 mg morphine = 1.5 mg hydromorphone

Select agent:

SYNTHETIC OPIOID - ICU PREFERRED

Potency: 80-100x morphine
100 mcg fentanyl = 10 mg morphine IV

Dosing:
- PRN bolus: 25-200 mcg IV q30-60min
- Infusion: 50-150 mcg/hr (typical range)
- Onset: <1 minute
- Duration: 30-60 min per bolus

Advantages:
- Fastest onset of opioids
- NO active metabolites (safe in renal failure)
- Minimal histamine release
- No accumulation even with prolonged infusion
- Preferred in renal failure

Cautions:
- Not for opioid-naive (start with bolus, assess)
- Respiratory depression (requires vent or close monitoring)
- Bradycardia (mu-opioid effect)
- Tolerance develops with prolonged use

Clinical Pearl: 83.7% of mechanically ventilated patients receive fentanyl

Fentanyl Infusion: PRN bolus 25-200 mcg IV q30-60min; infusion 50-150 mcg/hr IV — Fastest onset of the opioids (<1 min); NO active metabolites — safe in renal failure; 83.7% of mechanically ventilated ICU patients receive fentanyl.

SYNTHETIC OPIOID - FENTANYL ALTERNATIVE

Potency: 5-7x morphine
1.5 mg hydromorphone = 10 mg morphine = 100 mcg fentanyl

Dosing:
- PRN bolus: 0.3-2 mg IV q1-2h
- Infusion: 0.2-1 mg/hr
- Onset: 10-15 min (slower than fentanyl)
- Duration: 3-4 hours

Advantages:
- Excellent analgesia
- NO active metabolites (safe in renal failure)
- Can substitute for fentanyl allergy

Cautions:
- Slower onset than fentanyl (10-15 min vs <1 min)
- Respiratory depression
- Tolerance develops

Comparison to Fentanyl:
- No demonstrated advantage in reducing vent days
- No difference in ICU length of stay
- Use based on preference or fentanyl intolerance

Hydromorphone (Dilaudid): PRN bolus 0.3-2 mg IV q1-2h; infusion 0.2-1 mg/hr IV — Fentanyl alternative; NO active metabolites; slower onset (10-15 min vs <1 min for fentanyl).

Endocrine

Critical Care Endocrine Drips

DKA/HHS:
- Insulin infusion protocols
- When to reduce rate (glucose 250)
- Transition to subcutaneous

Adrenal Crisis:
- Hydrocortisone 100 mg IV bolus
- 200 mg/24h replacement

Variceal Bleeding:
- Octreotide infusion
- Reduces splanchnic blood flow

Select condition:

DIABETIC KETOACIDOSIS INSULIN DRIP

Starting:
- Bolus: 0.1 U/kg IV (optional, can omit)
- Infusion: 0.1 U/kg/hr (range 0.07-0.14)
- Max for nursing: 15 U/hr (notify provider if higher needed)

Titration Goal:
- Glucose drop: 50-70 mg/dL/hr
- Too fast → reduce rate
- Not dropping → increase q1-2h

WHEN GLUCOSE REACHES 250:
- DO NOT STOP INSULIN (need to clear ketoacidosis)
- Reduce rate to 0.05 U/kg/hr
- Add D5-1/2 NS at 150 mL/hr

DKA Resolution Criteria:
- pH >7.3
- Anion gap ≤12
- Bicarb ≥18 mEq/L OR BHB <0.6 mmol/L

Transition to SQ:
- Estimate: (current U/hr) x 24 = daily SQ dose
- Give first SQ dose, continue drip 1-2h overlap
- Stop drip 2h after SQ given

CRITICAL - POTASSIUM:
- Cannot give insulin if K <2.5 (fatal arrhythmias)
- Replete K aggressively before/during insulin

DKA Insulin Protocol: Optional bolus 0.1 units/kg IV, then 0.1 units/kg/hr continuous infusion (range 0.07-0.14 units/kg/hr); nursing max 15 U/hr IV — Target glucose drop 50-70 mg/dL/hr. At glucose 250: reduce to 0.05 units/kg/hr and add D5-½NS — do NOT stop the infusion. Cannot give insulin if K <2.5 mEq/L (fatal arrhythmias).

DKA insulin: do NOT stop when glucose reaches 250 - reduce rate and add dextrose

ACUTE ADRENAL INSUFFICIENCY

Emergency Treatment - DO NOT DELAY:

Hydrocortisone:
- Initial: 100 mg IV/IM IMMEDIATELY
- 24h replacement: 200 mg total
- Option 1: 100 mg infusion over 24h + 50 mg q6h boluses
- Option 2: 100 mg in 100 mL NS at 10-12 mL/hr
- Repeat 100 mg in first few hours if needed

Fluid Resuscitation:
- 1 L 0.9% NaCl IV over 30 min
- Continue aggressive fluids
- Add vasopressors as needed

After Stabilization (Days 2-3):
- Reduce to 100-150 mg/day
- Taper over 4-5 days to maintenance (15-20 mg/day)
- No separate mineralocorticoid if hydrocortisone ≥100 mg/day

Alternative if No Hydrocortisone:
- Methylprednisolone 40 mg IV daily
- Less mineralocorticoid activity

Response: BP improvement expected within 4-6 hours

Adrenal Crisis: Hydrocortisone 100 mg IV/IM immediately, then 200 mg total over 24h (100 mg infusion + 50 mg q6h boluses, or 100 mg/100 mL NS at 10-12 mL/hr) IV — Do NOT delay treatment; 1 L 0.9% NaCl over 30 min plus aggressive fluids; taper over 4-5 days to maintenance 15-20 mg/day after stabilization.

SOMATOSTATIN ANALOG - VARICEAL HEMORRHAGE

Mechanism:
- Decreases splanchnic blood flow
- Reduces portal pressure
- Safer than vasopressin

Dosing:
- Bolus: 50-100 mcg IV
- Infusion: 50 mcg/hr for 3-5 days
- Alternative: 25 mcg/hr (also effective, cheaper)

When to Start:
- IMMEDIATELY upon clinical suspicion
- Do NOT wait for endoscopy confirmation

Duration: Continue 3-5 days
- Paired with endoscopic therapy
- NOT a replacement for sclerotherapy/banding

Efficacy: Controls bleeding in ~80% of variceal hemorrhage

Combine With:
- Antibiotics (ceftriaxone 1g IV daily)
- PPI
- Endoscopic therapy

Advantages over Vasopressin:
- Fewer complications
- Better outcomes
- No need for nitrate co-administration

Octreotide (Variceal Bleeding): Bolus 50-100 mcg IV, then infusion 50 mcg/hr for 3-5 days (alternative 25 mcg/hr) IV — Start IMMEDIATELY on clinical suspicion of variceal bleeding — do not wait for endoscopy; pair with antibiotics, PPI, and endoscopic therapy.

Antiarrhythmics

Life-Threatening Arrhythmia Management

VF/pVT (Cardiac Arrest):
- Amiodarone 300 mg IV or Lidocaine 1-1.5 mg/kg
- Either acceptable per AHA 2018

Wide-Complex Tachycardia (Stable):
- Amiodarone or procainamide

Amiodarone:
- Max 2.2 g/24h
- Multiple class effects (I, II, III, IV)
- Long half-life (15-100 days)

Lidocaine:
- Max 4.5 mg/kg (toxicity threshold)
- Faster onset than amiodarone
- Shorter duration (10-20 min)

Select agent:

CLASS III ANTIARRHYTHMIC (+ I, II, IV properties)

ACLS Dosing (VF/pVT):
- First dose: 300 mg IV/IO bolus
- Second dose: 150 mg if VF persists
- Max 24h: 2.2 g

ICU Infusion Protocol:
- Bolus: 150 mg IV over 10 min
- Phase 1: 1 mg/min for 6 hours (360 mg)
- Phase 2: 0.5 mg/min for 18+ hours
- Repeat 150 mg bolus q10-30 min for breakthrough

Onset: 15-30 min (slower than lidocaine)
Half-life: 15-100 days (very long!)

Adverse Effects:
- Bradycardia, heart block (2-5%)
- Hypotension (especially IV bolus)
- QT prolongation (torsades risk 2-5%)
- Thyroid dysfunction (long-term)
- Pulmonary toxicity (long-term)
- Phlebitis (use central line if possible)

Avoid if: Baseline QT prolongation

Amiodarone: ACLS: 300 mg IV/IO bolus, then 150 mg if VF persists (max 2.2 g/24h); ICU infusion: 150 mg over 10 min, then 1 mg/min ×6h, then 0.5 mg/min ×18+h IV — Onset 15-30 min; half-life 15-100 days; QT prolongation/torsades risk 2-5%; avoid if baseline QT prolonged.

Amiodarone max 2.2g/24h; lidocaine max 4.5 mg/kg (toxicity threshold)

CLASS IB ANTIARRHYTHMIC

ACLS Dosing (VF/pVT):
- First dose: 1-1.5 mg/kg IV bolus
- Repeat: 0.5-0.75 mg/kg q5-10 min
- MAX CUMULATIVE: 4.5 mg/kg (toxicity threshold)

Example (70 kg):
- First: 70-105 mg
- Repeat: 35-52 mg
- Max total: 315 mg

ICU Infusion:
- Load: 1-1.5 mg/kg
- Maintenance: 1-4 mg/min
- Max: 3 mg/min in heart disease/elderly/liver failure

Onset: <1 minute (FASTER than amiodarone)
Duration: 10-20 min (short, allows rapid titration)

Toxicity Signs (dose-dependent):
- 5-10 mcg/mL: Drowsiness, paresthesias
- 10-15 mcg/mL: Tremors, confusion, tinnitus
- 15-25 mcg/mL: Seizures, LOC
- >25 mcg/mL: Cardiovascular collapse

Management: Stop drug, benzos for seizures, lipid emulsion if severe

Lidocaine: ACLS: 1-1.5 mg/kg IV bolus, repeat 0.5-0.75 mg/kg q5-10min (max cumulative 4.5 mg/kg); ICU infusion 1-4 mg/min (max 3 mg/min in heart disease/elderly/liver failure) IV — Onset <1 min — faster than amiodarone; toxicity is dose-dependent: seizures/LOC at 15-25 mcg/mL, cardiovascular collapse >25 mcg/mL.

Rate Control

For Stable AFib/Aflutter with RVR

Diltiazem:
- Non-dihydropyridine CCB
- 30% HR reduction by 30 min
- First-line in COPD/asthma (vs beta-blockers)

Esmolol:
- Ultra-short acting beta-blocker
- Onset 60 sec, offset 10-20 min
- Preferred in ACS

Both agents:
- Avoid in decompensated heart failure
- Avoid if WPW with AF (use procainamide)

Select agent:

NON-DIHYDROPYRIDINE CCB - AV NODAL BLOCKER

IV Bolus Dosing:
- First: 0.25 mg/kg over 2 min (e.g., 17.5 mg for 70 kg)
- Second (if needed): 0.35 mg/kg after 15 min (~24.5 mg)
- Max single bolus: 30 mg

IV Infusion:
- Start after bolus controls rate
- Range: 5-15 mg/hr
- Typical: 10 mg/hr

Onset: 2-3 min
Duration: 30 min to 2 hours

Advantages:
- First-line in COPD/asthma (vs beta-blockers)
- ~30% HR reduction by 30 min

Avoid:
- Decompensated heart failure
- SBP <90
- Severe bradycardia/AV block
- WPW with AF (use procainamide)

Alternative Protocol:
- IV diltiazem bolus + PO diltiazem 60 mg
- Avoids continuous infusion

Diltiazem (Rate Control): 0.25 mg/kg IV bolus over 2 min (~20-25 mg); if inadequate after 15 min, 0.35 mg/kg second bolus; then infusion 5-15 mg/hr (typical 10 mg/hr) IV — First-line in COPD/asthma; ~30% HR reduction by 30 min; avoid in decompensated HF, SBP <90, WPW with A-Fib.

ULTRA-SHORT ACTING BETA-1 BLOCKER

Dosing:
- Load: 0.25-0.5 mg/kg IV bolus (lower than HTN dosing)
- Infusion: 50 mcg/kg/min, titrate up q5 min
- Range: 50-300 mcg/kg/min
- Max: 300 mcg/kg/min

Onset: 60 seconds
Offset: 10-20 min (esterase metabolism)

Best Uses:
- AFib/flutter with RVR in ACS
- Perioperative tachycardia
- When rapid titration needed
- When complications from beta-blockade are a concern

Key Advantage:
- If hypotension/bradycardia occurs, wears off in 10-20 min
- No accumulation with prolonged use

Avoid:
- Asthma/severe COPD
- Decompensated heart failure
- Severe bradycardia/AV block
- WPW with AF

Esmolol (Rate Control): Load 0.25-0.5 mg/kg IV bolus (lower than HTN dosing — drug-store fixed loading value is 500 mcg/kg, the upper end of this range); infusion 50 mcg/kg/min titrated up q5min; range 50-300 mcg/kg/min IV — Ultra-short acting — wears off in 10-20 min if hypotension/bradycardia occurs; avoid in asthma/severe COPD, decompensated HF, WPW with A-Fib.
