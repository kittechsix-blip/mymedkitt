ICH: Reverse the Blood Thinner, Drop the Pressure

Adults ≥18 with spontaneous (non-traumatic) intracerebral hemorrhage in the ED/ICU. Excludes traumatic ICH, subarachnoid hemorrhage, ischemic stroke, and pediatrics — each needs a distinct pathway.

⚠ ICH is two clocks at once — reverse anticoagulation AND lower BP simultaneously. Both are time-critical; do not sequence them.
⚠ Do NOT delay anticoagulant reversal for lab results. If there is clinical suspicion of anticoagulation, give empiric reversal immediately.
⚠ Warfarin ICH: 4-factor PCC 25–50 IU/kg + Vitamin K 10 mg IV — target INR <1.4. PCC alone rebounds without Vitamin K.
⚠ Dabigatran ICH: Idarucizumab 5 g IV (two 2.5 g boluses) — immediate, complete reversal.
⚠ Xa-inhibitor ICH: 4-factor PCC 50 IU/kg — the most widely available option if andexanet alfa is unavailable.
⚠ Heparin ICH: Protamine 1 mg per 100 U heparin given in the last 2–3h, max single dose 50 mg — watch for anaphylaxis and hypotension.
⚠ Do NOT transfuse platelets for antiplatelet-associated ICH — the PATCH trial showed worse outcomes.
⚠ Cerebellar hematoma >15 mL or with brainstem compression / hydrocephalus = emergent surgical evacuation. Do NOT just place an EVD — herniation can occur within 12–24h.

1 · First 10 minutes

Why ICH is deadly
Spontaneous ICH is 10–15% of all strokes but carries ~40% 30-day mortality — the hematoma is the injury, and it is still growing. Expansion risk is highest in the first 3 hours, so every minute of delay costs brain.[1][2]
Two clocks, run them together
Reverse anticoagulation AND lower the blood pressure at the same time. These are the two interventions that limit hematoma growth. Do not finish one before starting the other.[1]
  1. Non-contrast CT head — confirm diagnosis, estimate hematoma volume by ABC/2.
  2. Two large-bore IVs; send STAT coags and check the antithrombotic medication list.
  3. Activate stroke / neurosurgery; keep the patient NPO (potential surgical candidate).
  4. Give empiric anticoagulant reversal now if there is clinical suspicion — do NOT wait for the INR.

2 · Evaluation & imaging

  • CBC, BMP, Mg, Phos; INR, PTT, fibrinogen.
  • Anti-Xa level if on apixaban / rivaroxaban (do not delay reversal for it).
  • Type & screen; toxicology if warranted; ABG/VBG if intubated.
Hematoma volume — ABC/2
A × B × C ÷ 2 (cm) = volume in mL. A = largest axial diameter · B = perpendicular diameter on the same slice · C = number of slices with blood × slice thickness. <30 mL = relatively favorable; >30 mL = moderate-to-severe.[3]

On CTA, a spot sign flags patients at high risk of active extravasation and expansion. Swirl and black-hole signs on non-contrast CT carry the same warning.[3]

4 · Lower the blood pressure

Treat pain and anxiety first
Hypertension in ICH is often secondary to pain, agitation, or under-sedation — especially in intubated patients. Adequate analgesia/sedation alone can bring the pressure down before you titrate a drip.[1]

5 · Location & surgery

Hematoma location determines the surgical question. Supratentorial bleeds (basal ganglia/thalamus/lobar) are ~80%; the cerebellum is the one location where surgery is unambiguously life-saving. Intraventricular extension occurs in >40% and worsens prognosis.[3][12]

Supratentorial hematoma — usually medicalSTITCH I/II
Routine early surgery does not help deep supratentorial ICH (STITCH-I, STITCH-II).[9][10] Reserve craniotomy for a large lobar clot with midline shift, refractory ICP, or a resectable lesion (AVM, tumor). Deep basal-ganglia bleeds are managed medically.
Cerebellar hematoma — surgical emergencyact fast
Evacuate emergently if hematoma >15 mL (≈ >3 cm), neurological deterioration, brainstem compression, or hydrocephalus.[1] Patients can herniate within 12–24h. An EVD alone is not sufficient — the posterior fossa clot must come out. With timely surgery, cerebellar ICH prognosis is better than other types.
Intraventricular hemorrhage & EVDhydrocephalus
Place an EVD for hydrocephalus that is lowering consciousness. It improves survival but may not improve good functional outcome. Intraventricular tPA (CLEAR III) improved survival without better function — it does not replace the drain.[13]

7 · Disposition & prognosis

All ICH → ICU
Admit every ICH to the ICU for continuous neuro monitoring and BP control. Neuro checks q1h; serial CT at 6h, 24h, and with any decline. Transition IV antihypertensives to PO after >24h if stable; start early rehab consultation.

Prognosticate with the ICH Score (0–6, 30-day mortality) and the FUNC Score (90-day function), but avoid early self-fulfilling nihilism — hold goals-of-care conversations with the family, especially for severe bleeds.[3]
