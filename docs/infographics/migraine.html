Migraine: Rule Out Red Flags, Treat the Cocktail, Rescue Live

Adults ≥18 in the ED with acute headache being evaluated for migraine (meeting or probably meeting ICHD-3 criteria) after SNNOOP10 red flags are excluded. Not a standalone tool for diagnosing SAH, meningitis, GCA, or stroke — those require their own workup. Excludes pediatric headache and confirmed secondary headache.

⚠ Rule out secondary causes FIRST. SNNOOP10 red flags — thunderclap onset, worst headache of life, new neurologic deficit, fever + neck stiffness, new headache onset age >50, papilledema, post-traumatic, pregnancy/postpartum, or a progressively worsening course over days-weeks — mandate a secondary-cause workup (CT ± LP ± CTA ± ESR/CRP) before treating as migraine.
⚠ Avoid opioids — Level A against. Opioids (including hydromorphone) are ineffective for acute migraine and worsen rebound / medication-overuse headache.
⚠ Triptans and DHE are contraindicated in CAD or prior MI/stroke, uncontrolled hypertension, hemiplegic or basilar migraine, within 24h of an ergot derivative, or within 14 days of an MAOI.
⚠ Valproate IV is contraindicated in pregnancy (teratogenic — neural tube defects) and in hepatic disease or a pancreatitis history.

2 · Diagnosis (ICHD-3)

ICHD-3 migraine without aura requires ≥5 attacks meeting B–D below (A). Migraine with aura adds fully reversible visual, sensory, or speech symptoms lasting 5-60 min, followed by headache within 60 min.[1][3]

  1. B. Duration 4-72 hours (untreated)
  2. C. ≥2 of 4: unilateral location, pulsating quality, moderate-severe intensity, aggravated by routine physical activity
  3. D. During headache, ≥1 of: nausea and/or vomiting, OR photophobia AND phonophobia
  4. E. Not better explained by another diagnosis

Medication overuse headache: ≥15 days/month with regular acute-med overuse ≥3 months — withdraw the offending medication.

6 · One-glance algorithm

Headache — assess for migraine — Primary headache (migraine, tension-type, cluster) accounts for most ED headache visits — but red flags must be excluded first.

SNNOOP10 red flag? — Thunderclap onset, worst headache of life, new neuro deficit, fever+neck stiffness, age >50 new onset, papilledema, post-traumatic, pregnancy, progressive course.

Secondary-cause workup — CT head non-contrast for thunderclap (~95% sens for SAH <6h); LP for meningitis or if CT negative + high suspicion; ESR/CRP for GCA if age >50.

Meets ICHD-3 migraine criteria? — ≥5 attacks, 4-72h duration, ≥2 of (unilateral / pulsating / moderate-severe / worse with activity), plus N/V or photophobia+phonophobia.

Other primary headache — Tension-type: bilateral, non-pulsating, NSAIDs. Cluster: unilateral orbital pain + autonomic features, O₂ + sumatriptan SC.

Severity? — Mild = oral therapy. Moderate/Severe = IV cocktail. Status migrainosus (>72h) = cocktail + aggressive hydration ± DHE.

Oral NSAID + triptan — Ibuprofen/naproxen + sumatriptan combination; escalate to parenteral therapy if no improvement in 1-2h.

IV migraine cocktail — Diphenhydramine FIRST (akathisia prophylaxis), prochlorperazine 10 mg over 15 min (Level A), ketorolac 15-30 mg, NS bolus if dehydrated.

Status migrainosus: cocktail + IV fluids — 1-2L NS + standard cocktail + dexamethasone; if refractory, consider the DHE protocol (admission, telemetry) or IV valproate/magnesium.

Response at 30-60 min? — Good (≥50% pain reduction, tolerating PO) → disposition. Partial/none → rescue therapy.

Rescue options — GON block Level A; sumatriptan SC 6 mg Level B; valproate/magnesium Level C. Avoid opioids (Level A against).

Greater occipital nerve block — 2-3 mL 0.5% bupivacaine per side; onset 5-10 min, no systemic effects.

Sumatriptan SC 6 mg — May repeat ×1 after ≥1h, max 12 mg/24h. Contraindicated in CAD, prior MI/stroke, uncontrolled HTN, hemiplegic/basilar migraine.

Valproate IV 500-1000 mg — Level C, third-line. Contraindicated in pregnancy (teratogenic), hepatic disease, pancreatitis history.

SPG block (intranasal) — 2% lidocaine, cotton applicator or atomizer. Level U evidence but safe, non-invasive alternative to GON block.

Improved after rescue? — Improved → disposition. Still refractory → consider admission/DHE/neurology.

Refractory — admit / DHE / neurology — Admission criteria: refractory to ≥3 parenteral treatments, severe dehydration, unable to tolerate PO, or need for the DHE protocol.
