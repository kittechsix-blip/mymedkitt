Acute Migraine in the ED: Red Flags First, Then the Cocktail

Adults and adolescents presenting to the ED with headache when a primary migraine is suspected. Use only AFTER SNNOOP10 red flags are screened. Excludes pregnancy/postpartum, definite secondary headache, and children who have not been weight-dosed.

⚠ Rule out secondary causes FIRST. Ottawa SAH Rule: sudden-onset worst headache, thunderclap onset, age ≥40 with new headache = CT + LP if negative. A red flag stops the migraine pathway.
⚠ Avoid opioids — they cause rebound headache and medication overuse, and are inferior to the cocktail. They are not a migraine treatment.
⚠ Triptans contraindicated in basilar/hemiplegic migraine, uncontrolled HTN, recent stroke/MI, and CAD. Screen before giving any triptan.
⚠ Give diphenhydramine BEFORE the dopamine antagonist to prevent akathisia and acute dystonia from prochlorperazine/metoclopramide.

2 · Confirm migraine (ICHD-3)

  • ≥ 5 attacks meeting the criteria below
  • Duration 4–72 hours untreated
  • ≥ 2 of 4: unilateral · pulsating · moderate-severe · aggravated by routine activity
  • During headache ≥ 1 of: nausea/vomiting · photophobia AND phonophobia
  • Not better explained by another diagnosis

Migraine with aura: add fully reversible visual, sensory, or speech symptoms lasting 5–60 min, followed by headache within 60 min.

5 · Disposition

Before discharge: dexamethasone
Dexamethasone 10 mg IV/IM before discharge prevents 48–72 h recurrence (NNT = 9).[3]
  1. Discharge triptan (sumatriptan 100 mg) if no contraindication, plus an NSAID and PRN antiemetic
  2. Return precautions: worst-headache-of-life, fever/neck stiffness, focal deficit, confusion, seizure, or a headache unlike their usual

Admit for
Thunderclap onset, new neurologic deficit, inability to exclude a secondary cause, or migraine refractory to ≥ 3 parenteral treatments → admit for the DHE protocol with telemetry and neurology consult.
