Peripartum Cardiomyopathy: Recognize, Stabilize, Treat by Pregnancy Status

Adults with suspected or confirmed peripartum cardiomyopathy (HF with LVEF <45%, no other cause found) presenting from the last month of pregnancy through ~6 months postpartum. Excludes pre-existing cardiomyopathy diagnosed before pregnancy and pediatrics.

⚠ Cardiogenic shock (SBP <90 or vasopressor-dependent, end-organ hypoperfusion, acute pulmonary edema): high-flow O2/NIPPV or early intubation, 2 large-bore IVs, arterial + central line, left lateral tilt if pregnant, activate cardiology/OB-MFM/ICU STAT. Target MAP >65 mmHg.
⚠ ACE inhibitors and ARBs are CONTRAINDICATED before delivery (teratogenic) — use hydralazine + isosorbide dinitrate for pregnant patients; switch to ACE inhibitor immediately after delivery.
⚠ Do NOT use warfarin during pregnancy (teratogenic) — use enoxaparin; warfarin is safe postpartum and for breastfeeding.
⚠ Bromocriptine (ESC Class IIb) MUST be combined with anticoagulation (minimum prophylactic LMWH) — it carries increased MI/stroke risk on its own and is contraindicated if the patient wishes to breastfeed.
⚠ Avoid nitroprusside in pregnancy — cyanide toxicity risk; avoid NSAIDs throughout — they worsen heart failure and increase afterload.
⚠ Severe LVEF <30%: plan early delivery around 37 weeks — advanced PPCM carries high decompensation risk in late pregnancy. If cardiac arrest occurs, perform perimortem C-section within 4–5 minutes.
⚠ Contraception is essential after PPCM — subsequent pregnancy with unrecovered LVEF (<50%) carries 30–50% recurrence risk and ~15–25% mortality; counsel against pregnancy until recovery.

1 · Recognize & risk-stratify

The disease
Heart failure from LV systolic dysfunction (EF <45%) developing from the last month of pregnancy to ~5 months postpartum, with no other cause found. Incidence 1 in 1,000-4,000 live births. Mortality 6-10% in the US (up to 28% some regions). 50-70% recover EF with aggressive treatment.
  • African American race (highest incidence, most severe presentation, lowest recovery, 2× longer to recover)
  • Age ≥30 years, multiparity, multiple gestation (twins/triplets)
  • Preeclampsia/eclampsia (OR 3.28), gestational hypertension, obesity, diabetes
  • Prior PPCM — recurrence risk 30-50%
  • Additional associations: prolonged tocolytic use, anemia, family history of cardiomyopathy
Believe her
New dyspnea at rest, PND, unable to lie flat, new/worsening edema, persistent tachycardia (>100 bpm rest), JVD/S3/crackles, hypoxemia — these are red flags over normal pregnancy. If a pregnant/postpartum woman says something is wrong with her breathing, believe her.
  1. Echocardiogram (diagnostic): LVEF <45% (often <35% at presentation), LV dilation, RV dysfunction, functional MR/TR, look for LV thrombus
  2. BNP/NT-proBNP: markedly elevated; >1860 pg/mL predicts persistent LV dysfunction
  3. Troponin: often mildly elevated; T >0.4 ng/mL predicts worse outcomes
  4. ECG (sinus tachycardia, nonspecific ST-T, Q waves mimicking MI), CXR (cardiomegaly, pulmonary edema), CBC/BMP/LFTs
Low suspicion — outpatient pathway
Mild exertional dyspnea only, stable vitals, no orthopnea/PND: schedule outpatient echo within 1-2 weeks, consider BNP. Clear return precautions for orthopnea, PND, worsening dyspnea at rest, new edema, palpitations/syncope. PPCM is a diagnosis you cannot afford to miss — when in doubt, get the echo.

▶ What to do now

Cardiogenic shock — this overrides everything
High-flow O2/NIPPV or early intubation if failing (ketamine preferred for induction; avoid hypotension during intubation), 2 large-bore IVs, arterial + central line, left lateral tilt if pregnant. Activate cardiology, OB/MFM, and ICU STAT. Start dobutamine now (see dose below); add norepinephrine if MAP <65.
Still pregnant — hydralazine + nitrates, NOT ACE inhibitors
ACE inhibitors/ARBs are teratogenic — do not start before delivery. Use hydralazine + isosorbide dinitrate for afterload/preload reduction; metoprolol tartrate is the beta-blocker of choice in pregnancy.
Postpartum — start ACE inhibitor now
Start lisinopril or enalapril immediately after delivery; carvedilol is the preferred postpartum beta-blocker. Spironolactone can be added if not breastfeeding.

Do NOT clear a patient for another pregnancy with unrecovered EF (<50%) without counseling — 30-50% recurrence risk, ~15-25% mortality.
