Emergency Joint Arthrocentesis: Indication, Safe Tap, and Fluid Interpretation

Adults presenting to the ED with an acutely painful/swollen native joint concerning for infection, crystal arthritis, hemarthrosis, or diagnostic uncertainty. Excludes pediatric joints, established prosthetic-joint infection requiring surgical washout planning, and non-emergent/outpatient arthrocentesis.

⚠ Treat septic arthritis as a time-sensitive, joint-threatening infection: obtain blood cultures when feasible, start empiric antibiotics after cultures/aspiration when this does not dangerously delay care, consult orthopedics, and admit.
⚠ Do not rely on fever, serum WBC, ESR, or CRP alone to exclude septic arthritis.
⚠ Crystals do not exclude infection — crystal arthritis and infection can coexist; do not let crystals falsely reassure you.
⚠ Avoid passing through overlying cellulitis/abscess when an alternate route or specialist/image-guided approach is feasible.
⚠ Do not withhold a clinically urgent diagnostic tap solely for abnormal coagulation labs without weighing septic joint risk.
⚠ Do not delay antibiotics for a dangerous infection while waiting for an impossible tap — escalate to ultrasound guidance, radiology, or orthopedics when the tap is unsafe or not feasible.

1 · Is Aspiration Indicated?

Emergency joint arthrocentesis is used for ED aspiration of an acutely painful/swollen joint when infection, crystal arthritis, hemarthrosis, or diagnostic uncertainty is clinically important.

Do not rely on fever, serum WBC, ESR, or CRP alone to exclude septic arthritis. Septic arthritis remains a clinical diagnosis until synovial fluid is evaluated.

PathCriteria
Tap neededAcute monoarthritis, hot/swollen joint, immunocompromise, prosthetic joint concern, or unclear effusion
No tap nowNo effusion, clear nonarticular cause, or needs image-guided/specialist pathway

2 · Contraindication Screen & Approach

Avoid passing through overlying cellulitis/abscess when an alternate route or specialist/image-guided approach is feasible. Relative cautions: anticoagulation/coagulopathy, prosthetic joint, small/deep joint, difficult anatomy, and inability to keep a sterile field. Do not withhold a clinically urgent diagnostic tap solely for abnormal coagulation labs without weighing septic joint risk.

ApproachCriteria
Knee / large accessible jointProceed with sterile landmark or ultrasound-assisted aspiration
Hip, shoulder, small/deep joint, prosthetic jointConsider ultrasound guidance, radiology, orthopedics, or proceduralist support

3 · Knee Tap Technique + Local Anesthesia

  1. Position knee slightly flexed or extended with support.
  2. Prep widely and maintain sterile field.
  3. Use local lidocaine without injecting into the joint if avoidable.
  4. Common route: superolateral or lateral approach into suprapatellar recess.
  5. Aspirate gently; redirect only after withdrawing to safe tissue plane.
  6. Stop if patient has severe pain, paresthesia, or no safe trajectory.
Lidocaine (plain, no epinephrine)
Max plain dose without epinephrine: 4.5 mg/kg (cap 300 mg)
Local infiltration for the tap itself. This is lidocaine's general systemic-toxicity ceiling — drug-store has no arthrocentesis-specific dosing entry — so treat it as a safety cap, not a procedure-specific volume. Aspirate before injecting.
—Lidocaine max plain dose (4.5 mg/kg, cap 300 mg)

4 · Image-Guided / Specialist Route

Use ultrasound to confirm effusion and needle path when anatomy is unclear, effusion is small, or prior attempt failed.

Escalate early for hip aspiration, prosthetic joint aspiration, small/deep joints, overlying infection blocking safe route, or anticipated need for operative washout.

5 · Send The Fluid Correctly

Prioritize tests: Gram stain and culture, cell count with differential, crystal analysis, and glucose/protein only if local practice uses them. If fluid volume is limited, culture and cell count usually matter most. Blood cultures are recommended when septic arthritis is possible.

1Gram stain and culture
2Cell count with differential
3Crystal analysis
4Glucose/protein only if local practice uses them

Crystal Arthritis Result
Crystals support gout or CPPD, but infection is not excluded when fever, immunocompromise, bacteremia risk, prosthetic joint, severe pain, or highly inflammatory fluid is present. If the clinical story is low risk for infection, treat crystal arthritis and arrange follow-up.

Bloody Fluid / Hemarthrosis
Bloody aspirate suggests hemarthrosis or traumatic tap. Reassess trauma, anticoagulation, fracture, ligament injury, and need for imaging or specialist input. Avoid repeated traumatic passes when diagnostic yield is low.

Use history, imaging, anticoagulation status, and exam to determine disposition.
Low-Inflammation Fluid
Low-inflammatory fluid lowers concern for septic arthritis but does not replace clinical judgment. Reassess for osteoarthritis flare, trauma/internal derangement, bursitis, cellulitis, radiculopathy, or referred pain.

Disposition depends on exam, culture plan, pain control, function, and follow-up reliability.

7 · Stop / Escalate

Stop / Escalate
Stop blind ED arthrocentesis and escalate when there is no safe route, overlying infection blocks all approaches, prosthetic/deep joint requires specialty pathway, patient cannot cooperate safely, or prior attempt caused severe pain/paresthesia.

Escalate to ultrasound guidance, radiology, orthopedics, or empiric treatment when the tap is unsafe or not feasible.
