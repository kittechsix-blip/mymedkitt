Difficult Airway: Bougie-First

Adults ≥18 undergoing emergency intubation in the ED with a predicted or encountered difficult airway. Excludes pediatrics, elective OR airways, and awake fibreoptic-only pathways — each needs a modified protocol.

⚠ Assess difficult airway with LEMON criteria before induction
⚠ Have surgical airway kit at bedside before paralysis
⚠ Use video laryngoscopy for all intubations (14.3% improvement in first-pass)
⚠ Use bougie-first technique for predicted difficult airways (98% vs 87% success)
⚠ Feel for tracheal clicks as bougie advances along tracheal rings
⚠ Hold bougie in place while loading ETT over it

1 · Why bougie-first

The core evidence
Bougie-first intubation achieves 98% first-pass success vs 87% with tube-and-stylet in difficult airways (BEAM trial).[1] The benefit is strongest in poor laryngoscopic views (CL III–IV): RR 1.60 for first-pass success.[5]
Skill, not the device
The BOUGIE trial showed no benefit at low-experience sites (median 10 prior uses).[2] Operator skill drives success — practise the technique before you need it. Pair it with video laryngoscopy: 14.3% absolute first-pass improvement over direct laryngoscopy.[3]

2 · Assess before you paralyse

  • L — Look: facial trauma, short neck, small mouth, prominent teeth, large tongue, beard, obesity, blood/secretions
  • E — Evaluate 3-3-2: 3 fingers mouth opening · 3 fingers mentum-to-hyoid · 2 fingers hyoid-to-thyroid notch
  • M — Mallampati: III (base of uvula only) or IV (hard palate only) predicts difficulty
  • O — Obstruction: tumor, abscess, angioedema, epiglottitis
  • N — Neck mobility: immobilisation, arthritis, prior fusion

Position + preoxygenate

4 · Bougie technique + confirmation

Cormack-LehaneViewAction
I–IIaCords / partial cordsPass ETT directly
IIbArytenoids onlyBougie indicated
IIIEpiglottis onlyBougie strongly indicated
IVNo glottis or epiglottisBougie or rescue
  1. Best view — laryngoscopy, identify epiglottis or cords
  2. Insert — coude tip anterior, advance midline under the epiglottis into the trachea
  3. Confirm — tracheal clicks at ~15–25 cm; hold-up sign (resistance at carina ~30–35 cm). Free passage past 40 cm = esophagus
  4. Railroad ETT over the bougie; 90° counter-clockwise rotation if it catches on arytenoids
  5. Hold the bougie still while an assistant loads the tube, then withdraw the bougie
Confirm with waveform capnography
Waveform capnography is the gold standard: 100% sensitivity (non-arrest), 99–100% specificity.[12] Look for a sustained 4-phase waveform over 5–6 breaths — brief esophageal CO₂ tapers after 2–3 breaths. If there is any doubt, remove the tube.
Cap the attempts
Between attempts: mask ventilate, reposition, change device (DL→VL), change to the most experienced operator, add the bougie, suction. Declare a failed airway after 3 attempts — each attempt worsens trauma and edema.

5 · Failed airway & CICO

Declare it out loud
Say *"This is a failed airway."* The Vortex has three upper-airway lifelines — face mask, supraglottic airway, ETT. Adequate oxygenation by any lifeline = green zone: time to think and call for help.[11]
CICO / CICV → front-of-neck access
When all three lifelines fail after best effort, that is CICO. The 2024 Canadian Airway Focus Group moved the trigger to CICV (can't intubate, can't ventilate) — do not wait for desaturation.[18] Go to the neck now.
  • Can oxygenate (green zone): i-gel SGA rescue (size 3: 30–60 kg, size 4: 50–90 kg, size 5: >90 kg); >95% ventilation-rescue success. Call anesthesia/ENT, plan definitive airway — do NOT make repeated blind attempts
  • Cannot oxygenate (CICO): scalpel-finger-bougie cricothyrotomy immediately

6 · Scalpel-finger-bougie cricothyrotomy

Surgical, not needle
NAP4: needle cricothyrotomy fails 60% of the time; surgical succeeds 100%.[6] DAS 2015 recommends the scalpel-bougie technique over needle/cannula.[19]
ItemSpecification
Scalpel#10 blade — broad, matches tube width
Bougie15 Fr coude-tip, lubricated
ETT6.0 mm cuffed (DAS guideline)
  1. Stabilise the larynx with the laryngeal handshake (non-dominant hand)
  2. Vertical skin incision ~4 cm over the cricothyroid membrane (CTM), exposes landmarks even when obscured
  3. Palpate CTM with a finger; horizontal stab through its lower half (avoid superior vessels and cords)
  4. Finger in, then bougie into the trachea; railroad a 6.0 cuffed ETT over the bougie
  5. Confirm ETCO₂, secure with ties (blood defeats tape), CXR, convert to formal tracheostomy in 24–72 h
