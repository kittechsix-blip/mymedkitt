Pre-assessment

Bougie-first intubation achieves 98% first-pass success vs 87% with stylet in difficult airways (BEAM trial). The benefit is strongest in poor laryngoscopic views (CL III–IV): RR 1.60 for first-pass success. Video laryngoscopy adds 14.3% absolute improvement in first-pass success over direct laryngoscopy (DEVICE trial). The BOUGIE trial found no benefit at sites with low bougie experience — operator skill, not device alone, drives success.

LEMON score ≥4 predicts difficult intubation. Also screen backup pathways: MOANS (difficult BMV), RODS (difficult SGA), SHORT (difficult cricothyrotomy). No absolute contraindications exist for emergency cricothyrotomy.

▶ Predicted difficulty → approach

Low risk → standard RSI
No difficult airway predictors, normal anatomy. Proceed with standard RSI; keep the bougie at bedside as rescue if a Grade IIb+ view is encountered.
Moderate/high risk → bougie-first RSI
Some LEMON predictors present, or LEMON ≥4 with compromised backup pathways. Plan bougie-first from the start — full equipment set at bedside before paralysis.
Extreme risk → consider awake intubation
Predicted fail intubation + fail mask + fail SGA, or cannot tolerate apnea. Consider awake fiberoptic intubation (<2-3% of ED intubations); the only absolute contraindication is patient refusal. Call anesthesiology/ENT early. If awake is not feasible, proceed to bougie-first RSI with the surgical airway kit open at bedside.

Bougie equipment & insertion technique

ItemSpecification
Bougie15 Fr × 60 cm coude-tip (Frova or SunMed)
LaryngoscopeVideo preferred (DEVICE trial: 85% vs 71% first-pass). C-MAC or standard-geometry GlideScope
ETT7.0-7.5 mm (women), 7.5-8.0 mm (men), pre-lubricated
BackupSecond ETT, i-gel SGA, surgical airway kit
  1. Laryngoscopy — obtain best possible view; identify epiglottis or cords.
  2. Insert bougie — coude tip anterior, advance along midline under epiglottis into trachea (CL III: advance behind epiglottis tip).
  3. Confirm tracheal placement — tracheal clicks ~15-25 cm (unreliable alone); hold-up sign ~30-35 cm at carina.
  4. Railroad ETT — slide pre-lubricated ETT over bougie; rotate 90° counterclockwise if it catches on arytenoids; maintain laryngoscopy until the tube passes the cords.
  5. Remove bougie once ETT is at appropriate depth.
  6. Confirm with waveform capnography immediately.
Common errors
Do NOT preload the bougie through the ETT (restricts maneuverability). Maintain coude-tip-anterior orientation. Do not advance too aggressively (esophageal placement risk). Rotate the ETT if it catches on arytenoids. Do not remove the laryngoscope before the ETT passes the cords. Do not rely on tracheal clicks alone for confirmation. ETT depth check: 21 cm at teeth (women), 23 cm (men).

Attempt limitation

Limit intubation attempts — each attempt causes airway trauma, edema, and bleeding, making subsequent attempts harder.

  • Mask ventilate / apneic oxygenation between attempts
  • Reposition — optimize ear-to-sternal-notch alignment
  • Change device (DL → VL, or different blade)
  • Change operator — most experienced available
  • Use bougie if not already attempted
  • Suction aggressively

Tube confirmation

Waveform capnography (gold standard) sensitivity is 100% (non-arrest), 96.8% (cardiac arrest); specificity 99-100%. Look for a sustained 4-phase waveform × 5-6 breaths — brief CO₂ from esophageal placement tapers after 2-3 breaths. If ANY doubt about tube placement, remove the tube.

Confirmed — post-intubation management
Secure tube, depth check (21 cm at teeth women, 23 cm men), CXR (rule out right mainstem/pneumothorax), continuous waveform capnography. Ventilator: TV 6-8 mL/kg IBW, RR 14-18, PEEP 5 cm H₂O, wean FiO₂ to SpO₂ 92-96%. Post-intubation sedation is rate-titrated (ketamine 0.5-2 mg/kg/hr, propofol 5-50 mcg/kg/min, or fentanyl 25-100 mcg/hr + midazolam) — see the myMedKitt Pharmacy module for full infusion ranges.
No ETCO₂ — esophageal until proven otherwise
Remove the tube immediately, mask ventilate with two-person BVM technique, apply apneic oxygenation (15 L/min NC), and reassess attempt count. If this was attempt #3, or the patient is desaturating, proceed to Failed Airway Rescue.

Failed airway — Vortex / CICV

Declare "This is a failed airway." The Vortex approach names three upper-airway lifelines: face mask (BVM), supraglottic airway (SGA), and endotracheal tube (ETT). Adequate oxygenation by ANY lifeline is the Green Zone — time to think, plan, and call for help.

CICV — act before desaturation
2024 Canadian Airway Focus Group update: the trigger changed from CICO to CICV (Can't Intubate, Can't Ventilate) — do not wait for desaturation to declare and act. The hardest part is deciding to cut; procedures performed too late are the primary cause of bad outcomes.

SGA bridge — not a definitive airway
SGA is a bridge, not a definitive airway for most situations. Maintain the SGA and continuous monitoring, consult anesthesiology/ENT urgently, and admit to ICU with the failed-airway plan documented for future providers.

Surgical airway — scalpel-finger-bougie

NAP4: scalpel-bougie over needle
Needle cricothyrotomy carries a 60% failure rate; surgical cricothyrotomy achieves a 100% success rate (NAP4). DAS 2015 explicitly recommends the scalpel-bougie technique over needle/cannula.
ItemSpecification
Scalpel#10 blade (broad)
BougieStandard 15 Fr coude-tip, lubricated
ETT6.0 mm cuffed (DAS guideline)
Other10 mL syringe (cuff), capnography, ties for securing

The cricothyroid membrane measures 9-19 mm horizontal × 9-20 mm vertical in adults. Use the laryngeal handshake to palpate it between the thyroid and cricoid cartilage. In obese patients, use a vertical skin incision (~4 cm) and full head extension to expose landmarks first.

  1. Stabilize larynx (laryngeal handshake).
  2. Vertical skin incision (~4 cm) over the cricothyroid membrane.
  3. Palpate the CTM with a finger through the incision.
  4. Horizontal stab through the CTM (lower half, avoiding superior vessels and vocal cords).
  5. Extend the incision — rotate the scalpel 180° and extend in the opposite direction, blade stays in.
  6. Finger in — replace the scalpel with the non-dominant index finger into the airway lumen.
  7. Bougie insertion — slide the lubricated bougie alongside the finger into the trachea; advance to resistance at 10-15 cm.
  8. Railroad ETT (6.0 mm cuffed) over the bougie until the cuff disappears into the airway.
  9. Inflate the cuff, remove the bougie.
  10. Confirm with waveform capnography.
Post-cricothyrotomy management
Secure the tube with ties (not tape — blood on skin prevents adhesion). CXR to confirm position and rule out pneumothorax. Continuous waveform capnography. Convert to formal tracheostomy within 24-72 hours — consult ENT/surgery. Document reason for surgical airway and notify all future providers.
