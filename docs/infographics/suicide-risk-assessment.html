Suicide Risk Assessment — ED Screening, Stratification & Safe Discharge

Adults and adolescents presenting to the ED with suicidal ideation, self-harm, or a positive behavioral-health screen. Excludes patients who cannot be assessed because they are actively intoxicated (assess only after observation and sobriety) and excludes formal capacity/legal-hold determinations, which follow local statute.

⚠ Universal C-SSRS screening is standard of care — recent behavior (Q6), intent (Q4), or active planning (Q5) = HIGH RISK: immediate 1:1 observation, remove all means from the room, and psychiatric consult.
⚠ Patients cannot be accurately assessed for suicide risk while intoxicated — observe until clinically sober and re-administer the C-SSRS before finalizing risk level or disposition.
⚠ Lethal means counseling is mandatory, not optional — firearms account for 50% of suicides; temporary storage outside the home (trusted contact, safe with separated ammunition, gun range, or police) must be discussed and documented for every at-risk patient.
⚠ Stanley-Brown safety planning must be created WITH the patient, not FOR them — a copy goes to the patient AND the support person; contract-for-safety alone has limited value as the sole discharge criterion.
⚠ ALL discharge criteria must be met before ED discharge — safety plan completed, lethal means restricted, reliable support person present, and outpatient follow-up confirmed within 24-72 hours. Missing any one criterion means reconsidering discharge or consulting psychiatry.
⚠ Suicide within 7 days of ED discharge is a Joint Commission sentinel event (2024) — document screening tool/results, risk and protective factors, means counseling, safety plan, and disposition rationale on every patient.
⚠ Refusal to restrict lethal means may itself indicate higher risk — document the refusal and counseling provided, and treat persistent refusal with elevated risk as grounds for psychiatric admission or extended observation.

1 · Universal screening

Universal screening is standard of care — Joint Commission requires validated screening for all behavioral-health presentations. Asking directly does NOT increase risk. Obtain collateral from family/friends and document thoroughly.

ED-SAFE Study: universal screening changed detection from 2.9% → 5.7% and reduced subsequent attempts by 30%.[3]

  1. Identify risk using validated screening
  2. Communicate with the patient about risk
  3. Assess warning signs and protective factors
  4. Reduce access to lethal means
  5. Refer to mental health resources
  6. Educate patient and family

Columbia Suicide Severity Rating Scale (C-SSRS) — ask in order. If NO to Q2, skip to Q6.

  1. Q1: Wished you were dead or to go to sleep and not wake up? (past month)
  2. Q2: Actually had thoughts of killing yourself? (past month)
  3. Q3: Thinking about how you might do this? (plan)
  4. Q4: Had intention of acting on these thoughts? (intent)
  5. Q5: Started to work out the details of how to kill yourself? (planning)
  6. Q6: Done, started, or prepared anything to end your life? (past 3 months — behavior)
C-SSRS answer patternRisk tier
YES to Q6 — recent suicidal behavior in past 3 monthsHIGH
YES to Q4 or Q5 — intent or active planningHIGH
YES to Q2 or Q3 — ideation with or without methodMODERATE
YES to Q1 only — passive ideationLOW
NO to all — negative screenNEGATIVE

▶ What to do now

Intoxicated — cannot assess yet
Patients cannot be accurately assessed for suicide risk while intoxicated. Observe until clinically sober, then re-administer the C-SSRS and reassess. Chronic alcohol/substance use remains a risk factor even after acute SI resolves.[5][6]
HIGH risk — act now
1:1 observation / suicide precautions. Remove all potential means from the room (cords, sharps, medications). Do NOT leave the patient alone. Contact psychiatric consultation.[5][6]
MODERATE risk — full assessment
Ideation present without clear intent or recent behavior. Assess duration/frequency of thoughts, specific method, access to means, support system, and what has kept the patient from acting. Contract for safety alone has limited value — weigh risk vs. protective factors; err toward psychiatric consultation if uncertain.[5][6]
LOWER risk — passive ideation
Still requires full risk/protective-factor assessment, safety planning, lethal means counseling, and outpatient referral. Consider hospitalization if recent escalation, poor insight, severe depression/psychosis, or no social support.[5][6]
Negative screen
Continue standard care for the presenting complaint. Address underlying stressors, provide 988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline information, and offer brief supportive counseling if appropriate.[1][2]

3 · Safety planning & lethal means

Stanley-Brown 6-step collaborative safety plan — create WITH the patient, not FOR them. Give a copy to the patient AND the support person.[7]

  1. Warning signs: what thoughts, moods, or situations signal a crisis is starting
  2. Internal coping: what the patient can do alone to take their mind off problems
  3. Social distractions: who they can be around that helps without discussing the crisis
  4. Support contacts: names + numbers of friends/family to call for help
  5. Professional resources: 988, local crisis team, ED callback number, outpatient provider
  6. Lethal means restriction: how access to harmful means will be limited

Reducing access to lethal means during crisis saves lives. Firearms account for 50% of suicides.[8]

MeansAction
FirearmsStore outside home temporarily (trusted friend/family, gun range, police); lock in safe with ammunition stored separately; cable lock through action; remove firing pin
MedicationsGive to family member to dispense; lock in safe/lockbox; dispose of unused medications; limit quantities prescribed
Other meansSecure sharps, ropes, cords; car keys to support person if driving into traffic considered
Support personTakes possession or supervises access; educated on importance; follow-up call planned at 48-72h to verify implementation

4 · Psychiatric disposition

Consult psychiatry for formal evaluation and disposition recommendation. Prepare for the consultant: C-SSRS results, risk and protective factors identified, collateral information obtained, medical clearance completed, patient's stated wishes. While awaiting consult: maintain appropriate observation level, continue supportive care, avoid confrontational approach, allow support person presence if calming.

What did the psychiatric consultant recommend?[5][6]

Psychiatric admission indicated. Indications: active SI with intent and plan, recent serious attempt, unable to contract for safety, no social support, psychotic symptoms, severe intoxication with persistent SI when sober, unable to engage in safety planning.

Actions: maintain 1:1 observation, medical clearance per local protocol, contact inpatient psychiatry for bed, remain in ED on precautions if no beds, document risk assessment and rationale.

If patient refuses: evaluate capacity, consider emergency detention/hold per state law, document imminent danger to self.[5][6]

Partial hospitalization / IOP — appropriate when moderate risk but stable, good home support, able to contract for safety overnight, and able to attend daily programming. Discharge requirements: safety plan completed, lethal means counseling done, program intake scheduled for the next business day, 24/7 crisis resources provided, support person educated, follow-up call planned.[5][6]

5 · Safe discharge checklist

ALL criteria must be met for safe ED discharge. Missing ANY criterion → reconsider discharge or consult psychiatry.[5][6]

  • Low-to-moderate risk after full assessment
  • Patient engaged in safety planning
  • Written safety plan given to patient AND support person
  • Lethal means counseling completed with specific plan
  • Reliable support person present and educated
  • Outpatient follow-up confirmed within 24-72 hours
  • 988 and crisis resources provided
  • Patient is sober
  • Post-discharge call planned (24-72 hours)

Joint Commission (NPSG.15.01.01) requirements. Suicide within 7 days of ED discharge is now a sentinel event (updated 2024).[2]

  • Validated screening tool used (C-SSRS) and results
  • Risk factors identified (specific ones listed)
  • Protective factors identified
  • Collateral obtained (family, records, outpatient providers)
  • Mental status examination
  • Lethal means assessment and counseling provided
  • Risk stratification with rationale
  • Safety plan completed (copy in chart)
  • Disposition decision and clinical rationale
  • Specific follow-up plan with appointment details

9 · Do NOT

Do NOT attempt to assess suicide risk in an intoxicated patient — observe until clinically sober and re-administer the C-SSRS before finalizing risk level or disposition.

Do NOT skip documentation — suicide within 7 days of ED discharge is a Joint Commission sentinel event; document screening tool/results, risk/protective factors, means counseling, safety plan, and disposition rationale.
