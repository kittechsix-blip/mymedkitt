Neck Pain Hub: Exclude the Killers Before Calling It Mechanical

Adults presenting to the ED with neck pain, used as a triage/navigation hub to exclude time-critical dangerous causes before a benign mechanical pattern is diagnosed. Excludes children (peds neck pain is not covered), and is not a substitute for the deep-dive consult once a specific threat (dissection, meningitis, deep neck infection, spinal infection, myelopathy, cardiac/aortic) is identified — this hub routes to those consults rather than replacing them.

⚠ Neck pain plus a neuro deficit, fever, airway symptoms, or trauma is not routine neck pain — pick the dangerous lane first; mechanical neck pain is a diagnosis of exclusion.
⚠ Five DO NOT MISS diagnoses: cervical artery dissection, meningitis, epidural abscess/spinal infection, deep neck space infection (Ludwig's, retropharyngeal), and cervical cord compression/unstable fracture.
⚠ Trauma with neurologic or high-risk mechanism features (anticoagulation, elderly fall, distracting injury, intoxication) is treated as C-spine injury until cleared — these features can hide clinically important cervical injury.
⚠ When CNS infection (meningitis/encephalitis) is plausible, empiric antibiotics ± dexamethasone/acyclovir are time-sensitive and must not wait for CT/LP logistics — absence of the classic fever/stiff-neck/AMS triad does not exclude meningitis.
⚠ Avoid neck manipulation when cervical artery dissection is suspected — a young patient with posterior headache/neck pain plus dizziness/ataxia is not automatically benign vertigo or muscle strain.
⚠ Do NOT paralyze a difficult infected airway casually — plan an awake/controlled airway with help when deep neck space infection shows drooling, trismus, muffled voice, or stridor.
⚠ Fever is absent in many spinal epidural abscess/vertebral osteomyelitis cases — IVDU, bacteremia, immunosuppression, diabetes, recent spine procedure, or focal percussion tenderness should raise suspicion; MRI is the key test.
⚠ Bilateral hand clumsiness, gait instability, and hyperreflexia are not routine radiculopathy — avoid manipulation and obtain MRI when myelopathy/cord compression is suspected.
⚠ Jaw/neck pain can be an anginal equivalent, especially in older adults, women, and diabetics — get an ECG within 10 minutes when cardiac or aortic ischemia is possible.
⚠ Do not manipulate, discharge a patient with myelopathy, or delay antibiotics/airway planning for deep neck space or CNS infection — these admit/ICU/transfer regardless of how the rest of the exam looks.

3 · Branch-specific first 5 minutes

Trauma / C-spine injury
Immobilize if unstable mechanism or neuro deficit, document neuro exam before analgesia/sedation if possible, CT C-spine by validated trauma criteria/local protocol, CTA neck if blunt cerebrovascular injury risk, spine consult for fracture/instability/neuro deficit. Pitfall: elderly ground-level falls, anticoagulation, intoxication, and distracting injury can hide clinically important cervical injury. Open Cervical Spine Injuries.
Meningitis / encephalitis
Droplet precautions if meningococcal concern, blood cultures if they do not delay therapy, empiric antibiotics plus dexamethasone when bacterial meningitis plausible, acyclovir when encephalitis plausible; CT-before-LP only when indicated, but treatment should not wait for CT/LP logistics. Pitfall: absence of classic triad does not exclude meningitis. Open Meningitis / Encephalitis.
Cervical artery dissection
Focused posterior circulation exam, Horner check, CTA head/neck if suspicion is meaningful, avoid neck manipulation, stroke alert if deficits/window. Pitfall: young patient with posterior headache/neck pain and dizziness/ataxia is not automatically benign vertigo or muscle strain. Open Cervical Artery Dissection and Acute Ischemic Stroke if focal deficits are present.
Deep neck space infection / airway risk
Airway assessment before supine CT, ENT/anesthesia early if voice change, drooling, floor-of-mouth elevation, stridor, or trismus; IV antibiotics covering oral flora; CT neck with IV contrast when stable enough. Pitfall: do not paralyze a difficult infected airway casually — plan awake/controlled airway with help. Open Deep Neck Space Infections.
Spinal epidural abscess / vertebral osteomyelitis
Neuro exam, ESR/CRP, blood cultures if febrile or high suspicion, MRI cervical spine with and without contrast; whole-spine MRI when symptoms do not localize or risk is high. Start antibiotics after cultures when sepsis/neuro deficit; do not delay treatment in unstable patients. Pitfall: fever is absent in many SEA cases — IVDU, bacteremia, immunosuppression, diabetes, recent spine procedure, or focal percussion tenderness should raise suspicion. Open Cervical Spine Pain - Non-Traumatic.
Myelopathy / radiculopathy / cord compression
Document strength/reflexes/sensation/gait, check for Hoffman/clonus/hyperreflexia, look for urinary symptoms, avoid manipulation, MRI when myelopathy/progressive deficit/cord compression is suspected. Pitfall: bilateral hand clumsiness, gait instability, and hyperreflexia are not routine radiculopathy. Open Cervical Spine Pain - Non-Traumatic.
Cardiac / aortic neck pain
ECG within 10 minutes when ischemia is possible, bilateral arm BP/pulses when dissection fits, troponin pathway, CTA aorta for tearing pain/pulse deficit/neuro features. Pitfall: jaw/neck pain can be an anginal equivalent, especially in older adults, women, and diabetics. Open Chest Pain Hub, STEMI, NSTEMI, or Aortic Dissection as indicated.

4 · Rescue bundle & reassessment

  • Analgesia: acetaminophen/NSAID when safe; avoid masking evolving neuro deficits with heavy sedation.
  • Gentle range-of-motion only when trauma, infection, myelopathy, dissection, and airway infection are not suspected.
  • Focused repeat neuro exam: strength, sensation, reflexes, gait, cranial nerves when headache/dizziness.
  • Infection screen when risk exists: temp, CBC, ESR/CRP, blood cultures if febrile/high suspicion.
  • Avoid chiropractic/manipulation advice when dissection, myelopathy, fracture, or instability is possible.

6 · Disposition

Admit / ICU / transfer
Admit/ICU/transfer for deep neck infection with airway concern, meningitis/encephalitis, dissection/stroke, unstable C-spine injury, epidural abscess/vertebral osteomyelitis, myelopathy/progressive deficit, suspected malignancy cord compression, uncontrolled pain with red flags, or unsafe follow-up. Use the deep-dive consult and involve airway/ENT/neuro/spine early based on the threat.
Observation / serial exam
Observe when severe pain persists, red-flag labs/imaging are pending, swallowing/voice symptoms need serial exams, intoxication or unreliable exam limits clearance, or neuro symptoms are transient/equivocal.
Discharge checklist
Discharge only when: benign mechanical pattern, vitals stable, pain improved, normal neuro exam, no airway/swallow concern, no infection/vascular/trauma red flags, and follow-up is realistic.
  • Fever or confusion
  • Severe headache
  • Weakness/numbness or gait trouble
  • Vision or speech symptoms
  • Syncope or chest pain
  • Trouble swallowing, voice change, or drooling
  • Worsening pain
  • New bowel/bladder symptoms
