Lower GI Bleed: Stabilize, Stratify, Localize, Intervene

Adults ≥18 with lower gastrointestinal bleeding (hematochezia, maroon stool, or melena from a source distal to the ligament of Treitz) in the ED. Excludes confirmed primary upper GI bleeding, pediatrics, and pregnancy-specific protocols — each needs a different pathway.

⚠ Up to 15% of apparent lower GI bleeding is actually a brisk upper GI source — get an NGT aspirate or early EGD in the unstable patient with an unclear bleeding source.
⚠ Shock Index (HR/SBP) >1.0 suggests significant bleeding — assess hemodynamics before risk-scoring or imaging.
⚠ Activate the massive transfusion protocol for shock index >1.0 with ongoing bleeding, estimated blood loss >30% of blood volume, or lactate >4 mmol/L; target a 1:1:1 pRBC:FFP:platelet ratio.
⚠ Do NOT give tranexamic acid (TXA) — the ACG guideline recommends against antifibrinolytics in LGIB; the HALT-IT trial showed no mortality benefit and increased venous thromboembolism.
⚠ Anticoagulation reversal (Vitamin K + 4-factor PCC for warfarin, idarucizumab for dabigatran, andexanet alfa or 4-factor PCC for Xa inhibitors) should not be withheld for hemodynamically unstable, life-threatening bleeding.
⚠ Surgery is indicated for hemodynamic instability despite resuscitation, massive transfusion (>6 units in 24h), failed endoscopic/IR therapy, perforation, bowel ischemia, or known surgical pathology — do not delay surgical consultation for these findings.

1 · Initial assessment

Lower GI bleeding originates distal to the ligament of Treitz — hematochezia, maroon stool, or melena (if slow transit). First priority: assess hemodynamic status — HR, BP, orthostatic vitals, Shock Index (HR/SBP; >1.0 suggests significant bleeding), mental status, and skin perfusion.

Rule out upper GI source in the unstable patient
Up to 15% of apparent LGIB is actually a brisk upper GI bleed. Get an NGT aspirate or early EGD if hemodynamically unstable with an unclear source.
  1. Two large-bore IVs — 18G or larger
  2. Type and crossmatch — anticipate transfusion
  3. Activate MTP if Shock Index >1.0 with ongoing bleeding, estimated blood loss >30% of blood volume, or lactate >4 mmol/L
  4. Rule out UGIB: NGT aspirate or early EGD if hematemesis/coffee-ground emesis, BUN/Cr ratio >30, or unstable with unclear source
  5. Resuscitate → proceed to resuscitation

2 · What to do now

Unstable — resuscitate now
Large-bore IV access, type & cross, MTP if Shock Index >1.0 with ongoing bleeding, and rule out a brisk upper GI source before proceeding to resuscitation.
Low risk — outpatient pathway
Oakland ≤8 = 95% safe-discharge probability. Confirm stable throughout observation, stopped/minimal bleeding, tolerating PO, reliable follow-up, and no high-risk features (anticoagulation, malignancy, cirrhosis).
Intermediate risk — individualize
Oakland 9-16: admit if ongoing bleeding, anticoagulation, comorbidities, or unreliable follow-up. Discharge with close follow-up if bleeding stopped, Hgb stable, young/healthy, and follow-up is excellent.
High risk — admit, NPO, reverse anticoagulation
Oakland >16 (or admitted intermediate-risk): NPO, telemetry, serial Hgb, GI consult, and reverse anticoagulation if present, then proceed to resuscitation.

CTA abdomen/pelvis, arterial and venous phases. Sensitivity 85-90% for active bleeding detecting rates ≥0.3-0.5 mL/min. Findings: contrast extravasation, "spot sign". Positive → IR embolization or surgical planning. Negative → colonoscopy or nuclear scan.

Timing: NONURGENT colonoscopy for most hospitalized patients — urgent colonoscopy within 24h does NOT improve rebleeding, mortality, transfusion, or length of stay; in stable patients, colonoscopy within 14 days is as effective as within 24h. Urgent only for ongoing hemodynamically significant bleeding. Therapeutics: hemostatic clips, epinephrine injection, thermal coagulation (APC/bipolar), band ligation.

Tagged RBC scintigraphy detects bleeding rates as low as 0.1-0.4 mL/min, imaged up to 24h after injection. Less anatomically precise than CTA. Positive → guides angiography/embolization or surgical planning. Negative → elective colonoscopy or capsule endoscopy.

7 · Intervention

Based on imaging and clinical status, select the intervention pathway above.

Selective mesenteric catheterization; provocative angiography if no active bleeding seen; embolize with microcoils, particles, or gelfoam. Success 70-90% for acute control. Complications: bowel ischemia 5-10%, access-site hematoma, rebleeding 15-25%. Monitor for abdominal pain/fever post-procedure; surgery consult if bowel necrosis signs.

Hemostatic clips first-line (diverticular, post-polypectomy bleeding). Epinephrine 1:10,000 injection — temporary, combine with clips/thermal. APC for angiodysplasia, bipolar coagulation for point bleeding. Band ligation for internal hemorrhoids/rectal varices. Observe for rebleeding 24-48h post-procedure.

Surgical indications
Instability despite resuscitation, massive transfusion requirement (>6 units in 24h), failed endoscopic and IR therapy, perforation or bowel ischemia, or known surgical pathology. Segmental colectomy if source localized; subtotal colectomy (higher morbidity) if not. Localize source pre-operatively when possible.

Bleeding stopped, no intervention needed — proceed to disposition.

9 · Disposition

ICU admission
Hemodynamic instability requiring vasopressors, MTP activated, ongoing active bleeding, or post-intervention monitoring. Orders: continuous telemetry, serial Hgb q4-6h, serial lactate, type & crossmatch 4+ units, NPO, GI and/or surgical consultation, IR on standby.
Floor admission
Hemodynamically stable, Oakland >8 or high-risk features, requires monitoring/intervention, or anemia requiring transfusion but stable. Orders: telemetry, serial Hgb q6-8h × 24h, type & screen, NPO until colonoscopy, GI consultation, hold anticoagulation per GI guidance.
Discharge
Oakland ≤8, hemodynamically stable, bleeding stopped, no high-risk features, reliable follow-up available. Plan: GI follow-up for outpatient colonoscopy within 14 days, clear liquid diet × 24h then advance, hold anticoagulation per GI guidance, iron if anemic. Return precautions: recurrent bright red blood per rectum, lightheadedness/syncope, hematemesis or melena, abdominal pain.
