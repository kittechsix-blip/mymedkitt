Pneumothorax POCUS: Lung Sliding, Lung Point, and Management

Adults with suspected pneumothorax (chest trauma, sudden dyspnea, pleuritic chest pain, decreased breath sounds, subcutaneous emphysema, post-procedure) undergoing bedside POCUS evaluation in the ED. Excludes pediatric-specific dosing and definitive surgical/VATS decision-making beyond ED disposition.

⚠ Suspected tension pneumothorax (hypotension + tracheal deviation + distended neck veins + severe respiratory distress): needle decompression at the 2nd ICS midclavicular line OR 4th-5th ICS anterior axillary line — do NOT wait for imaging.
⚠ No lung point with absent sliding throughout the entire hemithorax: suspect massive pneumothorax with complete collapse — confirm with CXR, but if hemodynamically unstable, treat as tension pneumothorax immediately.
⚠ Asymptomatic or minimally symptomatic primary spontaneous pneumothorax (PSP): conservative management is first-line regardless of size (ERS/EACTS/ESTS 2024, BTS 2023).
⚠ Symptomatic PSP requiring intervention: needle aspiration is preferred over chest tube as the first invasive step (ERS 2024 strong recommendation).
⚠ If a chest drain is used for PSP: small-bore pigtail (<14 Fr) is preferred; large-bore (>28 Fr) is reserved for SSP with hemothorax or air leak.
⚠ Lung point on POCUS is pathognomonic for pneumothorax (100% specificity).
⚠ Normal lung sliding, B-lines, or seashore sign excludes pneumothorax at the scanned locations (NPV >99%).
⚠ Occult pneumothorax found on POCUS requires a chest tube if positive-pressure ventilation is planned.

1 · POCUS Indications & Technique

POCUS is highly sensitive/specific for pneumothorax, especially in supine trauma patients. Can detect as little as 20-50 mL of air.

Indications:
• Chest trauma
• Sudden dyspnea
• Pleuritic chest pain
• Decreased breath sounds
• Subcutaneous emphysema
• Post-procedure evaluation

Probe: High-frequency linear probe

Position: Scan anterior chest at 2nd-4th intercostal spaces, mid-clavicular line

Steps:
1. Compare bilateral lung sliding
2. Use M-mode to confirm findings
3. Look for lung point if no sliding

In trauma, this is part of the eFAST exam. Supine position — air rises anteriorly.

2 · Findings Interpretation

Assess for lung sliding — shimmering movement of the visceral pleura against the parietal pleura during respiration.

Also look for:
• Comet tail artifacts (B-lines) — presence EXCLUDES pneumothorax
• Seashore sign on M-mode (normal)
• Barcode/stratosphere sign on M-mode (abnormal)

Normal: Pneumothorax Excluded
Lung sliding, comet tails, and B-lines exclude pneumothorax at the scanned locations. Negative predictive value >99%.

If clinical suspicion persists despite normal POCUS: scan additional rib spaces, consider CT chest, repeat imaging if symptoms change.
Trauma — eFAST Protocol
Supine position — air rises anteriorly. Up to 50% of pneumothoraces are occult on initial CXR but visible on CT — POCUS detects occult PTX missed on supine CXR.

Occult PTX management: observation appropriate for small, stable occult PTX; chest tube if positive-pressure ventilation planned or PTX enlarging.

3 · Pneumothorax Confirmation

Abnormal: Stratosphere Sign
Absent lung sliding with stratosphere sign on M-mode (barcode sign) suggests pneumothorax.

Important: absent sliding alone is not diagnostic — can also be seen in mainstem intubation, pleural adhesions, apnea, or severe ARDS. Check multiple rib spaces and look for the lung point.

The lung point is pathognomonic for pneumothorax — where lung sliding appears and disappears with respiration at the border of the collapsed lung. Specificity: 100%. Slide the probe laterally from the area of absent sliding until intermittent sliding is seen.

5 · Definitive Management

Conservative Management — Asymptomatic/Minimally Symptomatic PSP
Conservative (watch-and-wait) management is first-line for asymptomatic or minimally symptomatic primary spontaneous pneumothorax (PSP), regardless of size.

Evidence: Brown et al (NEJM 2020 PSP-CMT trial) — conservative care non-inferior to chest drain for 8-week radiologic resolution, with fewer adverse events and lower 1-year recurrence. Pooled meta-analysis (11,922 cases, 8 studies): recurrence RR 0.98 (95% CI 0.75-1.28).

Admission for observation still appropriate for: secondary spontaneous PTX (SSP — underlying lung disease), uncertain follow-up, large symptomatic PSP awaiting intervention.
  • Observation 4-6 hours, repeat CXR — if stable, discharge with close follow-up (48-72h)
  • Ambulatory management (Heimlich valve) if local infrastructure supports it
  • Supplemental O2 increases reabsorption rate (~2%/day → ~4%/day) but is not mandatory in modern conservative protocols

6 · Management algorithm

POCUS Indication + Technique — Detects 20-50 mL air. Indicated for chest trauma, sudden dyspnea, pleuritic chest pain, decreased breath sounds, subcutaneous emphysema, post-procedure eval. Linear probe, 2nd-4th ICS MCL, M-mode confirmation.

Lung Sliding Present? — Look for comet tails (B-lines), seashore sign (normal) vs barcode/stratosphere sign (abnormal) on M-mode.

Normal — PTX Excluded — Lung sliding + B-lines + seashore sign exclude PTX at scanned locations. NPV >99%.

Absent Sliding — Stratosphere Sign — Barcode sign suggests PTX but is not diagnostic alone (mainstem intubation, adhesions, apnea, ARDS). Check multiple rib spaces; look for lung point.

Trauma — eFAST Context — Supine — air rises anteriorly. Up to 50% of PTX occult on initial CXR. Chest tube if PPV planned or PTX enlarging.

Lung Point Identified? — Intermittent sliding at the border of the collapsed lung — pathognomonic, 100% specificity. Slide probe laterally to find it.

PTX Confirmed — Lung point = 100% specificity. Size by location: anterior only = small, mid-axillary = moderate, posterior = large.

No Lung Point — Massive PTX? — Absent sliding throughout entire hemithorax, no lung point. Confirm with CXR. If unstable, treat as tension.

Signs of Tension? — Hypotension, tracheal deviation, distended neck veins, severe respiratory distress. Do NOT wait for imaging.
