Pelvic Inflammatory Disease — ED Management

Sexually active females, ED. CDC 2021 minimum criteria: pelvic/lower abdominal pain + cervical motion, uterine, or adnexal tenderness. Excludes patients in whom ectopic pregnancy has not yet been excluded — pregnancy test and, if ectopic is possible, imaging are mandatory before treating as PID.

⚠ Pregnancy test is mandatory before treating as PID — ectopic pregnancy must always be excluded; imaging is mandatory before treating as PID if ectopic pregnancy is possible
⚠ Ruptured TOA = surgical emergency — presents with peritonitis, septic shock; requires emergent laparotomy
⚠ Admit if: TOA present, pregnancy, severe illness, unable to tolerate/follow outpatient regimen, surgical emergency cannot be excluded, or no clinical response to oral antibiotics within 72 hours
⚠ Mandatory follow-up in 48-72 hours for every outpatient-treated patient — admit for IV antibiotics if no improvement
⚠ All sexual partners within the past 60 days must be treated (EPT is a legal option in most states) — GC and CT are reportable diseases

Diagnosis

Pelvic inflammatory disease is infection of the upper genital tract (uterus, fallopian tubes, ovaries, peritoneum). CDC 2021 minimum criteria — treat if ALL present: sexually active female, pelvic or lower abdominal pain, and Cervical motion tenderness, OR uterine tenderness, OR adnexal tenderness (unilateral or bilateral). Low threshold to treat — missed PID leads to infertility, ectopic pregnancy, and chronic pelvic pain.

  • Clinical: oral temp >101°F (38.3°C), mucopurulent cervical discharge, friable cervix
  • Laboratory: wet-prep WBC >1 per epithelial cell, elevated ESR/CRP, positive GC/CT NAAT, positive cervical culture
  • Imaging/definitive: thickened fluid-filled tubes or tubo-ovarian complex on US, tubo-ovarian abscess, laparoscopic findings, endometritis on biopsy
  • Obtain: pregnancy test, GC/CT NAAT, wet prep, consider HIV testing
Criteria not met — consider alternative diagnoses
Ectopic pregnancy (always check pregnancy test), ovarian cyst rupture/torsion, endometriosis, ovarian tumor, appendicitis, IBD, diverticulitis, UTI/pyelonephritis, nephrolithiasis, mesenteric lymphadenitis, muscle strain. If ectopic pregnancy is possible, imaging is mandatory before treating as PID.

Workup and imaging

Standard workup: pregnancy test (mandatory — ectopic must be excluded), GC/CT NAAT, wet prep, CBC/CMP/CRP if systemic illness suspected, HIV testing offered to all. Pelvic ultrasound if: pelvic mass palpated, TOA suspected, diagnosis uncertain, pregnancy test positive, or moderate-severe illness.

Suggestive of PID: thickened fluid-filled fallopian tubes, free pelvic fluid, tubo-ovarian complex. Tubo-ovarian abscess: complex multiloculated pelvic mass, may involve tube, ovary, or bowel — requires IV antibiotics and possible drainage. Alternative findings: simple ovarian cyst, ectopic pregnancy, appendicitis.

Risk assessment — admission criteria

  • Surgical emergency cannot be excluded (ectopic, appendicitis)
  • Tubo-ovarian abscess present
  • Pregnancy
  • Severe illness (high fever, nausea/vomiting, severe pain)
  • Unable to tolerate or follow outpatient regimen
  • No clinical response to oral antibiotics within 72 hours
  • Additional considerations: immunocompromised, IUD in place, adolescent (compliance concerns)

If no improvement in 72 hours: admit for IV antibiotics, consider imaging for TOA, reassess diagnosis. GC and CT are reportable diseases — the health department may assist with partner notification.

Tubo-ovarian abscess

TOA requires IV antibiotics and possible drainage
IV antibiotics (same regimens as severe PID) plus metronidazole 500 mg IV q8h for enhanced anaerobic coverage. Serial imaging to monitor response.
  • Drainage indications: abscess >3-5 cm (varies by institution), failure to improve on antibiotics after 48-72h, ruptured TOA (surgical emergency)
  • Drainage options: IR — CT or US-guided percutaneous; surgical — laparoscopy or laparotomy
  • Ruptured TOA: surgical emergency, presents with peritonitis and septic shock, requires emergent laparotomy

Disposition

Discharge with outpatient treatment. Prescriptions: doxycycline 100 mg PO BID × 14 days + metronidazole 500 mg PO BID × 14 days; IM ceftriaxone given in ED. Follow-up 48-72 hours mandatory; GC/CT retest in 3 months. Complete all antibiotics, partners must be treated, no intercourse until treatment complete + partners treated. Return for worsening pain, fever >101°F, vomiting, or inability to take meds.

Admit for IV antibiotics. IV antibiotics per CDC regimen, IV fluids, pain management, NPO if surgery possible, OB/GYN consultation. Monitoring: vitals q4h, daily abdominal/pelvic exam, WBC/temp trending. Transition to oral after 24-48h afebrile with clinical improvement, complete 14-day total course. If TOA: IR or surgical consultation for drainage, serial imaging.

Urgent/emergent surgical consultation. Indications: ruptured TOA with peritonitis, failed medical management of TOA, septic shock, diagnostic uncertainty (cannot exclude surgical emergency). Surgical options: laparoscopy for drainage/washout, laparotomy for ruptured TOA, possible salpingectomy or oophorectomy if necrotic tissue. Pre-operative: broad-spectrum IV antibiotics, aggressive fluid resuscitation, blood type and screen, NPO.
