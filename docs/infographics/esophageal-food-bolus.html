Esophageal Food Bolus Impaction — ED Disimpaction

Adults with acute esophageal food bolus impaction ("steakhouse syndrome") in the ED. Excludes pediatric patients and caustic ingestion. Button battery, sharp bone, magnet, or other true foreign body route to the separate GI Foreign Body pathway, not this one.

⚠ Drooling / cannot swallow saliva = complete obstruction = emergent endoscopy within ~6 hours — aspiration risk.
⚠ Crepitus, severe/tearing chest or back pain, fever, tachycardia/sepsis, or Hamman crunch = suspected perforation: STOP — no pharmacologic disimpaction, no blind push, no effervescent agents. NPO, resuscitate, water-soluble contrast CT chest or esophagram (avoid barium), broad-spectrum antibiotics, emergent thoracic/GI surgery + gastroenterology consult.
⚠ Button battery, sharp bone, magnet, or other true foreign body is NOT a simple food bolus — route to the GI Foreign Body pathway; a button battery is a same-hour emergency.
⚠ Never leave a food bolus impacted beyond 24 hours — prolonged contact causes pressure necrosis, ulceration, and perforation risk.
⚠ Do NOT use papain / meat tenderizer — associated with esophageal perforation and severe mucosal injury. Effervescent/gas-forming agents are contraindicated in a complete obstruction.

Overview

Adult who suddenly cannot swallow during a meal ("steakhouse syndrome"). The bolus is almost always lodged at a pathologic narrowing. Triage by secretions, exclude perforation, exclude a hidden dangerous object, then relieve the obstruction and find the cause.

  1. Can the patient handle secretions? Drooling/cannot swallow saliva = complete obstruction = emergent.
  2. Screen for perforation (crepitus, chest/back pain, fever) — if present, STOP, image, no push/pharm.
  3. Exclude a button battery, sharp bone, or other true foreign body.
  4. Pharmacologic bridge (glucagon) is low-yield but reasonable while arranging endoscopy.
  5. Endoscopy is definitive — push or retrieve.
  6. Treat the underlying cause; biopsy for eosinophilic esophagitis.

Timing & ASGE windows

Timing drives urgency (ASGE/ESGE). Complete obstruction (cannot manage secretions): endoscopy emergently, within ~6 hours — aspiration risk. Partial obstruction / food bolus without complete obstruction: endoscopy urgently, within 24 hours. A bolus should not be left impacted beyond 24 hours — prolonged contact causes pressure necrosis, ulceration, and perforation risk. Do not delay definitive endoscopy waiting for a pharmacologic agent to work in a patient with complete obstruction.

Pharmacologic bridge

Pharmacology is an adjunct, not a substitute for endoscopy. Success rates are low and it must never delay scope in complete obstruction.

1 mg IV slow push (may repeat once after 10-20 min if no response)

Relaxes lower esophageal sphincter smooth muscle. Common: nausea/vomiting — warn the patient. Avoid in pheochromocytoma/insulinoma.

Papain / meat tenderizer is contraindicated — associated with esophageal perforation and severe mucosal injury. Effervescent/gas-forming agents are contraindicated in a complete obstruction — trapped gas above a complete block raises perforation risk.

Suspected perforation

Suspected Perforation — STOP
Do not attempt pharmacologic disimpaction, blind passage, or effervescent agents. Make the patient NPO; resuscitate; IV access, fluids, broad-spectrum antibiotics covering oral/anaerobic flora. Imaging: CT chest with water-soluble (not barium) oral contrast or a water-soluble contrast esophagram. Avoid barium when perforation is suspected. Consult thoracic/GI surgery and gastroenterology emergently.

Dangerous object

Dangerous Object — Use GI Foreign Body Pathway
A button battery in the esophagus can cause liquefactive necrosis within hours and is a same-hour emergency; sharp/pointed objects, long (>5-6 cm) objects, and multiple magnets also need urgent endoscopy. Go to GI Foreign Body for object-specific timing, battery protocol, sharp-object handling, and magnet rules.

Definitive endoscopy

Flexible endoscopy is the definitive treatment and is successful in the large majority of cases. Push technique: gently advance the bolus into the stomach once the distal lumen is confirmed patent. Retrieval: extract piecemeal with a Roth net, snare, or forceps; an overtube protects the airway and allows repeated passes. Airway protection (overtube or intubation) for large/complete obstructions with aspiration risk.

Find the underlying cause

A food bolus impaction is a symptom of a narrowed esophagus until proven otherwise. Common structural causes: Schatzki ring, peptic stricture, eosinophilic esophagitis, and (especially in older patients with weight loss/progressive dysphagia) malignancy. Eosinophilic esophagitis (EoE) is the leading cause of food impaction in young adults, especially men, and recurrent impactions.

  • Obtain esophageal biopsies (typically ≥6, proximal and distal) at the index endoscopy — even if the mucosa looks normal — unless contraindicated.
  • Start a PPI (omeprazole or pantoprazole) — diagnostic-therapeutic for EoE and treats peptic stricture.
  • Dilation of a ring/stricture may be done at the same or a follow-up procedure per the endoscopist.
Omeprazole
20–40 mg PO once daily
PO
Drug-store has no EoE-specific dosing row — this is the GERD/erosive-esophagitis regimen, the closest matching indication.

Disposition & follow-up

  • Discharge: comfortable, tolerating secretions and oral intake, no perforation concern — continue a PPI, arrange GI follow-up for biopsy results, EoE management, and dilation planning.
  • Counsel on recurrence prevention: chew thoroughly, avoid large meat boluses, treat reflux/EoE.
  • Admit / observe for: perforation concern, difficult or incomplete extraction, significant mucosal injury, aspiration, inability to tolerate oral intake, or significant comorbidity.
  • Return precautions: recurrent obstruction, chest/back pain, fever, vomiting blood, or inability to swallow.
