Right Heart Failure: Recognize, Protect the RV, Find the Trigger

Adults with suspected or confirmed right heart failure in the ED/ICU (massive/submassive PE, RV MI, acute-on-chronic PAH crisis, sepsis-induced RV failure, post-LVAD/post-cardiac-surgery RV failure, ARDS-related cor pulmonale). Excludes pediatrics and isolated left heart failure — screen out mimics (tamponade, tension pneumothorax, LHF, hypovolemia, distributive shock, constriction) before applying this pathway.

⚠ Unstable RHF (SBP<90, lactate>4, AMS): norepinephrine FIRST, not phenylephrine, to MAP ≥65. Bedside POCUS within 90 seconds. NO large-volume bolus (>500 mL). 12-lead + V4R always. Peri-arrest suspected massive PE → alteplase 50 mg IV push.
⚠ STOP large-volume IV fluids (>500 mL) in known/suspected PAH or chronic RV failure — a dilated RV pushes the septum into the LV and worsens RV ischemia. RV MI is the one exception (it is preload-dependent).
⚠ RV MI: do NOT give nitrates, morphine, diuretics, or acute beta-blockers/ACE-inhibitors — these drop preload and can be fatal in a preload-dependent ventricle. Give a 500 mL crystalloid bolus, then dobutamine, then norepinephrine if MAP<65, and activate the cath lab.
⚠ Before intubating severe RHF/PAH (Farkas protocol): norepinephrine running to MAP ≥75 BEFORE induction, ketamine + low-dose etomidate — AVOID a propofol bolus, which drops SVR/contractility and can precipitate arrest.
⚠ Never abruptly discontinue home PAH-targeted therapy (epoprostenol, treprostinil, sildenafil) — rebound pulmonary hypertension crisis can be fatal within hours.
⚠ Massive PE (sustained SBP<90 or arrest): systemic alteplase 100 mg IV over 2 hours, or 50 mg IV push during arrest/peri-arrest; catheter-directed therapy or mechanical thrombectomy if lytic is contraindicated.
⚠ Norepinephrine is the first-line pressor in every RHF phenotype — phenylephrine alone raises pulmonary vascular resistance and worsens RV failure.
⚠ Bedside POCUS (RV/LV ratio, D-sign, TAPSE) in any unexplained shock or dyspnea BEFORE intubation — RV physiology is the opposite of LV physiology, and maneuvers that help LV failure (fluids, afterload reducers, propofol, phenylephrine) frequently kill RHF patients.

1 · Recognize the phenotype

Clear lungs + elevated JVP + cool extremities (vs LHF: wet lungs + JVP + cool). Hypotension out of proportion to exam. Hypoxia without obvious LV dysfunction. Refractory to fluids — or gets worse with fluids. Common ED triggers: massive/submassive PE, RV MI, acute-on-chronic PAH crisis, sepsis-induced RV failure, post-LVAD RV failure, severe ARDS.[1][2]

Why this consult exists
RV physiology is the opposite of LV physiology. Things that help LV failure — fluids, afterload reducers, propofol, phenylephrine — frequently kill RHF patients.[1]

Set the patient state above (Hemodynamically unstable · Known PAH crisis) — the single next action highlights below and in the flow diagram.

Unstable now — this overrides everything

NO large-volume bolus — 250 mL crystalloid only if IVC <2 cm and collapsing. 12-lead ECG + V4R always. Peri-arrest suspected massive PE → 50 mg IV push alteplase, repeat once at 15 min if no ROSC, continue CPR ≥60 min. Activate PERT/cards/pulm early.[3][4][5]

2 · Identify the driving trigger

Etiology dictates therapy. Use POCUS + ECG + history to pick the dominant trigger: acute PE (RV strain + sudden dyspnea + risk factors), RV MI (inferior STEMI + V4R elevation), acute-on-chronic PAH crisis (known PH, off meds, infection), sepsis-induced RV failure, post-LVAD or post-cardiac-surgery RV failure, or severe ARDS / hypoxic pulmonary vasoconstriction.

4 · RV MI, PAH crisis, sepsis, post-LVAD, ARDS

RV MI — preload-dependent resuscitation
Confirm with V4R lead — ST elevation ≥1 mm = RV infarction (40% of inferior MIs). STRONG WARN — do NOT give: nitrates, morphine, diuretics, beta-blockers acutely, ACE-inhibitors acutely. DO: 500 mL crystalloid bolus, repeat to JVP/POCUS endpoint; dobutamine 2-10 mcg/kg/min if volume alone is insufficient; norepinephrine if MAP<65; activate the cath lab — primary PCI is definitive; maintain sinus rhythm (atrial kick ≈25% of RV output vs 15% for LV); pace AV-sequentially if heart block.

Acute-on-chronic PAH crisis — highest-mortality phenotype
Triggers: missed home meds, infection, surgery, dehydration, arrhythmia, anemia. STRONG WARN: do NOT abruptly stop home prostacyclin (epoprostenol, treprostinil) — rebound PH crisis can be fatal within hours; do NOT give a large fluid bolus (>500 mL); do NOT intubate without preparation (induction-related arrest is the leading cause of death); do NOT use phenylephrine alone. DO: continue/restart home prostacyclin; inhaled NO 20-40 ppm or inhaled epoprostenol 50 ng/kg/min or inhaled milrinone 5 mg (off-label); norepinephrine first; add vasopressin 0.03 U/min (pulmonary-sparing); dobutamine or milrinone if low CO; treat the trigger; call the PH center early.
Sepsis-induced RV failure — restrained fluid strategy
Mechanisms: acute pulmonary hypertension (cytokines, hypoxic vasoconstriction, microthrombi), septic cardiomyopathy, ARDS-related cor pulmonale. Restrained fluids — POCUS-guided, 250 mL aliquots, stop when IVC plethoric or RV dilates further. Norepinephrine first + vasopressin 0.03 U/min. Source control + early antibiotics unchanged. Dobutamine if low CO despite adequate MAP. Lung-protective ventilation if intubated (TV 4-6 mL/kg, PEEP titrated to RV not LV).
Post-LVAD or post-cardiac-surgery RV failure
Occurs in 10-40% of LVAD recipients — increased venous return from improved CO unmasks a marginal RV. Contact the LVAD coordinator immediately. Inhaled pulmonary vasodilators first-line. Milrinone 0.25-0.75 mcg/kg/min preferred (afterload reduction plus inotropy) over dobutamine. Avoid systemic vasodilators (drop LVAD flow alarms). RV mechanical support: Impella RP, ProtekDuo, VA-ECMO. Never adjust LVAD speed without coordinator guidance. Post-cardiac-surgery RHF: same bundle + rule out tamponade.

This module is reference content for any RHF phenotype above — the source consult reaches it from the toolbar/branch-point menu rather than the linear card flow, so it is not drawn as a connected flow step below.

Fluid strategy — the biggest mistake is reflexive fluid loading
A dilated RV pushes the septum into the LV, drops LV filling, raises wall stress, and worsens RV ischemia. Decision rule: IVC small/collapsing + small RV → 250-500 mL bolus, reassess. IVC plethoric OR RV already dilated → NO fluid, go straight to vasopressors/inotropes. Each bolus reassessed at the bedside — no '30 mL/kg' reflex. Exception: RV MI is preload-dependent and does benefit from volume early, still POCUS-guided. Diuresis if volume-overloaded: furosemide 40 mg IV or higher per home dose, ultrafiltration if refractory.

6 · RV-protective intubation (Farkas protocol)

Intubation is a leading cause of death in severe RHF/PAH
Induction agents drop SVR + preload; positive-pressure ventilation drops RV preload AND raises PVR; hypoxia or hypercapnia at any point can arrest these patients.
  1. Norepinephrine running to MAP ≥75 BEFORE induction — push-dose phenylephrine is NOT enough.
  2. If known PAH: inhaled NO 20-40 ppm or inhaled epoprostenol running pre-induction.
  3. Pre-oxygenate with HFNC + apneic oxygenation.
  4. Plan for bedside POCUS during/after intubation.
  5. 2 IV access, arterial line if time permits.

9 · Do NOT

Critical pitfalls6 items
Do NOT bolus large-volume fluid (>500 mL) in known/suspected PAH or chronic RV failure — worsens RV dilation and ischemia. RV MI is the one exception.
Do NOT give nitrates, morphine, or diuretics in RV MI — preload-dependent physiology; use volume + dobutamine instead.
Do NOT use phenylephrine alone as the primary pressor — it raises pulmonary vascular resistance more than systemic resistance and worsens RV failure.
Do NOT push a propofol bolus for RSI in severe RHF/PAH — drops SVR and contractility; frequent cause of peri-intubation arrest.
Do NOT abruptly discontinue home PAH-targeted therapy (epoprostenol, treprostinil, sildenafil) — rebound crisis can be fatal.
Do NOT apply the RHF playbook before excluding mimics — tamponade, tension pneumothorax, and pericardial constriction need different management entirely.

Milrinone 0.25-0.75 mcg/kg/min preferred (afterload reduction plus inotropy) over dobutamine. Avoid systemic vasodilators (drop LVAD flow alarms). RV mechanical support: Impella RP, ProtekDuo, VA-ECMO. Never adjust LVAD speed without coordinator guidance. Post-cardiac-surgery RHF: same bundle + rule out tamponade.

Milrinone: = computed 0.25 mcg/kg/min starting rate IV infusion — Titrate to max 0.75 mcg/kg/min. AVOID a loading dose in RV failure — risk of hypotension.

Occurs in 20-25% of ARDS patients and doubles mortality. Lung- and RV-protective ventilation: TV 4-6 mL/kg PBW, plateau pressure ≤27 cm H2O, driving pressure ≤14 cm H2O, PEEP titrated to RV (not compliance alone), pH ≥7.20-7.25 (avoid <7.15), prone position if PaO2/FiO2 <150, inhaled NO or inhaled epoprostenol if refractory, VV-ECMO if refractory (VA-ECMO if cardiogenic component), cisatracurium 48h if PaO2/FiO2 <120.

5 · Hemodynamic ladder — fluids & pressors (reference)

This module is reference content for any RHF phenotype above — the source consult reaches it from the toolbar/branch-point menu rather than the linear card flow, so it is not drawn as a connected flow step below.

A dilated RV pushes the septum into the LV, drops LV filling, raises wall stress, and worsens RV ischemia. Decision rule: IVC small/collapsing + small RV → 250-500 mL bolus, reassess. IVC plethoric OR RV already dilated → NO fluid, go straight to vasopressors/inotropes. Each bolus reassessed at the bedside — no '30 mL/kg' reflex. Exception: RV MI is preload-dependent and does benefit from volume early, still POCUS-guided. Diuresis if volume-overloaded: furosemide 40 mg IV or higher per home dose, ultrafiltration if refractory.

Agent | Dose | Role

Norepinephrine | 0.05-1.0 mcg/kg/min | First-line — preserves RV coronary perfusion, modest pulm-vasoconstrictor

Vasopressin | 0.03 U/min | Add-on — V1 receptors spare the pulmonary vasculature

Phenylephrine | — | AVOID alone — raises PVR more than SVR, worsens RV

Dobutamine | 2-10 mcg/kg/min | β-1 inotropy + mild β-2 pulm vasodilation

Milrinone | 0.25-0.75 mcg/kg/min | PDE-3 inhibitor — RV inotropy + pulm vasodilation; preferred in PAH-RHF, watch SVR

Inhaled NO / epoprostenol | 20-40 ppm / 50 ng/kg/min neb | Selective pulmonary vasodilation

Norepinephrine — unstable RHF starting rate: = computed 0.1 mcg/kg/min starting rate IV infusion — Titrate to MAP ≥65 (or ≥75 pre-RSI). Range across the RHF ladder: 0.05-1.0 mcg/kg/min.

Vasopressin: 0.03 U/min IV infusion, fixed/non-titratable — Add-on to norepinephrine. Does not raise pulmonary vascular resistance.

6 · RV-protective intubation (Farkas protocol)

Induction agents drop SVR + preload; positive-pressure ventilation drops RV preload AND raises PVR; hypoxia or hypercapnia at any point can arrest these patients.

  • Norepinephrine running to MAP ≥75 BEFORE induction — push-dose phenylephrine is NOT enough.
  • If known PAH: inhaled NO 20-40 ppm or inhaled epoprostenol running pre-induction.
  • Pre-oxygenate with HFNC + apneic oxygenation.
  • Plan for bedside POCUS during/after intubation.
  • 2 IV access, arterial line if time permits.

Norepinephrine — pre-induction target: titrate to MAP ≥75 before induction IV infusion — Must be running BEFORE the induction agent is pushed.

Agent | Dose | Verdict

Ketamine | 1-2 mg/kg IV | Preferred — preserves SVR, modest pulm vasodilator effect

Etomidate (low-dose) | 0.15-0.3 mg/kg IV | Preferred — lower than the usual 0.3 mg/kg dose; hemodynamically neutral

Propofol bolus | — | AVOID — drops SVR and contractility, frequent arrest

High-dose midazolam | — | Avoid

Ketamine: = computed dose (1.5 mg/kg representative point of the 1-2 mg/kg range) IV push — Preserves SVR — preferred induction agent for RV-protective RSI.

Etomidate (RV-protective low dose): = computed dose (0.15 mg/kg — low end of the 0.15-0.3 mg/kg range) IV push — Lower than the usual 0.3 mg/kg RSI dose. Hemodynamically neutral.

Rocuronium: = computed 1.2 mg/kg IV push — Onset 60-90 sec, duration 40-60 min. Sugammadex available for reversal.

  • Tidal volume 4-6 mL/kg PBW.
  • PEEP ≤5 initially, titrate up only by RV response.
  • Plateau pressure ≤27 cm H2O.
  • pH ≥7.25 (raise rate before tidal volume).
  • FiO2 to SpO2 ≥92% — hypoxia drives pulmonary vasoconstriction.
  • Avoid hyperventilation despite the urge — raises intrathoracic pressure.

7 · Disposition — ICU, PERT, PH center, ECMO, GOC

Pathway | Trigger

ICU | Massive/submassive-high PE, vasopressor/inotrope requirement, mechanical ventilation, lactate >4, active arrhythmia, post-lytic monitoring

PERT activation | Any massive/submassive PE, bleeding contraindication to lytic, decompensation despite anticoagulation, need for CDT/surgical embolectomy

PH center transfer | PAH crisis on home prostacyclin, new suspected PAH, need for advanced therapies (IV epo, inhaled treprostinil, transplant eval)

ECMO referral | Massive PE with arrest/refractory shock, ARDS with refractory hypoxia + RHF, bridge to definitive therapy

Group 1 PAH 5-year mortality without transplant ~50% — discuss prognosis early. Recurrent ICU admissions for PAH crisis should trigger advanced-care planning. Post-arrest PE survival is poor — engage family realistically.

8 · One-glance algorithm

RHF phenotype recognized — Clear lungs + elevated JVP + cool extremities. Hypotension out of proportion to exam. Hypoxia without LV dysfunction. Refractory to (or worse with) fluids.

Unstable — norepi + POCUS 90 sec + no big bolus + V4R — Norepi 0.1 mcg/kg/min to MAP≥65. POCUS in 90 sec. 250 mL crystalloid only if IVC<2cm and collapsing. 12-lead+V4R. Peri-arrest PE: alteplase 50 mg push.

POCUS — RV/LV ratio, D-sign, McConnell, TAPSE — RV/LV ratio ≥1.0, D-sign, McConnell >94% specific, TAPSE <17mm, IVC>2.1cm <50% collapse, 60/60 sign.

RV-protective intubation (Farkas) — Norepi to MAP≥75 pre-induction. Ketamine + low-dose etomidate. AVOID propofol bolus. TV 4-6, PEEP≤5, pH≥7.25.

PAH crisis — never stop home prostacyclin — Continue/restart home prostacyclin. Inhaled NO/epo. Norepi+vasopressin 0.03 U/min. Dobutamine/milrinone. Call PH center.

What is driving the RV failure? — PE, RV MI, PAH crisis, sepsis, post-LVAD, or ARDS — POCUS+ECG+history pick the dominant trigger.

Not RHF — exclude mimic, reroute — Tamponade, tension pneumothorax, severe LHF, hypovolemia, distributive shock, constriction.

Disposition — ICU / PERT / PH center / ECMO / GOC — ICU for pressors/vent/lactate>4/arrhythmia. PERT for any massive/submassive PE. PH center for prostacyclin patients. ECMO for refractory shock.

Acute PE — severity stratification — Massive / submassive intermediate-high / intermediate-low / low-risk by hemodynamics, RV strain, biomarkers.

RV MI — volume + dobutamine, NO nitrates — NO nitrates/morphine/diuretics/acute BB/ACEi. YES 500 mL bolus, dobutamine 2-10 mcg/kg/min, cath lab now.

Sepsis-induced RHF — restrained fluids — 250 mL aliquots POCUS-guided. Norepi+vasopressin. Dobutamine PRN. Lung-protective vent.

Post-LVAD RHF — call coordinator now — iNO/inhaled epo. Milrinone 0.25-0.75 mcg/kg/min. RV MCS. Never adjust LVAD speed unsupervised.

ARDS cor pulmonale — lung + RV-protective vent — Plateau≤27, driving pressure≤14, PEEP for RV, pH≥7.20, prone if P/F<150, ECMO if refractory.

Massive PE — reperfuse now — Alteplase 100 mg/2h or 50 mg push peri-arrest. CDT/thrombectomy if lytic contraindicated. ECMO bridge.

Submassive int-high — catheter over lytic — Heparin + PERT. CDT/thrombectomy preferred. MOPETT half-dose 50 mg an option. Full lyse if decompensating.

Submassive int-low — anticoagulate + monitor — Heparin/LMWH + monitored bed. DOAC transition at 24-48h if stable. 3-month CTEPH screen.

Low-risk PE — HESTIA-eligible, outpatient DOAC — Apixaban 10 mg BID×7d→5 mg BID or rivaroxaban 15 mg BID×21d→20 mg daily. Follow-up <72h.

9 · Do NOT

Critical pitfalls

Do NOT bolus large-volume fluid (>500 mL) in known/suspected PAH or chronic RV failure — worsens RV dilation and ischemia. RV MI is the one exception.

Do NOT give nitrates, morphine, or diuretics in RV MI — preload-dependent physiology; use volume + dobutamine instead.

Do NOT use phenylephrine alone as the primary pressor — it raises pulmonary vascular resistance more than systemic resistance and worsens RV failure.

Do NOT push a propofol bolus for RSI in severe RHF/PAH — drops SVR and contractility; frequent cause of peri-intubation arrest.

Do NOT abruptly discontinue home PAH-targeted therapy (epoprostenol, treprostinil, sildenafil) — rebound crisis can be fatal.

Do NOT apply the RHF playbook before excluding mimics — tamponade, tension pneumothorax, and pericardial constriction need different management entirely.
