Hemodialysis Emergencies: AEIOU Triage

Adult ESRD/hemodialysis patients presenting to the ED with an HD-related complication. Excludes peritoneal dialysis and pediatric ESRD.

⚠ Hyperkalemia is the most common life-threatening HD emergency: ECG changes may precede the lab potassium result — treat empirically on high suspicion, do not wait for the K+ level.
⚠ Treat the ECG, not just the K+ number: ECG can look normal despite a high K+, and severe changes can occur at K+ levels lower than the textbook thresholds.
⚠ Severe hyperkalemia with ECG changes: calcium (gluconate or chloride) is FIRST for cardiac membrane stabilization. Kayexalate and patiromer/SZC are NOT emergent therapies — they take hours to work and do not belong in the acute treatment sequence.
⚠ Uremic pericarditis: do NOT anticoagulate — hemorrhagic pericardial effusion risk. Dialyze WITHOUT heparin. NSAIDs are contraindicated in ESRD.
⚠ Preserve HD vascular access at all costs: no blood draws, no IVs, no BP cuffs on the access arm; never tourniquet proximal to a fistula/graft.

1 · AEIOU Triage

AEIOU framework for ESRD/hemodialysis complications:

• Access complications (bleeding, clotted, infected)
• Electrolyte emergencies (hyperkalemia, hypocalcemia)
• Infection (catheter sepsis, endocarditis)
• Overload (pulmonary edema, HTN emergency)
• Uremia (encephalopathy, pericarditis, bleeding)
