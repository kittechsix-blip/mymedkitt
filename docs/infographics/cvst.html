Cerebral Venous Sinus Thrombosis: Diagnose, Anticoagulate Anyway, Stratify, Escalate

Adults ≥18 in the ED/inpatient setting with suspected or confirmed cerebral venous sinus thrombosis (CVST). Excludes pediatric CVST and neonatal CVST — each needs a modified pathway.

⚠ ICH in CVST is NOT a contraindication to anticoagulation — proceed with LMWH. Hemorrhage here is a consequence of venous congestion, not a bleeding risk from the thrombosis itself.
⚠ Start enoxaparin 1 mg/kg SC q12h immediately — do not delay initiation for imaging completion or because ICH is present.
⚠ No seizure prophylaxis unless a seizure has already occurred — primary prophylaxis is not recommended (Canadian Stroke Best Practices: strong recommendation against).
⚠ Pregnant or postpartum: LMWH throughout — DOACs and warfarin are contraindicated (teratogenic).
⚠ Decompressive craniectomy for herniation signs or CT showing midline shift ≥5 mm — do not delay surgical consultation.
⚠ D-dimer NPV is 95-99.8%, but up to 10% of CVST patients have a normal D-dimer — do not use a normal D-dimer alone to exclude CVST, especially with isolated headache or symptoms >1 week.

1 · Presentation & seizures

Who gets CVST
CVST causes 0.5-3% of all strokes, primarily in young adults with a 3:1 female predominance. High-risk groups: pregnancy/puerperium (9 per 100,000 deliveries), OCP users, prothrombotic conditions, and malignancy (~6% of cases).

Classic presentation: headache >90% of cases, seizures 20-40%, focal neurologic deficits 20-50%, papilledema (suggests elevated ICP). Dominant syndromes: isolated intracranial hypertension, focal neurologic syndrome, encephalopathy/altered mental status, or new-onset seizure.

Acute seizure — treat, but do not pre-treat
Standard seizure protocol: Lorazepam first, then load an AED — Levetiracetam is preferred. No seizure prophylaxis unless a seizure has already occurred — Canadian Stroke Best Practices issues a strong recommendation against primary prophylaxis. After a first seizure, continue an AED in patients with supratentorial lesions. Risk factors: supratentorial lesions, motor deficits, superior sagittal sinus involvement, cortical vein thrombosis, hemorrhagic transformation.

5 · Escalation if deteriorating

Monitor for progressive neurologic decline, worsening mental status, new/expanding hemorrhage, or signs of herniation despite adequate anticoagulation.

Endovascular therapy — reserved, low-quality evidence
Reserved for: neurologic deterioration despite adequate anticoagulation, an absolute contraindication to anticoagulation, extensive thrombosis with severe deficit, or deep venous system involvement with altered mental status. Modalities: catheter-directed thrombolysis, mechanical thrombectomy, or combined approach. ESO: evidence is very low quality — "cannot provide a recommendation."
Decompressive craniectomy — malignant CVST
Indications: clinical herniation signs (3rd nerve palsy, GCS decline) or CT showing midline shift ≥5 mm, effacement of basal cisterns, or significant mass effect from hemorrhagic venous infarction. Outcomes: >50% favorable outcomes, ~20% mortality — better than medical management alone for a malignant course. Post-op: resume heparin 12 hours post-op at half-dose, full dose at 24 hours.

No special population factors
Standard pathway: LMWH → transition to a DOAC (or warfarin) at 5-15 days, duration set by etiology (3 months to indefinite).
Pregnant or postpartum
LMWH throughout pregnancy (does not cross placenta), continue ≥6 weeks postpartum (minimum 3 months total). DOACs and warfarin are contraindicated (warfarin: first-trimester teratogenicity — nasal hypoplasia, stippled epiphyses). Vaginal delivery is safe with a responsive team; C-section is not routinely required. Neuraxial anesthesia: hold LMWH 24h prior, contraindicated if herniation risk. CVST alone is NOT a contraindication to future pregnancy — use prophylactic LMWH during pregnancy and 6 weeks postpartum, and avoid estrogen-containing contraceptives.

Active malignancy
LMWH preferred for initial and long-term therapy — used in 71-74% of cancer-associated CVST cases; DOAC evidence is insufficient in malignancy-related CVST. Duration: minimum 3 months, or duration of active malignancy; consider indefinite if high recurrence risk. Higher intracranial hemorrhage risk (13-fold in brain cancer). Prior CVST does NOT negatively impact cancer survival.
OCP / estrogen user
OCP-associated CVST risk: OR 7.59 (95% CI 3.82-15.09) vs non-users — third-generation pills (desogestrel, gestodene) carry the highest risk. Discontinue all estrogen-containing contraceptives. Safe alternatives: progestin-only methods (levonorgestrel IUD, progestin-only pills, medroxyprogesterone) or non-hormonal (copper IUD, barrier methods). Future pregnancy is safe with prophylaxis; recommend thrombophilia workup.
