Supraglottic Airway — ED Rescue Tool

Adults in the ED, prehospital handoff, or cardiac arrest needing rescue oxygenation after failed intubation/BVM, during low-interruption arrest airway management, or as a bridge to a definitive airway. Excludes routine elective OR airway management and pediatric sizing (i-gel adult sizing table starts at 30 kg).

⚠ An SGA is a rescue bridge to restore oxygenation — it is NOT proof of a protected airway and does NOT eliminate aspiration risk.
⚠ Screen for RODS before relying on an SGA: Restricted mouth opening, Obstruction at or below the glottis, Distorted airway anatomy, Stiff lungs or c-spine.
⚠ Use a second-generation SGA with a gastric drainage channel when available.
⚠ Limit SGA insertion attempts to 3 total, changing something meaningful (size, device, position, operator) each attempt.
⚠ Confirm ventilation with chest rise, EtCO2 waveform/color change, SpO2 trend, bilateral breath sounds, and acceptable leak — confirmation of ventilation is NOT confirmation of aspiration protection.
⚠ Obstruction below/at the glottis will NOT be bypassed by an SGA — high pressures or distal obstruction require escalation toward a definitive airway or cricothyrotomy.
⚠ Regurgitation or a soiled airway: suction aggressively first. An SGA does not protect against aspiration.
⚠ Failed intubation + failed SGA + failed BVM = CICO (Cannot Intubate, Cannot Oxygenate) — declare CICO and proceed immediately to emergency cricothyrotomy; do not delay for repeat SGA attempts.

Frame

Use an SGA to rapidly restore oxygenation/ventilation when BVM is failing, intubation has failed, or cardiac arrest airway control must not interrupt compressions. An SGA buys oxygenation and time — it does not seal the trachea or eliminate aspiration risk.

Choose the clinical lane: this determines how aggressively you plan for intubation or cricothyrotomy after oxygenation is restored.

What to do now

Failed intubation/BVM — Plan B rescue
Declare failed intubation, call for help, oxygenate with the SGA, and stop the spiral of repeated laryngoscopy. If RSI cricoid pressure is blocking placement, release it with suction ready. Once ventilation works, pause and choose: awaken if appropriate, intubate through/after SGA if stable, or continue to definitive rescue.
Cardiac arrest — low-interruption airway
Do not let airway placement interrupt high-quality compressions. SGA is acceptable when ETI success is low, training opportunities are limited, or access is poor. After placement, ventilate about 10/min with continuous compressions. If ventilation fails, remove and return to BVM while preparing the next airway step.
Bridge to a definitive airway
Place SGA when oxygenation is the immediate problem and intubation setup needs time. Best bridge examples: post-failed-attempt desaturation, delayed expert arrival, difficult access, or short transport from prehospital bay to resus room. If there is airway obstruction below the glottis, SGA will not bypass it.
Considering SGA as primary airway
Skip straight to RODS screening below — a primary-airway decision lives or dies on the RODS screen, not on the entry scenario.

CICO

Cannot Intubate, Cannot Oxygenate
If intubation failed, SGA failed, and BVM cannot oxygenate: declare Cannot Intubate, Cannot Oxygenate and perform emergency front-of-neck access. Do not delay for more supraglottic attempts. Go to Emergency Cricothyrotomy.

Management algorithm

SGA — ED Rescue Tool — Restores oxygenation quickly; does not seal the trachea or eliminate aspiration risk.

Why are you placing the SGA? — Failed intubation/BVM, cardiac arrest, bridge, or primary airway — each frames how aggressively you plan the next airway step.

Oxygenate first (Plan B / arrest / bridge) — Whichever the scenario, restoring oxygenation with the SGA comes before further airway attempts.

RODS: will the SGA fail? — Restricted mouth, Obstruction at/below glottis, Distorted anatomy, Stiff lungs/c-spine — screen before relying on the device.

Reasonable SGA candidate — Adequate mouth opening, anatomy can accept the device, expected pressures within SGA seal capability. Prefer second-generation SGA with gastric drain.

Aspiration risk present? — SGA is not a cuffed tracheal tube — use it if oxygenation requires it, but plan early conversion when aspiration risk is high.

Choose device — Prefer a second-generation device with a gastric drainage channel; size by device instructions and anatomy.

Size selection — i-gel adult: size 3 = 30-60 kg, size 4 = 50-90 kg, size 5 = >90 kg. Cuffed LMA: follow device package, avoid overinflation.

Prep: suction, lube, backup — Suction, BVM/PEEP valve, EtCO2, oxygen, securing tape, gastric tube if supported, definitive airway backup. Lubricate posterior surface only.

Insertion attempt result — Insert along the hard palate until seated; avoid force.

Confirm effective ventilation — Chest rise, EtCO2 waveform/color change, improving SpO2, bilateral breath sounds, acceptable leak. Confirmation of ventilation is not confirmation of aspiration protection.

Troubleshoot: leak / poor seal — Deeper seating, jaw thrust, reposition if safe, resize, cuff-pressure check, lower inspiratory pressure. Stop at attempt 3 or if oxygenation is falling.

Troubleshoot: obstruction / high pressure — Biting, laryngospasm, kinked circuit, malposition, bronchospasm, pulmonary edema, ARDS, or distal obstruction. SGA fails when resistance is distal or pressures exceed seal capability.

Regurgitation / soiled airway — Turn/suction aggressively, clear the pharynx, reassess. If oxygenation adequate, prepare definitive cuffed airway; if not, remove/replace only as part of an active rescue plan.

Now what? — Once oxygenation is restored, choose the endpoint — do not drift with an SGA in place when a definitive airway is clearly needed.

Intubate through SGA — Only with stable oxygenation, a compatible device, and a trained operator. Fiberoptic/video guidance — blind intubation through an SGA is not recommended.

Convert to definitive airway — Prepare suction, VL/DL, bougie, smaller ETT, backup SGA, and cric kit before removing a working device.

Failed SGA ventilation — Limit to 3 total attempts, changing something meaningful each time (size, device, position, operator). If BVM works, regroup; if BVM also fails, this is CICO.

CICO — cannot intubate, cannot oxygenate — Declare CICO and perform emergency front-of-neck access. Do not delay for more supraglottic attempts.

Disposition / handoff — Document indication, device, size, attempts, EtCO2/ventilation evidence, complications, and whether the SGA is bridge or definitive plan.
