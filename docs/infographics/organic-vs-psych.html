Organic vs Psychiatric Emergency

Adults and children presenting to the ED with a new or changed behavioral/psychiatric complaint. Guides differentiation of organic (medical) from primary psychiatric causes, targeted workup, and disposition. Excludes patients already in extremis (unstable airway/breathing/circulation) who need standard resuscitation first.

⚠ Glucose <60: hypoglycemia can mimic ANY psychiatric presentation (anxiety, agitation, confusion, psychosis, catatonia, seizures) — check fingerstick glucose immediately on every behavioral patient and treat before proceeding.
⚠ Any vital sign abnormality must be explained and addressed before psychiatric disposition — do NOT attribute fever, tachycardia, hypertension, or hypoxia to "anxiety" without investigation.
⚠ Do NOT transfer to a psychiatric facility with pending workup or abnormal vital signs — document "not medically cleared for psychiatric facility" and continue monitoring.
⚠ Elderly patient with new behavioral change = delirium until proven otherwise (missed 76% of the time in the ED). If CAM positive or uncertain, admit for medical workup — do NOT send to a psychiatric facility.
⚠ If sedation is needed in a patient with possible delirium, avoid benzodiazepines (worsen delirium) and avoid physical restraints where possible — use antipsychotics and verbal de-escalation instead.
⚠ Without collateral history establishing baseline mental status, a delirium diagnosis is unreliable — if no collateral is available, err on the side of medical workup.

1 · The challenge

19-80% of presentations attributed to psychiatric illness have an underlying medical cause. Missed medical diagnosis rates: 8-48% in EDs, with the highest rates on first presentation.

Key principle: approach behavioral complaints like general medical conditions — ABCs, thorough history (including collateral), physical exam, and selective testing.

History is most sensitive: a complete history was 94% sensitive for identifying medical conditions — more than physical exam or lab tests.

2 · What to do now

Hypoglycemia (glucose <60) — treat immediately
Hypoglycemia can cause ANY psychiatric presentation — anxiety, agitation, confusion, psychosis, catatonia, seizures. D50W 25g (50 mL) IV push (adults). D10W 2-5 mL/kg (pediatrics) — avoid D50W in children. Glucagon 1mg IM if no IV access. Recheck glucose in 15 minutes; if mental status does not improve, proceed with full organic workup.
Abnormal vital signs — organic cause likely
Do NOT attribute abnormal vitals to "anxiety" without investigation. Fever suggests infection/NMS/serotonin syndrome/thyroid storm/excited delirium. Tachycardia suggests sympathomimetic toxidrome, withdrawal, thyrotoxicosis, PE, or pheochromocytoma. Proceed to targeted workup.
Elderly (≥65) — delirium until proven otherwise
Delirium is missed 76% of the time in ED elderly patients. Screen with CAM or bCAM (84% sensitive, 96% specific, <2 minutes). If CAM positive or uncertain: admit for medical workup — do NOT send to a psychiatric facility.
Red flags present — proceed to targeted workup
63% of patients with new-onset psychiatric symptoms had an organic etiology (Henneman 1994). High-risk features include new onset, age >45 with first presentation, altered consciousness, fluctuating course, visual hallucinations, disorientation, memory impairment, focal deficits, and abnormal vital signs.
No red flags — low-risk pathway
Known psychiatric history, similar to prior presentations, alert/oriented, normal vital signs and exam, age <45, no recent medication/substance changes. ACEP 2017: "Do not routinely order laboratory testing on patients with acute psychiatric symptoms" who meet low-risk criteria.
Workup abnormal — medical condition identified
Classify as causal (treat medical condition, psych symptoms should improve), contributory (treat both, may need medical stabilization first), or incidental (address per standard care, may proceed with psych eval). Reassess mental status after treatment.
Workup pending — do not transfer yet
Continue monitoring vital signs; maintain a safe environment. Avoid physical restraints if possible (worsens delirium) — prefer verbal de-escalation. If sedation is needed, avoid benzodiazepines (worsen delirium) — use antipsychotics. Document "not medically cleared for psychiatric facility." Do NOT transfer with pending workup or abnormal vital signs.
High risk — psychiatric admission
High risk for suicide/harm to others, gravely disabled, needs medication stabilization or ECT, or no adequate outpatient support. Before transfer: confirm vital signs normal/stable, no pending abnormal labs, medical causes ruled out/treated, and document "medically STABLE for inpatient psychiatric care."
Moderate risk — psychiatric evaluation
Psychiatry consult, crisis team evaluation, and social work in the ED. If disposition is unclear, observe 4-8 hours with serial reassessment. Average ED length of stay for psychiatric patients: 15-30 hours.
Low risk — outpatient follow-up
No SI/HI or reliable denial, can contract for safety, has adequate support, can self-care, and has a follow-up plan. Provide crisis hotline 988, a safety plan, and involve family in discharge planning.
