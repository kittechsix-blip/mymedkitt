algorithm

Pneumonia: CAP Severity, HAP/VAP, Complicated & Special Populations

Adults ≥18 with suspected or confirmed pneumonia (CAP, HAP/VAP, complicated pneumonia, aspiration, or influenza pneumonia) in the ED or inpatient setting. Excludes pediatric pneumonia, immunocompromised-host opportunistic-infection workup, and ventilator-management decisions beyond antibiotic selection.

⚠ Severe CAP → ICU: 1 major criterion (septic shock requiring vasopressors, or respiratory failure requiring mechanical ventilation) OR 3+ minor criteria → direct ICU admission, regardless of severity score.
⚠ Macrolide monotherapy is NO LONGER recommended for CAP — S. pneumoniae resistance >30% in the US; only use if local resistance <25%.
⚠ MRSA coverage: add if prior MRSA isolation, IV antibiotics or hospitalization within 90 days, ESRD, injection drug use, lung abscess/empyema, or recent influenza.
⚠ Pseudomonas coverage: add only for prior isolation, bronchiectasis, severe COPD (FEV1 <25%), tracheostomy history, or lung abscess/empyema — do NOT add routinely for isolated/uncomplicated COPD.
⚠ ACCP Stage 3-4 parapneumonic effusion (pH <7.2, glucose <40, positive culture, loculated, or frank pus) requires drainage — antibiotics alone are not sufficient.
⚠ Aminoglycosides: NEVER as monotherapy for HAP/VAP — increased mortality.
⚠ Aspiration pneumonia: do NOT routinely add anaerobic coverage — only if empyema, necrotizing pneumonia, lung abscess on CT, or severe periodontal disease with putrid sputum.
⚠ Duration discipline: CAP 5 days total if afebrile ≥48h and clinically stable; HAP/VAP 7 days total, de-escalate at 48h (low-MDR) or 48-72h (high-MDR) if cultures negative.

Outpatient, healthy — no comorbidities
Amoxicillin 1g PO TID × 5 days (preferred), or Doxycycline 100 mg IV or PO q12h × 5 days. Respiratory fluoroquinolone (levofloxacin 750 mg IV/PO daily × 5 days or moxifloxacin 400mg daily × 5 days) if allergic to both. Macrolide monotherapy NO LONGER recommended — resistance >30%.
Outpatient, with comorbidities
Levofloxacin 750 mg IV/PO daily × 5 days or moxifloxacin 400mg daily × 5 days (preferred monotherapy), OR amoxicillin-clavulanate 875/125mg TID + azithromycin/doxycycline, OR cefpodoxime 200mg BID + azithromycin × 5 days. Close follow-up in 48-72h.
Admitted, non-severe (no Pseudomonas risk)
Ceftriaxone 1-2g IV daily + azithromycin (500 mg IV on day 1, then 250 mg PO daily for 4 additional days), OR ampicillin-sulbactam 3g IV Q6h + azithromycin, OR levofloxacin 750 mg IV/PO daily × 5 days monotherapy. If MRSA risk flagged, add vancomycin or linezolid (below). 5 days total if afebrile ≥48h and clinically stable. Stop MRSA coverage at 48h if cultures negative.
Admitted, MRSA/Pseudomonas risk present
Anti-pseudomonal beta-lactam — cefepime 2g IV Q8h, OR piperacillin-tazobactam 4.5g IV Q6h, OR meropenem 1g IV Q8h (if ESBL concern) — PLUS azithromycin 500mg IV daily OR levofloxacin 750mg IV daily (dual Pseudomonas coverage). Add vancomycin/linezolid if MRSA flagged (below). De-escalate at 48h based on cultures/susceptibilities.
Severe CAP — ICU admission
Ceftriaxone 2g IV daily + azithromycin 500mg IV daily OR levofloxacin 750mg IV daily. Add cefepime 2g IV Q8h or piperacillin-tazobactam 4.5g IV Q6h + levofloxacin if Pseudomonas risk. Add vancomycin/linezolid if MRSA flagged (below). Consider methylprednisolone 40mg IV daily × 5 days if refractory shock. 7 days minimum; reassess at 48-72h.
MRSA risk flagged — add coverage
Add vancomycin 15-20 mg/kg IV Q8-12h (target trough 15-20 mcg/mL; ICU/high-concern loading dose 25-30 mg/kg), OR linezolid 600 mg IV or PO q12h. Discontinue if no MRSA cultured within 48 hours. MRSA nasal PCR (~95% NPV) can guide early discontinuation.

7 · Complicated pneumonia — effusion, empyema, abscess

Complicated pneumonia includes parapneumonic effusion, empyema, lung abscess, and necrotizing pneumonia. Chest imaging (CT preferred if suspected) → if effusion present, diagnostic thoracentesis for pH, glucose, LDH, protein, cell count, gram stain, culture. Confirm exudate first via Light's Criteria (any 1: pleural/serum protein ratio >0.5, pleural/serum LDH ratio >0.6, pleural LDH >2/3 upper limit of serum normal).

Stage 1-2 — antibiotics alone
Standard CAP regimen by severity, 5-7 days, drainage NOT required — 95% resolve with antibiotics alone. Repeat imaging 48-72h; re-tap (pH/glucose/culture) if enlarging, persistent fever >72h, deteriorating, or new loculations — reclassify to drainage pathway if now Stage 3-4.

Intrapleural fibrinolytics — MIST2 protocol
tPA 10mg + DNase 5mg in 30mL saline via chest tube, clamp 1 hour, BID × 3 days (6 doses total). MIST2 (NEJM 2011): combination 90% success, 4% surgery rate, vs tPA alone 55%, DNase alone 22%, placebo 16% success. Contraindicated: bronchopleural fistula, active bleeding/coagulopathy, recent surgery (<2 weeks). Assess response after 3 days — if inadequate, proceed to surgery.
Empyema (Stage 4) — frank pus, immediate drainage required
Antibiotics with anaerobic coverage critical: ampicillin-sulbactam 3g IV Q6h, or piperacillin-tazobactam 4.5g IV Q6h, or ceftriaxone 2g IV + metronidazole 500mg IV Q8h. Add vancomycin/linezolid if MRSA risk. Duration 2-4 weeks total (IV → PO). Common organisms: Strep (S. milleri), S. aureus (incl. MRSA), anaerobes, gram-negatives.
Surgical optionIndications
VATS (preferred first-line)Failed chest tube ± fibrinolytics (48-72h no improvement), multiloculated empyema, thick fibrinous peel, large/organizing effusion. Success 85-90%, mortality 0-2%.
Thoracotomy + decorticationVATS failure (10-15% conversion), trapped lung, Stage IV organized empyema, bronchopleural fistula, suspected malignancy. Mortality 2-8%, prolonged air leak 10-20%.

Early surgery (within 7-10 days) has better outcomes — after 3-4 weeks, fibrosis makes decortication more difficult. Post-op: continue IV antibiotics 2-4 weeks; CT at 6-8 weeks to assess resolution.

Lung abscess — antibiotics first-line
Success rate 63-95% with antibiotics alone. Preferred: ampicillin-sulbactam 3g IV Q6h or amoxicillin-clavulanate 875/125mg PO TID. Alternative: ceftriaxone + clindamycin 600mg IV Q6h, or ceftriaxone + metronidazole 500mg IV Q8h. Duration: 3-4 weeks total (minimum) — IV until clinical improvement (7-14 days), then PO to complete course. Drain if: >6cm, no improvement after 7-14 days, immunocompromised, or MRSA/Pseudomonas/Klebsiella (higher failure rate). Percutaneous CT-guided drainage: 84% success.

8 · Special populations

Aspiration pneumonia — modern approach
Do NOT routinely cover anaerobes. Standard treatment = standard CAP regimen (beta-lactam + macrolide, or respiratory FQ). Add anaerobic coverage ONLY if empyema, necrotizing pneumonia, lung abscess (on CT), or severe periodontal disease + putrid sputum: piperacillin-tazobactam 4.5g IV Q6h monotherapy, ampicillin-sulbactam 3g IV Q6h, or standard CAP + metronidazole 500mg IV Q8h. Duration 5-7 days (longer if abscess/empyema). Aspiration pneumonitis (chemical inflammation) does NOT need antibiotics unless bacterial superinfection develops.
Influenza pneumonia
Oseltamivir 75 mg PO BID x 5 days (start within 48h, but benefit possible even later), or peramivir 600mg IV × 1 if unable to take PO. Bacterial superinfection risk 26-77% of hospitalized patients. Add bacterial coverage (standard CAP regimen) if: severe disease at presentation, deterioration after initial improvement, or persistent/recurrent fever after 3-5 days. MRSA coverage NOT routine. Can stop bacterial antibiotics at 48-72h if stable, cultures negative, low suspicion.

Red flags requiring extended observation

  • PCP follow-up within 1 week
  • Repeat chest X-ray at 6-8 weeks (smoker, age >50, or persistent symptoms)

Inpatient reassessment at 48-72h
Signs of response: defervescence, improving oxygenation, decreasing WBC, declining procalcitonin, hemodynamic stability. If NOT improving: re-culture (blood, sputum, consider BAL), CT chest for complications, broaden coverage, consider resistant organisms or alternate diagnosis (PE with infarct, malignancy, organizing pneumonia, drug-induced pneumonitis, heart failure). De-escalate based on cultures; procalcitonin can guide stopping antibiotics.

10 · Do NOT

Critical pitfalls6 items
Do NOT use macrolide monotherapy for CAP — S. pneumoniae resistance >30% in the US.
Do NOT use aminoglycosides as monotherapy for HAP/VAP — increased mortality.
Do NOT withhold drainage for an ACCP Stage 3-4 effusion (pH <7.2, glucose <40, positive culture, loculated, or frank pus) — antibiotics alone are not sufficient.
Do NOT add Pseudomonas coverage routinely for isolated COPD — reserve for prior isolation, bronchiectasis, severe COPD, tracheostomy, or abscess/empyema.
Do NOT routinely add anaerobic coverage for aspiration pneumonia — only if empyema, necrotizing pneumonia, abscess, or severe periodontal disease with putrid sputum.
Do NOT delay early surgical referral for empyema past 7-10 days — after 3-4 weeks, fibrosis makes decortication harder.

2 | >10mm, free-flowing, pH ≥7.2, glucose ≥60, negative culture | Low | No

3 | pH <7.2 OR glucose <40 OR positive culture OR loculated | Moderate | Yes

4 | Frank pus | High | Yes

Standard CAP regimen by severity, 5-7 days, drainage NOT required — 95% resolve with antibiotics alone. Repeat imaging 48-72h; re-tap (pH/glucose/culture) if enlarging, persistent fever >72h, deteriorating, or new loculations — reclassify to drainage pathway if now Stage 3-4.

Effusion characteristic | Drainage method | Success

Free-flowing (no loculations) | Chest tube (≥14 Fr) or pigtail catheter | ~90%

Loculated/septated | tPA + DNase (MIST2) with chest tube, or early VATS | 90% (vs 16% tube alone)

Large (>50% hemithorax) or organizing (pleural thickening >2mm) | Consider early VATS | Higher tube failure rate

tPA 10mg + DNase 5mg in 30mL saline via chest tube, clamp 1 hour, BID × 3 days (6 doses total). MIST2 (NEJM 2011): combination 90% success, 4% surgery rate, vs tPA alone 55%, DNase alone 22%, placebo 16% success. Contraindicated: bronchopleural fistula, active bleeding/coagulopathy, recent surgery (<2 weeks). Assess response after 3 days — if inadequate, proceed to surgery.

Antibiotics with anaerobic coverage critical: ampicillin-sulbactam 3g IV Q6h, or piperacillin-tazobactam 4.5g IV Q6h, or ceftriaxone 2g IV + metronidazole 500mg IV Q8h. Add vancomycin/linezolid if MRSA risk. Duration 2-4 weeks total (IV → PO). Common organisms: Strep (S. milleri), S. aureus (incl. MRSA), anaerobes, gram-negatives.

Surgical option | Indications

VATS (preferred first-line) | Failed chest tube ± fibrinolytics (48-72h no improvement), multiloculated empyema, thick fibrinous peel, large/organizing effusion. Success 85-90%, mortality 0-2%.

Thoracotomy + decortication | VATS failure (10-15% conversion), trapped lung, Stage IV organized empyema, bronchopleural fistula, suspected malignancy. Mortality 2-8%, prolonged air leak 10-20%.

Early surgery (within 7-10 days) has better outcomes — after 3-4 weeks, fibrosis makes decortication more difficult. Post-op: continue IV antibiotics 2-4 weeks; CT at 6-8 weeks to assess resolution.

Success rate 63-95% with antibiotics alone. Preferred: ampicillin-sulbactam 3g IV Q6h or amoxicillin-clavulanate 875/125mg PO TID. Alternative: ceftriaxone + clindamycin 600mg IV Q6h, or ceftriaxone + metronidazole 500mg IV Q8h. Duration: 3-4 weeks total (minimum) — IV until clinical improvement (7-14 days), then PO to complete course. Drain if: >6cm, no improvement after 7-14 days, immunocompromised, or MRSA/Pseudomonas/Klebsiella (higher failure rate). Percutaneous CT-guided drainage: 84% success.

8 · Special populations

Do NOT routinely cover anaerobes. Standard treatment = standard CAP regimen (beta-lactam + macrolide, or respiratory FQ). Add anaerobic coverage ONLY if empyema, necrotizing pneumonia, lung abscess (on CT), or severe periodontal disease + putrid sputum: piperacillin-tazobactam 4.5g IV Q6h monotherapy, ampicillin-sulbactam 3g IV Q6h, or standard CAP + metronidazole 500mg IV Q8h. Duration 5-7 days (longer if abscess/empyema). Aspiration pneumonitis (chemical inflammation) does NOT need antibiotics unless bacterial superinfection develops.

Oseltamivir 75 mg PO BID x 5 days (start within 48h, but benefit possible even later), or peramivir 600mg IV × 1 if unable to take PO. Bacterial superinfection risk 26-77% of hospitalized patients. Add bacterial coverage (standard CAP regimen) if: severe disease at presentation, deterioration after initial improvement, or persistent/recurrent fever after 3-5 days. MRSA coverage NOT routine. Can stop bacterial antibiotics at 48-72h if stable, cultures negative, low suspicion.

9 · Disposition & monitoring

Discharge criteria and inpatient-monitoring guidance from the source consult (not wired into the interactive flow above — see brief.md for why).

Discharge criterion (ALL must be met)

Temperature <37.8°C for ≥24h

Heart rate <100 bpm

Respiratory rate <24

Systolic BP ≥90 mmHg

O2 sat ≥90% on room air (or baseline)

Tolerating oral intake

Normal mentation (or baseline)

Able to take oral antibiotics

Persistent hypoxia, hemodynamic instability, altered mentation, inability to tolerate PO, unreliable follow-up, or complicated pneumonia (effusion, abscess).

  • Complete total 5-day antibiotic course (IV + PO)
  • PCP follow-up within 1 week
  • Repeat chest X-ray at 6-8 weeks (smoker, age >50, or persistent symptoms)
  • Pneumococcal and influenza vaccination if not up to date
  • Return precautions: worsening dyspnea, persistent fever >48h, new chest pain, hemoptysis, confusion
  • Smoking cessation counseling for all smokers

Signs of response: defervescence, improving oxygenation, decreasing WBC, declining procalcitonin, hemodynamic stability. If NOT improving: re-culture (blood, sputum, consider BAL), CT chest for complications, broaden coverage, consider resistant organisms or alternate diagnosis (PE with infarct, malignancy, organizing pneumonia, drug-induced pneumonitis, heart failure). De-escalate based on cultures; procalcitonin can guide stopping antibiotics.

10 · Do NOT

Critical pitfalls

Do NOT use macrolide monotherapy for CAP — S. pneumoniae resistance >30% in the US.

Do NOT use aminoglycosides as monotherapy for HAP/VAP — increased mortality.

Do NOT withhold drainage for an ACCP Stage 3-4 effusion (pH <7.2, glucose <40, positive culture, loculated, or frank pus) — antibiotics alone are not sufficient.

Do NOT add Pseudomonas coverage routinely for isolated COPD — reserve for prior isolation, bronchiectasis, severe COPD, tracheostomy, or abscess/empyema.

Do NOT routinely add anaerobic coverage for aspiration pneumonia — only if empyema, necrotizing pneumonia, abscess, or severe periodontal disease with putrid sputum.

Do NOT delay early surgical referral for empyema past 7-10 days — after 3-4 weeks, fibrosis makes decortication harder.
