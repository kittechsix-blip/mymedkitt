algorithm

Cervical Spine Pain — Non-Traumatic (ED Workup)

Adults in the ED with neck pain WITHOUT acute trauma. Excludes cervical spine trauma (separate consult), pediatric torticollis, and post-traumatic neck pain. Trauma must be excluded by history before using this pathway.

⚠ Fever + neck pain + IVDU or immunosuppression = MRI now — spinal epidural abscess is the classic miss, and most patients are missed because they look "well enough" on the first visit.
⚠ Hoffman + Lhermitte + gait change = emergent MRI — degenerative cervical myelopathy is a surgical emergency, not normal aging.
⚠ Posterior headache + neck pain in a patient under 50 → screen for vertebral artery dissection with CTA or MRA neck.
⚠ Rheumatoid arthritis + occipital headache or new neuro signs = flex/ext XR BEFORE any neck manipulation or intubation — assume atlantoaxial subluxation.
⚠ Bilateral arm weakness with hand intrinsic wasting = cord, not nerve root — image the spinal cord, not just the disc.
⚠ New severe neck pain in a patient with known cancer = metastatic cord compression (MSCC) until proven otherwise — start dexamethasone before MRI confirmation if suspicion is high.
⚠ A normal WBC does NOT exclude spinal epidural abscess — WBC is normal in ~30% of cases; ESR + CRP are the more sensitive screen.

Algorithm

Cervical spine pain, non-traumatic — first-pass triage — Trauma already excluded by history. Always screen red flags FIRST — mechanical neck pain is the most common diagnosis but also the most commonly missed-something diagnosis.

Which red-flag pattern? — Fever/IVDU/immunosuppression → infection. Cancer + new severe pain → MSCC. Sudden posterior HA + age<50 → VAD. RA + occipital HA/new neuro → AAS. Age<40 + AM stiffness>1h → inflammatory.

Radiculopathy — nerve-root map + provocative tests — Dermatomal arm pain/paresthesia, myotome weakness, or reduced reflex in a single root distribution. Spurling test has the highest specificity. Lhermitte sign positive → escalate to myelopathy workup.

Mechanical / myofascial pain — diagnosis of exclusion — Axial neck pain, no dermatomal radiation, no UMN signs, normal neuro exam. Made only AFTER excluding red flags and radicular/myelopathic features.

Myelopathy screen — UMN signs — Hand clumsiness, gait disturbance, bilateral arm/leg symptoms, sphincter changes. Check Hoffman, Lhermitte, hyperreflexia, clonus, Babinski, tandem gait, hand intrinsic wasting.

Radiculopathy vs myelopathy — side-by-side comparator — One arm, dermatomal, LMN, decreased reflexes = radiculopathy (usually outpatient). Bilateral, UMN signs, gait change, hand clumsiness = myelopathy (image now). Mixed presentations exist — treat as myelopathy for urgency.

Spinal epidural abscess / discitis — Do NOT wait for the fever+pain+deficit triad — only ~15% have all three. ESR+CRP both elevated, blood cultures x2, MRI with and without contrast (not CT/XR). Empiric vancomycin + ceftriaxone or cefepime.

Metastatic cord compression (MSCC) — Oncologic emergency. MRI whole spine (skip lesions common). Start dexamethasone immediately — do not wait for MRI confirmation if suspicion is high. Ambulatory status at treatment is the strongest predictor of preserved function.

Vertebral artery dissection — Leading cause of stroke under 45. Sudden posterior neck pain/occipital HA, often post-manipulation. CTA neck (faster/more available) or MRA, both 95-100% sensitive. See the Cervical Artery Dissection consult for the full pathway.

RA atlantoaxial subluxation — Pannus erodes the C1 transverse ligament — cord at risk during neck flexion (intubation, manipulation, exam). Flex/ext XR (open-mouth odontoid + lateral). No manipulation, no aggressive intubation positioning — awake fiberoptic if airway needed.

Inflammatory spondyloarthropathy — Age<40, morning stiffness>1h, improves with activity, insidious onset. ESR/CRP, HLA-B27, rheum referral. NSAIDs first-line. Caveat: ank spond patients need imaging after even minor trauma — fused spine fractures easily.

Radiculopathy — conservative management — No imaging if <6 weeks, no red flags, no progressive deficit. MRI if progressive motor deficit, suspected myelopathy, >6 weeks refractory, or red flags. NSAIDs/APAP first-line; short steroid or gabapentin course for severe pain.

Degenerative cervical myelopathy — management — Surgical decompression recommended for moderate-severe DCM. MRI within 24h, spine surgery consult. Admit if rapidly progressive, severe gait dysfunction/falls, new sphincter dysfunction, or severe DCM.

Cord syndromes (non-traumatic context) — Central cord (arms>legs, most common non-traumatic incomplete syndrome), Brown-Sequard (ipsilateral motor/proprioception, contralateral pain/temp), anterior cord (motor+pain/temp lost, dorsal columns spared, worst prognosis), posterior cord (rare).

Disposition — where does this patient go? — Match severity: emergent surgery, admit, urgent spine-clinic follow-up, or routine PCP/PT follow-up.

Emergent OR / surgical disposition — MSCC with deficit (after starting dexamethasone), SEA with deficit, severe rapidly progressive myelopathy, RA AAS with new cord signs needing airway protection. Direct MD-MD handoff to accepting surgeon.

Admit — neurosurgery / medicine — Spinal infection, moderate-severe DCM, VAD (telemetry + stroke workup), severe radiculopathy with deficit, MSCC without immediate surgical indication. Serial neuro exams q4h.

Discharge — urgent spine clinic follow-up — Stable radiculopathy deficit, suspected mild DCM, refractory pain after 4-6 weeks. MRI scheduled, spine clinic within 1-2 weeks, written return precautions.

Discharge — routine follow-up — No red flags, normal neuro exam, mechanical pattern, symptoms <6 weeks. NSAIDs + APAP, PCP follow-up 1-2 weeks, written return precautions.

Spinal infection, moderate-severe DCM with stable but progressive symptoms, VAD (telemetry + stroke workup), severe radiculopathy with deficit, MSCC without immediate surgical indication. Serial neuro exams q4h, specialty consult, MRI complete, DVT prophylaxis, Foley if cord dysfunction.

Common ED pitfalls

"Just mechanical" without screening red flags — always ask fever, IVDU, cancer hx, RA, sudden onset, posterior HA.

Sending an RA patient to the floor for intubation — flex/ext XR first, awake fiberoptic if any AAS concern.

Missing posterior HA + neck pain as VAD — age <50 + posterior HA = CTA neck.

Calling bilateral hand clumsiness "carpal tunnel" — check Hoffman, gait, reflexes; myelopathy is often missed for years.

Assuming normal ESR/CRP excludes abscess — sensitivity 94-100% only when BOTH are elevated; very early or partial-treated cases can have normal values.

Assuming normal WBC excludes infection — WBC is normal in ~30% of spinal epidural abscess.

Routine XR for mechanical pain — low yield, exposes radiation, can mislead.

Long-term soft collar — worsens outcomes; max 1-2 weeks.

Labeling a cancer patient's new severe pain "musculoskeletal" — MRI whole spine + start dexamethasone.

Discharging a myelopathy patient — even "mild" Hoffman+gait change needs MRI + spine consult.
