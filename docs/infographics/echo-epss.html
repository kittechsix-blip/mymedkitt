Echo — EPSS for Systolic Function

Point-of-care assessment of left ventricular systolic function via E-point septal separation (EPSS), measured in the parasternal long-axis (PLAX) view with M-mode through the mitral valve leaflet tips. EPSS may be inaccurate in aortic regurgitation, mitral stenosis, hypertrophic cardiomyopathy (HCM), and non-sinus rhythms. Pediatric thresholds differ from adult thresholds (6.0 mm overall; 4.9 mm for ages 0-3 years).

EPSS overview

E-point septal separation (EPSS) >7 mm is a validated threshold for detecting reduced left ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF). EPSS measures the shortest distance between the anterior mitral valve leaflet at its peak opening (E-point) and the interventricular septum during early diastole. A wider gap suggests reduced LV contractility and/or LV dilation. EPSS >7 mm suggests LVEF <50%; EPSS >10 mm indicates more severe dysfunction. The measurement remains valid even with abnormal septal motion, including paradoxical septal movement.

100%Sensitivity for LVEF ≤30% at EPSS >7mm
52%Specificity at EPSS >7mm

Technique — positioning, PLAX, M-mode

  • Position patient supine or left lateral decubitus (brings heart closer to chest wall)
  • Use a phased-array cardiac transducer (2-5 MHz)
  • Place transducer at the 3rd-4th intercostal space, right next to the sternum
  • Probe marker points toward the patient's left hip (standard mode)
  • Hug the sternum, sliding up/down rib spaces to find the best window
  • Visualize interventricular septum, anterior mitral valve leaflet, and LV cavity
  • Remember the 3 L's: parasternal Long axis should have the Left ventricle on the Left side of the screen
  1. With a good PLAX view on screen, activate M-mode
  2. Place the M-mode cursor perpendicular to the interventricular septum
  3. Cursor should pass through the tips of the mitral valve leaflets at the level where they open in early diastole
  4. Ensure both septum and anterior mitral leaflet are clearly visible on the M-mode tracing
  5. Adjust sweep speed to see 3-4 cardiac cycles
  6. If unclear, return to 2D mode and readjust cursor position

Measure the shortest distance between the peak of the anterior mitral valve leaflet (E-point) and the interventricular septum during early diastole. On the M-mode tracing, the anterior mitral leaflet traces a characteristic pattern: the E-point is the tallest peak (maximal opening in early diastole); the A-point is a smaller peak just before systole from atrial contraction. Measure from the E-point peak to the nearest edge of the septum. EPSS reflects both the amplitude of mitral valve excursion and left ventricular size, particularly outflow tract dilation.

▶ What to do now

EPSS < 7 mm — normal systolic function likely
Preserved LVEF (≥50%) likely. Combine with visual estimation of global cardiac function across multiple views. Consider MAPSE or fractional shortening if clinical suspicion for dysfunction persists.
EPSS 7-10 mm — grey zone
Look for additional signs of fluid overload to clarify whether systolic function is truly reduced: bilateral B-lines on lung ultrasound and IVC diameter/collapsibility on subcostal view.
Grey zone + fluid overload — likely reduced EF
EPSS 7-10 mm with B-lines and/or IVC plethora supports reduced LV systolic function. Obtain additional echo views (A4C, PSAX), consider formal echocardiography, and evaluate for underlying etiology.
Grey zone, no overload — indeterminate
Specificity is moderate (52%) at the >7 mm threshold — false positives can occur in this borderline range. Integrate with visual estimation of global function; consider MAPSE or fractional shortening; obtain formal echocardiography if suspicion persists.
EPSS > 10 mm — reduced systolic function
Strongly suggests severe reduction in ejection fraction (100% sensitivity for LVEF ≤30% at EPSS >7 mm). Assess for fluid overload, obtain additional echo views, consider formal echocardiography, and initiate heart failure management as clinically indicated.

EPSS assessment algorithm

EPSS for Systolic Function — EPSS >7 mm is a validated threshold for reduced LVEF. Invalid in AR, MS, HCM, and non-sinus rhythms.

Obtain PLAX view — Probe at 3rd-4th ICS near sternum, marker to left hip. Visualize septum, anterior MV leaflet, LV cavity.

Activate M-mode through MV leaflet tips — Cursor perpendicular to septum, through tips of mitral valve leaflets in early diastole.

Measure E-point to septum gap — Shortest distance from the E-point peak (tallest peak on M-mode) to the nearest edge of the septum.

Interpret EPSS value — <7mm normal, 7-10mm grey zone, >10mm reduced systolic function.

Normal systolic function likely — EPSS <7mm — preserved LVEF (≥50%) likely. Correlate clinically; consider MAPSE/FS if suspicion persists.

Grey zone: assess B-lines / IVC — EPSS 7-10mm. Scan bilateral anterior chest for B-lines; assess IVC diameter and respiratory variation.

Reduced systolic function (HF likely) — EPSS >10mm — 100% sensitivity for LVEF ≤30%. Initiate HF workup, consider formal echo.

Grey zone + overload — likely reduced EF — B-lines and/or IVC plethora present with borderline EPSS — supports reduced systolic function. Recommend formal echo.

Grey zone, no overload — indeterminate — No B-lines, IVC normal. Specificity moderate (52%) at >7mm — consider MAPSE/FS, formal echo if suspicion persists.
