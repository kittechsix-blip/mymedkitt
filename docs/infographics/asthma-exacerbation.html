Status Asthmaticus: Maximize Medical Therapy Before the Tube

Adults ≥18 in the ED with an acute asthma exacerbation. Excludes pediatrics, pregnancy, COPD/ACO, and cardiogenic causes of wheeze — each needs a modified approach.

⚠ Silent chest = life-threatening. No wheezing means no air movement, not improvement. Silent chest, altered mental status, or bradycardia signals impending respiratory arrest.
⚠ Intubation is HIGH RISK in asthma. Paralysis removes the patient's compensation and air trapping causes hypotension. Exhaust maximal medical therapy + BiPAP + ketamine before intubating.
⚠ If an intubated asthmatic becomes hypotensive or arrests: DISCONNECT the ETT and compress the chest for 30–60 seconds to release trapped air, then reconnect at a LOW rate with a LONG expiratory time.
⚠ Sedate BiPAP with ketamine, never propofol/benzos/opioids in the spontaneously breathing asthmatic — those blunt respiratory drive and can precipitate arrest.
⚠ For DSI/intubation use ketamine + rocuronium and AVOID propofol, etomidate, and succinylcholine as the sole induction strategy; preoxygenate on BiPAP before you paralyze.

1 · Classify severity

Severity drives the intensity of therapy. Classify from speech, work of breathing, vitals, and **peak expiratory flow (PEF)**.{{ref1}}{{ref2}} A **silent chest** is not reassurance — it means no air is moving.

Feature | Mild–Moderate | Severe | Life-threatening

Speech | Phrases / sentences | Words only | Too breathless / drowsy

RR / HR | RR <30, HR <120 | RR ≥30, HR ≥120 | Bradycardia / hypotension

SpO₂ | 90–95% | <90% | <90% despite O₂

PEF (% predicted) | >50% | ≤50% | <25% or unable

Chest | Wheeze | Loud wheeze | **Silent chest**

No wheezing = no air movement. Combined with drowsiness, exhaustion, or bradycardia it indicates critically severe obstruction and impending arrest — move straight to the critical pathway.

2 · Maximize medical therapy

**The best treatment for status asthmaticus is to not intubate the patient.**{{ref3}} Stack bronchodilators, steroids, and magnesium aggressively and reassess by PEF at 60–90 min.

Albuterol (SABA): 2.5-5mg nebulized q20min x 3 doses Nebulized — First-line for every severity. Escalate to continuous nebulization if severe.

Ipratropium: 0.5mg nebulized Nebulized with first 3 albuterol doses (first hour only) — Adds benefit in the first hour; no added benefit beyond it.

Methylprednisolone 125mg IV: 125 mg IV (or prednisone 40–60 mg PO) — Give early — early systemic corticosteroids reduce admission rates.

For severe asthma, **continuous** nebulization at **10-15mg/hr** albuterol is superior to intermittent dosing.{{ref7}} Monitor K⁺ (beta-agonist–driven hypokalemia), tachycardia, and lactic acidosis.

Magnesium sulfate: 2 grams IV over 20 minutes IV — A bronchodilator for SEVERE asthma; may repeat ×1. Greatest benefit when PEF <25% predicted.

IV magnesium to prevent hospitalization (severe asthma): NNT = 4

4 · BiPAP, DSI & the vent

BiPAP buys time for drugs to work. Start LOW: asthmatics already have auto-PEEP, so external PEEP can worsen hyperinflation.

Setting | Start | Why

EPAP (PEEP): 5 cm H2O | Low | Auto-PEEP already present

IPAP: 10-12 cm H2O | Titrate ↑ by 2 q5–10 min | Comfort / tidal volume

FiO₂ | 100% → wean to SpO₂ 92–95% | Correct hypoxemia

  • Ketamine 1–1.5 mg/kg IV to dissociate (patient keeps breathing).
  • Preoxygenate on BiPAP or NRB for 3–5 minutes.
  • When SpO₂ is optimal (ideally >95%): ketamine induction + rocuronium 1.2 mg/kg.
  • Intubate once paralyzed; use the largest ETT possible.

Vent parameter | Setting | Rationale

Mode / VT | Volume control, 6–8 mL/kg IBW | Avoid overdistension

Rate | 8-12 breaths/min | LOW rate — time to exhale

I:E ratio | 1:4 to 1:5 | LONG expiratory time

PEEP | 0–5 cm H₂O | Start very low

Accept a pCO₂ of **60-80 mmHg** (pH >7.15) to avoid dangerous breath-stacking. Do **not** raise the rate to normalize the CO₂ — the lungs are obstructed, not dead.{{ref14}}{{ref15}}

Assume dynamic hyperinflation first: **disconnect the ETT and compress the chest 30–60 s** to release trapped air, then reconnect at a LOW rate with LONG expiration. If it persists, rule out pneumothorax → needle decompression.{{ref3}}

7 · Do NOT

Critical pitfalls

**Do NOT read a silent chest as improvement** — it is impending arrest.

**Do NOT sedate BiPAP with propofol, high-dose benzos, or opioids** — use ketamine.

**Do NOT chase a normal CO₂ on the vent** — accept permissive hypercapnia; raising the rate causes breath-stacking and hypotension.

**Do NOT forget the disconnect maneuver** — hypotension after intubation is air trapping until proven otherwise.

**Do NOT stop steroids or continue plain SABA alone in severe asthma** — go continuous and add magnesium.
