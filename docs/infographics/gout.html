Gout & Pseudogout — ED Management

Adults with acute monoarticular or oligoarticular joint pain concerning for gout or CPPD (pseudogout) in the ED. Excludes pediatrics. Septic arthritis workup/antibiotic management is a separate consult — this covers the gout/pseudogout diagnostic and treatment pathway only.

⚠ Crystals do NOT rule out septic arthritis — gout/CPPD joints are at INCREASED risk of infection. Never delay treatment for aspiration if suspicion for septic arthritis is low, but never skip aspiration/cultures when suspicion is present.
⚠ Suspected septic arthritis (fever >38.5C, immunocompromise, synovial WBC >85,000/mm3 even with crystals, recent joint surgery/injection, IVDU, overlying skin infection, bacteremia/endocarditis, prosthetic joint): aspirate and send Gram stain + culture; if toxic-appearing treat for BOTH infection and crystal arthritis; hold empiric steroids until cultures obtained if stable.
⚠ Serum urate may be NORMAL during an acute flare (up to 40% of cases) — a normal level does NOT rule out gout.
⚠ Do NOT start or stop urate-lowering therapy (allopurinol/febuxostat) during an acute flare — continue any existing ULT; starting NEW ULT during a flare requires anti-inflammatory prophylaxis.
⚠ The old high-dose colchicine regimen (1 mg q1-2h until GI toxicity) is OBSOLETE and toxic — use low-dose only: 1.2 mg x1, then 0.6 mg 1 hour later, max 1.8 mg/day.
⚠ Do NOT use low-dose aspirin or allopurinol/febuxostat alone as acute-flare treatment — neither is anti-inflammatory and both can prolong the attack.

Overview

Gout (monosodium urate/MSU crystal deposition) and pseudogout/CPPD (calcium pyrophosphate crystal deposition) both present as acute monoarthritis. Neither can be reliably distinguished from septic arthritis on clinical grounds alone, and crystals do NOT exclude concurrent infection.

Gout & pseudogout algorithm

Acute monoarthritis: gout or pseudogout? — Both present as acute monoarthritis and cannot be reliably distinguished from septic arthritis clinically. Crystals do NOT rule out concurrent infection.

Aspirate now, or empiric treatment reasonable? — Aspirate for first presentation/uncertain diagnosis or suspected infection. Empiric treatment acceptable for classic podagra with known crystal-proven gout history and ACR/EULAR score >=8.

Aspirate: crystal analysis, Gram stain, culture, cell count — Sensitivity for crystal detection ~84%. Synovial WBC 2,000-50,000 = inflammatory (crystal/RA/reactive); >50,000 = septic range, but gout/CPPD can exceed 50,000/uL.

High-risk for septic arthritis: aspirate + treat both — Fever >38.5C, immunocompromise, synovial WBC >85,000/mm3, recent joint surgery/injection, IVDU, overlying skin infection, bacteremia/endocarditis, or prosthetic joint. Crystals do NOT exclude infection.

ACR/EULAR criteria, serum urate, imaging — Score >=8 = gout (Sens 92%, Spec 89%). Serum urate may be NORMAL in up to 40% of flares. Double-contour sign on US: Sens ~77%, Spec ~95%.

Crystal analysis result? — MSU = needle-shaped, strong negative birefringence, yellow parallel. CPP = rhomboid, weak positive birefringence, blue parallel.

Gout confirmed (MSU crystals) — Initiate anti-inflammatory therapy immediately. Continue any existing ULT during the flare — do not stop it.

CPPD confirmed (CPP crystals): treat like gout, screen secondary causes — No urate-lowering therapy dissolves CPP crystals. Screen for hyperparathyroidism, hemochromatosis, hypomagnesemia, hypothyroidism if age <55, recurrent, or polyarticular. Same NSAID/colchicine/steroid dosing as gout.

No crystals / not performed: empiric treatment if low suspicion for infection — Appropriate for classic podagra with known gout history, ACR/EULAR score >=8, low suspicion for septic arthritis. Reassess in 24-48h; lower threshold for aspiration if treatment fails.

Acute flare treatment: NSAID, colchicine, or steroid by ACR 2020 priority — Priority: NSAIDs first if no contraindications, then colchicine (best within 36h), then glucocorticoids, then IL-1 inhibitors for refractory cases. Do NOT use low-dose aspirin or allopurinol/febuxostat alone.

NSAID / colchicine / steroid contraindications? — NSAID CI: CKD (CrCl<60), GI bleed, CVD, anticoagulation. Colchicine CI: severe CKD/hepatic impairment, CYP3A4/P-gp inhibitors. Steroid relative CI: diabetes, active infection.

NSAID: naproxen or indomethacin, full anti-inflammatory dose — Naproxen 750mg x1 then 250mg q8h (or 500mg BID x3-5d then 250mg BID); indomethacin 50mg TID x2-3d then 25mg TID. Avoid if CrCl<60.

Colchicine: 1.2mg then 0.6mg 1h later — Max 1.8mg/day. Most effective within 24-36h of onset. Reduce 50% for CYP3A4/P-gp inhibitors; do not repeat within 2 weeks if CrCl<30 or on dialysis.

Corticosteroid: prednisone or IM triamcinolone/methylprednisolone — Prednisone 30-40mg daily x5d (or 0.5mg/kg/day weight-based option); IM triamcinolone 60mg or methylprednisolone 80-120mg x1 for polyarticular/unable-to-take-PO.

Intra-articular steroid: single joint with effusion — Aspirate first, then inject triamcinolone 10-40mg (or methylprednisolone 10-80mg) by joint size. Avoid if suspected septic arthritis until Gram stain back.

IL-1 inhibitor: refractory / all agents contraindicated — Anakinra 100mg SC daily x3-5d, or canakinumab 150mg SC x1 (no repeat before 12 weeks). Screen for infection first; not first-line due to cost/infection risk.

Monitor response; reassess at 24-48h — Improvement expected within 24-48h. If no improvement by 48-72h: reconsider diagnosis (septic arthritis?), verify dosing, consider aspiration, or add a second agent.

Disposition? — Most gout flares are managed outpatient. Admit if septic arthritis not excluded, polyarticular with systemic illness, unable to take PO, or refractory pain.

Discharge (± admission-then-discharge) with return precautions — Discharge: complete anti-inflammatory course, ice/rest/elevate, return for fever/worsening/no improvement by 48-72h/new joints. Admission route: IV methylprednisolone 60-125mg if unable to tolerate PO, serial exams, repeat arthrocentesis if cultures pending, then transitions to these same discharge criteria.

Rheumatology referral, ULT counseling, summary — Refer for tophaceous gout, >=2 flares/year despite ULT, CKD complicating management, or intolerance to multiple agents. ULT target urate <6 mg/dL. Do NOT start allopurinol in the ED — start outpatient with anti-inflammatory prophylaxis.
