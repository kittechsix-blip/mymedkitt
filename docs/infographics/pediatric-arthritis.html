Pediatric Arthritis — ED Evaluation & Management

Pediatric patients in the emergency department presenting with joint pain or arthritis, from a single joint to polyarticular involvement, acute to chronic. Covers the sick-vs-well framework, septic arthritis risk stratification (Kocher/Caird), Lyme arthritis, JIA (diagnosis of exclusion, age <16, duration ≥6 weeks per ILAR), reactive arthritis, PSRA vs acute rheumatic fever (Jones criteria), and the malignancy/orthopedic-mimic differential (SCFE, Perthes, osteomyelitis, growing pains). Excludes adult rheumatologic arthritis and non-ED chronic rheumatology follow-up management.

Initial assessment

Joint pain accounts for 5% of pediatric ED visits but includes life- and limb-threatening diagnoses. Framework: (1) Is the child sick or well? (2) Is there TRUE arthritis (swelling, warmth, effusion, decreased ROM)? (3) Monoarticular or polyarticular? (4) What age? (5) Is there fever?

  1. Septic arthritis — cartilage destruction within hours
  2. Osteomyelitis — often presents as joint pain
  3. Malignancy — leukemia presents with bone/joint pain in 25-43%
  4. SCFE — orthopedic emergency if unstable
  5. Non-accidental injury — always consider in young children
  6. Inflammatory arthritis — requires timely diagnosis
Pain vs. swelling pearl
Pain > swelling suggests infection or malignancy. Swelling > pain suggests inflammatory arthritis.
  • Assess if child is systemically ill or well-appearing
  • Obtain arthrocentesis for suspected septic arthritis
  • Apply Kocher criteria for septic arthritis risk stratification
  • Give empiric IV antibiotics if high suspicion for septic joint
  • Obtain hip ultrasound for all pediatric hip pain
  • Rule out SCFE with frog-leg lateral radiograph in adolescents
  • Consider malignancy in children with night pain, systemic symptoms
  • Urgent orthopedics consult for suspected septic arthritis

▶ What to do now

ILL-appearing → treat as septic until proven otherwise
Fever, toxic, refuses weight-bearing, severe pain with movement → arthrocentesis + empiric IV antibiotics + STAT orthopedics. Do NOT delay antibiotics for aspiration.
Hip pain → apply Kocher/Caird criteria

Acute knee → always examine the hip

Chronic (>6wk) monoarticular → suspect JIA

Oligo/polyarticular → broad differential

Treatment failure
Minimal response to oral antibiotics: IV ceftriaxone 2-4 weeks. Symptoms may persist for weeks after starting antibiotics — this does NOT indicate treatment failure.

Most Lyme arthritis is managed outpatient: 28-day oral antibiotic course, NSAIDs for symptom relief, follow-up with PCP/ID in 2-4 weeks. Full resolution may take 4-6 weeks after completing antibiotics. Follow-up serology is NOT recommended — antibodies persist long after successful treatment.

JIA & chronic arthritis

Juvenile Idiopathic Arthritis: arthritis of unknown etiology before age 16, persisting ≥6 weeks, diagnosis of exclusion. Suspect when: chronic symptoms, morning stiffness improving with activity, swelling > pain, no fever (except systemic JIA), normal/mildly elevated inflammatory markers, not acutely toxic.

Do NOT start steroids
ED role: rule out infection and malignancy, start symptom management, arrange rheumatology referral, screen for uveitis if indicated. Do NOT start steroids without rheumatology input — can mask malignancy.

Reactive, post-infectious arthritis & acute rheumatic fever

Post-viral reactive arthritis: most common infectious cause, self-limiting, resolves ~6 weeks, treated with NSAIDs/supportive care. Post-enteric reactive arthritis (Salmonella, Shigella, Campylobacter, Yersinia): onset 1-4 weeks after GI illness, HLA-B27 association, may become chronic. Lyme arthritis: late manifestation, requires antibiotics.

2015 Revised Jones Criteria: requires evidence of prior GAS infection + 2 major OR 1 major + 2 minor criteria. Major: carditis (clinical or subclinical echo), polyarthritis (monoarthritis in high-risk populations), chorea, erythema marginatum, subcutaneous nodules. Minor: fever (≥38.5°C low-risk, ≥38.0°C high-risk), elevated ESR/CRP, prolonged PR interval (if no carditis).

  1. Throat culture, ASO titer (peaks 3-5wk post-infection), anti-DNase B (peaks 6-8wk)
  2. ECG (PR prolongation) and echocardiogram (valvulitis, regurgitation, pericarditis) — carditis can be silent
  3. CBC, ESR, CRP, BMP

GAS eradication: Penicillin V 250-500 mg PO BID × 10 days, OR Amoxicillin 50 mg/kg/day (max 1 g) × 10 days, OR Benzathine penicillin G 600,000 U (<27 kg) or 1.2 MU (≥27 kg) IM ×1.

Leukemia mimics arthritis
Leukemia presents with bone/joint pain in 25-43% of cases and may be misdiagnosed as JIA, growing pains, or trauma. Pain suggesting malignancy: severe, out of proportion, night pain waking the child, localized to bone (not joint), progressive/unremitting. Systemic features: fever, fatigue, weight loss, pallor, easy bruising, lymphadenopathy, hepatosplenomegaly. Key lab red flag: thrombocytopenia (<300 x 10⁹/L) — most specific.

ALWAYS consider SCFE in an adolescent (especially obese) with hip, thigh, or knee pain, a limp without clear cause, or hip stiffness. Obligate external rotation with hip flexion is pathognomonic. X-ray: AP + frog-leg lateral if stable; AP + cross-table lateral if unstable (do NOT frog-leg an unstable SCFE). Klein's line should intersect the epiphysis; a positive Trethowan sign (it does not) suggests SCFE.

SCFE management
Strict non-weight bearing immediately. Do NOT attempt reduction — increases AVN risk. Urgent orthopedic consultation STAT. Stable SCFE (can bear weight): urgent surgery within 24-48h. Unstable SCFE (cannot bear weight): emergency surgery within hours. Treatment: in-situ percutaneous screw fixation. 30% slip the contralateral hip (70% within 18 months).

Diagnostic criteria (ALL must be present): age 3-12 years, bilateral lower limb pain (NOT joints), evening/night only (never on waking), no limp, normal exam, no systemic symptoms. Red flags that are NOT growing pains: limp, morning stiffness, joint swelling, unilateral pain, pain during activity, systemic symptoms, night pain waking the child, point tenderness, refusal to bear weight. If classic history AND normal exam: no workup needed.

Reassurance is the treatment if criteria met and exam normal: massage, stretching, heat, acetaminophen/ibuprofen PRN. Return if limping develops, pain present in the morning or during activities, fever, joint swelling/redness, weight loss/fatigue, or pain localized to one area only. Pearl: if not confident in the diagnosis, get basic labs (CBC, ESR, CRP) — low cost compared to missing pathology.

HSP (IgA vasculitis) — most common vasculitis in children. Classic tetrad: palpable purpura (100%, lower extremities/buttocks), arthritis/arthralgia (50-80%), abdominal pain (50-75%), renal involvement (20-50%). Arthritis: large joints, oligoarticular, periarticular swelling, non-erosive, may precede rash in 25%. Treatment: mild — supportive/rest; moderate — NSAIDs (avoid if renal involvement) or acetaminophen; severe — prednisone 1-2 mg/kg/day × 1-2 weeks. Monitoring: UA weekly ×4wk then monthly ×6mo, blood pressure, nephrology referral if hematuria/proteinuria/HTN. Pearl: steroids relieve symptoms but do NOT prevent renal disease.

Other joint (ankle/elbow/wrist/shoulder) — Same workup as knee: systemic assessment, labs, X-ray, ultrasound if effusion, arthrocentesis if high concern for septic.

JIA subtypes (ILAR classification) — 7 JIA subtypes; oligoarticular most common (ANA+ = uveitis risk); systemic JIA has quotidian fever + MAS risk.

Reactive & post-infectious arthritis — Post-infectious arthritis: viral (self-limiting), post-enteric, Lyme (abx needed), post-strep (differentiate PSRA vs ARF).

Arthrocentesis or observation? — Arthrocentesis is diagnostic AND therapeutic. Indications: CRP>20, non-weight-bearing, deterioration, high anxiety/limited follow-up.

Lyme-endemic area, fits pattern? — Large joint swelling with minimal pain, endemic area, well-appearing → consider Lyme. Otherwise proceed with standard septic workup.

JIA: ED management (NSAIDs, rule out mimics) — CBC/diff, ESR, CRP, ANA, X-ray to rule out mimics. NSAIDs first-line. Urgent rheumatology + ophthalmology (uveitis screen) referral.

PSRA vs Acute Rheumatic Fever — PSRA: non-migratory, slow NSAID response; ARF: migratory, dramatic NSAID response, risk of carditis; Jones criteria for ARF dx.

Synovial fluid interpretation — WBC >50,000/uL highly suspicious for septic; >100,000/uL very specific; <2,000/uL rules out septic arthritis.

Observation protocol (4-6h, serial exams) — Serial exams q2h, NSAID trial, repeat CRP. Concerning signs prompt arthrocentesis. Mandatory 24h follow-up if discharged.

Lyme arthritis: antibiotic treatment — Amoxicillin (<8yr) or doxycycline (≥8yr) × 28 days. IV ceftriaxone if oral treatment failure.

ARF workup & management — Confirm strep infection (ASO, anti-DNase B), ECG + echo for carditis. Penicillin for GAS eradication, high-dose aspirin, secondary prophylaxis.

Septic arthritis workup — Cartilage destruction within 8h; ortho STAT for OR drainage; do NOT delay abx for aspiration if septic; age-based pathogens.

Suspected JIA disposition — Discharge with scheduled NSAIDs + urgent rheumatology (1-2wk) + ophthalmology if ANA+. Admit if systemic JIA with concerning features.

Lyme arthritis disposition — Outpatient: 28-day antibiotic course + NSAIDs. Follow-up 2-4wk. Serology NOT useful for monitoring response.

ARF disposition — Admit most cases for cardiac monitoring. Cardiology follow-up mandatory. Secondary prophylaxis duration depends on carditis severity.

Empiric IV antibiotics — Low MRSA: cefazolin; high MRSA: vancomycin; <5yr add Kingella coverage; vancomycin does NOT cover Kingella.

Septic arthritis disposition — Admit for IV antibiotics + operative joint drainage (hip: OR washout mandatory). Orthopedic consult mandatory. Excellent prognosis if treated <24-48h.

Septic arthritis: risk stratification, workup & treatment

Criteria present | Probability of septic hip

0 | <0.2%

1 | 3.0%

2 | 40.0%

3 | 93.1%

4 | 99.6%

criteria: 0 criteria 0-0; 1 criterion 1-1; 2 criteria 2-2; 3 criteria 3-3; 4 criteria 4-4

  • Fever (history of temperature >38.5°C)
  • Non-weight bearing
  • ESR >40 mm/hr
  • WBC >12,000/mm³
  • Caird modification adds a 5th criterion: CRP >20 mg/L

CRP >20 mg/L is the strongest single independent predictor; refusal to bear weight has OR 14.5. If CRP <20 mg/L AND weight-bearing: <1% chance of septic arthritis. Kocher/Caird criteria predate recognition of Kingella kingae as a major pathogen, which presents with milder inflammatory markers.

0 criteria = <0.2% probability of septic arthritis. Likely transient synovitis if well-appearing, afebrile/low-grade, will bear weight, CRP <20 mg/L. Management: NSAIDs (ibuprofen 10 mg/kg q6-8h or naproxen 5-7 mg/kg q12h), rest, follow-up in 24-48 hours. Expected course: resolves in 1-2 weeks. If no improvement in 48-72 hours, reconsider diagnosis and obtain labs/imaging.

1-2 criteria = 3-40% probability — the diagnostic gray zone. CRP <20 mg/L AND weight-bearing → very low risk (<1%), consider observation. CRP >20 mg/L OR non-weight-bearing → strongly consider arthrocentesis. Factor in family reliability for follow-up and clinical gestalt. Pearl: when in doubt, tap the joint.

  • CRP >20 mg/L
  • Non-weight bearing
  • Clinical deterioration during observation
  • High parental anxiety / limited follow-up access
  • Provider clinical concern
  • Ultrasound guidance confirms effusion and identifies optimal needle entry

Parameter | Septic | Inflammatory | Non-inflammatory

WBC count | >50,000/uL | 2,000-50,000/uL | <2,000/uL

PMN % | >90% | 50-80% | <25%

Glucose | Decreased | Normal-low | Normal

Gram stain | May be + | Negative | Negative

In-ED observation 4-6 hours: serial exams q2h, ibuprofen trial 10 mg/kg, repeat CRP if elevated, reassess weight-bearing. Concerning signs (worsening pain, fever, non-weight-bearing, rising CRP) prompt arthrocentesis. If discharged: NSAIDs around-the-clock ×48h, strict return precautions, mandatory PCP/ED follow-up in 24 hours.

Cartilage destruction begins within 8 hours of infection. Do NOT delay antibiotics for arthrocentesis if the patient is septic — blood cultures + empiric antibiotics can proceed while arranging OR drainage. Orthopedics STAT for operative planning.

Age | Likely pathogens

<3 months | GBS, S. aureus, gram-negatives, N. gonorrhoeae

3 months-5 years | Kingella kingae, S. aureus, Strep species

>5 years | S. aureus, Strep pyogenes, N. gonorrhoeae (adolescents)

Cefazolin: 40 mg/kg/dose IV q8h (max 2000 mg/dose) IV — Low-MRSA setting first-line. Covers Kingella. Total duration 2-4 weeks, culture-directed.

Vancomycin: 15 mg/kg/dose IV q6h IV — High-MRSA setting. Requires TDM. Vancomycin does NOT cover Kingella — add a beta-lactam (cefazolin) in young children.

Ceftriaxone (add-on for sexually active adolescents): 50 mg/kg IV/IM ×1 (max 2 g) IV/IM — Add for possible N. gonorrhoeae coverage in sexually active adolescents.

Admit for IV antibiotics and operative joint drainage — hip requires OR washout; knee may attempt serial aspirations. Orthopedic surgery consultation is mandatory. Prognosis is excellent if treated within 24-48 hours. Delayed treatment risks cartilage destruction, growth plate damage, chronic osteomyelitis, joint ankylosis.

Knee monoarthritis & Lyme arthritis

  • Septic arthritis — ill, febrile, refuses weight-bearing
  • Transient synovitis — less common than hip, but occurs
  • Lyme arthritis — large, swollen knee with minimal pain
  • Reactive arthritis — post-infectious
  • Early JIA — oligoarticular subtype
  • Trauma — fracture, ligament injury

Feature | Lyme | Septic

Pain | Minimal/mild | Severe

Systemic symptoms | Absent | Present

Kocher criteria | ≤1 | Multiple

CRP | Mildly elevated | Very elevated

Weight bearing | Often preserved | Refuses

Children <8 years: Amoxicillin 50 mg/kg/day divided TID (max 500 mg/dose) × 28 days. Alternative: azithromycin 10 mg/kg day 1, then 5 mg/kg days 2-10.

Amoxicillin: 50 mg/kg/day PO divided TID (max 500 mg/dose) × 28 days PO — First-line for children <8 years with Lyme arthritis.

Children ≥8 years: Doxycycline 4.4 mg/kg/day divided BID (max 100 mg/dose) × 28 days. Short-course doxycycline (≤21 days) is now considered safe at any pediatric age.

Doxycycline: 4.4 mg/kg/day PO divided BID (max 100 mg/dose) × 28 days PO — First-line for children ≥8 years with Lyme arthritis.

Minimal response to oral antibiotics: IV ceftriaxone 2-4 weeks. Symptoms may persist for weeks after starting antibiotics — this does NOT indicate treatment failure.

Most Lyme arthritis is managed outpatient: 28-day oral antibiotic course, NSAIDs for symptom relief, follow-up with PCP/ID in 2-4 weeks. Full resolution may take 4-6 weeks after completing antibiotics. Follow-up serology is NOT recommended — antibodies persist long after successful treatment.

JIA & chronic arthritis

Juvenile Idiopathic Arthritis: arthritis of unknown etiology before age 16, persisting ≥6 weeks, diagnosis of exclusion. Suspect when: chronic symptoms, morning stiffness improving with activity, swelling > pain, no fever (except systemic JIA), normal/mildly elevated inflammatory markers, not acutely toxic.

ED role: rule out infection and malignancy, start symptom management, arrange rheumatology referral, screen for uveitis if indicated. Do NOT start steroids without rheumatology input — can mask malignancy.

Subtype | Frequency | Key features

Oligoarticular | 50-80% | ≤4 joints in first 6mo, peak 2-4yr, ANA+ 70% → high uveitis risk

Polyarticular RF-negative | 15-25% | ≥5 joints, bimodal age, symmetric large+small joints

Polyarticular RF-positive | 5% | ≥5 joints, adolescent girls, erosive (similar to adult RA)

Systemic JIA | 5-15% | Quotidian fever ≥2wk, salmon rash, HSM/LAD, risk of MAS

Enthesitis-related (ERA) | — | Boys >8yr, lower extremity, HLA-B27+, may develop axial disease

Psoriatic arthritis | — | Arthritis + psoriasis or dactylitis + family history

Undifferentiated | — | Does not fit other categories

NSAIDs are first-line: Naproxen 5-7 mg/kg/dose PO q12h (max 500 mg/dose, 1000 mg/day) or Ibuprofen 10 mg/kg/dose PO q6-8h (max 400 mg/dose, 2400 mg/day). May take 2-4 weeks for full anti-inflammatory effect.

Naproxen: 5 mg/kg/dose PO q12h PO — First-line NSAID for JIA (low end of 5-7 mg/kg range). Max 500 mg/dose, 1000 mg/day.

Naproxen: 7 mg/kg/dose PO q12h PO — High end of 5-7 mg/kg range. Max 500 mg/dose, 1000 mg/day.

Ibuprofen: 10 mg/kg/dose PO q6-8h PO — Alternative NSAID. Max 400 mg/dose, 40 mg/kg/day or 2400 mg/day.

Ophthalmology referral is urgent if uveitis suspected (eye pain, redness, photophobia, vision changes), and routine for ANA+ oligoarticular JIA — screen even if asymptomatic.

Discharge criteria: infection ruled out (or very low probability), malignancy not suspected, stable symptoms, pain controlled with oral NSAIDs, reliable follow-up. Discharge plan: scheduled NSAIDs, gentle ROM, rheumatology referral within 1-2 weeks, ophthalmology referral if ANA+. Admit if systemic JIA with concerning features (high fever, MAS concern), unable to maintain hydration, or diagnostic uncertainty with malignancy concern.

Reactive, post-infectious arthritis & acute rheumatic fever

Post-viral reactive arthritis: most common infectious cause, self-limiting, resolves ~6 weeks, treated with NSAIDs/supportive care. Post-enteric reactive arthritis (Salmonella, Shigella, Campylobacter, Yersinia): onset 1-4 weeks after GI illness, HLA-B27 association, may become chronic. Lyme arthritis: late manifestation, requires antibiotics.

Feature | PSRA | ARF

Latent period | <10 days | 2-3 weeks

Arthritis pattern | Non-migratory, prolonged | Migratory, short-lived

Arthritis duration | Weeks to months | Days (rarely >3 weeks)

NSAID response | Slow/incomplete | Dramatic, rapid

Age | Bimodal (8-14, 21-37) | Peak ~12 years

Carditis | Very rare, can occur late | Major criterion, early

HLA-B27 | Normal frequency | Not associated

2015 Revised Jones Criteria: requires evidence of prior GAS infection + 2 major OR 1 major + 2 minor criteria. Major: carditis (clinical or subclinical echo), polyarthritis (monoarthritis in high-risk populations), chorea, erythema marginatum, subcutaneous nodules. Minor: fever (≥38.5°C low-risk, ≥38.0°C high-risk), elevated ESR/CRP, prolonged PR interval (if no carditis).

  • Throat culture, ASO titer (peaks 3-5wk post-infection), anti-DNase B (peaks 6-8wk)
  • ECG (PR prolongation) and echocardiogram (valvulitis, regurgitation, pericarditis) — carditis can be silent
  • CBC, ESR, CRP, BMP

GAS eradication: Penicillin V 250-500 mg PO BID × 10 days, OR Amoxicillin 50 mg/kg/day (max 1 g) × 10 days, OR Benzathine penicillin G 600,000 U (<27 kg) or 1.2 MU (≥27 kg) IM ×1.

Penicillin V (GAS eradication): 250-500 mg PO BID × 10 days PO — Follow with secondary prophylaxis. Alternative: amoxicillin 50 mg/kg/day, or benzathine penicillin G IM ×1.

Aspirin (anti-inflammatory): 50 mg/kg/day PO divided q4-6h PO — High-dose aspirin for ARF; range 50-75 mg/kg/day divided q4-6h. Monitor salicylate levels, max ~4 g/day. Naproxen if aspirin-intolerant.

Secondary prophylaxis: Penicillin G 1.2 MU IM q4 weeks OR Penicillin V 250 mg BID. Duration: 5 years or until age 21 (no carditis) to lifelong (severe carditis).

Admit if any cardiac involvement, moderate-severe arthritis requiring IV pain control, diagnostic uncertainty, or need for monitored high-dose aspirin initiation. May discharge if mild arthritis only, cardiology has cleared, and reliable follow-up within 24-48 hours. Cardiology follow-up mandatory for ALL ARF patients, even without initial carditis. Pearl: carditis is the only manifestation causing permanent damage.

Reference: malignancy red flags, orthopedic mimics, SCFE, growing pains & HSP (informational content — not wired to a decision branch in the source tree)

Leukemia presents with bone/joint pain in 25-43% of cases and may be misdiagnosed as JIA, growing pains, or trauma. Pain suggesting malignancy: severe, out of proportion, night pain waking the child, localized to bone (not joint), progressive/unremitting. Systemic features: fever, fatigue, weight loss, pallor, easy bruising, lymphadenopathy, hepatosplenomegaly. Key lab red flag: thrombocytopenia (<300 x 10⁹/L) — most specific.

Feature | Leukemia | JIA

Pain:swelling | Pain >> swelling | Swelling >> pain

Morning stiffness | Absent | Present

HSM/LAD | Present | Absent (except systemic JIA)

Platelet count | Low/normal | Normal/elevated

Night pain | Prominent | Uncommon

  • Hard stops: hepatosplenomegaly or lymphadenopathy + MSK complaints; any unexplained cytopenia; pancytopenia
  • Heme/onc referral if >1 month MSK pain plus HSM/LAD or systemic symptoms with neutrophils <2×10⁹/L or platelets <300×10⁹/L
  • Workup: CBC with manual differential, peripheral blood smear, LDH, uric acid, CMP, X-ray of painful areas
  • Do NOT start steroids — masks diagnosis. A normal CBC does NOT rule out leukemia.

Condition | Age | Key features

SCFE | 8-15 years | Obese, obligate external rotation with hip flexion, hip/groin/thigh/KNEE pain; emergency if unstable

Legg-Calvé-Perthes | 4-8 years | Idiopathic AVN of femoral head, insidious limp, X-ray may be normal early

Osteomyelitis | Any age | Point tenderness over bone, X-ray normal 1-2 weeks, MRI definitive

Growing pains | 3-12 years | Bilateral lower limbs, evening/night only, no limp, normal exam, diagnosis of exclusion

ALWAYS consider SCFE in an adolescent (especially obese) with hip, thigh, or knee pain, a limp without clear cause, or hip stiffness. Obligate external rotation with hip flexion is pathognomonic. X-ray: AP + frog-leg lateral if stable; AP + cross-table lateral if unstable (do NOT frog-leg an unstable SCFE). Klein's line should intersect the epiphysis; a positive Trethowan sign (it does not) suggests SCFE.

Strict non-weight bearing immediately. Do NOT attempt reduction — increases AVN risk. Urgent orthopedic consultation STAT. Stable SCFE (can bear weight): urgent surgery within 24-48h. Unstable SCFE (cannot bear weight): emergency surgery within hours. Treatment: in-situ percutaneous screw fixation. 30% slip the contralateral hip (70% within 18 months).

Diagnostic criteria (ALL must be present): age 3-12 years, bilateral lower limb pain (NOT joints), evening/night only (never on waking), no limp, normal exam, no systemic symptoms. Red flags that are NOT growing pains: limp, morning stiffness, joint swelling, unilateral pain, pain during activity, systemic symptoms, night pain waking the child, point tenderness, refusal to bear weight. If classic history AND normal exam: no workup needed.

Reassurance is the treatment if criteria met and exam normal: massage, stretching, heat, acetaminophen/ibuprofen PRN. Return if limping develops, pain present in the morning or during activities, fever, joint swelling/redness, weight loss/fatigue, or pain localized to one area only. Pearl: if not confident in the diagnosis, get basic labs (CBC, ESR, CRP) — low cost compared to missing pathology.

HSP (IgA vasculitis) — most common vasculitis in children. Classic tetrad: palpable purpura (100%, lower extremities/buttocks), arthritis/arthralgia (50-80%), abdominal pain (50-75%), renal involvement (20-50%). Arthritis: large joints, oligoarticular, periarticular swelling, non-erosive, may precede rash in 25%. Treatment: mild — supportive/rest; moderate — NSAIDs (avoid if renal involvement) or acetaminophen; severe — prednisone 1-2 mg/kg/day × 1-2 weeks. Monitoring: UA weekly ×4wk then monthly ×6mo, blood pressure, nephrology referral if hematuria/proteinuria/HTN. Pearl: steroids relieve symptoms but do NOT prevent renal disease.

Schedule doses (not PRN) for anti-inflammatory effect; may take 2-4 weeks for full effect. Disposition | Criteria

Admit | Septic arthritis (confirmed/high suspicion), ARF with cardiac involvement, systemic JIA with concerning features, diagnostic uncertainty with malignancy concern, unable to maintain hydration/pain control, social concerns

Discharge | Infection ruled out or very low probability, pain controlled orally, able to ambulate safely, reliable follow-up arranged, caregiver understands return precautions

Admission orders: IV access/fluids as needed, antibiotics if septic arthritis, scheduled pain management, consults (orthopedics/rheumatology/ID as indicated), daily CBC/CRP until trending down. Nursing: vitals q4h (q2h if septic), strict I/O, elevate affected limb, weight-bearing status per orthopedics.

Discharge medications: scheduled NSAID (not PRN), antibiotics if indicated (e.g., Lyme). Activity as tolerated; no contact sports until cleared if JIA suspected. Follow-up: PCP within 1-2 days (observation) or 1 week; rheumatology within 1-2 weeks if JIA suspected; ophthalmology if ANA+ or eye symptoms. Return immediately for fever >38.5°C, inability to bear weight, severe/worsening pain, new joint involvement, rash (especially purpura), eye pain/redness/vision changes.

Pre-transfer: stabilize (IV access, pain control, antibiotics if indicated), complete basic workup to send with patient, direct MD-to-MD communication with accepting facility, document weight-bearing status/restrictions. Send copies of all imaging and lab results. Ambulance if unstable or septic; private vehicle acceptable if stable with reliable family.
