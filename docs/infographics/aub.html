Overview

Emergency management of acute AUB in nonpregnant reproductive-aged women. Acute AUB is heavy bleeding requiring immediate intervention, and may occur spontaneously or within chronic AUB (present >6 months).

Etiologies classified by PALM-COEIN:
• PALM (structural) — Polyp, Adenomyosis, Leiomyoma, Malignancy/hyperplasia
• COEIN (non-structural) — Coagulopathy, Ovulatory dysfunction, Endometrial, Iatrogenic, Not classified

Do not miss coagulopathy
Up to 13% of women with heavy menstrual bleeding have von Willebrand disease; up to 20% have an underlying coagulopathy.

Pregnancy exclusion

Pregnancy MUST be ruled out before initiating any hormonal therapy. Urine hCG (qualitative) for rapid screening; serum beta-hCG (quantitative) if clinical suspicion despite negative urine test. All hormonal treatments for AUB are contraindicated in pregnancy.

This AUB consult covers nonpregnant reproductive-aged women only. For vaginal bleeding in pregnancy, evaluate per trimester:
• 1st trimester: Ectopic pregnancy, threatened/missed abortion, molar pregnancy
• 2nd/3rd trimester: Placenta previa, placental abruption, preterm labor

Obtain OB consultation. Consider Rh status (RhoGAM if Rh-negative).

Workup

Bleeding history: amount (pads/hour, clots), duration, timing, chronic (>6mo) vs acute. Coagulopathy screen: heavy bleeding since menarche, PPH, surgical/dental bleeding, easy bruising, epistaxis, family history. Associated symptoms: fever (infection), pelvic pain (cyst/torsion/adenomyosis), weight gain/hirsutism (PCOS), fatigue/cold intolerance (hypothyroidism). Meds: anticoagulants, HRT, OCPs, IUD, tamoxifen. Exam: speculum (source, cervical lesions, discharge), bimanual (uterine size/contour, adnexal mass, CMT).

Core labs: CBC with platelets, type & screen, pregnancy test, TSH, iron studies if anemic, chlamydia screening. If coagulopathy suspected: PT/PTT/INR/fibrinogen, Coagulopathy Screening Tool; if positive → vWF antigen, ristocetin cofactor, Factor VIII. Consider: LFTs, prolactin, quantitative hCG.

Surgical / procedural management

Indications: hemodynamically unstable despite medical therapy, failure of medical management, suspected structural pathology requiring tissue diagnosis, or contraindications to all medical therapies.

Options (GYN to determine):
• D&C with hysteroscopy — diagnostic + therapeutic, preferred if intrauterine pathology suspected. D&C alone (without hysteroscopy) is inadequate and provides only temporary reduction.
• Endometrial ablation — only if childbearing complete and malignancy excluded.
• Uterine artery embolization (UAE) — for fibroids or refractory bleeding.
• Hysterectomy — definitive treatment for refractory cases.

Recommendation
Emergent GYN consult. Admit for procedural intervention.

Disposition

Admit if: hemodynamic instability, Hgb <7 g/dL (or symptomatic anemia requiring transfusion), failed outpatient management, need for procedural intervention, or suspected malignancy requiring urgent workup.

Discharge if: hemodynamically stable, bleeding controlled or significantly improved, Hgb stable and adequate, reliable GYN follow-up available.

Medroxyprogesterone Acetate (MPA): 20 mg PO TID × 7 days PO — 76% efficacy (median 3 days). Preferred when estrogen contraindicated (VTE, migraine with aura, smoker >35). Especially appropriate for obese/PCOS patients. Medroxyprogesterone.

Norethindrone Acetate: 5-10 mg PO TID × 7 days PO — Alternative progestin if MPA unavailable.

Tranexamic Acid (Lysteda): 1.3 g PO TID × 5 days PO — Reduces bleeding 30-55%. Can use alone or as adjunct to hormonal therapy. Does not affect fertility or cycle regularity. Tranexamic Acid.

Tranexamic Acid: 10 mg/kg IV (max 600 mg/dose) q8h IV — IV route for acute severe bleeding or when PO not tolerated. Contraindicated in active thromboembolic disease.

Surgical / procedural management

Indications: hemodynamically unstable despite medical therapy, failure of medical management, suspected structural pathology requiring tissue diagnosis, or contraindications to all medical therapies.

Options (GYN to determine):
• D&C with hysteroscopy — diagnostic + therapeutic, preferred if intrauterine pathology suspected. D&C alone (without hysteroscopy) is inadequate and provides only temporary reduction.
• Endometrial ablation — only if childbearing complete and malignancy excluded.
• Uterine artery embolization (UAE) — for fibroids or refractory bleeding.
• Hysterectomy — definitive treatment for refractory cases.

Surgical Management Options — full procedure comparison.

Emergent GYN consult. Admit for procedural intervention.

Disposition

Admit if: hemodynamic instability, Hgb <7 g/dL (or symptomatic anemia requiring transfusion), failed outpatient management, need for procedural intervention, or suspected malignancy requiring urgent workup.

Discharge if: hemodynamically stable, bleeding controlled or significantly improved, Hgb stable and adequate, reliable GYN follow-up available.

Long-term Maintenance Options.

Option | Notes

Levonorgestrel IUD (Mirena) | Most effective long-term option; reduces blood loss up to 97%; lasts 5-8 years

Cyclic OCPs | Monthly or extended-cycle; good first-line if contraception also needed

Progestin therapy | Oral (MPA, norethindrone), DMPA 150 mg IM q3mo, or etonogestrel implant — preferred when estrogen contraindicated

Tranexamic acid | 1.3 g PO TID during first 5 days of menses; non-hormonal option

NSAIDs | Reduce blood loss 20-50% via prostaglandin inhibition; avoid in bleeding disorders

Admission orders: GYN consult (or Medicine with GYN consult), continue medical management (IV estrogen, TXA, progestins as indicated), serial CBC q6-8h, type & crossmatch active, transfuse PRBCs if Hgb <7 or symptomatic, hematology consult if coagulopathy identified, VTE prophylaxis once hemostasis achieved.

Conjugated Estrogen (Premarin): 25 mg IV q4-6h IV — Continue until bleeding controlled (max 24h), then transition to oral therapy.

Tranexamic Acid: 1 g IV q8h IV — Can be used as adjunct to hormonal therapy until bleeding controlled.

AUB Discharge Instructions — shareable patient handout. Complete full 7-day course of chosen oral regimen (OCP or MPA). GYN follow-up within 1-2 weeks. Return precautions: soaking >1 pad/hour for 2+ hours, lightheadedness/dizziness/syncope, persistent heavy bleeding despite completing regimen, fever. Obese/PCOS patients need long-term hormonal management to prevent endometrial hyperplasia.

Ferrous Sulfate: 325 mg PO daily PO — Start in all patients with significant anemia. Take with vitamin C for absorption.
