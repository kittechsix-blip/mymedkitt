Acute Jaundice Hub: Sick Check, Time-Critical Exclusions, Disposition

Adults with acute jaundice in the ED. Sick-check-first hub covering 9 time-critical exclusions (cholangitis, APAP hepatotoxicity, acute liver failure, massive hemolysis, ischemic hepatitis, malignant obstruction, Wilson disease, Budd-Chiari, autoimmune hepatitis flare), the undifferentiated initial-bundle pathway, imaging triage, and disposition. Excludes pediatrics, neonatal jaundice/kernicterus, and pregnancy-specific cholestasis (HELLP/AFLP) — each needs its own pathway.

⚠ Yellow patient at the door: West Haven grade ≥2 (lethargy, disorientation) + jaundice = treat as acute liver failure until proven otherwise. Hypotension + jaundice + fever = Reynolds pentad until proven otherwise — resuscitate in parallel with the workup, do not wait for labs.
⚠ Pick the most acute pivot first: Charcot triad/Reynolds pentad → cholangitis; any acetaminophen exposure (including chronic supra-therapeutic dosing) → treat empirically; encephalopathy + INR ≥1.5 with no known cirrhosis → acute liver failure. Send an APAP level on every jaundiced + altered patient regardless of reported history.
⚠ Cholangitis: do NOT delay antibiotics for the ERCP slot — piperacillin-tazobactam OR ceftriaxone + metronidazole within 1 hour of recognition, blood cultures first. Do NOT mistake Reynolds pentad for hepatic encephalopathy — it is septic shock with a biliary source; source control changes the trajectory.
⚠ Acetaminophen: do NOT trust a low APAP level in a delayed presentation — NAC is still indicated based on AST/ALT and clinical pattern. Do NOT discontinue NAC at 21 hours unless ALL of: APAP undetectable (<10 mcg/mL), INR ≤1.5, ALT <1000 and trending down (≤50% of peak), no encephalopathy.
⚠ Acute liver failure: do NOT correct INR with FFP/PCC unless actively bleeding or going for an invasive procedure — INR is the prognostic marker and correcting it blinds the transplant team. Do NOT delay the transplant center call to "stabilize first" — call within 1 hour of recognition.
⚠ Massive hemolysis: do NOT delay plasma exchange in suspected TTP — mortality is ~90% untreated, <20% with PEX. Do NOT give platelets in TTP unless catastrophic bleeding — they can worsen microthrombi.
⚠ Transplant center transfer: do NOT delay transfer for confirmatory imaging or "stabilization" — the receiving center wants the patient early, before cerebral edema closes the window. Do NOT extubate or wean drips immediately pre-transport.
⚠ ICU admission: do NOT correct INR with FFP unless actively bleeding or pre-procedure. Do NOT delay the transplant center call to "see if they get better" — these patients can crash overnight.

1 · Sick Check & the 4 Pivot Questions

5 DO-NOT-MISS on sight: acute cholangitis (antibiotics in 1 hour), acute liver failure (encephalopathy + INR ≥1.5, no prior cirrhosis), acetaminophen toxicity (acute or chronic supra-therapeutic), massive hemolysis (pallor + jaundice + falling H/H), and ischemic hepatitis / sepsis (recent shock, AST/ALT >1000). Sort sick vs not-sick before any framework.[1][2]

Resus trigger — start in parallel with workup
West Haven grade ≥2 (lethargy, disorientation) + jaundice = treat as acute liver failure until proven otherwise. Hypotension + jaundice + fever = Reynolds pentad until proven otherwise. If ANY of: West Haven ≥2 encephalopathy, INR ≥1.5 with AMS, Reynolds pentad, hypotension + jaundice, severe coagulopathic bleeding — Bay 1, IV ×2 large-bore, monitor, type and cross, lactate, ammonia, N-acetylcysteine ready, transplant center on the line.

Charcot triad → cholangitis until proven otherwise
Blood cultures ×2 BEFORE antibiotics, then antibiotics within 1 hour: piperacillin-tazobactam OR ceftriaxone + metronidazole. RUQ US, emergent GI for ERCP. See Tokyo TG18 grading below.
APAP exposure → treat empirically
Send an APAP level immediately even if delayed or denied. Start NAC empirically if ingestion >150 mg/kg, chronic supra-therapeutic dosing with elevated transaminases, delayed presentation with elevated AST/ALT, or encephalopathy + jaundice of unclear cause.
Encephalopathy + INR ≥1.5 → acute liver failure
King's College criteria from arrival. NAC for ALL ALF (not just APAP). Glucose monitoring q1h. Vitamin K but NOT FFP. Transplant center call within 1 hour of recognition.
Recent shock + AST/ALT >1000 → ischemic hepatitis

None of the 4 pivots fit → initial bundle
No life-threat hit yet. Undifferentiated jaundice gets the broader initial bundle (IV, NPO, ondansetron, full hepatic/hemolysis/autoimmune serology panel, mandatory APAP level) and reassessment at 60-90 minutes.

2b · Time-Critical Exclusions — Deeper Dive

Ischemic hepatitis ("shock liver")AST/ALT thousands after a hemodynamic insult

AST and ALT in the thousands (often >25× ULN) within 24-72 hours of a hemodynamic insult — cardiac arrest, septic shock, hemorrhagic shock, severe heart-failure exacerbation, post-arrest hypoperfusion, severe hypoxia. LDH usually elevated proportionally: AST/LDH ratio <1.5 favors ischemic hepatitis over viral or APAP.

Bilirubin relatively spared early (contrast with ALF, where bili climbs early). INR moderately elevated, NOT >2 in pure shock liver. AST/ALT peak within 1-3 days and fall RAPIDLY (halving every 24-48 h) if upstream perfusion is restored.

Fix the upstream cause
This is the only liver-directed therapy that works — optimize MAP ≥65, treat the cardiogenic/septic/hemorrhagic shock per its own pathway. Send APAP level and viral serologies (overlap presentations exist). Repeat AST/ALT/INR/bili at 12-24 h — pattern confirms or refutes the diagnosis. Do NOT give NAC routinely for shock liver — reserve for high APAP suspicion or persistent transaminitis.
Painless obstructive jaundiceAge ≥50 + weight loss + painless = malignant until proven otherwise

Painless jaundice + weight loss + age ≥50 = malignant biliary obstruction until proven otherwise. Pancreatic head adenocarcinoma is the most common culprit; cholangiocarcinoma, ampullary tumors, and porta hepatis nodes also drive this presentation. Courvoisier sign (palpable, non-tender, distended gallbladder) is specific but not sensitive (<25%).

Direct hyperbili dominant (conjugated fraction >50%). Alk phos and GGT elevated out of proportion to AST/ALT. Weight loss + new-onset DM in an older adult should specifically trigger pancreatic cancer workup.

RUQ US → CT pancreatic protocol → ERCP
RUQ US first (dilated CBD >6 mm <60 yo, >8 mm >60 yo). CT abdomen/pelvis with pancreatic protocol contrast for mass/staging. MRCP if no clear mass on CT. GI for ERCP with stenting and brushings. Admit for biliary decompression workup — outpatient discharge is generally not appropriate at first presentation.

Transplant is the only definitive therapy
Wilsonian ALF has the worst transplant-free survival of any ALF subtype (<10%). Chelation (penicillamine, trientine) does NOT rescue fulminant Wilsonian ALF — do not chelate first and "see how it goes." Hepatology consult emergent; transplant center transfer.
Budd-Chiari syndromeAbdominal pain + ascites + hepatomegaly + hypercoagulable state

Triad: abdominal pain + ascites + hepatomegaly + jaundice in a patient with hypercoagulable risk factors (myeloproliferative neoplasm, OCP, pregnancy/postpartum, malignancy, antiphospholipid syndrome, Factor V Leiden, PNH, Behçet disease, hepatic vein web). Caudate lobe sparing on imaging (separate venous drainage to IVC).

Doppler US first, then anticoagulate + TIPS
Hepatic vein Doppler ultrasound — non-invasive, sensitive (~85%) — shows absent/reversed flow, web, or thrombus. CT/MR venogram for confirmation and intervention planning. Hepatology + IR consult emergent; TIPS is typical first-line, transplant if decompensated. Anticoagulation (heparin gtt or LMWH) while bridging. Do NOT defer the hypercoagulability workup to outpatient.
Autoimmune hepatitis flareYoung/middle-aged female + AST/ALT often >1000 + elevated IgG

Young or middle-aged female + jaundice + AST/ALT often >1000 (sometimes >2000) + elevated IgG (>2× ULN highly suggestive) + positive ANA/SMA/anti-LKM1. Co-existing autoimmune disease (Hashimoto, celiac, T1DM, vitiligo) raises pretest probability. Can present as acute liver failure or sub-fulminant pattern.

Hepatology drives steroid timing, not the ED
Send ANA, SMA, anti-LKM1, anti-SLA, anti-mitochondrial, IgG, viral hepatitis panel, ceruloplasmin (if young), APAP level, β-hCG. RUQ US to rule out obstruction. Do NOT start steroids empirically without hepatology buy-in unless ALF physiology and no alternative — steroids can worsen undiagnosed viral or HSV hepatitis. AIH-ALF (encephalopathy + INR ≥1.5) meets King's College criteria via the non-APAP track → transplant center transfer.

3 · Initial Bundle + Reassess

  • IV ×1-2, monitor. NS or LR 1-2 L bolus if volume-responsive; otherwise maintenance.
  • NPO until disposition/dietitian/hepatology guidance.
  • Antiemetic: ondansetron 4 mg IV. Avoid sedating antiemetics if any AMS risk.
  • Pain control: acetaminophen-free preparations only; opioids sparingly; avoid NSAIDs if cirrhosis suspected.
  • Labs: CBC + smear, CMP with fractionated bilirubin, GGT + alk phos, AST/ALT, INR/PT/PTT/fibrinogen, lipase/amylase, ammonia (on ice), APAP level (mandatory), lactate/ABG, type and screen, blood cultures if febrile.
  • Hepatitis panel (HAV IgM, HBsAg, anti-HBc IgM, HCV Ab + RNA, HEV IgM), HIV, iron studies + ferritin, ceruloplasmin if <40 yo, ANA/SMA/anti-LKM1/IgG, anti-mitochondrial Ab if cholestatic, β-hCG, LDH + haptoglobin + retic if hemolysis concern.
  • Reassess at 60-90 minutes for response, lab returns, and emergence of red flags.

4 · Imaging Decision

ModalityRole
RUQ USFirst study in nearly every jaundice patient — cheap, fast, no contrast. Detects biliary dilation (~85% sensitivity), gallstones, sludge, cholecystitis features, gross mass, ascites.
MRCPBiliary tree mapping — non-invasive, no contrast. Use when US shows dilation without clear cause, or suspected stricture/cholangiocarcinoma/PSC. Not first-line if unstable.
CT abdomen/pelvis (pancreatic protocol if mass suspected)Suspected malignancy, pancreatitis with jaundice overlay, abscess, trauma, indeterminate US with persistent suspicion.
CT angiography / venogramSuspected Budd-Chiari, portal vein thrombosis, hepatic artery thrombosis (post-transplant). Pairs with hepatic vein Doppler.
Hepatobiliary scintigraphy (HIDA)Clinical cholecystitis with non-diagnostic US, suspected biliary leak, biliary dyskinesia workup — not first-line in the ED.
ERCPTherapeutic + diagnostic combined — stone extraction, stenting, biliary decompression, brushings. Performed by GI; not an ED study.
MRI brain (if encephalopathic)Rule out cerebral edema in Grade III-IV encephalopathy before LP or transfer. Head CT first (faster).
Normal imaging never excludes hepatocellular causes
US misses early obstruction before ducts dilate — clinical picture overrides. CT misses early ischemic hepatitis; lab pattern drives that diagnosis. APAP, viral, and autoimmune causes are LAB diagnoses, not imaging diagnoses.

5 · Disposition

Transplant center NOW
King's College criteria met, Wilsonian ALF (poor prognosis regardless of criteria), ALF with rapidly progressing encephalopathy/cerebral edema concern, or refractory metabolic disturbance. Pre-transfer: confirm accepting attending + transport mode, type and CROSS 4 units pRBC + 4 units FFP for procedural needs, intubate ahead of transport if Grade III-IV encephalopathy, continue NAC, Vitamin K but not FFP unless bleeding/procedure, complete handoff (timeline, all lab trends, interventions, drips, code status).
ICU admit
Septic shock from cholangitis (Tokyo Grade III), ischemic hepatitis with ongoing hemodynamic instability, ALF not yet meeting transplant criteria but trending wrong, Reynolds pentad without immediate ERCP availability, massive hemolysis with end-organ damage, severe coagulopathic bleeding, Wilsonian ALF awaiting transfer, or encephalopathy Grade ≥2 with airway concern. Bundle: hourly neuro/glucose checks, q2-4h coag panel (do NOT correct INR with FFP unless bleeding/procedure), q6h ammonia, Vitamin K 10 mg IV daily, low threshold for empiric antibiotics.
GI / Medicine floor
Stable obstructive jaundice awaiting ERCP, stable hepatitis workup-in-progress, stable post-transfusion hemolysis, mild pancreatitis-associated jaundice, or Tokyo Grade I-II cholangitis after antibiotics and clinical improvement. Requirements: vitals stable (no vasopressor need), West Haven grade 0-1, INR <2, no active bleeding, able to protect airway, pain controlled.
  1. Isolated indirect hyperbili pattern fitting Gilbert (total bili usually <5, often <3; direct <20% of total; normal AST/ALT/alk phos/GGT/CBC/smear) — confirm with outpatient repeat.
  2. Universal prerequisites: INR <1.5, no encephalopathy (West Haven 0), AST/ALT not rising, bili not rapidly climbing, tolerating PO.
  3. β-hCG documented in reproductive-age females. APAP level documented as negligible.
  4. Written return precautions (worsening yellowing, confusion, new RUQ pain/fever, intractable vomiting, worsening dark urine/light stool, bleeding, unresponsive itching).
  5. 48-72 h follow-up arranged (booked, not just recommended) with hepatology or PCP.
  6. Avoid hepatotoxic agents until cleared: no acetaminophen, no NSAIDs, no alcohol, no new supplements.
Do NOT discharge if ANY of
Encephalopathy (even mild). INR <1.5 not met (i.e., INR ≥1.5 of any etiology). AST/ALT climbing on serial labs. Bili rapidly climbing. New jaundice age ≥50 without a clear non-malignant explanation. Painless obstructive pattern. Unable to confirm pregnancy status. Unable to arrange rapid outpatient follow-up or abstain from hepatotoxic agents reliably.

N-Acetylcysteine — ALL acute liver failure: 100 mg/kg IV over 16 hours (repeat) IV — = computed dose at current weight, capped at 100 kg. Continue until liver clearly improving AND APAP level is zero — not just APAP-caused ALF.

Glucose monitoring q1h — hypoglycemia from failed gluconeogenesis is common and lethal. Vitamin K 10 mg IV to correct the nutritional component, but do NOT correct INR with FFP — INR is the prognostic marker and correcting it blinds the team. Transplant center call within 1 hour of recognition — do not wait for labs to finalize.

Smear/lab finding | Etiology | Action

Schistocytes | TTP, HUS, DIC, malignant HTN, HELLP, mechanical valve | Emergent plasma exchange (TTP); DIC — treat the underlying cause, replace factors (FFP, cryo, platelets) only if actively bleeding or pre-procedure, no PEX

Spherocytes | Autoimmune hemolytic anemia or hereditary spherocytosis | Steroids, IVIG, least-incompatible transfusion (AIHA)

Bite cells / Heinz bodies | G6PD deficiency precipitant (drug, fava bean, infection) | Remove precipitant; supportive transfusion

Sickled cells | Sickle cell crisis with hemolytic component | Pain control, oxygen if SpO2 <95%, exchange transfusion for severe crisis

LDH >1000 with haptoglobin <10 mg/dL = ~90% specific for hemolysis. TTP (MAHA + thrombocytopenia + fever + AMS + renal failure) mortality is ~90% untreated, <20% with PEX — do not delay plasma exchange. Do NOT give platelets in TTP (can worsen microthrombi) unless catastrophic bleeding.

2b · Time-Critical Exclusions — Deeper Dive

Ischemic hepatitis ("shock liver")

AST and ALT in the thousands (often >25× ULN) within 24-72 hours of a hemodynamic insult — cardiac arrest, septic shock, hemorrhagic shock, severe heart-failure exacerbation, post-arrest hypoperfusion, severe hypoxia. LDH usually elevated proportionally: AST/LDH ratio <1.5 favors ischemic hepatitis over viral or APAP.

Bilirubin relatively spared early (contrast with ALF, where bili climbs early). INR moderately elevated, NOT >2 in pure shock liver. AST/ALT peak within 1-3 days and fall RAPIDLY (halving every 24-48 h) if upstream perfusion is restored.

This is the only liver-directed therapy that works — optimize MAP ≥65, treat the cardiogenic/septic/hemorrhagic shock per its own pathway. Send APAP level and viral serologies (overlap presentations exist). Repeat AST/ALT/INR/bili at 12-24 h — pattern confirms or refutes the diagnosis. Do NOT give NAC routinely for shock liver — reserve for high APAP suspicion or persistent transaminitis.

Painless obstructive jaundice

Painless jaundice + weight loss + age ≥50 = malignant biliary obstruction until proven otherwise. Pancreatic head adenocarcinoma is the most common culprit; cholangiocarcinoma, ampullary tumors, and porta hepatis nodes also drive this presentation. Courvoisier sign (palpable, non-tender, distended gallbladder) is specific but not sensitive (<25%).

Direct hyperbili dominant (conjugated fraction >50%). Alk phos and GGT elevated out of proportion to AST/ALT. Weight loss + new-onset DM in an older adult should specifically trigger pancreatic cancer workup.

RUQ US first (dilated CBD >6 mm <60 yo, >8 mm >60 yo). CT abdomen/pelvis with pancreatic protocol contrast for mass/staging. MRCP if no clear mass on CT. GI for ERCP with stenting and brushings. Admit for biliary decompression workup — outpatient discharge is generally not appropriate at first presentation.

Wilson disease — de novo presentation

Lab pattern | Value

Alkaline phosphatase | Alkaline phosphatase <40 IU/L (low — opposite of obstruction)

Alk phos:total bili ratio | Alk phos to total bili ratio <4 (highly specific for Wilsonian ALF)

AST:ALT ratio | AST:ALT ratio >2.2 (mitochondrial injury pattern)

Coombs-negative hemolytic anemia with indirect bili rise. Low serum ceruloplasmin (<20 mg/dL) and 24-h urine copper >100 μg — order both from the ED, along with slit-lamp exam for Kayser-Fleischer rings (present in ~50% of hepatic-presentation Wilson).

Wilsonian ALF has the worst transplant-free survival of any ALF subtype (<10%). Chelation (penicillamine, trientine) does NOT rescue fulminant Wilsonian ALF — do not chelate first and "see how it goes." Hepatology consult emergent; transplant center transfer.

Budd-Chiari syndrome

Triad: abdominal pain + ascites + hepatomegaly + jaundice in a patient with hypercoagulable risk factors (myeloproliferative neoplasm, OCP, pregnancy/postpartum, malignancy, antiphospholipid syndrome, Factor V Leiden, PNH, Behçet disease, hepatic vein web). Caudate lobe sparing on imaging (separate venous drainage to IVC).

Hepatic vein Doppler ultrasound — non-invasive, sensitive (~85%) — shows absent/reversed flow, web, or thrombus. CT/MR venogram for confirmation and intervention planning. Hepatology + IR consult emergent; TIPS is typical first-line, transplant if decompensated. Anticoagulation (heparin gtt or LMWH) while bridging. Do NOT defer the hypercoagulability workup to outpatient.

Autoimmune hepatitis flare

Young or middle-aged female + jaundice + AST/ALT often >1000 (sometimes >2000) + elevated IgG (>2× ULN highly suggestive) + positive ANA/SMA/anti-LKM1. Co-existing autoimmune disease (Hashimoto, celiac, T1DM, vitiligo) raises pretest probability. Can present as acute liver failure or sub-fulminant pattern.

Send ANA, SMA, anti-LKM1, anti-SLA, anti-mitochondrial, IgG, viral hepatitis panel, ceruloplasmin (if young), APAP level, β-hCG. RUQ US to rule out obstruction. Do NOT start steroids empirically without hepatology buy-in unless ALF physiology and no alternative — steroids can worsen undiagnosed viral or HSV hepatitis. AIH-ALF (encephalopathy + INR ≥1.5) meets King's College criteria via the non-APAP track → transplant center transfer.

3 · Initial Bundle + Reassess

  • IV ×1-2, monitor. NS or LR 1-2 L bolus if volume-responsive; otherwise maintenance.
  • NPO until disposition/dietitian/hepatology guidance.
  • Antiemetic: ondansetron 4 mg IV. Avoid sedating antiemetics if any AMS risk.
  • Pain control: acetaminophen-free preparations only; opioids sparingly; avoid NSAIDs if cirrhosis suspected.
  • Labs: CBC + smear, CMP with fractionated bilirubin, GGT + alk phos, AST/ALT, INR/PT/PTT/fibrinogen, lipase/amylase, ammonia (on ice), APAP level (mandatory), lactate/ABG, type and screen, blood cultures if febrile.
  • Hepatitis panel (HAV IgM, HBsAg, anti-HBc IgM, HCV Ab + RNA, HEV IgM), HIV, iron studies + ferritin, ceruloplasmin if <40 yo, ANA/SMA/anti-LKM1/IgG, anti-mitochondrial Ab if cholestatic, β-hCG, LDH + haptoglobin + retic if hemolysis concern.
  • Reassess at 60-90 minutes for response, lab returns, and emergence of red flags.

Ondansetron: 4 mg IV IV — Standard antiemetic dose for the initial bundle. Avoid sedating antiemetics if any AMS risk.

Re-examine West Haven grade, vitals trend, lab returns, imaging findings, PO tolerance. Lab pivot identified (high APAP, cholangitis, hemolysis, etc.) → return to the time-critical exclusions and follow that branch. No clear pivot, ongoing concern → proceed to imaging decision or ED observation with serial exams. New encephalopathy / INR climbing / acidosis / hypotension → STOP, go directly to the ALF pathway, transplant center call now. Stable + benign isolated unconjugated hyperbili (Gilbert / mild hemolysis) → discharge bundle with outpatient hepatology follow-up.

4 · Imaging Decision

Modality | Role

RUQ US | First study in nearly every jaundice patient — cheap, fast, no contrast. Detects biliary dilation (~85% sensitivity), gallstones, sludge, cholecystitis features, gross mass, ascites.

MRCP | Biliary tree mapping — non-invasive, no contrast. Use when US shows dilation without clear cause, or suspected stricture/cholangiocarcinoma/PSC. Not first-line if unstable.

CT abdomen/pelvis (pancreatic protocol if mass suspected) | Suspected malignancy, pancreatitis with jaundice overlay, abscess, trauma, indeterminate US with persistent suspicion.

CT angiography / venogram | Suspected Budd-Chiari, portal vein thrombosis, hepatic artery thrombosis (post-transplant). Pairs with hepatic vein Doppler.

Hepatobiliary scintigraphy (HIDA) | Clinical cholecystitis with non-diagnostic US, suspected biliary leak, biliary dyskinesia workup — not first-line in the ED.

ERCP | Therapeutic + diagnostic combined — stone extraction, stenting, biliary decompression, brushings. Performed by GI; not an ED study.

MRI brain (if encephalopathic) | Rule out cerebral edema in Grade III-IV encephalopathy before LP or transfer. Head CT first (faster).

US misses early obstruction before ducts dilate — clinical picture overrides. CT misses early ischemic hepatitis; lab pattern drives that diagnosis. APAP, viral, and autoimmune causes are LAB diagnoses, not imaging diagnoses.

5 · Disposition

King's College criteria met, Wilsonian ALF (poor prognosis regardless of criteria), ALF with rapidly progressing encephalopathy/cerebral edema concern, or refractory metabolic disturbance. Pre-transfer: confirm accepting attending + transport mode, type and CROSS 4 units pRBC + 4 units FFP for procedural needs, intubate ahead of transport if Grade III-IV encephalopathy, continue NAC, Vitamin K but not FFP unless bleeding/procedure, complete handoff (timeline, all lab trends, interventions, drips, code status).

Septic shock from cholangitis (Tokyo Grade III), ischemic hepatitis with ongoing hemodynamic instability, ALF not yet meeting transplant criteria but trending wrong, Reynolds pentad without immediate ERCP availability, massive hemolysis with end-organ damage, severe coagulopathic bleeding, Wilsonian ALF awaiting transfer, or encephalopathy Grade ≥2 with airway concern. Bundle: hourly neuro/glucose checks, q2-4h coag panel (do NOT correct INR with FFP unless bleeding/procedure), q6h ammonia, Vitamin K 10 mg IV daily, low threshold for empiric antibiotics.

Stable obstructive jaundice awaiting ERCP, stable hepatitis workup-in-progress, stable post-transfusion hemolysis, mild pancreatitis-associated jaundice, or Tokyo Grade I-II cholangitis after antibiotics and clinical improvement. Requirements: vitals stable (no vasopressor need), West Haven grade 0-1, INR <2, no active bleeding, able to protect airway, pain controlled.

  • Isolated indirect hyperbili pattern fitting Gilbert (total bili usually <5, often <3; direct <20% of total; normal AST/ALT/alk phos/GGT/CBC/smear) — confirm with outpatient repeat.
  • Universal prerequisites: INR <1.5, no encephalopathy (West Haven 0), AST/ALT not rising, bili not rapidly climbing, tolerating PO.
  • β-hCG documented in reproductive-age females. APAP level documented as negligible.
  • Written return precautions (worsening yellowing, confusion, new RUQ pain/fever, intractable vomiting, worsening dark urine/light stool, bleeding, unresponsive itching).
  • 48-72 h follow-up arranged (booked, not just recommended) with hepatology or PCP.
  • Avoid hepatotoxic agents until cleared: no acetaminophen, no NSAIDs, no alcohol, no new supplements.

Encephalopathy (even mild). INR <1.5 not met (i.e., INR ≥1.5 of any etiology). AST/ALT climbing on serial labs. Bili rapidly climbing. New jaundice age ≥50 without a clear non-malignant explanation. Painless obstructive pattern. Unable to confirm pregnancy status. Unable to arrange rapid outpatient follow-up or abstain from hepatotoxic agents reliably.
