Salicylate Toxicity: Alkalinize, Don't Intubate

Adults and adolescents in the ED with suspected or confirmed salicylate poisoning. Excludes isolated topical exposure and neonates; chronic toxicity, pregnancy, and ESRD warrant early specialist input.

⚠ Life-threatening >300mg/kg — consult nephrology EARLY for HD, anticipate rapid deterioration
⚠ Volume resuscitate with isotonic bicarb or LR — NEVER normal saline; give D50W for any AMS even with normal glucose
⚠ BZD for seizures, empiric D50W even with normal glucose (neuroglycopenia) — seizures = severe, needs HD
⚠ NaHCO3 bolus 1-2 mEq/kg then infusion in D5W — target urine pH 7.5-8.0; NEVER use NS carrier; acetazolamide CONTRAINDICATED
⚠ Intubation causes cardiac arrest by eliminating compensatory hyperventilation — HFNC preferred, avoid RSI if possible
⚠ Hypokalemia BLOCKS urinary alkalinization — replete K+ to 4.0-4.5 mEq/L; add 20-40 mEq KCl per liter of bicarb

1 · Recognize & classify

The mixed acid-base picture
Salicylate stimulates the medulla (respiratory alkalosis) and uncouples oxidative phosphorylation (anion-gap metabolic acidosis). The classic ABG is a mixed respiratory alkalosis + metabolic acidosis. Unexplained tachypnea, tinnitus, altered mental status, or an anion-gap acidosis should trigger a level.[10]

Order: salicylate level, ABG/VBG, BMP (Na, Cl, HCO₃, K, Cr, glucose), lactate, and an acetaminophen level (common co-ingestant). Enter weight, level, and K⁺ above — the doses and pathway below react to them.[1][2]

Oil of wintergreen
1 mL of methyl salicylate ≈ 1,400 mg aspirin — a single teaspoon (~7,000 mg) can be lethal in a child. Enteric-coated tablets delay peak absorption 12–24 h.[1][3]

3 · Resuscitation, seizures & the dangerous airway

The dangerous airway
Compensatory hyperventilation (RR 30–40) is the only thing holding the pH up. Intubation removes it → pH crashes → more non-ionized salicylate crosses the BBB → arrest. Prefer HFNC. If you must intubate: most experienced operator, bolus NaHCO₃ first, then set RR 30–35 to match minute ventilation and target pH — not pCO₂.[2][3]

Volume resuscitate — patients run 4–6 L down (diaphoresis, tachypnea, vomiting). Use isotonic bicarbonate (pH <7.5) or LR/Plasmalyte (at target pH); NEVER normal saline (acidifying). Give Dextrose D50W for any AMS even with a normal serum glucose (neuroglycopenia).[2][4]

5 · Alkalinization — the cornerstone

Why it works
Alkaline urine ion-traps salicylic acid in the renal tubule so it cannot be reabsorbed — urine pH 8.0 gives 10-fold the renal clearance of pH 6.0. Bicarbonate helps even if the patient is already alkalemic: the goal is urinary, not serum, alkalinization.[2][6][8]
Sodium bicarbonate (urinary alkalinization)
1–2 mEq/kg bolus → 150 mEq in 1 L D5W
IV, 150–200 mL/hr
D5W carrier (CNS glucose support). Targets: serum pH 7.45–7.55, urine pH 7.5–8.0. NEVER normal saline as carrier (SID = 0, acidifying). Do NOT exceed serum pH 7.60.
Hypokalemia blocks alkalinization
With low K⁺ the distal tubule reabsorbs K⁺ and excretes H⁺ instead → acidic urine despite systemic alkalosis, and no amount of bicarb overcomes it. Replete KCl IV 20–40 mEq/hr (cardiac monitoring >20 mEq/hr), add 20–40 mEq per liter of bicarb, target K⁺ ≥ 4.0–4.5.[2]
Acetazolamide is CONTRAINDICATED
It lowers serum pH and displaces salicylate from albumin, raising free (toxic) drug. Never use it to alkalinize the urine here.[2][13]

6 · Hemodialysis (EXTRIP)

HD is extremely effective — it removes salicylate and corrects acidosis at once. Consult nephrology early: access and setup take 1–2 h, and patients who need emergent HD often deteriorate faster than logistics allow.[2][5]

  • Salicylate > 100 mg/dL (recommended) — regardless of symptoms
  • Altered mental status
  • ARDS / non-cardiogenic pulmonary edema requiring supplemental O₂
  • pH ≤ 7.20 despite resuscitation
  • Standard therapy failing / clinical deterioration
  • Impaired kidney function

Standard intermittent HD is preferred over CRRT (higher clearance) and is safe even in hypotension (no fluid removed). Continue the Sodium Bicarbonate infusion during and after HD. Rebound is expected as tissue stores redistribute — recheck a level 2 h post-HD; some massive ingestions need 2–3 sessions.[2][5]

8 · Do NOT

Critical pitfalls

**Do NOT use normal saline** — it is acidifying and blunts alkalinization. Use D5W-based bicarb or LR/Plasmalyte.

**Do NOT intubate reflexively.** Removing compensatory hyperventilation can precipitate cardiac arrest. Prefer HFNC; if unavoidable, pre-bolus NaHCO₃ and match minute ventilation.

**Do NOT give acetazolamide** — it lowers serum pH and displaces salicylate from albumin, raising free toxic drug.

**Do NOT ignore K⁺.** Hypokalemia blocks urinary alkalinization — replete to 4.0–4.5 mEq/L.

**Do NOT apply the Done nomogram**, and never trust a single declining level in chronic toxicity — tissue redistribution causes rebound.
