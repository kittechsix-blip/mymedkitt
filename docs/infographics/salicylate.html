Salicylate Toxicity: Alkalinize the Urine, Fear the Airway

Adults and children in the ED with known or suspected salicylate (aspirin) toxicity — acute, chronic, or unknown-timing ingestion. Pediatric dosing uses the same mg/kg calculators as adults; verify local pediatric protocols for very young children. Excludes isolated non-salicylate co-ingestions requiring a different antidote pathway. Poison Control: 1-800-222-1222.

⚠ NEVER use normal saline for resuscitation or as the bicarbonate carrier — it is acidifying and worsens salicylate toxicity. Use isotonic bicarbonate (pH <7.5) or LR/Plasmalyte (at target pH), and D5W as the infusion carrier.
⚠ Intubation is dangerous. The patient's compensatory hyperventilation is the only thing maintaining pH — eliminating it during RSI frequently precipitates cardiac arrest. Avoid intubation if at all possible; if unavoidable, match pre-intubation minute ventilation (RR 30-35) and target pH, not pCO2.
⚠ Acetazolamide is contraindicated — it lowers serum pH and displaces salicylate from albumin, increasing free (toxic) drug levels.
⚠ Hypokalemia blocks urinary alkalinization — no amount of bicarbonate will achieve alkaline urine without adequate K⁺ repletion (target K⁺ ≥4.0-4.5 mEq/L).
⚠ Chronic toxicity is more lethal at the same serum level as acute toxicity and is frequently misdiagnosed as sepsis, CHF, or dementia in elderly patients. The Done nomogram does NOT apply to chronic toxicity and is no longer recommended even for acute ingestion.
⚠ Seizures indicate severe poisoning and near-certain need for hemodialysis — treat aggressively with benzodiazepines and give empiric dextrose (neuroglycopenia can occur with normal serum glucose).

1 · Recognize & resuscitate

Clinical suspicion triggers: unexplained tachypnea/hyperpnea, tinnitus, altered mental status, unexplained anion-gap metabolic acidosis, or a mixed respiratory alkalosis + metabolic acidosis.[1][2][3][4] Order: salicylate level, ABG/VBG, BMP (Na, Cl, HCO3, K, Cr, glucose), lactate, and an acetaminophen level (common co-ingestant).

Chronic toxicity is underrecognized and more lethal
Chronic toxicity is more lethal than acute toxicity at the same serum level — often elderly patients on chronic aspirin, Pepto-Bismol, or topical salicylates, misdiagnosed as sepsis, CHF, or dementia.[2][4][12][13] The Done nomogram does NOT apply to chronic toxicity and is no longer recommended even for acute ingestion — a level of 40 mg/dL in chronic toxicity can be as dangerous as >100 mg/dL acutely.[13]

1 mL of methyl salicylate = ~1,400 mg aspirin. A single teaspoon (~5 mL) = ~7,000 mg — potentially lethal in a child.[1][3]
  1. Volume resuscitate — patients are often 4-6L depleted (diaphoresis, tachypnea, vomiting, diarrhea).[2]
  2. Use isotonic bicarbonate if pH <7.5, or LR/Plasmalyte if already at target pH. NEVER normal saline (acidifying).[2]
  3. Check fingerstick glucose — give dextrose empirically for any altered mental status, even with a normal serum glucose (neuroglycopenia).[2][4]

Give even with NORMAL serum glucose — salicylate uncouples oxidative phosphorylation in CNS mitochondria, so CSF glucose drops before serum glucose.[2][4]

2 · Salicylate level & hemodialysis threshold

Urinary alkalinization is the cornerstone of treatment — urine pH 8.0 increases renal salicylate clearance 10-fold vs pH 6.0.[2][6][8] Classic acid-base pattern: mixed respiratory alkalosis + anion-gap metabolic acidosis.[9] Enter the measured level above; the EXTRIP hemodialysis band and the algorithm below update live.

EXTRIP indications — regardless of level
Hemodialysis is recommended regardless of level for: altered mental status, ARDS/non-cardiogenic pulmonary edema requiring supplemental O2, standard therapy failing/clinical deterioration, or pH ≤7.20 despite resuscitation.[5][11] Consult nephrology early — HD access and setup take 1-2+ hours, and this class of patient can deteriorate faster than logistics allow.[2]

THE DANGEROUS AIRWAY
Intubation in salicylate toxicity is EXTREMELY DANGEROUS and frequently causes cardiac arrest.[2][3][4] The patient's compensatory hyperventilation (often RR 30-40) is the ONLY thing maintaining pH. Eliminate it during intubation and pH crashes → more non-ionized salicylate crosses the BBB → brainstem depression → arrest. HFNC may help blow off CO2 without the risks of intubation.[2]
  1. Pre-intubation: sodium bicarbonate bolus 1-2 mEq/kg IV, given >5-10 min before paralysis to allow CO2 blow-off.[2][3][4]
  2. Most experienced operator, rapid sequence; consider ketamine (maintains respiratory drive longest).[2]
  3. Post-intubation: TV 8 mL/kg, RR 30-35 (match pre-intubation minute ventilation) — target pH, NOT pCO2. ABG within 10 minutes.[2][3][4]
  4. This patient REQUIRES EMERGENT hemodialysis — intubation without HD has very high mortality.[2][3][4]

5 · Alkalinization — the cornerstone

Alkaline urine ionizes salicylic acid → trapped in renal tubules → cannot be reabsorbed. Even if the patient is already alkalemic from respiratory alkalosis (pH up to 7.55), bicarbonate is still beneficial — the goal is urinary, not serum, alkalinization.[2]

6 · GI decontamination

Activated charcoal effectively adsorbs salicylate; delayed gastric emptying extends the effective window to 2-4 hours. Do NOT induce emesis; gastric lavage is rarely indicated.[1][3][7]

Activated charcoal — single dose
1 g/kg
PO or NG, max 50 g
Most effective within 1 hour, consider up to 2-4 hours for salicylates (delayed gastric emptying, bezoar formation). A second dose may be given 4 hours later if levels continue rising.[1][3][7]

Enteric-coated or massive ingestion / suspected bezoar: multi-dose charcoal 25 g q2-4h for 3-4 additional doses, or whole bowel irrigation (PEG solution via NG at 1.5-2 L/hr) for confirmed bezoar or persistently rising levels despite charcoal.[1][3]

7 · Hemodialysis (EXTRIP)

  1. Standard intermittent HD preferred over CRRT — much higher clearance; safe even in hypotension since fluid isn't being removed.[2][5]
  2. Continue sodium bicarbonate infusion DURING and AFTER HD — tissue stores redistribute afterward.[2][5]
  3. Recheck salicylate level 2 hours post-HD — rebound is expected; repeat HD if level rebounds >30 mg/dL or symptoms recur.[2][5]
  4. Some massive ingestions need 2-3 HD sessions.[2][5]

8 · Monitoring & disposition

Serial labs: salicylate level q2h until peaked and declining ×2, then q4h; ABG/VBG q2-4h; BMP q4h; urine pH hourly during alkalinization; fingerstick glucose q1h if AMS/on dextrose.[1][2][3] Stop alkalinization when two consecutive levels decline, level <30 mg/dL, and the patient is asymptomatic with normal RR — then follow labs 2-4 more hours (rebound risk).[2]

Critical pitfalls7 items
Do NOT use normal saline for resuscitation or as the bicarbonate carrier — it is acidifying and worsens salicylate toxicity.[2]
Do NOT give acetazolamide to alkalinize urine — it lowers serum pH and displaces salicylate from albumin, raising free toxic drug levels.[2][10][12]

Do NOT stop alkalinization based on a single declining level — tissue redistribution causes rebound; require two consecutive declining levels.[2]

Do NOT discharge an intentional ingestion without mandatory psychiatric evaluation.[1]
