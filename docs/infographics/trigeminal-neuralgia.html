Trigeminal Neuralgia — ED Approach

Adults presenting to the ED with paroxysmal unilateral facial pain. Covers ICHD-3 diagnosis, secondary-cause screening, first-line carbamazepine with mandatory HLA-B*1502 safety gate, pharmacologic ladder, refractory/surgical referral, and disposition. Excludes pediatric TN (rare, not addressed) and non-ED outpatient long-term surgical decision-making beyond referral criteria.

⚠ HLA-B*1502 SCREEN BEFORE first carbamazepine or oxcarbazepine dose in Han Chinese, Thai, Vietnamese, Filipino, Malay, Indonesian, or Southern Indian ancestry — several hundred-fold increased risk of Stevens-Johnson syndrome/TEN if positive.
⚠ HLA-B*1502 POSITIVE → AVOID BOTH carbamazepine AND oxcarbazepine (cross-reactivity documented) — use baclofen, gabapentin, or lamotrigine instead.
⚠ Any new rash on carbamazepine, oxcarbazepine, or lamotrigine → STOP the drug immediately and refer for ED evaluation — SJS/TEN risk is highest in the first 2 months.
⚠ CBZ-induced hyponatremia → do NOT switch to oxcarbazepine — oxcarbazepine causes MORE frequent, more severe hyponatremia (15-30% vs CBZ ~10%). Switch to a non-Na-lowering agent (gabapentin, baclofen, lamotrigine) instead.
⚠ Lamotrigine titration MUST follow the FDA-mandated slow protocol (25 mg PO qHS, increase every 2 weeks) — SJS/TEN risk is dose- AND titration-rate-related.
⚠ Carbamazepine is Pregnancy Category D (teratogenic — neural tube defects, craniofacial anomalies) — avoid in pregnancy; plan a switch to baclofen/gabapentin/lamotrigine BEFORE conception with neurology input.

1 · Initial assessment

Paroxysmal, unilateral electric-shock/stabbing facial pain confined to one or more divisions of the trigeminal nerve (V1/V2/V3 — most commonly V2/V3; pure V1 is rare). Each paroxysm lasts seconds to <2 minutes; triggered by innocuous stimuli (chewing, talking, brushing teeth, shaving, cold wind, light touch).

2 · What to do now

HLA-B*1502 positive
AVOID BOTH carbamazepine AND oxcarbazepine — cross-reactivity is well documented. Use the adjunct ladder instead: Baclofen, Gabapentin, or Lamotrigine.
At-risk ancestry, HLA-B*1502 unknown
Do NOT start carbamazepine. Order HLA-B*1502 testing (send-out, 1-3 days). Bridge with Gabapentin 300 mg PO qHS or Baclofen 5 mg PO TID, titrated up, with close outpatient follow-up.
Non-at-risk ancestry, or HLA-B*1502 negative
Start carbamazepine 100 mg PO BID. Titrate by 100-200 mg every 3 days to 600-1200 mg/day (max 1600 mg/day). Counsel on the rash-stop rule at initiation.
CBZ intolerant — hyponatremia
Do NOT switch to oxcarbazepine (worse hyponatremia risk — 15-30% vs CBZ ~10%). Correct sodium, then switch to a non-Na-lowering agent: Gabapentin, Baclofen, or Lamotrigine.
CBZ intolerant (non-Na cause) or inadequate response
Move to oxcarbazepine 150 mg PO BID (titrate to 1200 mg/day) or add an adjunct (baclofen, gabapentin). HLA-B*1502 risk carries over to oxcarbazepine.
Refractory by AAN/EFNS criteria
Failure of ≥2 first-line agents at adequate dose/duration = surgical referral. Do not delay — refer to neurosurgery with TN experience (MVD / rhizotomy / gamma knife).

Second-line when CBZ ineffective/not tolerated. Fewer drug interactions than CBZ (weaker CYP3A4 inducer).

Gabapentin

PO

Any rash → STOP lamotrigine immediately and refer same-day. SJS/TEN risk is dose- AND titration-rate-related — never skip the slow ladder. If on valproate, use HALF the doses above.

7 · Refractory & surgical options

AAN/EFNS definition of medical refractoriness: failure of ≥2 first-line agents at adequate dose and duration with disabling pain. This triggers surgical referral — do not delay further medical trials at the cost of months of severe pain and weight loss.

ProcedureInitial reliefDurabilityBest for
Microvascular decompression (MVD)~90%~70% at 10 yrTN1 + vascular loop, fit, <70 yo
Percutaneous glycerol rhizotomy~80%50% recurrence at 5 yrPoor surgical candidate; repeatable
Balloon compression / RF rhizotomy~92%~70% at 1 yr, decliningSelective fiber injury; controllable
Stereotactic radiosurgery (gamma knife)~70-80%~50% at 5 yrElderly/frail, MS-associated TN, surgery refusal

MVD — best long-term outcome for classical (TN1) primary TN with imaging-proven vascular loop: ~90% initial pain relief, sustained ~70% at 10 years. For MS-associated TN, gamma knife is preferred (lower MVD success in MS-TN).

Admit

Under neurology guidance for active TN crisis after failed PO trials. Correct sodium cautiously if CBZ/oxcarbazepine-related (≤8 mEq/L per 24h, cap 10 mEq/L, to avoid osmotic demyelination).
Stop / Pitfalls — do NOT9 critical pitfalls

Do NOT start carbamazepine/oxcarbazepine before HLA-B*1502 screening in at-risk ancestry with status unknown.

Do NOT start chronic CBZ in atypical TN (age <40, bilateral, sensory deficit, V1-only, constant baseline) before MRI.

Do NOT switch to oxcarbazepine for CBZ-induced hyponatremia — oxcarbazepine causes MORE hyponatremia.

Do NOT continue carbamazepine, oxcarbazepine, or lamotrigine after any new rash — stop immediately, evaluate same-day.

Do NOT skip the slow lamotrigine titration ladder (25 mg q2wk) — SJS risk is titration-rate-related.

Do NOT continue carbamazepine or valproate through pregnancy without a neurology-guided switch plan (both teratogenic).

Do NOT delay surgical referral once AAN/EFNS refractory criteria are met (≥2 first-line agents failed at adequate dose/duration).

Do NOT discharge without addressing ED fluid + nutrition deficits — severe TN reliably causes dehydration and weight loss.

Do NOT skip suicidal-ideation screening — TN has one of the highest suicide rates among chronic pain syndromes.
