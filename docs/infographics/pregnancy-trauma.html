Pregnancy in Trauma: Maternal Resuscitation, Fetal Assessment & Disposition

Pregnant trauma patients of any gestational age presenting to the ED. Excludes non-pregnant trauma and isolated obstetric emergencies without a trauma mechanism.

⚠ MOTHER FIRST — fetal survival depends on maternal survival. Resuscitate the mother aggressively; pregnant patients can lose 30% blood volume before showing shock, and the fetus suffers first.
⚠ LEFT UTERINE DISPLACEMENT after 20 weeks — supine positioning can reduce cardiac output 25-30% via aortocaval compression. Manual displacement or 15-30° left tilt. Do NOT compromise CPR quality for positioning.
⚠ Non-reassuring fetal status (persistent bradycardia <110 bpm, recurrent late decelerations, loss of variability, prolonged deceleration >2 min, sinusoidal pattern): optimize maternal status first, STAT OB consultation, prepare for emergent cesarean delivery.
⚠ Placental abruption is the #1 cause of fetal death after trauma — it is a CLINICAL diagnosis; ultrasound sensitivity is only 25% and cannot rule it out. Fibrinogen <200 mg/dL suggests significant abruption/DIC. Can occur up to 24 hours after trauma.
⚠ Uterine rupture: sudden severe abdominal pain + loss of fetal station + easily palpable fetal parts = rupture until proven otherwise. Immediate laparotomy — do not delay for imaging. Time to delivery 10-35 minutes to avoid fetal brain injury.
⚠ Maternal cardiac arrest with uterus ≥20 weeks: if no ROSC by 4 minutes, BEGIN RESUSCITATIVE HYSTEROTOMY at bedside — do NOT move to the OR. Goal is delivery by 5 minutes of arrest to relieve aortocaval compression.
⚠ Resuscitative hysterotomy's primary benefit is MATERNAL. Maternal survival has been documented at 15 minutes and neonatal survival at 30 minutes — do not abandon delivery even if the 5-minute goal is missed.
⚠ Emergent cesarean delivery indications: persistent fetal bradycardia, prolonged deceleration unresponsive to resuscitation, sinusoidal pattern, severe abruption, uterine rupture, or maternal cardiac arrest. Decision-to-incision goal <30 minutes (5 minutes in cardiac arrest).

1 · Mother first — overview

Trauma is the leading non-obstetric cause of maternal death, affecting 6-7% of pregnancies. The golden rule: the best fetal resuscitation is maternal resuscitation — fetal survival depends on maternal survival.

Key physiologic changes: blood volume ↑ 50% (can lose 30% before shock signs); cardiac output ↑30-50% with baseline HR ↑15-20 bpm; "physiologic anemia" (Hgb 11 g/dL is normal); IVC compression after 20 weeks causes supine hypotension syndrome; uterine blood flow 500-700 mL/min at term. Blood is shunted away from the uterus to preserve maternal organs — the fetus suffers first.

2 · What to do now

Maternal cardiac arrest — 4-minute rule
Manual left uterine displacement (assign a team member), standard ACLS drugs and defib doses, IV access above the diaphragm preferred, smaller ETT (6.0-7.0). If no ROSC by 4 minutes and uterus is ≥20 weeks (fundus at/above umbilicus): begin resuscitative hysterotomy at bedside, goal delivery by 5 minutes. Do NOT move to the OR.
<20 weeks — standard trauma care
No aortocaval compression concern, left tilt not required. Still get Rh status + RhIg if Rh-negative, OB consultation, pelvic ultrasound to confirm pregnancy status. CT and other necessary imaging should not be withheld.
≥20 weeks / unknown — left uterine displacement now
Manual displacement (stand at patient's right side, push uterus left and upward) or 15-30° left lateral tilt. Reduces IVC/aortocaval compression, which can drop cardiac output 25-30% when supine. Continue to labs (type/screen, Kleihauer-Betke, fibrinogen), imaging as clinically indicated, and continuous fetal monitoring.
Low-risk monitoring complete
4-6 hours of reassuring monitoring with no contractions, no bleeding, negative/low KB → proceed to disposition.
High-risk features — 24h monitoring, evaluate injuries
≥6 contractions/hour, vaginal bleeding, significant abdominal tenderness, non-reassuring FHR, positive KB, high-energy mechanism, seatbelt sign, or visible abdominal injury → extend to 24 hours and evaluate for a pregnancy-specific injury.
Non-reassuring FHR NOW — fetal distress
Persistent bradycardia (<110 bpm), recurrent late decelerations, loss of variability, prolonged deceleration (>2 min), or sinusoidal pattern: optimize maternal status (left displacement, O2, IV fluids, correct hypotension, stop oxytocin), STAT OB consultation, prepare for emergent cesarean delivery if no improvement.
Placental abruption — clinical diagnosis
#1 cause of fetal death after trauma (1-5% of minor trauma, 40-50% of major trauma). Diagnose clinically — ultrasound sensitivity is only 25%, it cannot rule this out. Fibrinogen <200 mg/dL suggests significant abruption/DIC. Mild/stable: observation, serial exams. Moderate/severe: emergent cesarean. Can occur up to 24 hours after trauma.
Uterine rupture — immediate laparotomy
Sudden severe pain + loss of fetal station + easily palpable fetal parts = rupture until proven otherwise. Immediate laparotomy — do not delay for imaging. Massive transfusion protocol 1:1:1 PRBC:FFP:Platelets. Time to delivery 10-35 minutes to avoid fetal brain injury.
Preterm labor — tocolytics NOT recommended
≥6 contractions/hour is significant. Hydration, observation, OB consultation. Tocolytics are generally not recommended in the trauma setting — they may mask abruption symptoms and beta-agonists cause tachycardia that confuses shock assessment. Steroids (24-34 weeks) and magnesium sulfate neuroprotection (<32 weeks) are OB-directed decisions if delivery is anticipated.
Fetomaternal hemorrhage / Rh — give RhIg empirically
Order Kleihauer-Betke on all Rh-negative trauma patients, but do not wait for the result — give standard RhIg immediately, then add vials if KB shows a large fetomaternal hemorrhage.
Discharge criteria met
4-6h monitoring complete, reassuring FHR throughout, <6 contractions/hour, no bleeding, no significant tenderness, stable vitals, negative/trivial KB, RhIg given if Rh-negative. OB follow-up in 24-48h; return immediately for bleeding, decreased fetal movement, regular contractions, severe pain, leaking fluid, or dizziness.
Admit for 24h observation
L&D admission with continuous fetal monitoring, serial abdominal exams, repeat KB at 24h if initially positive, betamethasone if 24-34 weeks and delivery possible. Discharge after 24h if stable and reassuring.
Emergent cesarean delivery
Activate OR (or bedside if arrest), blood products available, NICU team notified, prepare for hysterectomy. Decision-to-incision goal <30 minutes (cardiac arrest: 5 minutes).

8 · Kleihauer-Betke & RhIg dosing

Kleihauer-Betke test: detects and quantifies fetal red blood cells in maternal circulation. Fetal hemoglobin (HbF) resists acid elution while adult hemoglobin does not — fetal cells stain pink, adult cells appear as ghosts. Turnaround 30-60 min. Limitations: does NOT diagnose abruption (can be negative with abruption present), false positives in sickle cell disease, thalassemia, and HPFH, and it is operator-dependent. Only 0.01-0.03 mL of fetal blood is needed to isoimmunize an Rh-negative mother.

9 · Cardiac arrest & resuscitative hysterotomy

ACLS elementPregnancy modification
PositioningManual left uterine displacement — assign a dedicated team member
DefibrillationSame doses; remove fetal monitors first
CompressionsMay need slightly higher hand position on sternum
IV accessAbove the diaphragm preferred (IVC compression)
IntubationSmaller ETT (6.0-7.0); increased aspiration risk
MedicationsNo modification — standard ACLS drugs
  1. Vertical midline incision, umbilicus to pubic symphysis — through skin, subcutaneous tissue, fascia; bluntly dissect rectus muscles
  2. Enter peritoneum, identify uterus
  3. Vertical uterine incision, low segment to fundus — avoid the bladder inferiorly; cut through an anterior placenta if present
  4. Insert fingers, lift the uterine wall away from the fetus
  5. Extend the incision with Mayo scissors
  6. Deliver the fetus head-first, clamp and cut the cord, hand off to the neonatal team
  7. Remove the placenta, pack the uterus with towels
  8. Continue maternal resuscitation

Indication: maternal cardiac arrest with uterus ≥20 weeks (fundus at/above umbilicus) and no ROSC within 4 minutes. Goal: relieve aortocaval compression — the primary benefit is maternal. Deliver by 5 minutes of arrest.

Maternal: continue CPR — cardiac output should improve once aortocaval compression is relieved; pack the uterus for hemorrhage control; may need definitive uterine repair or hysterectomy. Neonatal: immediate resuscitation, prepare for profound asphyxia, full NRP including intubation/compressions if needed. Outcomes: maternal survival ~54% in some series when performed within 5 minutes; neonatal survival ~45% overall, better if >24 weeks. Maternal survival documented at 15 min, neonatal survival at 30 min — even delayed delivery may have benefit; do not abandon if >5 minutes.

10 · Disposition

  • Minimum 4-6 hours monitoring completed
  • Reassuring fetal heart rate pattern throughout
  • <6 contractions per hour
  • No vaginal bleeding
  • No significant abdominal tenderness
  • Maternal vitals stable
  • Negative or trivial Kleihauer-Betke
  • RhIg given if Rh-negative

Return immediately for: vaginal bleeding, decreased fetal movement, regular contractions, severe abdominal pain, leaking fluid, dizziness/lightheadedness. Follow-up: OB within 24-48 hours. Activity: rest 24-48 hours, no strenuous activity.

ACLS element | Pregnancy modification

Positioning | Manual left uterine displacement — assign a dedicated team member

Defibrillation | Same doses; remove fetal monitors first

Compressions | May need slightly higher hand position on sternum

IV access | Above the diaphragm preferred (IVC compression)

Intubation | Smaller ETT (6.0-7.0); increased aspiration risk

Medications | No modification — standard ACLS drugs

  • Vertical midline incision, umbilicus to pubic symphysis — through skin, subcutaneous tissue, fascia; bluntly dissect rectus muscles
  • Enter peritoneum, identify uterus
  • Vertical uterine incision, low segment to fundus — avoid the bladder inferiorly; cut through an anterior placenta if present
  • Insert fingers, lift the uterine wall away from the fetus
  • Extend the incision with Mayo scissors
  • Deliver the fetus head-first, clamp and cut the cord, hand off to the neonatal team
  • Remove the placenta, pack the uterus with towels
  • Continue maternal resuscitation

Indication: maternal cardiac arrest with uterus ≥20 weeks (fundus at/above umbilicus) and no ROSC within 4 minutes. Goal: relieve aortocaval compression — the primary benefit is maternal. Deliver by 5 minutes of arrest.

Maternal: continue CPR — cardiac output should improve once aortocaval compression is relieved; pack the uterus for hemorrhage control; may need definitive uterine repair or hysterectomy. Neonatal: immediate resuscitation, prepare for profound asphyxia, full NRP including intubation/compressions if needed. Outcomes: maternal survival ~54% in some series when performed within 5 minutes; neonatal survival ~45% overall, better if >24 weeks. Maternal survival documented at 15 min, neonatal survival at 30 min — even delayed delivery may have benefit; do not abandon if >5 minutes.

10 · Disposition

  • Minimum 4-6 hours monitoring completed
  • Reassuring fetal heart rate pattern throughout
  • <6 contractions per hour
  • No vaginal bleeding
  • No significant abdominal tenderness
  • Maternal vitals stable
  • Negative or trivial Kleihauer-Betke
  • RhIg given if Rh-negative

Return immediately for: vaginal bleeding, decreased fetal movement, regular contractions, severe abdominal pain, leaking fluid, dizziness/lightheadedness. Follow-up: OB within 24-48 hours. Activity: rest 24-48 hours, no strenuous activity.

  • ≥6 contractions per hour
  • Vaginal bleeding
  • Abdominal tenderness
  • Non-reassuring FHR (even if now normalized)
  • Positive Kleihauer-Betke (>5 fetal cells/500)
  • High-energy mechanism
  • Seatbelt sign
  • Significant maternal injuries requiring admission
  • Concern for abruption
  • Preterm labor (<37 weeks)

Category | Indication

Fetal | Persistent bradycardia (<110 bpm), prolonged unresponsive deceleration, sinusoidal pattern, evidence of severe abruption

Maternal | Uterine rupture, severe hemorrhage with instability, cardiac arrest (resuscitative hysterotomy)
