B12 Deficiency & Subacute Combined Degeneration: Recognize Without Anemia, Never Folate Alone

Adults presenting with a myelopathy pattern (paresthesias, sensory ataxia, spastic weakness) or unexplained macrocytic anemia/cognitive change concerning for B12 (cobalamin) deficiency. Excludes established alternative diagnoses (e.g. confirmed compressive myelopathy on imaging).

⚠ Recognize subacute combined degeneration: dorsal-column sensory loss (vibration/proprioception) PLUS corticospinal upper-motor-neuron signs.
⚠ Neuro damage can occur WITHOUT anemia or macrocytosis — do not wait for a high MCV before working up a compatible myelopathy.
⚠ B12 in the 200-400 pg/mL gray zone: send MMA + homocysteine (both elevated in true deficiency) — this range does not exclude deficiency.
⚠ Do NOT give folate alone — it masks the hematologic warning sign of anemia while neurologic degeneration continues; replace B12 first or together.
⚠ Nitrous oxide (whippets) causes acute subacute combined degeneration with a NORMAL serum B12 — diagnose via MMA/homocysteine; stop exposure.
⚠ Replace B12 EARLY (parenteral for significant neuro disease) — late treatment leaves permanent deficits.
⚠ Non-responder or normalized B12 still declining: check copper + ceruloplasmin (near-identical myelopathy).

1 · Recognition — the combined pattern

Subacute combined degeneration (SCD) is demyelination of the dorsal columns and the lateral corticospinal tracts from cobalamin (vitamin B12) deficiency — "combined" means both tract systems at once, producing sensory loss plus upper-motor-neuron signs. B12 is a cofactor for methionine synthase (myelin methylation) and methylmalonyl-CoA mutase; deficiency impairs myelin synthesis and lets neurotoxic metabolites accumulate, and it also impairs DNA synthesis, causing megaloblastic anemia and CNS effects.[1][2]

The trap that defines this consult
The neurologic disease can appear before — or without — any anemia. A normal CBC does not clear B12 deficiency in a patient with a compatible myelopathy.[1][2][3]

2 · Etiology — sets the treatment route and duration

  • Pernicious anemia — autoimmune loss of intrinsic factor (anti-IF/anti-parietal-cell antibodies); the classic malabsorptive cause; lifelong replacement
  • Dietary — strict vegan/vegetarian (B12 is animal-sourced)
  • Malabsorption — ileal resection/Crohn, gastrectomy/bariatric surgery, bacterial overgrowth
  • Drugs — long-term metformin and PPIs/H2 blockers
  • Nitrous oxide (N2O) abuse ("whippets") — oxidizes cobalamin to its inactive form, a functional deficiency
  • Age — atrophic gastritis and reduced acid-dependent absorption

3 · Diagnosis — mind the gray zone

B12 low — deficiency confirmed
Serum B12 low confirms deficiency. Proceed to etiology workup and start treatment; MMA/homocysteine can still support the diagnosis but are not required to act.[3]
Gray zone (200-400 pg/mL) — order MMA + homocysteine
The 200-400 pg/mL gray zone does not exclude deficiency: ~50% of subclinical deficiency has a "normal" B12. Methylmalonic acid (MMA) is the most specific confirmatory test — elevated; order it with homocysteine whenever B12 is borderline/low-normal or the clinical picture fits. Homocysteine is sensitive but less specific (also raised by folate deficiency, renal impairment). MMA best separates B12 from folate deficiency.[3][10]
B12 normal — still consider the clinical picture
A normal B12 does not exclude deficiency if the clinical picture fits (classic SCD pattern, N2O exposure). Send MMA + homocysteine if suspicion remains — this is exactly how N2O-induced disease is diagnosed despite a normal measured B12.[3][4][5]

CBC/smear — macrocytosis, hypersegmented neutrophils; may be normal despite neuro disease. Anti-intrinsic-factor/anti-parietal-cell antibodies for pernicious anemia. MRI spine — T2 hyperintensity in the dorsal columns (inverted-"V"/"dot" sign), cervical/thoracic, non-enhancing — indistinguishable from copper-deficiency myelopathy.[3][10]

4 · Two traps — folate alone and nitrous oxide

Trap 1 — do NOT give folate alone
Folate corrects the megaloblastic anemia — removing the hematologic warning sign — while neurologic degeneration continues unchecked; some literature argues folate may even accelerate neuro damage. Assess B12 status before or alongside any folate, and replace B12 first or together.[4][11]

Trap 1 — never folate alone. Folate masks the anemia while neurologic damage continues. Assess B12 status before or alongside any folate, and replace B12 first or together.[4][11]

Trap 2 — nitrous oxide causes SCD with a NORMAL serum B12
N2O oxidizes the cobalt core of cobalamin, inactivating methionine synthase — a functional deficiency. ~29% of N2O neuro cases have a normal measured B12; diagnosis hinges on elevated MMA + homocysteine (raised in >80%). Draw biochemical samples before starting B12 (markers normalize quickly), but do not delay treatment for testing. Stop the exposure; B12 repletion ± methionine adjunct is a judgment call. N2O-induced disease can also mimic Guillain-Barré — keep it in mind in young patients with myeloneuropathy.[4][5][7]

Trap 2 — N2O ('whippets') causes SCD with a normal serum B12 in a substantial minority of cases; diagnosis then relies on MMA + homocysteine, not the B12 level itself.[4][5][7]

6 · Differential — don't miss copper

Copper deficiency myelopathy — the near-identical mimic
Clinically AND radiologically near-identical to B12 SCD — same dorsal-column sensory ataxia + corticospinal signs and the same inverted-"V" MRI sign. Check serum copper + ceruloplasmin, especially since the patient keeps deteriorating despite B12 repletion. Causes: bariatric/gastric surgery (~47%), zinc excess (denture cream, supplements; ~16%), malabsorption, often with cytopenias. The two can coexist.[13][14]

Copper deficiency myelopathy ("human swayback") is clinically and radiologically near-identical to B12 SCD — same dorsal-column ataxia + corticospinal signs and inverted-"V" MRI sign. Check serum copper + ceruloplasmin if the patient keeps deteriorating despite B12 repletion and a normalized B12 level, or has a bariatric/zinc history.[13][14]
