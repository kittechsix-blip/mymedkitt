Pediatric DKA: Fluids First, Insulin After — Watch the Brain

Children and adolescents (approx <18 yr) with diabetic ketoacidosis in the ED/PICU. Excludes adult DKA, HHS without DKA, and neonatal hypoglycemia — each needs a different protocol. Cerebral-edema vigilance is mandatory throughout.

⚠ PECARN: fluid rate and Na content do NOT change neuro outcomes; bolus only if unstable (10 mL/kg); deficit + maintenance over 24-48h.
⚠ Insulin starts AFTER 1h fluids — 0.05-0.1 U/kg/hr IV, NO bolus; target glucose drop 50-75 mg/dL/hr; faster = CE risk.
⚠ CE = #1 killer; 4-12h into tx; Muir criteria 92/96 sens/spec; clinical dx (~40% initial CT normal); risk factors: <5yo, new-onset, severe acidosis, bicarb use.
⚠ Diagnose: glucose >200 + venous pH <7.30 OR HCO3 <18 + ketonemia/ketonuria (ISPAD)
⚠ Severity: Mild pH 7.20-7.29 / Moderate 7.10-7.19 / Severe <7.10
⚠ Bolus only if hemodynamically unstable: 10 mL/kg 0.9% NS over 20-30 min, reassess

CEREBRAL EDEMA — treat NOW, do not wait for CT
Head of bed to 30°. Give an osmotic agent immediately: Mannitol 0.5–1 g/kg IV over 10–15 min (see live dose below) or 3% NaCl 5–10 mL/kg over 30 min. Intubate if GCS <8. This is the #1 killer of children in DKA.[4]
Unstable — resuscitate first
Give 10 mL/kg 0.9% NS over 20–30 min, then reassess perfusion (see live bolus volume below). Repeat once if still poorly perfused; a second bolus → escalate to PICU and hunt for sepsis / cardiogenic shock. Avoid reflexive 20 mL/kg boluses.[2]
Stable — run the standard timeline
No bolus. Start deficit + maintenance fluids now; start insulin only AFTER 1 hour of fluids at 0.05–0.1 U/kg/hr with NO bolus. Recheck neuro status hourly — most cerebral edema hits 4–12 h into treatment.[1]

3 · Insulin & potassium

Start insulin AFTER 1 hour of fluids — never concurrently
A UK national study linked insulin in the first hour to higher cerebral-edema risk; ISPAD 2022 reinforces the delay. Give NO IV bolus — there is no efficacy benefit in children and a real edema signal.[1]
K⁺ < 3.3 → HOLD insulin
Give 0.5–1 mEq/kg KCl IV (max 40 mEq/hr) over 1 h and recheck K⁺ before starting insulin. Insulin drives K⁺ intracellularly and can precipitate fatal hypokalemia.[1]
K⁺ 3.3–5.5 → insulin + replace K⁺ together
Add 20–40 mEq/L KCl to the fluids once urine output is established (often split half KCl / half K-phosphate). Recheck K⁺ q2h.[1]
K⁺ > 5.5 → no K⁺ yet
Withhold potassium, start insulin, and recheck K⁺ in 1–2 h — it will fall quickly once insulin and fluids take effect.[1]

4 · Dextrose — the two-bag system

Add dextrose at glucose 250–300 — do NOT stop insulin
The anion gap, not the glucose, decides when insulin stops. Keep insulin running and add sugar so ketosis keeps clearing.[1]

Two-bag system: Bag A = 0.45% NS + KCl, no dextrose; Bag B = same + D10. Both run through one line at a fixed total rate; vary the A:B ratio to titrate effective dextrose from D2.5 to D10 without touching the insulin drip.[1]

5 · Cerebral edema — the #1 killer

Recognize early — most CTs are normal
Cerebral edema causes 20–25% mortality and permanent neuro deficit in 10–25% of survivors. It typically strikes 4–12 h into treatment: the child who looked better, then suddenly deteriorates. ~40% of initial CTs are normal — this is a clinical diagnosis.[4][5]

Muir/Glaser bedside criteria (Diabetes Care 2004) — 92% sensitive, 96% specific. Diagnose on any 1 diagnostic (abnormal motor/verbal response to pain, decorticate/decerebrate posturing, cranial-nerve palsy, abnormal neurogenic breathing) OR any 2 major (altered mentation, sustained bradycardia, age-inappropriate incontinence).[4]

First-line osmotic therapy — Mannitol 0.5–1 g/kg IV over 10–15 min (choose ONE agent). Live upper-bound (1 g/kg) dose for this child:

7 · Do NOT

Critical pitfalls

**Do NOT give an insulin bolus** in a child — no benefit, real cerebral-edema risk.

**Do NOT start insulin in the first hour** of fluids — wait until ≥1 h of resuscitation.

**Do NOT wait for a CT** before treating cerebral edema — ~40% of early scans are normal; treat on Muir criteria.

**Do NOT reflexively bolus 20 mL/kg** in a stable child — use 10 mL/kg only when truly unstable.

**Do NOT resolve on glucose** — require anion gap <12 and overlap SC insulin before stopping the drip.
