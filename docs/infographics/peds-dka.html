Pediatric DKA: Fluids First, Insulin After — Watch the Brain

Children and adolescents (approx <18 yr) with diabetic ketoacidosis in the ED/PICU, per ISPAD 2022. Excludes adult DKA, HHS without ketosis, neonatal hypoglycemia, and inborn errors of metabolism — each needs a different protocol. Cerebral-edema vigilance is mandatory throughout.

⚠ Insulin starts AFTER 1 hour of fluids, NOT concurrently, and is a continuous infusion with NO IV bolus — bolus insulin adds no benefit in children and is linked to higher cerebral-edema risk.
⚠ Hold insulin if K⁺ <3.3 mEq/L — replete potassium first, then start the infusion once K⁺ ≥3.3.
⚠ The child who is "improving" then deteriorates 4–12 h into treatment has cerebral edema until proven otherwise — apply Muir criteria at every neuro check and treat immediately without waiting for imaging (~40% of early CT scans are normal).
⚠ Do NOT stop insulin for a falling glucose — add dextrose (two-bag system) and keep the infusion running; the anion gap, not the glucose, decides when insulin stops.
⚠ Avoid reflexive 20 mL/kg boluses in a stable child — give 10 mL/kg 0.9% NS only if hemodynamically unstable, and reassess before repeating.
⚠ Give the first SC long-acting insulin dose 30–60 minutes before stopping the IV infusion — stopping without overlap lets DKA recur within hours.
⚠ Bicarbonate is almost never indicated — it increases cerebral-edema risk and provides no benefit except life-threatening hyperkalemia or pH <6.9 with cardiac dysfunction.

CEREBRAL EDEMA — treat NOW, do not wait for CT
Head of bed to 30°. Give an osmotic agent immediately: Mannitol 0.5–1 g/kg IV over 10–15 min (see live dose below) or 3% NaCl 5–10 mL/kg over 30 min. Intubate if GCS <8. This is the #1 killer of children in DKA.[4]
Unstable — resuscitate first
Give 10 mL/kg 0.9% NS over 20–30 min, then reassess perfusion (see live bolus volume below). Repeat once if still poorly perfused; a second bolus → escalate to PICU and hunt for sepsis / cardiogenic shock. Avoid reflexive 20 mL/kg boluses.[2]
Stable — run the standard timeline
No bolus. Start deficit + maintenance fluids now; start insulin only AFTER 1 hour of fluids at 0.05–0.1 U/kg/hr with NO bolus. Recheck neuro status hourly — most cerebral edema hits 4–12 h into treatment.[1]

3 · Insulin & potassium

Start insulin AFTER 1 hour of fluids — never concurrently
A UK national study linked insulin in the first hour to higher cerebral-edema risk; ISPAD 2022 reinforces the delay. Give NO IV bolus — there is no efficacy benefit in children and a real edema signal, so this cockpit does not offer a bolus calculator.[1]

5 · Cerebral edema — the #1 killer

Recognize early — most CTs are normal
Cerebral edema causes 20–25% mortality and permanent neuro deficit in 10–25% of survivors. It typically strikes 4–12 h into treatment: the child who looked better, then suddenly deteriorates. ~40% of initial CTs are normal — this is a clinical diagnosis.[4][5]

Muir/Glaser bedside criteria (Diabetes Care 2004) — 92% sensitive, 96% specific. Diagnose on any 1 diagnostic (abnormal motor/verbal response to pain, decorticate/decerebrate posturing, cranial-nerve palsy, abnormal neurogenic breathing) OR any 2 major (altered mentation, sustained bradycardia, age-inappropriate incontinence) OR 1 major + 2 minor.[4]
