HIV Post-Exposure Prophylaxis (PEP)

Patients presenting for HIV post-exposure prophylaxis evaluation after an occupational (needlestick/sharps/mucous membrane) or nonoccupational (sexual, injection drug use, other) exposure. Excludes patients who are already HIV-positive (not PEP candidates — refer for ART) and exposures presenting >72 hours after the event (PEP not recommended beyond this window due to lack of efficacy data).

⚠ PEP must be initiated within 72 hours of exposure — efficacy decreases with delay and is not recommended after the window closes
⚠ Do NOT delay PEP for baseline lab results — start medications immediately and adjust later based on labs
⚠ PEP is prophylaxis for HIV-negative or unknown-status individuals only — known HIV-positive patients are not PEP candidates
⚠ Negligible-risk fluids (urine, saliva, sweat, tears without visible blood) do not warrant PEP — no documented transmission cases
⚠ Confirmed HIV-negative source with no window-period concern = PEP not indicated
⚠ Prescribe the full 28-day course from the ED — do not dispense starter packs, as patients may not follow up for refills

HIV assessment

Confirm the exposed patient's HIV status before proceeding — PEP applies only to patients who are HIV-negative or of unknown status.

Not a PEP Candidate — Known HIV-Positive
Patient is already HIV-positive. PEP is prophylaxis for HIV-negative individuals only. Refer to an HIV specialist for ART management and verify viral load and treatment adherence.

If the source is known HIV-positive on ART with a sustained undetectable viral load (<200 copies/mL), transmission risk is negligible (U=U). PEP may still be offered per patient preference.

PEP Not Indicated — Source Confirmed HIV-Negative
Source is confirmed HIV-negative — no HIV transmission risk from this exposure. If the source HIV test was a rapid test, confirm with a lab-based assay; consider the window period if the source has recent high-risk behavior.

Baseline labs still recommended: HIV Ag/Ab combo (4th gen); Hepatitis B surface Ag, surface Ab, core Ab; Hepatitis C Ab.

Initiation

Start PEP Immediately
Do not delay PEP for test results. Start medications now; adjust later based on labs.
  • HIV Ag/Ab combo (4th generation)
  • Hepatitis B surface Ag, surface Ab, core Ab
  • Hepatitis C Ab
  • BMP (creatinine for renal dosing)
  • CBC
  • Pregnancy test (if applicable)
  • STI screening (GC/CT, syphilis RPR)
  • Occupational exposures: also test the source patient for HIV, HBV, and HCV

Screen for pregnancy or renal impairment (CrCl <50 mL/min) — both affect regimen selection and dosing.

Pregnancy — Safe to Proceed
PEP is safe and recommended in pregnancy. Use the same preferred regimen. Avoid efavirenz-based regimens. OB/HIV specialist consultation recommended.

Prescribe the Full Course
Prescribe the full 28-day course from the ED. Do not dispense starter packs — patients may not follow up for refills.

Monitoring: HIV Ag/Ab at baseline, 4–6 weeks, and 3 months; adherence check at 2 weeks; renal function if TDF used.

Follow-up & disposition

  1. Baseline: HIV Ag/Ab (4th gen) at initial visit
  2. 4–6 weeks: repeat HIV test
  3. 3 months: repeat HIV test (final if 4th gen used)
  • 2-week visit: assess adherence, side effects, renal function
  • Complete the full 28 days — early discontinuation reduces efficacy
  • Review baseline Hepatitis B surface Ag, surface Ab, core Ab results
  • If susceptible (all negative): start HBV vaccine series; give HBIG if source is HBV-positive
  • HCV: baseline + 6-month HCV Ab; if seroconversion occurs, early DAA treatment cures >95%

Occupational exposures: report to occupational health / employee health.

PrEP Transition
For patients with ongoing HIV risk, transition to PrEP after completing PEP and confirming HIV-negative status. No gap is needed between PEP and PrEP.

Prescribe the full 28-day course. Schedule follow-up at 2 weeks. Arrange HIV testing at 4–6 weeks and 3 months. Review HBV serology and vaccinate if susceptible. Discuss PrEP for ongoing risk. For occupational exposures, report to employee health.

PEP algorithm

Exposure Type — Classify exposure as occupational (needlestick/sharps/mucous membrane) or nonoccupational (sexual, IDU, other) to guide the PEP evaluation pathway.

Substantial-Risk Fluid? — Substantial risk: blood, semen/vaginal secretions, rectal secretions, breast milk, any visibly bloody fluid. Negligible risk: urine, saliva, sweat, tears without visible blood.

PEP Not Indicated (Negligible-Risk Fluid) — No documented cases of HIV transmission from negligible-risk fluids. Baseline HIV/HBV/HCV labs still recommended.

Time Since Exposure ≤ 72h? — PEP must be initiated as soon as possible. Efficacy decreases with delay and is not recommended after 72 hours.

PEP Not Indicated (>72 Hours) — PEP is not recommended beyond the 72-hour window due to lack of efficacy data. Discuss PrEP for ongoing risk; repeat HIV testing at 4–6 weeks and 3 months.

Patient HIV Status — Confirm the exposed patient is HIV-negative or unknown status — PEP applies only to these individuals.

Not a PEP Candidate (Known HIV+) — Patient is already HIV-positive. Refer to HIV specialist for ART management; PEP is not applicable.

Source HIV Status — If source is HIV+ on ART with sustained undetectable viral load (<200 copies/mL), transmission risk is negligible (U=U) — PEP may still be offered per patient preference.

PEP Not Indicated (Source Confirmed Negative) — No HIV transmission risk from this exposure. Confirm rapid test with lab-based assay if applicable; consider window period.

Start PEP Immediately — Do not delay PEP for test results. Start medications now; adjust later based on labs. Draw baseline HIV/HBV/HCV/BMP/CBC/pregnancy/STI labs.

Special Population? — Screen for pregnancy or renal impairment (CrCl <50) — both affect regimen selection and dosing.

Pregnancy Considerations — PEP is safe and recommended in pregnancy — use the same preferred regimen (Biktarvy); avoid efavirenz-based regimens; OB/HIV specialist follow-up.

Renal Impairment (CrCl <50) — TAF-based regimen preferred over TDF. Do not use Biktarvy if CrCl <30 — consult ID/pharmacy for alternative. Avoid TDF if CrCl <60.

28-Day Regimen — Preferred: Biktarvy 1 tablet PO daily x28 days. Alternative: TDF/FTC + Dolutegravir. Second alternative: TDF/FTC + Darunavir/Ritonavir. Prescribe the full course — no starter packs.

Follow-Up & Disposition — HIV testing at 4–6 weeks and 3 months; 2-week adherence/renal check; HBV vaccination if susceptible; HCV Ab at 6 months; PrEP transition for ongoing risk.
