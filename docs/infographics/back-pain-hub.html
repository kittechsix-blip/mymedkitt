Back Pain Hub — ED Triage

Adults presenting to the ED with back pain. Screens the time-critical mimics (cauda equina, spinal epidural abscess, AAA/aortic dissection, fracture/malignancy, renal/urinary mimics) before defaulting to a mechanical/radicular pathway. Not a treatment protocol for confirmed diagnoses — each critical lane hands off to its own dedicated consult.

⚠ Ruptured/leaking AAA — age ≥50 + smoker, pulsatile mass, syncope → bedside ultrasound now.
⚠ Spinal epidural abscess — fever, IVDU/immunosuppression, point tenderness, deficit → the classic triad is insensitive; no fever does NOT clear SEA.
⚠ Cauda equina syndrome — saddle anesthesia, urinary retention, bilateral leg weakness → emergency MRI; pain improving does NOT mean bladder/neurologic injury is improving.
⚠ Cord compression from malignancy — cancer history, night pain, progressive deficit → new severe back pain in a cancer patient is malignant spinal disease until reasonably excluded.
⚠ Aortic dissection / pyelonephritis-urosepsis — tearing pain/pulse deficit, or fever + CVA tenderness → do not anchor on musculoskeletal pain in an older vascular-risk patient.
⚠ Admit/transfer whenever cauda/conus symptoms, progressive neurologic deficit, suspected/confirmed spine infection, sepsis, AAA/dissection, unstable vitals, or cord compression concern are present.

Sick check first

Most back pain is mechanical. The ED job is to rapidly find the few that are spine, vascular, infectious, renal, traumatic, or malignant emergencies. First 60 seconds: general appearance, vitals trend, neuro exam (leg strength/sensation/reflexes, gait, saddle sensation, bowel/bladder), vascular/abdominal exam, infection risk, and fracture/cancer risk.

  1. Ruptured / leaking AAA — age ≥50 + smoker, pulsatile mass, syncope; bedside US now.
  2. Spinal epidural abscess — fever, IVDU/immunosuppression, point tenderness, deficit.
  3. Cauda equina syndrome — saddle anesthesia, urinary retention, bilateral leg weakness.
  4. Cord compression from malignancy — cancer history, night pain, progressive deficit.
  5. Aortic dissection / pyelonephritis-urosepsis — tearing pain/pulse deficit, or fever + CVA tenderness.

▶ What to do now

Cauda equina / conus medullaris
Open Cauda Equina Syndrome. Next 5 minutes: bladder scan/PVR, sacral exam, leg motor/reflex/sensation exam, urgent MRI lumbar spine, early spine/neurosurgery call. Pitfall: pain may be improving while bladder/neurologic injury is worsening.
Spinal epidural abscess / vertebral osteomyelitis
Open Low Back Pain Decision Support with spine-infection posture. Next 5 minutes: neuro exam, ESR/CRP, blood cultures when febrile/high-suspicion, MRI spine with and without contrast. Antibiotics after cultures when stable — do not delay for sepsis or a neurologic deficit.
AAA / aortic dissection back-pain variant
Open Aortic Aneurysm ED Management or Aortic Dissection. Next 5 minutes: two large-bore IVs, type/screen or MTP posture if unstable, bedside aorta ultrasound if available, CTA chest/abdomen/pelvis when stable, vascular/cardiothoracic surgery early.
Fracture / malignancy / metastatic cord compression
Open Low Back Pain Decision Support. Next 5 minutes: neuro exam, pain control, CT/radiographs for trauma/fracture by risk, MRI when cancer with neurologic symptoms or suspected cord/cauda compression. New severe back pain in a cancer patient is malignant spinal disease until reasonably excluded.
Renal / urinary / intra-abdominal mimic
Open Adult UTI or Urinary Retention when those fit. Next 5 minutes: UA, pregnancy test when applicable, renal function, bedside bladder scan if retention, CT/ultrasound strategy for stone/obstruction/pyelo complication by risk.
Radiculopathy / mechanical pattern
Open Low Back Pain Decision Support and the L4/L5/S1 dermatome/myotome/reflex map. Confirm no red flags, document motor/reflex/sensation, treat pain to enable mobility, avoid routine advanced imaging. Radicular pain does not equal cauda equina — screen bowel/bladder/saddle/bilateral weakness every time.

Back pain hub algorithm

Back pain — sick check first — 5 DO NOT MISS: ruptured/leaking AAA, spinal epidural abscess, cauda equina syndrome, cord compression from malignancy, aortic dissection/pyelo-urosepsis. Vitals, neuro, vascular/abdominal, infection, fracture/cancer risk first.

Which time-critical lane fits best? — Do not start with 'sciatica' until cauda, spine infection, AAA/dissection, fracture/cancer, and renal mimics are screened.

Cauda equina / conus medullaris — Urinary retention/incontinence, saddle anesthesia, bilateral sciatica, progressive bilateral leg weakness, fecal incontinence, severe deficit → emergency MRI + spine pathway.

Spinal epidural abscess / vertebral osteomyelitis — Fever, IVDU, diabetes, immunosuppression, recent bacteremia/procedure, focal spine tenderness → MRI is the key diagnostic test.

AAA / aortic dissection variant — Older vascular risk, abrupt severe back/abdominal pain, syncope, hypotension, pulse deficit → resuscitation diagnosis, image and consult early.

No immediate exclusion hit → initial bundle — Analgesia (acetaminophen, NSAID if safe, topical/local, selective muscle relaxer, avoid routine opioids), repeat neuro/gait exam, bladder scan/PVR if indicated, ESR/CRP by infection risk, encourage movement.

Fracture / malignancy / cord compression — Trauma, osteoporosis, chronic steroids, cancer, weight loss, night pain → imaging beyond routine mechanical back pain care.

Renal / urinary / intra-abdominal mimic — Flank pain, fever, urinary symptoms, stone-like colic, solitary kidney, pregnancy → treat by obstruction, infection severity, pregnancy, renal risk.

Radiculopathy / mechanical pattern — Unilateral radicular pain, positive straight-leg, no red flags, normal strength/bladder → outpatient only after red flags and function reassessed.

Reassess after initial bundle — Reassess in 30-60 minutes: ability to stand/walk, neuro exam, bladder symptoms, pain control, vitals. New neuro/bladder/fever/vascular features reset the workup.

Imaging decision — MRI lumbar spine for cauda/conus/progressive deficit/suspected SEA-NVO/malignancy-neuro/cord-cauda compression; CTA or bedside US for aorta; CT/radiographs for fracture; no routine imaging for uncomplicated mechanical pain/radiculopathy.

Disposition — Disposition follows neurologic safety, infection/vascular risk, mobility, and pain control.

Admit / transfer / specialty pathway — Cauda/conus symptoms, progressive neurologic deficit, suspected/confirmed SEA/NVO, sepsis, AAA/dissection, unstable vitals, fracture needing intervention, malignancy/cord compression concern, unable to ambulate safely, retention with neuro concern, or pain needing inpatient control.

Observation / serial exam — MRI/CTA/labs pending, pain prevents reliable exam/ambulation test, symptoms evolving, moderate infection risk with unclear labs, or unsafe social/transport situation.

Discharge checklist — No red flags, normal/baseline neuro exam, no bowel/bladder/saddle symptoms, ambulatory, pain controlled, no infection/vascular/trauma/cancer concern, realistic follow-up. Explicit cauda/infection/vascular return precautions.
