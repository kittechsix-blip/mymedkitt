Pediatric Electrolyte Emergencies: Identify, Treat, Disposition

Infants, children, and adolescents in the ED with a suspected acute electrolyte or glucose emergency (hyponatremia, hypernatremia, hyperkalemia, hypokalemia, hypocalcemia, hypercalcemia, hypoglycemia). Excludes adults, chronic/outpatient electrolyte management, and neonatal ICU-level inborn-error-of-metabolism workups beyond initial ED critical-sample collection.

⚠ Symptomatic hyponatremia (seizure, AMS, herniation signs) → 3% NaCl 3-5 mL/kg IV over 10-15 min (max 100 mL); may repeat ×2 if symptoms persist. Goal: raise Na 4-6 mEq/L acutely to reverse herniation — not to normalize Na.
⚠ Do NOT correct sodium faster than ≤8-10 mEq/L per 24 hours in either direction (≤6-8 mEq/L per 24h if hyponatremia is chronic or duration unknown) — overshoot causes irreversible osmotic demyelination syndrome (ODS).
⚠ Hypernatremia: replace the free water deficit over 48-72 hours (NOT 24h) and cap correction at ≤10-12 mEq/L per 24h — faster correction causes cerebral edema, seizures, herniation.
⚠ Hyperkalemia: the ECG — not the potassium number — drives urgency. Never delay treatment for a confirmatory recheck if any ECG change (peaked T, PR prolongation, wide QRS, sine wave) is present.
⚠ Hyperkalemia with ANY ECG change → calcium gluconate 60 mg/kg IV (max single dose 3 g) FIRST for membrane stabilization — it does NOT lower potassium; redose if ECG remains abnormal, then shift K+ with insulin + dextrose ± nebulized albuterol.
⚠ Hypokalemia IV replacement: peripheral IV max 0.5 mEq/kg/hr (max 10 mEq/hr). NEVER IV push potassium — fatal arrhythmia. Always replace magnesium concurrently — hypoMg makes hypoK refractory.
⚠ Symptomatic hypocalcemia (tetany, seizure, prolonged QT) → calcium gluconate 50-100 mg/kg IV over 5-10 min (max single dose 2 g) with continuous cardiac monitoring. Hold IV calcium if the patient is on digoxin.
⚠ Hypoglycemia is reversible brain injury — treat within minutes using age-specific dextrose concentration (Rule of 50s). D25W/D50W in neonates and young infants is hyperosmolar and causes venous sclerosis — use D10W only in that age group.
⚠ Check fingerstick glucose (accucheck) on every altered or seizing child immediately — hypoglycemia mimics everything and must never be missed.
⚠ Use balanced crystalloid (LR/Plasmalyte) over 0.9% NS for resuscitation — large-volume 0.9% NS causes hyperchloremic metabolic acidosis and worsens AKI risk.

1 · Triage & Fluid Choice

Why kids are different: higher total body water (75% in neonates vs 60% in adults) → larger fluid shifts; lower renal concentrating ability in infants → faster dehydration; immature cerebral autoregulation → faster brain edema with rapid Na shifts; smaller margin of error in dosing.

Red flags requiring immediate action: seizure, AMS, lethargy → check glucose AND Na immediately. Wide QRS, peaked T → check K immediately. Tetany, carpopedal spasm → check Ca and Mg. Severe dehydration with shock → balanced crystalloid bolus + electrolyte panel.

  • Accucheck FIRST in any altered or seizing child — hypoglycemia mimics everything
  • Two IVs if unstable; use IO if access difficult
  • Hydration exam: mucous membranes, cap refill, tears, fontanelle, skin turgor, UOP
  • Tetany signs: Chvostek (facial twitch with tap), Trousseau (carpopedal spasm with BP cuff)
  • Labs (single stick): BMP, iCal + Mg + Phos, VBG with lactate, CBC, urinalysis
Balanced crystalloid > 0.9% NS
Default to LR or Plasmalyte-A for resuscitation (10-20 mL/kg bolus). Large-volume 0.9% NS (Cl 154 mEq/L) causes hyperchloremic metabolic acidosis and worsens AKI risk. Maintenance fluids: isotonic IVF per AAP 2018 — do NOT use 0.45% or 0.2% NS for maintenance (causes iatrogenic hyponatremia).

2 · Identify the Disturbance

Multiple disturbances often coexist (e.g., DKA with K and Na changes). Treat the most life-threatening first — usually hyperkalemia with ECG changes or symptomatic hyponatremia. Select the dominant disturbance below.

3 · What to do now

Hyponatremia — symptomatic vs asymptomatic drives speed
Symptomatic (seizure, AMS, herniation) → 3% NaCl 3-5 mL/kg IV over 10-15 min (max 100 mL) now. Asymptomatic → workup (urine osm/Na, serum osm, cortisol) and correct no faster than ≤8-10 mEq/L per 24 hours.
Hypernatremia — slow rehydration over 48-72h
Resuscitate shock first with balanced crystalloid, then replace the free water deficit over 48-72 hours, capping correction at ≤10-12 mEq/L per 24h. Evaluate for diabetes insipidus or salt poisoning if the cause is unclear.
Hyperkalemia — ECG drives urgency
Rule out spurious hemolysis, but never delay treatment if ECG changes are present. Any ECG change → calcium gluconate 60 mg/kg IV (max 3 g) first, then shift K+ with insulin/dextrose ± albuterol.
Hypokalemia — replace slowly, check Mg
Oral KCl preferred when possible. IV peripheral capped at 0.5 mEq/kg/hr. Always replace magnesium concurrently — hypoMg makes hypoK refractory. NEVER IV push potassium.
Hypocalcemia — tetany & QT prolongation
Confirm with ionized calcium. Symptomatic (tetany, seizure, prolonged QT) → calcium gluconate 50-100 mg/kg IV over 5-10 min (max 2 g) with continuous cardiac monitoring. Hold IV calcium if on digoxin.
Hypercalcemia — hydrate first
Aggressive isotonic hydration is the foundation (0.9% NS, goal UOP >2 mL/kg/hr). Furosemide only AFTER volume repletion. Calcitonin 4 IU/kg SC/IM q12h bridges to bisphosphonate/denosumab for malignancy.
Hypoglycemia — Rule of 50s
Reversible brain injury — treat within minutes. Age-specific dextrose concentration: D10 (neonate/infant), D25 (child), D50 (adolescent) — never D25/D50 in young infants. Send a critical sample before dextrose if hypopituitarism/IEM suspected.

11 · Disposition

PICU criteria
Symptomatic severe disturbance (seizure, AMS, herniation, arrhythmia), hypertonic saline infusion required, hyperkalemia with ECG changes after initial treatment, AKI requiring dialysis, adrenal crisis/suspected CAH, persistent hypoglycemia needing high-GIR drip (>10 mg/kg/min). Continuous cardiac monitoring, hourly neuro checks, serial labs q2-4h.
Floor / step-down criteria
Mild-moderate disturbance, asymptomatic or symptoms resolved, stable on oral/low-rate IV replacement, telemetry if K/Ca abnormality with prior ECG changes. Serial labs q6-8h. Escalate to PICU for worsening symptoms/labs or new arrhythmia/AMS.
ED observation / discharge criteria
Mild, asymptomatic, single disturbance with clear reversible cause, labs normalized/trending, tolerating PO, reliable family, confirmed PCP follow-up 24-48h. Do NOT discharge: any neonate with unclear-cause electrolyte abnormality, any prior seizure/AMS this episode, unclear etiology.

Potassium Chloride (IV, peripheral): 0.5-1 mEq/kg IV over 1-2 hours Peripheral IV, ≤40 mEq/L concentration — Rate capped at 0.5 mEq/kg/hr (max 10 mEq/hr). NEVER IV push potassium — fatal arrhythmia. Continuous ECG monitoring during infusion.

Potassium Chloride (IV, central): Up to 1 mEq/kg/hr Central line, ≤80 mEq/L concentration — Rate capped at 20 mEq/hr — requires continuous cardiac monitoring.

Peripheral KCl rate (0.5 mEq/kg/hr, cap 10 mEq/hr): (computed)

Magnesium Sulfate: 25-50 mg/kg IV over 1-2h — Max 2 g. Replace Mg first or concurrently — hypomagnesemia causes refractory hypokalemia.

Stop offending diuretics (or switch to a K-sparing alternative). Replace volume (saline if dehydrated). In DKA: start K replacement once K <5.5 mEq/L and insulin infusion is running.

NEVER IV push potassium — fatal arrhythmia. Continuous ECG monitoring during IV replacement, especially central or rate >0.5 mEq/kg/hr. Recheck K every 1-2h during active replacement. Stop replacement at K ~4.0.

9 · Calcium disorders detail

Calcium Gluconate 10%: 50-100 mg/kg IV over 5-10 min — Max single dose 2 g. Continuous cardiac monitoring — bradycardia/asystole if pushed too fast. Recheck iCal at 15-30 min; redose if symptoms persist. Hold if on digoxin.

Calcium gluconate (75 mg/kg midpoint, cap 2000 mg): (computed)

Magnesium Sulfate: 25-50 mg/kg IV over 1-2 hours — Hypomagnesemia causes refractory hypocalcemia via impaired PTH release — always check and replace.

Calcium Carbonate/Citrate (Oral) + Vitamin D: 30-75 mg/kg/day elemental Ca divided, plus vitamin D replacement (ergocalciferol or cholecalciferol) — Asymptomatic mild hypocalcemia — oral replacement preferred over IV.

For recurrent symptomatic hypocalcemia: continuous IV infusion 0.5-1.5 mg/kg/hr elemental calcium.

Severity | Ca (mg/dL)

Mild | 10.5-12

Moderate | 12-14

Severe / crisis | >14 or symptomatic

Calcitonin (Salmon): 4 IU/kg SC/IM q12h — May escalate to 8 IU/kg q6h if inadequate. Onset 2-6h. Bridge to bisphosphonate/denosumab — tachyphylaxis in 2-3 days.

Calcitonin (4 IU/kg): (computed)

  • Aggressive isotonic hydration: 0.9% NS 20 mL/kg bolus, then 2-3× maintenance (goal UOP >2 mL/kg/hr); avoid LR (contains calcium)
  • Forced calciuresis: furosemide 1 mg/kg IV q6h AFTER volume-replete — never before
  • Reduce bone resorption: calcitonin, then bisphosphonate (malignancy) or glucocorticoids (vit D toxicity)
  • Dialysis for severe (>18 mg/dL), AKI, or CHF

10 · Hypoglycemia detail — Rule of 50s

Neonate: <40 mg/dL (some references <45-50). Infant/child: <60 mg/dL. Symptomatic at any level → treat.

Age | Dextrose | Dose

Neonate | D10W | 2-5 mL/kg IV (max 25 mL initial)

Infant (1-12 mo) | D10W | 2-5 mL/kg IV

Child (>1 yr) | D25W | 2 mL/kg IV (max 50 mL)

Adolescent | D50W | 1 mL/kg IV (max 50 mL)

Dextrose 10% (Neonate): 2-5 mL/kg IV — Max 25 mL initial. Never D25/D50 in neonates — hyperosmolar, causes venous sclerosis.

Dextrose 10% (Infant 1-12mo): 2-5 mL/kg IV — Never D25/D50 in young infants — hyperosmolar, causes venous sclerosis.

Dextrose 25% (Child >1yr): 2 mL/kg IV — Max 50 mL.

Dextrose 50% (Adolescent): 1 mL/kg IV — Max 50 mL.

Age-banded dextrose bolus: (computed)

No IV access: Glucagon 0.5 mg IM if <25 kg, 1 mg IM if ≥25 kg. Works only if glycogen stores present (fails in starvation, alcohol, sulfonylurea). Onset 5-15 min.

Glucagon: 0.5 mg IM — No IV access, weight <25 kg.

Glucagon: 1 mg IM — No IV access, weight ≥25 kg.

Start D10 IVF at maintenance + 50% (or GIR 6-8 mg/kg/min initially). Recheck glucose q15-30 min until stable >70 mg/dL, then q1-2h.

  • Sepsis — most common cause in the unwell child; full sepsis workup
  • Fasting / decreased intake — infants vulnerable; check feeding history
  • Insulin/sulfonylurea ingestion (accidental or Munchausen) — admit, prolonged monitoring; sulfonylurea toxicity → octreotide
  • Adrenal insufficiency (low cortisol, high ACTH; bronze skin if Addison) — give hydrocortisone 25 mg IV stress dose
  • Inborn errors of metabolism (especially recurrent or family history) — send critical sample before dextrose
  • Alcohol or salicylate ingestion
  • Hypoadrenalism / hypopituitarism

Critical sample (before dextrose if IEM/hypopituitarism suspected)

Plasma glucose, insulin, C-peptide, β-hydroxybutyrate, cortisol, GH, ACTH, lactate, ammonia, free fatty acids. Urine: ketones, reducing substances, organic acids. Saves you from a missed metabolic diagnosis.

11 · Disposition

Symptomatic severe disturbance (seizure, AMS, herniation, arrhythmia), hypertonic saline infusion required, hyperkalemia with ECG changes after initial treatment, AKI requiring dialysis, adrenal crisis/suspected CAH, persistent hypoglycemia needing high-GIR drip (>10 mg/kg/min). Continuous cardiac monitoring, hourly neuro checks, serial labs q2-4h.

Mild-moderate disturbance, asymptomatic or symptoms resolved, stable on oral/low-rate IV replacement, telemetry if K/Ca abnormality with prior ECG changes. Serial labs q6-8h. Escalate to PICU for worsening symptoms/labs or new arrhythmia/AMS.

Mild, asymptomatic, single disturbance with clear reversible cause, labs normalized/trending, tolerating PO, reliable family, confirmed PCP follow-up 24-48h. Do NOT discharge: any neonate with unclear-cause electrolyte abnormality, any prior seizure/AMS this episode, unclear etiology.
