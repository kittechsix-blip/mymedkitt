Torsades de Pointes: Magnesium, Not Amiodarone

Adults in the ED with polymorphic VT on a prolonged-QT background (Torsades de Pointes). Excludes polymorphic VT with a NORMAL QT (that is ischemic — treat with amiodarone/revascularization, the opposite of this pathway) and stable non-arrhythmic QT prolongation.

⚠ Do NOT give amiodarone, procainamide, sotalol, or any Class IA/III antiarrhythmic. They prolong QT and WILL worsen TdP — this is the most common lethal error in TdP management.
⚠ Pulseless TdP → unsynchronized defibrillation plus Magnesium 4 g IV push, with standard ACLS otherwise.
⚠ Give magnesium to EVERY TdP patient regardless of serum Mg level — serum Mg does not reflect intracellular stores, and it suppresses the early afterdepolarizations that drive TdP.
⚠ Congenital LQTS is treated OPPOSITELY: beta-blockers are first-line and isoproterenol is CONTRAINDICATED. In acquired TdP, isoproterenol and overdrive pacing are used and beta-blockers can worsen the pause-dependent mechanism.

1 · Recognize TdP

What it is
Torsades de Pointes is polymorphic ventricular tachycardia on a background of a prolonged QT interval — QRS amplitude waxes and wanes, "twisting" around the isoelectric line, typically 150–300 bpm and often self-terminating. It is classically initiated by a short–long–short sequence.[1]
The number that matters
A QTc >500 ms carries a 2–3× increased TdP risk. Measure it on a clean 12-lead the moment the patient is stable.[5]

Critical distinction: TdP (prolonged QT) requires the opposite treatment from polymorphic VT with a *normal* QT. Normal-QT polymorphic VT is ischemic — treat with amiodarone and revascularization. In TdP, amiodarone is harmful.[2]

2 · What to do now

Set the patient's state with the toggles above (Pulseless, Unstable, Congenital LQTS suspected). Exactly one hemodynamic instruction shows below; pulseless overrides unstable, which overrides stable. A congenital overlay appears on top when relevant.

Pulseless TdP → DEFIBRILLATE
Unsynchronized defibrillation at standard ACLS energy. Give Magnesium Sulfate 4 g IV push (rapid is acceptable in arrest). Run standard ACLS (CPR, epinephrine) and repeat defibrillation as needed. Do NOT give amiodarone. After ROSC → magnesium protocol below.[1]
Unstable TdP → SYNCHRONIZED CARDIOVERSION
Synchronized cardioversion at standard energy. Simultaneously give Magnesium Sulfate 4 g IV over 20 min (faster if deteriorating), stop all QT-prolonging drugs, and draw STAT K⁺, Mg, Ca, iCa. Do NOT give amiodarone or procainamide.[1]
Stable TdP → MAGNESIUM + WORK-UP
Start Magnesium Sulfate 4 g IV over 20 min immediately, regardless of serum Mg level. Stop all QT-prolonging meds, draw STAT electrolytes (K⁺, Mg, Ca, iCa, phosphate), place continuous monitoring, and get a 12-lead for baseline QTc. Then determine acquired vs congenital.[1]
⚠️ Congenital LQTS overlay — reverse two things
If congenital LQTS is suspected: beta-blocker (esmolol or propranolol) is first-line, and isoproterenol is CONTRAINDICATED (it paradoxically prolongs QT here). Magnesium dosing is unchanged. Avoid ALL sympathomimetics.[7]

8 · Disposition & risk factors

All TdP → ICU
Every TdP patient needs ICU admission with continuous telemetry. Continue the Mg infusion (24 h total), recheck electrolytes q2–4h, and get Cardiology for QTc monitoring and med review. Downgrade only when QTc <480 ms for 24 h, electrolytes are stable, and the offending agent has cleared.[4]
Risk factors (risk is multiplicative)modifiable + fixed
Modifiable
Hypokalemia (most important), hypomagnesemia, hypocalcemia, bradycardia, QT-prolonging drugs (especially several at once), CYP3A4/2D6 inhibitor + QT substrate, IV administration and rapid infusion of QT drugs.
Non-modifiable
Female sex, age >65, congenital LQTS, structural heart disease (LVH, CHF with reduced EF), hepatic/renal impairment. The classic scenario is drug + electrolyte + structural heart disease stacking together.
