Emergency department patients with suspected or confirmed ciguatera fish poisoning after reef fish ingestion (typically within 72 hours). Covers the full acute severity spectrum — GI-only, neurological, and cardiovascular presentations — through disposition and chronic post-ciguatera syndrome. Ciguatera is a clinical diagnosis; no rapid confirmatory lab test exists.

⚠ No rapid confirmatory lab test — ciguatera is diagnosed clinically from exposure history and exam.
⚠ AVOID ipecac and gastric lavage for GI decontamination.
⚠ AVOID opiates, barbiturates, beta-blockers, and calcium channel blockers — these worsen ciguatera's GI, neurologic, or cardiovascular effects.
⚠ IV mannitol evidence is mixed: Schnorf 2002 RCT showed no benefit over saline, while Palafox 1988 case series reported benefit — best evidence is within 72 hours of ingestion.
⚠ Pregnancy is one of the listed admission criteria for inpatient care.

Recognition & diagnosis

Ciguatera is the most common nonbacterial seafood poisoning worldwide (50,000-500,000 cases/year), caused by ciguatoxin from dinoflagellates that bioaccumulate in reef fish. The toxin is heat-stable (cooking does not destroy it), lipid-soluble, and has no taste, smell, or visual indicator. There is no rapid lab test — this is a clinical diagnosis.

  • GI symptoms appear early; neurological symptoms are the characteristic feature
  • Pathognomonic: HOT-COLD REVERSAL (cold feels burning, hot feels cold)
  • Cardiovascular instability in 20-40% of cases
  • Larger fish (>5-6 lbs) accumulate more toxin
  • Endemic to Caribbean, Pacific Islands, Hawaii, Florida Keys — but can occur anywhere contaminated fish is shipped

Hot-cold reversal is essentially pathognomonic for ciguatera — present in ~80% when specifically asked, and it is the single best feature distinguishing ciguatera from scombroid, shellfish poisoning, and GBS.

▶ What to do now

Severe neuro/CNS involvement → ICU
Ataxia, motor weakness, altered mental status, or seizures indicate severe poisoning. Admit to ICU/monitored bed, assess airway (intubate if respiratory muscle weakness), continuous cardiac monitoring, and consider IV mannitol 1 g/kg over 30-45 min.
Multi-system involvement → ICU
GI + neurological + cardiovascular symptoms together (or combined bradycardia and hypotension) require ICU admission with continuous cardiac monitoring, large-bore IV access, and airway assessment. Mortality is <1% but severe cases can progress rapidly in the first 24 hours.
Bradycardia (HR <50)
Most common cardiovascular manifestation. Symptomatic bradycardia: atropine 0.5-1 mg IV push, may repeat q3-5min to max 3 mg. AVOID beta-blockers and calcium channel blockers — they worsen bradycardia.
Hypotension (SBP <90)
First-line: NS/LR 20 mL/kg bolus over 30 min, repeat as needed. Norepinephrine if shock persists after fluids. Rule out other causes (sepsis, bleeding) and check for concurrent bradycardia.
Chest pain / palpitations, no instability
Obtain 12-lead ECG and troponin — ciguatoxin can cause direct myocardial injury and QT prolongation. Observe with telemetry; admit if ECG abnormal or troponin elevated.
Moderate: hot-cold reversal
Classic ciguatera presentation. Consider IV mannitol if within 72 hours. Amitriptyline 25-50 mg daily and gabapentin 300-400 mg TID for neuropathic pain. Recovery is often prolonged (weeks to months).
Mild: paresthesias only
Perioral and distal paresthesias without motor involvement. Supportive care; amitriptyline 25 mg PO daily if needed. Usually resolves within 1-2 weeks — monitor for progression to hot-cold reversal, weakness, or cardiac symptoms.
GI-predominant, early presentation
IV fluids for dehydration and antiemetics. Ask specifically about perioral tingling, temperature-perception changes, and weakness — neurological symptoms often evolve over the next 12-24 hours.

Mechanism
Ciguatoxin opens sodium channels, causing vagal stimulation to predominate alongside direct cardiac ion channel effects. Most bradycardia resolves within 2-5 days.

Severe / multi-system pathway

Multi-system involvement (all three symptom systems, or combined bradycardia + hypotension) requires ICU-level care. Immediate interventions: continuous cardiac monitoring, large-bore IV access, airway assessment. Mortality is <1% but severe cases can progress rapidly in the first 24 hours.

  • Bradycardia: atropine 0.5-1 mg IV, repeat q3-5min to max 3 mg; transcutaneous pacing if refractory
  • Hypotension: NS/LR 20 mL/kg bolus; norepinephrine if persistent shock
  • Consider IV mannitol 1 g/kg over 30-45 min if within 72 hours of ingestion
  • Frequent neuro checks, seizure precautions, respiratory status (may need intubation)
  • ECG (assess for bradycardia, QT prolongation)
  • Basic metabolic panel
  • Consider LP if meningitis in differential
  • MRI brain if symptoms atypical
  • Neurology consultation if motor weakness or altered mental status

Management algorithm

Ciguatera Fish Poisoning — Most common nonbacterial seafood poisoning worldwide — heat-stable toxin, no rapid lab test, clinical diagnosis.

Exposure history? — Symptoms typically begin 15 min to 6 hours after ingestion, but can be delayed up to 72 hours.

Unknown fish — assess risk by species/source — Highest risk: barracuda, moray eel, amberjack. Fish >5-6 lbs accumulate more toxin. Treat as presumptive ciguatera if tropical reef fish source with compatible symptoms.

No fish history — consider differential — Scombroid, shellfish poisoning, tetrodotoxin, GBS, botulism. Hot-cold reversal is essentially pathognomonic for ciguatera.

Symptom pattern? — GI symptoms first (1-2h), neurological follow (6-24h), cardiovascular in 20-40% of cases.

GI only: IV fluids, antiemetics — Nausea/vomiting (onset 1-2h), watery diarrhea, cramping. Ask about perioral tingling, temperature changes, weakness — signs of evolving neuro involvement.

Neurological severity? — Ask specifically about hot-cold reversal — patients may not volunteer this pathognomonic symptom.

Cardiovascular finding? — Cardiovascular toxicity in 20-40% of cases; bradycardia most common.

Severe: multi-system → ICU — Continuous cardiac monitoring, large-bore IV, airway assessment. Atropine for bradycardia, fluids for hypotension, consider mannitol. Mortality <1% but can progress rapidly in first 24 hours.

Mild: paresthesias — supportive care — Perioral/distal paresthesias, mild headache, metallic taste. Amitriptyline 25 mg PO daily if needed. Usually resolves in 1-2 weeks.

Moderate: hot-cold reversal — Hot-cold reversal in ~80% if asked. Consider IV mannitol within 72h. Amitriptyline 25-50 mg + gabapentin 300-400 mg TID.

Severe: motor/CNS → ICU — Ataxia, weakness, AMS, seizures. Continuous cardiac monitoring, frequent neuro checks, consider mannitol, airway assessment. Neurology consult if motor weakness or AMS.

Bradycardia: atropine — Symptomatic (HR<40 or symptoms): atropine 0.5-1 mg IV, repeat q3-5min to max 3mg. AVOID beta-blockers and CCBs.

Hypotension: IV fluids — NS/LR 20 mL/kg bolus first-line. Norepinephrine if refractory shock. Serial BPs q15min.

Mannitol 1.0 g/kg IV — Over 30-45 min, may repeat in 3-4h if partial response, max 2 doses/24h. Keep osmolality <320, replace urine output, strict I/Os.

Disposition? — Assess for admission criteria: stability, symptom severity, ability to tolerate PO, follow-up reliability.

Discharge home — Normal vitals, no severe neuro/CV symptoms, tolerating PO. Avoid ALL fish, alcohol, caffeine, nuts, chocolate for 6+ months. PCP follow-up 48-72h.
