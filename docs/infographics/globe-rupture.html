Open Globe Injury: Shield, Protect, Prophylax, Repair

Adults and children with suspected penetrating, perforating, or ruptured globe injury (± intraocular foreign body) in the ED. Excludes isolated closed-globe injuries once open globe is excluded, and post-operative/post-surgical globe complications.

⚠ Open globe = ophthalmic emergency. Once suspected, protect the eye with a rigid shield immediately — do NOT manipulate the eye, remove any foreign body, or check intraocular pressure (tonometry).
⚠ If obvious rupture is seen (extruding uveal tissue/vitreous, teardrop or peaked pupil), STOP examining immediately and shield — do not continue exam maneuvers.
⚠ Contraindicated in open globe: tonometry, forced lid retraction, an eye patch (compression), ocular ultrasound (probe pressure), and MRI if any metallic IOFB is suspected — the magnet can mobilize metal and destroy the eye.
⚠ Antiemetics are critical, not optional — vomiting/retching raises IOP dramatically and can extrude globe contents. Give ondansetron 4-8 mg IV (metoclopramide 10 mg IV second-line).
⚠ RSI anesthesia: avoid Succinylcholine — raises IOP up to 10 mmHg; use rocuronium instead. Ketamine also raises IOP via extraocular muscle contraction. Retrobulbar/peribulbar regional blocks are contraindicated — injection pressure can expulse globe contents.
⚠ All open globe injuries are admitted for emergent OR repair — keep NPO and give IV antibiotics (vancomycin + ceftazidime) promptly; delay increases endophthalmitis and sympathetic ophthalmia risk.

What open globe means

Teardrop pupil — why it happenspathophysiology + rupture hotspots

Aqueous leaking out creates a pressure gradient that pulls the iris toward the corneal/scleral defect — the pupil "points" at the injury site. Iris may prolapse through the wound. In penetrating injury, the teardrop points toward the laceration where contents are escaping.[1][2]

Rupture hotspot: sclera is thinnest at the rectus muscle insertions (equatorial zone) — where blunt ruptures commonly occur.

Seidel test — detecting aqueous leaktechnique + interpretation

Apply concentrated fluorescein to the suspected wound and examine under cobalt blue light. Positive: dark orange dye turns bright green as aqueous dilutes it, with streaming. Negative: dye stays orange, no streaming.[1][3]

Contraindicated if obvious rupture
Do not perform Seidel testing if the globe is obviously ruptured. A negative Seidel does NOT rule out open globe — the wound may be self-sealed, and posterior ruptures will not show a positive Seidel.

3 · Imaging and IOFB

CT orbits — first-line
Non-contrast, thin cuts (1-1.5 mm), axial/coronal/sagittal reformats. Detects metal >0.06 mm, glass >1.8 mm, stone well; may MISS wood, plastic, small vegetable matter. IOFB present in up to 40% of penetrating injuries.[1][2]
MRI absolutely contraindicated
If any suspicion of metallic foreign body, MRI is ABSOLUTELY CONTRAINDICATED — the magnet can mobilize metal and destroy the eye. Plain X-ray is obsolete. Avoid ultrasound in open globe (probe pressure); it can be used post-repair to assess the retina.[1][2]

5 · Consult and disposition

Emergent ophthalmology consultation on suspicion or diagnosis. Ideal repair window: 12-24 hours — delay increases endophthalmitis, choroidal hemorrhage, and sympathetic ophthalmia risk. If no ophthalmology available: transfer to an OR-capable facility, keep shield in place, continue antibiotics, document all findings.[1][2]

7 · Do NOT

Critical pitfalls

**Do NOT check intraocular pressure (tonometry)** or force lid retraction — both put pressure directly on an already-compromised globe.

**Do NOT patch the eye or perform ocular ultrasound** — compression and probe pressure both risk extruding globe contents.

**Do NOT order MRI** if there is any suspicion of a metallic intraocular foreign body — the magnet can mobilize metal and destroy the eye.

**Do NOT use succinylcholine for RSI** — raises IOP up to 10 mmHg. Use rocuronium instead.

**Do NOT withhold antiemetics** — vomiting/Valsalva raises IOP dramatically and can extrude globe contents.

**Do NOT rely on a negative Seidel test** to exclude open globe — the wound may be self-sealed, and posterior ruptures will not show a positive Seidel.
