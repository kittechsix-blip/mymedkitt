Atrial Fibrillation with RVR — ED Management

Adults ≥18 in the ED with atrial fibrillation with rapid ventricular response. Excludes pregnancy and pediatrics. WPW / pre-excitation and hemodynamic instability route to synchronized cardioversion, not AV-nodal blockade. Decompensated HFrEF excludes calcium channel blockers.

⚠ EF ≤40% = CCBs CONTRAINDICATED (Class 3: Harm) — must know EF before giving diltiazem/verapamil
⚠ WPW+AFib = life-threatening — AV nodal blockers ABSOLUTELY CONTRAINDICATED (may cause VF)
⚠ CCBs CONTRAINDICATED in HFrEF — use metoprolol, digoxin (positive inotropy), or amiodarone; IV Mg as adjunct
⚠ Cardioversion if unstable (shock, ischemia, severe CHF, severe dyspnea with RVR)
⚠ Diltiazem 0.25 mg/kg IV (15-25mg) over 2 min first-line for rate control
⚠ Check TSH + troponin in ALL patients - thyrotoxicosis and ACS are critical reversible causes

Initial assessment

Atrial fibrillation with rapid ventricular response is an irregularly irregular rhythm with a ventricular rate typically 120–180 bpm. Confirm with a 12-lead ECG and treat underlying precipitants alongside rate control.

  • Electrolyte abnormalities (K⁺, Mg²⁺)
  • Sepsis / infection
  • Pulmonary embolism
  • Thyrotoxicosis
  • Alcohol use / withdrawal
  • Pain / agitation, hypoxia / respiratory failure
  • Underlying cardiac disease (valvular, ischemic, HF)
Do not miss reversible causes
Check TSH + troponin in ALL patients — thyrotoxicosis and ACS are critical reversible causes.

▶ What to do now

UNSTABLE → Cardiovert now

WPW / pre-excited AF
AVOID all AV-nodal blockers (diltiazem, β-blockers, digoxin, adenosine) — they can accelerate conduction to VF. Use procainamide or electrical cardioversion.
Reduced EF (≤40%)
Avoid diltiazem / β-blocker (negative inotropes worsen HFrEF). Prefer amiodarone; digoxin is an option. Cardiovert if decompensating.
Stable, preserved EF → rate control
First-line: diltiazem 0.25 mg/kg IV (see dose below). Reassess in 15 min; second bolus 0.35 mg/kg if needed. A β-blocker (metoprolol / esmolol) is an alternative.

Unstable pathway — cardioversion

  1. Confirm synchronization is enabled on the defibrillator; apply pads anterior/lateral.
  2. Sedate: midazolam 3–5 mg IV bolus, additional 2 mg q2min PRN.
  3. Start at 200J biphasic (use maximal energy available); escalate if unsuccessful.

AF >48 hours
Do NOT electively cardiovert AF of >48 hours (or unknown duration) without a TEE excluding left atrial appendage thrombus, or ≥3 weeks of anticoagulation first.

First-line rate control

Beta-blockers (Class 1) and non-dihydropyridine CCBs are first-line for the stable patient with preserved EF. Do NOT combine a beta-blocker with a calcium channel blocker.

Diltiazem: 0.25 mg/kg IV bolus over 2 min IV — First-line CCB; ~90% rate control; onset within minutes. Contraindicated if EF ≤40% or HFrEF.

Diltiazem (second bolus): 0.35 mg/kg IV if inadequate response IV — Repeat bolus 15 min after the first if rate remains uncontrolled.

Metoprolol: 2.5–5 mg IV q5min, up to 15 mg total IV — Preferred in CAD; safe in COPD. Use lower doses in decompensated HF.

Esmolol: 500 mcg/kg IV loading dose IV — Ultra-short-acting; ideal when rapid titration is needed or hemodynamics uncertain.

HFrEF and WPW

Diltiazem and verapamil are Class 3: Harm in HFrEF — they worsen HF outcomes and increase mortality. Use metoprolol/esmolol, digoxin (positive inotropy), or amiodarone; IV magnesium as adjunct.

AV nodal blocking agents — beta-blockers, calcium channel blockers, digoxin, and IV amiodarone — are absolutely contraindicated. They may accelerate conduction through the accessory pathway and precipitate ventricular fibrillation.

Unstable pathway — cardioversion

  • Confirm synchronization is enabled on the defibrillator; apply pads anterior/lateral.
  • Sedate: midazolam 3–5 mg IV bolus, additional 2 mg q2min PRN.
  • Start at 200J biphasic (use maximal energy available); escalate if unsuccessful.
  • Post-conversion: amiodarone infusion to maintain sinus rhythm.

Anticoagulation

For known/chronic AF, score stroke risk with [CHA₂DS₂-VASc](#/calculator/cha2ds2vasc): score ≥2 (men) or ≥3 (women) → anticoagulate. DOACs ([apixaban](#/drug/apixaban/atrial fibrillation), [rivaroxaban](#/drug/rivaroxaban/atrial fibrillation)) are preferred over warfarin. New-onset AF in critical illness generally does NOT warrant anticoagulation — most revert to sinus as the illness resolves.

Do NOT electively cardiovert AF of >48 hours (or unknown duration) without a TEE excluding left atrial appendage thrombus, or ≥3 weeks of anticoagulation first.
