Cardiogenic Shock: Stabilize First, Phenotype, Escalate

Adults ≥18 in the ED/ICU with cardiogenic shock (inadequate perfusion from primary pump failure). Excludes pediatrics, primary distributive/hypovolemic/obstructive shock, and post-cardiotomy patients already on an established surgical protocol.

⚠ Time is myocardium. Recognize the SHOCK spiral: hypotension worsens coronary perfusion → more ischemia → more pump failure. Mortality is 40-50% — intervene early, do not wait for frank hypotension.
⚠ Push-dose epi while setting drips — avoid intubation. In the crashing patient (SCAI D-E), give push-dose pressors while infusions are prepared. Sedation + PPV will crash them further; if you must intubate, have push-dose epinephrine ready.
⚠ STEMI + shock = emergent cath — PCI is definitive. Do not delay the cath lab for additional workup. Avoid thrombolytics (inferior to PCI, contraindicated if MCS planned).
⚠ RV failure is a different animal: NO aggressive fluids. The RV cannot handle excess volume — give norepinephrine to maintain coronary perfusion, avoid nitrates in RV MI, and small 250 mL boluses only if clearly hypovolemic.
⚠ Avoid fluid boluses if POCUS shows B-lines or a hyperdynamic LV — most cardiogenic shock is cold-and-wet, not dry.
⚠ Avoid pure vasoconstrictors (phenylephrine, vasopressin) as monotherapy — they raise afterload without helping contractility.

2 · What to do right now

Crashing (SCAI D-E): stabilize first
Push-dose pressors while setting up infusions. Epinephrine 10-20 mcg IV push (1 mL of 10 mcg/mL); phenylephrine 100-200 mcg if pure vasodilation. Avoid intubation — if unavoidable, ketamine + push-dose epi ready. Call cardiology, CT surgery, ECMO. Do NOT sit in hypotensive shock while chasing the cause.[1]
AMI shock: activate the cath lab
PCI is definitive therapy — every minute of delay worsens outcomes. Stabilize hemodynamics, activate cath lab immediately, culprit-only PCI. Consider MCS (Impella/IABP) before or during PCI. Do NOT give thrombolytics if MCS is planned.[4]

No aggressive fluids. Small 250 mL boluses only if clearly hypovolemic, reassess. Norepinephrine) to hold coronary perfusion; dobutamine for inotropy (careful with milrinone — vasodilation). Reduce RV afterload (treat PE, optimize ventilation). Avoid intubation — PPV worsens RV failure; no nitrates in RV MI.[1]
Classic cold-and-wet cardiogenic shock
Norepinephrine first-line to MAP >65, then add dobutamine if cardiac index stays low. Diagnose the etiology in parallel with POCUS. Escalate to MCS early if pressor requirements climb or lactate will not clear.[1]

Critical pitfalls5 items
Do NOT let them sit in hypotensive shock while chasing the cause — stabilize first with push-dose pressors.
Do NOT give aggressive fluids in RV failure or a wet LV — B-lines or hyperdynamic LV means the tank is full.
