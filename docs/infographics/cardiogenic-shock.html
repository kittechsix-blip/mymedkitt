Cardiogenic Shock: Phenotype, Pressors, MCS

Adults in the ED with suspected or confirmed cardiogenic shock (primary cardiac pump failure causing inadequate tissue perfusion). Covers AMI, ADHF, and RV-failure phenotypes through pressor/inotrope selection and mechanical circulatory support (MCS) decision-making. Excludes pediatrics. Does not replace institutional VAD/ECMO team protocols.

⚠ RV failure — do NOT give aggressive fluids. The RV is preload-dependent but cannot handle excess volume. Use small 250 mL boluses only if clearly hypovolemic, then reassess.
⚠ RV MI — do NOT give nitrates. Preload-dependent physiology; nitrates can cause profound, refractory hypotension.
⚠ AMI + cardiogenic shock — do NOT delay cath lab activation for additional workup. PCI is definitive therapy; door-to-balloon time matters even more in shock.
⚠ Avoid pure vasoconstrictors (phenylephrine, vasopressin) as monotherapy — they increase afterload without improving contractility in cardiogenic shock.
⚠ Avoid intubation if possible in the crashing patient — sedation and positive-pressure ventilation can worsen hemodynamics. If intubation is unavoidable, use ketamine with a push-dose pressor ready.

6 · One-glance pathway

Cardiogenic shock: SHOCK spiral — Inadequate tissue perfusion from primary cardiac dysfunction. Hypotension → coronary hypoperfusion → worsening ischemia → further pump failure. Mortality 40-50% even with modern therapy.

SCAI shock stage? — SBP <90/MAP <65, cold/mottled skin, altered mental status, oliguria, elevated lactate. Stage A (at risk) through E (extremis) determine urgency.

Crashing (D-E): push-dose pressors, avoid intubation — Push-dose epinephrine/phenylephrine while setting up infusions. Avoid intubation if possible. Call for help; consider VA-ECMO if refractory.

At risk (A): serial lactates, low threshold pressors — Large anterior STEMI, EF <30%, recent high-risk PCI, acute myocarditis. Serial lactate q2-4h, continuous BP monitoring, POCUS. Do NOT wait for frank hypotension.

Identify the etiology — AMI, ADHF, RV failure, or other/mixed — POCUS is essential, do it now. Treatment differs by cause.

AMI + shock: emergent cath lab — STEMI+shock = emergent PCI. Stabilize hemodynamics, activate cath lab immediately, consider MCS before/during PCI. Avoid thrombolytics and excess fluids.

ADHF: warm=diurese, cold=inotropes — Warm shock (early): diuresis+inotropes, avoid pressors. Cold shock (late/severe): inotropes ± vasopressors, may need MCS. Identify the precipitant.

RV failure: NO aggressive fluids, norepi, no nitrates — RV is preload-dependent but can't handle excess volume. Small 250 mL boluses only if hypovolemic. Norepinephrine for coronary perfusion. If RV MI: no nitrates.

POCUS: LV, RV, IVC, B-lines — Cardiac power output (CPO) = MAP × CO / 451. CPO <0.6W = severe cardiogenic shock. Consider PA catheter in refractory shock (CI <2.2, PCWP >18 = classic).

Forrester phenotype: wet/dry, warm/cold? — Cold = cool extremities, delayed cap refill, mottling. Wet = JVD, rales, edema, B-lines. Most cardiogenic shock is cold and wet.

Cold & dry: 250 mL challenge, reassess — Uncommon in true cardiogenic shock — consider RV infarct, hypovolemia + poor cardiac function, or post-diuresis overshoot. Do NOT over-resuscitate.

Warm & wet: diurese, vasodilators if BP OK — Decompensated HF, not yet shock. Aggressive diuresis, vasodilators if BP tolerates. Monitor closely for progression to cold and wet.

Cold & wet: art line, central line, Foley, labs — Stabilize first, diagnose second. Arterial line (cuff pressures unreliable), central line, Foley, lactate/troponin/BNP, ECG+POCUS immediately. HFNC preferred over intubation.

Norepinephrine first-line + inotrope if CI low — Norepinephrine 5-30 mcg/min first-line. Add dobutamine, milrinone, or epinephrine if cardiac index remains low. Avoid pure vasoconstrictors as monotherapy.

Responding to pressors/inotropes? — Escalating pressor requirements, worsening lactate despite pressors, or multi-organ dysfunction signal treatment failure → consider MCS.

Optimize: preload, afterload, rhythm, metabolic — Avoid excess fluids, treat arrhythmias aggressively, correct acidosis/electrolytes, treat anemia (Hgb <7-8 impairs O2 delivery).

Stable on medical therapy, or need MCS? — Early MCS indicators: escalating pressors, lactate not clearing, SCAI C-D despite medical therapy, CPO <0.6W, need for PCI but too unstable.

MCS: IABP / Impella / VA-ECMO — Match device to need: IABP ~0.5 L/min, Impella CP 3-4 L/min, Impella 5.0/5.5 5+ L/min, VA-ECMO full support. ECMO is a bridge, not a destination.

Disposition: ICU always — All cardiogenic shock = ICU (CCU/CICU). Emergent cath lab if AMI. Transfer if MCS/ECMO not available locally — communicate early.
