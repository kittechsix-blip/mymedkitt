Pediatric Hypertensive Emergency: Confirm, Classify, Control Slowly

Children and adolescents with severe hypertension in the ED. Excludes routine outpatient BP screening and adult hypertensive emergency protocols (different weight-based dosing and thresholds).

⚠ Never use sublingual nifedipine in pediatric hypertensive emergency or urgency — unpredictable absorption causes dangerous, uncontrolled BP drops.[1][3][6]
⚠ The 25% rule: reduce BP by ≤25% of planned BP drop in the first 8 hours, another 25% over hours 8-16, and reach target over 24-48 hours total. Rapid normalization risks watershed cerebral infarction and worsens PRES.[1][3][6]
⚠ ANY end-organ damage (encephalopathy, seizure, AKI, LV failure, retinopathy) upgrades severe HTN to a hypertensive emergency requiring IV titratable therapy in the PICU.[2][3]
⚠ Avoid ACE inhibitors acutely in undifferentiated severe pediatric HTN — risk of profound BP drop if renovascular HTN is the cause; avoid furosemide as primary therapy unless volume overload is present.[3]
⚠ Nitroprusside is last-line only — cyanide toxicity risk beyond 24h or in renal failure, and it raises intracranial pressure.[2][3][6]
⚠ Confirm the BP before treating — a cuff too small reads falsely HIGH. Do NOT delay treatment for confirmation if end-organ damage signs (seizure, AMS, focal neuro deficit, pulmonary edema) are already present — treat empirically.[1][3]

1 · Recognize & classify

Category (AAP 2017)Definition
Normal BPSBP/DBP <90th %ile for age/sex/height
Elevated BP90th to <95th %ile, or 120/80 to <95th %ile in adolescents ≥13y
Stage 1 HTN95th %ile to <95th %ile + 12 mmHg, or 130/80 to 139/89 (≥13y)
Stage 2 HTN≥95th %ile + 12 mmHg, or ≥140/90 (≥13y)
Urgency vs. emergency
Hypertensive urgency: severe HTN (typically Stage 2 + significant elevation) with NO end-organ damage. Hypertensive emergency: severe HTN WITH acute end-organ damage (encephalopathy, seizure, retinopathy, AKI, LV failure).[2][3]
ED red-flag thresholds
BP >99th %ile + 5 mmHg, OR adolescent SBP >180 or DBP >120, OR any BP elevation with symptoms (headache, vomiting, vision change, seizure, altered mental status).[2]

Most pediatric severe HTN is secondary (renal, cardiac, endocrine) — unlike adults. Rapid lowering risks watershed cerebral infarction and posterior reversible encephalopathy syndrome (PRES); pediatric cerebral autoregulation cannot tolerate a fast drop.[1][3][4]

Confirm the BP before treating
Cuff size is the #1 source of error — bladder width must be 40% of mid-arm circumference; too small reads falsely HIGH. Use manual auscultation, check BOTH arms, and compare to a leg if coarctation is suspected. Do NOT delay treatment for confirmation if end-organ damage signs (seizure, AMS, focal neuro deficit, pulmonary edema) are already present — treat empirically.[1][3]

Hypertensive emergency — IV titratable therapy, PICU
First-line: Nicardipine 0.5-3 mcg/kg/min IV infusion (start 0.5, titrate by 0.5 q5min). Alternatives: Labetalol bolus 0.2-1 mg/kg IV (max 40 mg/dose) → infusion 0.25-3 mg/kg/h; Clevidipine 0.5-3.5 mcg/kg/min IV. Reduce ≤25% of planned BP drop in the first 8 hours.[2][3][6]
Hypertensive urgency — oral agent, gradual reduction
Pick one oral agent based on suspected etiology: Amlodipine 0.1-0.3 mg/kg/day PO (max 10 mg/day), Labetalol 1-3 mg/kg/day PO divided BID-TID, or isradipine 0.05-0.1 mg/kg/dose PO q6-8h. Recheck BP at 1-2h, 4h, 8h; consult nephrology/cardiology before discharge.[1][2][6]

4 · Monitoring & secondary-cause screen

Signs of TOO RAPID reduction
New confusion or somnolence, pallor/diaphoresis, new focal neuro deficit, decreased urine output (UOP <0.5 mL/kg/h suggests over-shoot or worsening AKI).[3][6]
Signs of INADEQUATE reduction
Persistent severe HTN at 1-2h, worsening symptoms, new end-organ damage signs — escalate therapy.[3][6]

Transition to oral once BP stable 6-12h on a steady drip rate; overlap the oral agent 4-6h before weaning the drip.[3][6]

5 · The 25% rule

The single most important treatment principle
Reduce ≤25% of planned BP drop in the FIRST 8 hours. Reduce the next 25% over hours 8-16. Achieve target BP over 24-48 hours total. Target <95th %ile for age/sex/height — avoid dropping below the 90th %ile acutely.[1][3][6]

Example (12-year-old, BP 200/130, planned target 130/85): planned MAP drop ~153→~100 = 53 mmHg. First 8h: ≤25% = ~13 mmHg → target MAP ~140. Next 8-16h: another ~13 mmHg → MAP ~127. 24-48h: gradual to target MAP ~100.[3][6]
