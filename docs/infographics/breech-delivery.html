Breech Delivery: Recognition, Classification, and Vaginal Delivery Maneuvers

Term/near-term singleton breech presentation in the ED — recognition, classification, the C-section-vs-vaginal decision, and vaginal breech delivery maneuvers when delivery is imminent. Excludes elective antepartum management (external cephalic version, scheduled C-section counseling), multifetal gestation, and preterm breech <34 weeks.

⚠ Breech in the ED is an obstetric emergency — call OB, Pediatrics/NICU, and Anesthesia STAT even if you plan a vaginal delivery; OR backup must be available.
⚠ Transverse lie: vaginal delivery is not possible — any attempt risks uterine rupture and fetal death. C-section is the only safe option.
⚠ Footling breech carries the highest cord-prolapse risk (15–20%) — check for a pulsatile cord immediately on every breech exam.
⚠ HANDS OFF THE BREECH — do NOT touch the fetus until the scapulae are visible. Traction on the body extends the fetal head and causes head entrapment.
⚠ Never grip the fetal abdomen during any breech maneuver — grip only the bony pelvis (iliac crests/sacrum). Abdominal pressure can damage the spleen, kidneys, or adrenals.
⚠ Mauriceau-Smellie-Veit: the mouth-finger goes on the MAXILLA, never the mandible — pulling on the mandible can dislocate the jaw.
⚠ Head entrapment causes fetal death in 5–10 minutes without rescue — give tocolysis and move immediately to emergent OR C-section rescue; do not cycle through multiple vaginal maneuvers without OR backup already mobilized.
⚠ Never give oxytocin IV push — can cause profound maternal hypotension; infuse only per the diluted regimen.

4 · Preparation & cord check

  • Sterile gloves x2, towels, drapes, warm saline, cord clamps x2, sterile scissors
  • Neonatal warmer ON and prewarming; full Neonatal Resuscitation setup
  • Minimum 4 personnel: delivering provider, assistant, nurse for mother, nurse/provider for baby

Maternal positioning: dorsal lithotomy, buttocks at the very edge of the bed, empty bladder (straight cath), IV x2 large-bore, type and screen blood available.

Episiotomy: routine episiotomy is NOT recommended. Selective only if the perineum is rigid or additional room is needed after the umbilicus delivers.

Perform a sterile vaginal exam immediately to rule out cord prolapse. A pulsatile cord at or below the presenting part = cord prolapse, a separate emergency (see Cord Emergencies consult).

5 · Vaginal breech — body delivery

The most important rule: hands off
Do NOT touch the fetus until the scapulae are visible at the introitus. Traction causes the fetal head to extend, which can cause head entrapment. Coach the mother: strong, sustained pushes with each contraction.
  1. Buttocks crown and deliver — sacrum should be anterior
  2. Body delivers to the umbilicus via maternal pushing
  3. Wrap the delivered body in a warm, moist sterile towel
  4. Watch for the back rotating posteriorly — gently rotate so the back is anterior
  5. Watch for nuchal arms (an arm crossing the face)
Grip the bony pelvis only
Thumbs on sacrum, fingers wrapping the iliac crests — the standard grip for all breech manipulation, including Løvset. Never grip the abdomen (risk of splenic, renal, or adrenal injury).
  1. Introduce two fingers alongside one extended fetal leg
  2. Press behind the knee (popliteal fossa) — causes reflex knee flexion
  3. Sweep the foot laterally (abduct) and deliver the foot
  4. Repeat on the contralateral leg
  5. Do not pull on the legs

Bring a loop of cord down gently — do NOT clamp yet, the baby is still receiving oxygenated blood. Assess pulsation. From umbilicus to delivery of the head you have 5–10 minutes maximum before significant fetal hypoxia.

6 · Arms & aftercoming head

  1. Grip: thumbs on sacrum, fingers on iliac crests — never the abdomen
  2. Apply gentle downward traction while rotating the body 180° clockwise
  3. Sweep the now-anterior arm across the face/chest to deliver it
  4. Reverse rotate 180° counter-clockwise for the second arm
  5. Sweep the second arm out the same way
  1. Body straddles the operator's dominant forearm, face down
  2. Dominant hand: middle finger in the mouth on the MAXILLA (not mandible), index/ring fingers on the malar bones
  3. Non-dominant hand: middle finger on occiput, index/ring fingers on each scapula/shoulder
  4. Apply gentle downward traction while keeping the head flexed
  5. As the occiput emerges under the symphysis, elevate the body upward
  6. Assistant applies suprapubic pressure to aid head flexion if needed
  1. Grasp the fetal ankles with one hand
  2. Let the body hang vertically from the perineum for 30–60 sec until the occiput appears
  3. Arc the body upward over the maternal abdomen in a wide, slow sweep
  4. Assistant gives suprapubic pressure during the arc

2 · What to do now

Proceed with the Precipitous Delivery consult for standard stepwise vaginal delivery management.

Planned C-section reduces perinatal mortality/serious morbidity from 5.0% to 1.6% (RR 0.33, NNT ~30). Continuous monitoring, left lateral tilt, IV ×2, NPO, type & screen. Do not rupture membranes.

Elevate the presenting part manually, knee-chest/Trendelenburg position, Terbutaline 0.25 mg SQ ×1, continuous fetal monitoring, STAT C-section. Do not attempt to replace the cord — cover with warm saline gauze.
