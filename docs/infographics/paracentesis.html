Diagnostic & Therapeutic Paracentesis — ED Management

ED patients with ascites needing diagnostic evaluation (possible SBP, tense ascites, respiratory compromise, abdominal pain, fever, encephalopathy, AKI, or new/worsening decompensation) or therapeutic drainage for symptom relief.

⚠ No safe ultrasound pocket = no blind paracentesis — the key stop point.
⚠ Most coagulopathy in cirrhosis is NOT an absolute contraindication to paracentesis; routine INR/platelet correction is usually not needed.
⚠ PMN ≥250 cells/mm³ = treat as SBP even if culture is pending or negative — this is a time-sensitive infection.
⚠ Give albumin 6–8 g per liter of ascites removed when >5 L is drained; not required for taps <4–5 L.
⚠ Stop and reassess for severe pain, suspected bowel puncture, persistent bleeding, hypotension, expanding abdominal wall hematoma, catheter malfunction, or unexpected bloody/bilious/feculent return.

Critical actions (reference)

  • Perform diagnostic paracentesis when SBP or decompensated ascites is clinically possible.
  • Use ultrasound to confirm a bowel-free fluid pocket.
  • Send cell count/differential, culture bottles, albumin, and total protein.
  • Treat PMN ≥250 cells/mm³ as SBP.
  • Give albumin 6–8 g/L of ascites removed when >5 L drained (AASLD 2021).

Why tap? Indication & contraindications

Use this for ED evaluation or drainage of ascites, especially with SBP, tense ascites, respiratory compromise, abdominal pain, fever, encephalopathy, AKI, or new/worsening decompensation.

Diagnostic paracentesis is high-yield in hospitalized or ED patients with cirrhosis/ascites and possible infection or decompensation.

  • Diagnostic tap: fever, pain, encephalopathy, AKI, GI bleed, shock, leukocytosis, new ascites, or admission with ascites
  • Therapeutic drainage: tense ascites, dyspnea, pain, impaired mobility, or large-volume symptom relief
  • No clear indication: no accessible fluid, or symptoms/diagnostic question do not justify the procedure
Contraindications / cautions
Most coagulopathy in cirrhosis is not an absolute contraindication to paracentesis.

Avoid or escalate for: no safe pocket, overlying cellulitis, severe bowel distension at target site, noncooperative patient without safe sedation plan, pregnancy/organomegaly at path, or suspected surgical abdomen where delay is harmful.

Site, setup, and technique

  • Adequate fluid pocket
  • Bowel-free needle path
  • Abdominal wall vessels when possible
  • Distance from skin to fluid
  • Best patient position

Common sites: left lower quadrant or right lower quadrant lateral to rectus; avoid scars, vessels, distended bowel, and infected skin.

  1. Consent/time-out when feasible
  2. Sterile prep/drape
  3. Mark site after ultrasound
  4. Local lidocaine down to peritoneum
  5. Diagnostic kit or catheter-over-needle system ready
  6. Tubes/bottles ready before puncture
Lidocaine
Local infiltration down to peritoneum
Local injection
No epinephrine; use Z-track or oblique tract to reduce leak when feasible.
  1. Enter at marked site with steady negative pressure.
  2. Stop when fluid returns.
  3. For diagnostic tap, collect labs and remove needle.
  4. For therapeutic tap, advance catheter, remove needle, connect drainage.
  5. Monitor pain, vitals, leak, bleeding, and flow.
  6. Stop if severe pain, bowel contents, blood under pressure, hypotension, or unsafe catheter position.

Send fluid labs

  • Cell count with differential
  • Culture inoculated into blood culture bottles at bedside when possible
  • Albumin and total protein

Add selectively: glucose, LDH, cytology, triglycerides, amylase, AFB/fungal testing when clinically indicated.

Management algorithm

Diagnostic & Therapeutic Paracentesis — ED evaluation or drainage of ascites — SBP, tense ascites, respiratory compromise, abdominal pain, fever, encephalopathy, AKI, or new/worsening decompensation.

Why are you tapping? — Diagnostic tap for possible SBP/decompensation, therapeutic drainage for symptom relief, or no clear indication.

Stop / complications — No safe pocket, severe pain, bowel/blood return, or instability requires stopping and escalating to US-guided proceduralist, IR, surgery, or GI.

Contraindications / cautions — No safe pocket is the key stop point; routine INR/platelet correction is usually not needed.

Ultrasound site selection — Confirm fluid pocket, bowel-free path, vessels, depth, and patient position before needle entry.

Setup / local anesthesia — Consent, prep/drape, mark site, lidocaine to peritoneum, tubes/bottles ready.

Needle / catheter technique — Steady negative pressure to fluid; diagnostic sample or catheter drainage; stop for pain/bowel/blood/instability.

Send fluid labs — Cell count/differential, culture bottles, albumin, and total protein are core tests.

How do results interpret? — PMN ≥250 is SBP; large-volume taps need albumin planning; atypical fluid needs broader workup.

SBP treatment pathway — PMN ≥250: antibiotics, admission, albumin when indicated, assess sepsis/AKI. Time-sensitive infection — do not wait for culture positivity.

Large-volume paracentesis — Large-volume drainage needs monitoring and albumin replacement planning to prevent post-paracentesis circulatory dysfunction.

Atypical fluid / secondary cause — Low SAAG, hemorrhagic, chylous, malignant, pancreatic, or secondary peritonitis patterns need escalation.

Routine / negative initial result — A negative tap does not replace clinical disposition assessment.
