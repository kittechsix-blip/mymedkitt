Intralipid (ILE) — Lipid Emulsion Rescue Therapy

Adults and pediatrics with local anesthetic systemic toxicity (LAST) or refractory lipophilic-drug overdose (CCB, beta-blocker, TCA, or other lipophilic agents) in the ED, OR, or procedural setting. LAST is the only indication with high-quality evidence; other uses are rescue therapy after standard antidotes/ACLS have failed. Excludes hydrophilic drug toxicity (digoxin, lithium, salicylates), where ILE is not indicated.

⚠ ILE is rescue/adjunct therapy — NOT first-line for most overdoses. Use only for LAST, or after standard therapy fails for CCB/BB/TCA/other lipophilic toxicity.
⚠ LAST: give ILE at the first signs of severe toxicity — do NOT wait for cardiac arrest. Stop the local anesthetic injection immediately.
⚠ ILE 20% bolus 1.5 mL/kg IV (lean body mass) over 2-3 minutes; infusion 0.25 mL/kg/min (double to 0.5 mL/kg/min if unstable); maximum ~10 mL/kg in the first 30 minutes.
⚠ During LAST cardiac arrest, use reduced-dose epinephrine (<1 mcg/kg per dose, 10-100 mcg) — standard 1 mg ACLS dosing may worsen arrhythmias and reduce ILE efficacy.
⚠ Avoid vasopressin, calcium channel blockers, beta-blockers, lidocaine/local anesthetics, and propofol during LAST resuscitation.
⚠ LAST arrest may require prolonged CPR (60+ minutes) before ROSC — continue resuscitation longer than usual; ILE provides ongoing drug sequestration.
⚠ Refractory LAST arrest despite ILE + ACLS: evaluate early for VA-ECMO/ECPR — earlier cannulation is associated with better outcomes.

Overview & mechanism

Intravenous Lipid Emulsion (ILE) is rescue therapy for local anesthetic systemic toxicity (LAST) and other lipophilic drug overdoses. Standard formulation: 20% Lipid Emulsion. ILE is NOT first-line for most overdoses — use as an adjunct when conventional therapy fails. ASRA recommended for LAST since 2010; endorsed by AHA 2023.

Three proposed mechanisms: (1) Lipid sink — an expanded plasma lipid compartment draws lipophilic drugs out of tissue (heart, brain); best supported for agents with log P > 2. (2) Metabolic/energetic — fatty acids restore mitochondrial function impaired by local anesthetics. (3) Direct cardiotonic — increased intracellular calcium in cardiomyocytes, independent of drug sequestration.

Amitriptyline4.9Good
Verapamil3.8Good
Metoprolol1.9Modest
Atenolol0.2Poor

▶ What to do now

LAST → give ILE now, do not wait for arrest
Stop the local anesthetic injection immediately. Give ILE at the first signs of severe toxicity — seizures refractory to benzodiazepines, hemodynamic instability, any arrhythmia, or arrest. ILE is standard of care per ASRA/AHA, alongside airway management and modified-dose ACLS.
CCB overdose → ILE only after HIET fails
First give aggressive IV fluids, IV calcium, high-dose insulin-euglycemic therapy (HIET), and vasopressors. Add ILE only for shock refractory to these, or cardiac arrest. Highly lipophilic CCBs (verapamil, diltiazem, amlodipine) are better ILE candidates.
Beta-blocker overdose → ILE only after glucagon + HIET fail
First give IV fluids, glucagon, calcium, HIET, vasopressors, and transcutaneous pacing if bradycardic. Add ILE only for shock refractory to these. Best evidence is for propranolol (most lipophilic); hydrophilic agents (atenolol, sotalol) are unlikely to benefit.
TCA overdose → sodium bicarbonate first
Sodium bicarbonate remains first-line — target serum pH 7.50-7.55, QRS narrowing is the treatment endpoint. ILE is reasonable for cardiac arrest or hemodynamic collapse refractory to bicarbonate. Evidence is mixed: no proven benefit over bicarbonate as first-line.
Other lipophilic toxicity → case-by-case
Use ILE when the agent is highly lipophilic (log P > 2), life-threatening toxicity is present, and standard antidotes have failed or are unavailable — reported successes include diphenhydramine, bupropion, clomipramine, quetiapine, flecainide, and lamotrigine. Not indicated for hydrophilic drug toxicity (digoxin, lithium, salicylates).

ILE dosing protocol

Standard ILE protocol (ASRA 2020 checklist). Use 20% Lipid Emulsion (Intralipid, Liposyn).

Lipid Emulsion 20%
1.5 mL/kg IV bolus (lean body mass) over 2-3 minutes
IV
May repeat bolus ×1-2 at 5-minute intervals if unstable or recurrent instability.
Lipid Emulsion 20%
0.25 mL/kg/min continuous infusion
IV
Continue at least 10-15 minutes after hemodynamic stability achieved.
Lipid Emulsion 20% (doubled rate)
0.5 mL/kg/min if circulatory stability not attained
IV
Double the infusion rate for ongoing instability or recurrent arrest.
—Maximum dose, first 30 minutes

Administration details

  • Peripheral IV is acceptable (20% lipid emulsion is safe peripherally); central line preferred if available but do NOT delay for access
  • Large bore (18G or larger) recommended for rapid bolus
  • Draw up in large syringes (60 mL) for bolus; use IV tubing (not a pump) for the initial bolus
  • Switch to a pump for the infusion phase
  • Can run through the same line as crystalloids; avoid mixing with other medications
  • Check expiration date; protect from light if stored long-term

CPR modifications for LAST arrest

Avoid during LAST resuscitation
Vasopressin, calcium channel blockers, beta-blockers, lidocaine or local anesthetics, and propofol for seizures initially (lipid vehicle, cardiac depression). Standard-dose epinephrine (1 mg) may worsen arrhythmias and reduce ILE efficacy — use reduced dosing instead.
<1 mcg/kg (start 10-100 mcg IV)Reduced epinephrine dose ceiling in LAST arrest

LAST patients may have ROSC after 60+ minutes of CPR — continue longer than usual before terminating resuscitation. Standard joules for defibrillation; may require multiple shocks as ILE takes effect.

Monitoring, endpoints & lab interference

  • Continuous cardiac monitoring (rhythm, rate), arterial line if available, pulse oximetry, capnography
  • ROSC (in arrest), MAP >65, heart rate normalization, QRS narrowing, resolution of seizures/mentation
  • Repeat bolus for persistent instability, recurrent arrhythmia, or no response after 5 minutes

Stop when stable BP (MAP >65) and rhythm for 10+ minutes with resolved neurologic symptoms. Once stable 10-15 minutes, decrease infusion rate by 50%, then stop if stable for another 15 minutes. Monitor 4-6 hours minimum after discontinuation.

Recrudescence
Toxicity may return 6-12 hours after ILE is stopped as it is metabolized, particularly with long-acting agents (bupivacaine, extended-release CCB). Re-start ILE if symptoms recur; admit to a monitored setting.
  • Lipemia falsely affects electrolytes, troponin, liver enzymes, lipase, drug levels, glucose, and magnesium
  • Draw labs BEFORE ILE if possible; note ILE administration on lab orders; use point-of-care glucose
  • Labs typically normalize 12-24 hours after ILE stopped; trend values rather than absolute numbers
  • Triglycerides expected markedly elevated (>1000 mg/dL) — not a contraindication to additional ILE

Complications & special populations

  • Pulmonary: ARDS (rare, massive doses), fat embolism syndrome (very rare)
  • Metabolic: hypertriglyceridemia (expected, transient), acute pancreatitis (rare, dose-related), hyperglycemia
  • Hematologic: fat overload syndrome — reported only with prolonged infusions >12 mL/kg
  • Egg/soy allergy and severe hyperlipidemia (TG >400) are RELATIVE, not absolute, contraindications — proceed with ILE in life-threatening toxicity regardless

Pediatrics: same dosing (1.5 mL/kg bolus, 0.25 mL/kg/min infusion, same max); use a pump for precise dosing. Pregnancy: ILE indicated for maternal LAST — treat the mother first; no dose adjustment. Obesity: dose by ideal body weight/lean body mass to avoid overdosing. Renal/hepatic impairment: no acute-rescue dose adjustment; lipid clearance may be delayed.

Disposition

Stable / ROSC → ICU admission
Admit to ICU or monitored setting for a minimum of 4-6 hours observation. Repeat BMP, troponin, LFTs, and triglycerides in 12-24 hours. Report all LAST cases to lipidrescue.org. Discharge once 6+ hours without recurrence, normal mentation, stable hemodynamics, and labs clearing.
Vasopressor-dependent → ICU admission
Continue vasopressors and wean as tolerated; may continue low-dose ILE infusion if recurrent instability. Toxicology consultation recommended. Hemodialysis is NOT helpful for lipophilic drugs; evaluate ECMO if refractory.
Refractory → evaluate VA-ECMO/ECPR
Contact an ECMO center immediately if not on-site. Continue high-quality CPR and ILE (at infusion rate) while arranging cannulation. Earlier ECMO cannulation is associated with better outcomes; without ECMO, refractory LAST arrest has a poor prognosis.
