Penile Zipper Entrapment Release

ED patients (pediatric and adult) with foreskin or penile shaft skin entrapped in a zipper slider — most commonly uncircumcised boys and intoxicated adults. Covers red-flag triage, analgesia/sedation selection, four bedside release techniques, wound care, tetanus/antibiotic decisions, and urology consult criteria. Cases with urethral injury, significant skin avulsion, or vascular compromise are routed to urology/plastics without bedside manipulation.

⚠ Vascular compromise (dusky/cyanotic skin) → time-sensitive, urology now
⚠ First-line technique = mineral-oil lubrication + gentle retrograde unzip if skin intact
⚠ Do NOT force a stuck slider — switch to a destruction technique
⚠ Median-bar cut with bone/wire cutter is the most reliable destruction technique

Overview & Red-Flag Screen

Foreskin or penile shaft skin caught in a zipper slider — common in uncircumcised boys and intoxicated adults. Disproportionately distressing — calm the patient and provide analgesia first, then select a technique. Goal: release skin with the least trauma; most cases are managed at bedside without urology consult.

  • Slider — the moving piece with the median bar connecting front and back faceplates
  • Median bar — the bridge inside the slider; cutting it releases both faceplates
  • Teeth — the interlocking elements; separating teeth distal to slider frees skin
  • Faceplates — front and back metal plates of the slider
Screen for red flags BEFORE touching the zipper
Bleeding from urethral meatus — suggests urethral injury, do not manipulate.

Significant skin avulsion or devascularized flap — large skin loss already present, do not unzip blindly.

Vascular compromise (dusky/cyanotic skin) — strangulating injury, time-sensitive.

Any of these → urology/plastics now, no bedside manipulation. Otherwise, skin entrapped and viable → proceed to assessment.
  • Age and cooperation (pediatric vs adult)
  • Exact location of entrapped skin (foreskin most common)
  • Position of the slider along the teeth (proximal/distal to skin)
  • Skin viability — pink and intact vs dusky
  • Swelling around the entrapment site
  • Time since entrapment (>2 hr → more edema, harder to release)

▶ What to do now

STOP — Urology/Plastics now
Findings suggest urethral injury, significant skin loss, or vascular compromise. ED bedside release is contraindicated — do NOT attempt zipper release.
Adult, cooperative → topical/local anesthesia
Topical 2% lidocaine gel (or LET/LAT) for ~10 min if skin is intact; add small subdermal wheals of plain 1% lidocaine if still tender. Stay subdermal — do NOT inject into the shaft proper.
Adult, anxious/swollen → dorsal penile nerve block
10 mL total plain 1% lidocaine, 10 and 2 o'clock at the penile base, deep to Buck's fascia. NEVER add epinephrine — the dorsal penile arteries are end-arteries.
Pediatric, uncooperative → sedate before touching the zipper
A struggling child + a zipper near the genitalia is a setup for iatrogenic injury. Intranasal midazolam 0.2 mg/kg (max 10 mg) for a brief attempt with mineral oil; full procedural sedation if destruction technique needed or first attempt fails.
Mineral-oil lubrication + retrograde unzip (first-line)
Soak entrapped skin in mineral oil 10–15 min, then gentle retrograde unzip with steady low force. If it does not budge after 1–2 gentle attempts, stop and escalate to median-bar cut — do not force.
Median-bar cut (preferred destruction technique)
Stabilize the slider with a hemostat, slide one jaw of a bone/wire cutter under the median bar, cut in one decisive bite. Faceplates fall apart and skin lifts free. Protect skin with a tongue depressor between slider and skin.
Lateral teeth separation
Insert a small flat-head screwdriver (or hemostat tip) between front/back teeth rows distal to the slider and twist gently to pry apart. Only if the slider is NOT jammed — if jammed, there is no safe access point, switch to median-bar cut.
Slider destruction (side cuts)
Cut along each side (faceplate seam) of the slider, top and bottom, with the skin protected by a tongue depressor. More cuts and more skin-contact risk than median-bar cut — use when median bar is inaccessible.
Skin intact or partial-thickness tear → local wound care
Irrigate with sterile saline, apply petrolatum/bacitracin, loose non-adherent dressing. Heals by secondary intention — primary closure rarely needed. Then address tetanus + antibiotics.
Full-thickness laceration or significant skin loss → urology/plastics
Repair belongs with urology/plastics, not ED primary closure. Document mechanism, time entrapped, techniques attempted, current skin status, NV exam, and analgesia given for the handoff.

Wound care

  1. Irrigate gently with sterile saline (low-pressure, just enough to clear debris)
  2. Pat dry, apply petrolatum or bacitracin ointment
  3. Loose, non-adherent dressing — change daily
  4. Leave most superficial wounds open — primary closure rarely needed
  5. Counsel: twice-daily gentle cleansing starting next day, reapply ointment, avoid tight clothing/sexual activity until healed, loose underwear 24-48 hr

Return precautions & disposition

  • Worsening swelling, redness, or pain after 24-48 h
  • Purulent discharge or foul odor
  • Fever >100.4°F (38°C)
  • New blood in urine or trouble urinating
  • Skin color change (dusky, black, or pale)
  • Recurrent erection without stimulation (concern for vascular injury)
Discharge criteria
Zipper released, skin viable, pain controlled with oral analgesics, tetanus status addressed, patient/caregiver understands wound care and return precautions. Follow-up with PCP or urology in 3-5 days. Admit/observe: procedural sedation patients per institutional recovery criteria, or any concern for urethral injury after release.

Urology / plastics consult criteria

  1. Do NOT attempt zipper release
  2. Cover wound with saline-moistened gauze
  3. Pain control + IV access
  4. Urinalysis, consider retrograde urethrogram if urethral injury suspected
  5. Urology consult (plastics if large skin avulsion)
  6. Update tetanus status
  • Suspected urethral injury — blood at meatus, hematuria, inability to void
  • Significant skin loss — full-thickness laceration, devascularized flap, or skin avulsion
  • Failure of bedside release after one destruction technique AND adequate analgesia
  • Recurrent or strangulating injury with vascular compromise
  • Complex pediatric case that cannot tolerate sedation

Documentation for the call: mechanism, time entrapped, technique(s) attempted, current skin status, neurovascular exam, analgesia given.
