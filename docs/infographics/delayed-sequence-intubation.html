Delayed Sequence Intubation: Sedate to Preoxygenate

Adults in the ED/ICU who need intubation but cannot tolerate standard preoxygenation (agitated, combative, delirious desaturators). Excludes the apneic/non-breathing patient, absent airway reflexes, ketamine allergy, and very high aspiration risk — those go straight to RSI.

⚠ DSI depends on spontaneous breathing. It is NOT apneic like RSI — do NOT use it in a non-breathing patient, one with absent airway reflexes, ketamine allergy, or very high aspiration risk.
⚠ Give ketamine as a SLOW push over 15-30 seconds. A rapid IV push risks transient apnea, which defeats the entire purpose of DSI.
⚠ Keep the nasal cannula at 15 L/min through the entire procedure — including paralysis. Only remove it at the moment of intubation.
⚠ Have RSI drugs drawn and ready the whole time. Be prepared to convert to RSI at any moment — apnea, instability, or SpO₂ still <95% after 10 minutes.

1 · The core idea

DSI = procedural sedation where the procedure is preoxygenation
Rather than pushing sedative and paralytic together (RSI), DSI gives a dissociative dose of ketamine that preserves spontaneous breathing and airway reflexes, so you can load oxygen BEFORE paralysis.[1]

4 · Triple 15 preoxygenation

  1. Layer 1 — Nasal cannula at 15 L/min, CONTINUOUS. Keep it on through the ENTIRE procedure including paralysis; remove only at the moment of intubation.
  2. Layer 2 — Secondary source: SpO₂ >95% → non-rebreather 15 L/min. SpO₂ ≤95% → BVM + PEEP valve 5-15 cm H₂O, or CPAP/NIV 5-15 cm H₂O.
  3. PEEP is critical — it prevents alveolar collapse and improves FRC.
SpO₂ ≥ 95% — you have a runway
Target met. Continue preoxygenation 3 more minutes for denitrogenation, then reassess whether the patient still needs the tube before you paralyze.[1]
SpO₂ < 95% — optimize, and watch the clock
Tighten the mask seal, raise PEEP (up to 15 cm H₂O), sit the patient more upright, suction, and confirm true dissociation. If SpO₂ stays below 95% after 10 minutes of optimal preoxygenation → abort to RSI.[1]

6 · Reassess, paralysis & pitfalls

Reassess BEFORE you paralyze
15-20% of DSI patients avoid intubation entirely once sedation improves oxygenation — especially asthma/COPD in an anxiety-driven spiral. If work of breathing is now minimal and the patient holds on NIV, let ketamine wear off and reassess.[1][2]

6 · Reassess, paralysis & pitfalls

**15-20%** of DSI patients avoid intubation entirely once sedation improves oxygenation — especially asthma/COPD in an anxiety-driven spiral. If work of breathing is now minimal and the patient holds on NIV, let ketamine wear off and reassess.{{ref1}}{{ref2}}

If intubation is still needed: **keep the nasal cannula at 15 L/min** during paralysis, give rocuronium **1.2 mg/kg** or succinylcholine **1.5 mg/kg**, wait 45-60 s, laryngoscopy, confirm with capnography.{{ref1}}{{ref6}}

Rocuronium 1.2 mg/kg: 84 mg

Succinylcholine 1.5 mg/kg: 105 mg

Critical pitfalls

**Do NOT use DSI in the apneic or non-breathing patient** — it is not apneic like RSI and depends on spontaneous ventilation.

**Do NOT push ketamine fast.** Give **1-1.5 mg/kg** over **15-30 seconds**; a bolus risks apnea.

**Do NOT remove the nasal cannula early.** Keep it at **15 L/min** through paralysis — pull it only at the moment of intubation.

**Do NOT delay indefinitely.** If SpO₂ stays <95% after **10 minutes**, abort to RSI — the patient is already better oxygenated than a baseline RSI.
