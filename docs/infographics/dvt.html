Deep Vein Thrombosis — ED Diagnosis & Management

Adults ≥18 in the ED with suspected or confirmed lower-extremity DVT. Excludes concurrent high-risk PE requiring separate resuscitation, and pediatric patients. Pregnancy, active cancer, severe renal impairment, and antiphospholipid syndrome route to distinct treatment pathways below.

⚠ Use Wells score + D-dimer to guide imaging — do not image every patient with suspected DVT.
⚠ Age-adjusted D-dimer cutoff = age × 10 µg/L for patients >50 years.
⚠ Repeat ultrasound in 5–7 days if initially negative with high clinical probability (Wells ≥2).
⚠ Pregnancy: LMWH is first-line — DOACs AND warfarin are CONTRAINDICATED.
⚠ Active cancer: apixaban or LMWH preferred — avoid warfarin.
⚠ Antiphospholipid syndrome: DOACs are INFERIOR to warfarin — use warfarin, target INR 2–3.
⚠ IVC filter ONLY for a true anticoagulation contraindication (active major bleeding, hemorrhagic stroke) — not for "high bleeding risk" alone.

Risk stratification

DVT affects ~1-2 per 1,000 adults annually; up to 50% of untreated proximal DVT progresses to PE. Clinical diagnosis alone is unreliable — use the Wells DVT Calculator to guide the need for D-dimer vs direct imaging.

Paralysis, paresis, or recent cast of lower extremity+1
Bedridden >3 days or major surgery within 12 weeks+1
Localized tenderness along deep venous system+1
Entire leg swollen+1
Calf swelling >3 cm vs asymptomatic leg+1
Pitting edema (greater in symptomatic leg)+1

DVT Unlikely (Wells ≤1): prevalence ~6%; a negative D-dimer safely excludes DVT (false-negative rate <1%).

DVT Likely (Wells ≥2): prevalence ~28%; D-dimer is NOT sufficient to exclude — proceed directly to compression ultrasound.

High-risk features: phlegmasia
Phlegmasia cerulea dolens (massive iliofemoral DVT, near-total venous obstruction): severe pain, cyanosis, massive edema — risk of venous gangrene, limb loss, shock. Manage with immediate anticoagulation, urgent vascular surgery / IR consult, and consider catheter-directed thrombolysis (CDT).

IVC filter & treatment failure

IVC filter — very limited indications
Absolute indication: acute proximal DVT/PE with a true contraindication to anticoagulation (active major bleeding, recent high-bleeding-risk surgery, hemorrhagic stroke) — NOT "high bleeding risk" alone. Retrievable filters preferred; attempt retrieval when anticoagulation becomes safe. Filters do NOT reduce mortality and increase long-term DVT risk.

Recurrent VTE on anticoagulation: first confirm compliance and therapeutic levels. On a DOAC: switch to LMWH or a different DOAC. On LMWH: increase dose 25-33%, check anti-Xa (target 0.6-1.0 IU/mL). On warfarin: switch to LMWH/DOAC, or raise INR target to 3-4. Consider IVC filter only if recurrence occurs despite therapeutic anticoagulation.

Extended options: continue full-dose DOAC, or reduced-dose after 6 months (apixaban 2.5 mg BID, rivaroxaban 10 mg daily — AMPLIFY-EXT, EINSTEIN-CHOICE).

Disposition

  1. Anticoagulation prescribed — first dose given in ED if appropriate
  2. Patient education: dosing, bleeding precautions, drug/food interactions, signs of PE
  3. Follow-up arranged: PCP or anticoagulation clinic in 1-2 weeks
  4. Return precautions: dyspnea, chest pain, syncope, major bleeding, worsening leg swelling

Admit for: phlegmasia, concurrent high-risk PE, high bleeding risk requiring monitoring, need for parenteral anticoagulation, inability to obtain oral anticoagulation, severe renal impairment, or social/compliance concerns. Start anticoagulation immediately — do not delay for imaging. Most patients are ready to transition to outpatient therapy within 24-48 hours.
