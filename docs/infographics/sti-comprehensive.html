STI Comprehensive — Syndrome-Based ED Management

Adults and adolescents presenting with suspected sexually transmitted infection syndromes in the ED/outpatient setting. Syndrome-based empiric treatment per CDC 2021 STI Treatment Guidelines plus 2024-2025 updates (DoxyPEP, ACOG 3× syphilis screening in pregnancy, ceftriaxone monotherapy for GC). Excludes neonatal HSV, congenital syphilis, and long-term HIV/ART management — see dedicated consults.

⚠ Doxycycline is CONTRAINDICATED in pregnancy — substitute azithromycin 1 g PO ×1 for chlamydia coverage.
⚠ Penicillin is the ONLY effective syphilis treatment in pregnancy — desensitize if PCN-allergic; doxycycline is NOT an acceptable substitute in pregnancy.
⚠ Azithromycin is NO LONGER co-treatment for gonorrhea — ceftriaxone monotherapy is standard (2021 update); do not rely on azithromycin alone for GC.
⚠ No reliable oral alternative exists for pharyngeal gonorrhea — ceftriaxone 500 mg IM ×1 is the only recommended regimen, and test of cure is REQUIRED 7-14 days post-treatment.
⚠ Fluoroquinolones and tinidazole are contraindicated in pregnancy.
⚠ Hospitalize PID if: pregnant, tubo-ovarian abscess, severe illness (temp >38.5°C, N/V), unable to tolerate oral therapy, or no improvement after 72h outpatient therapy.

Proctitis & pharyngeal GC/CT (folds sti-proctitis, sti-pharyngeal)

Proctitis (MSM focus): etiology GC, CT (incl. LGV), HSV, syphilis — differential includes mpox. Empiric: ceftriaxone 500mg IM ×1 + doxycycline 100mg PO BID ×7 days (extend to 21 days if LGV suspected). HSV proctitis: valacyclovir 1g BID ×7-10d.

Pharyngeal GC — no oral alternative
Ceftriaxone 500 mg IM ×1 is the ONLY recommended regimen — no reliable oral alternative exists. Test of cure REQUIRED 7-14 days post-treatment for ALL pharyngeal gonorrhea (culture preferred). Treatment failure: ceftriaxone 1g IM + azithromycin 2g PO; report to health department.

Special populations

Pregnancy — screening & drug safety

Routine screening at 1st prenatal visit: HIV, syphilis (RPR/VDRL + treponemal), Hepatitis B, chlamydia, gonorrhea (if <25 or high-risk). ACOG 2024 syphilis screening — now 3×: 1st prenatal visit, 28-32 weeks, and at delivery.

Penicillin is the ONLY effective syphilis treatment in pregnancy. If PCN-allergic, MUST desensitize. Vertical transmission prevention: treat syphilis ≥30 days before delivery for best outcomes; HSV suppression from 36 weeks if history of genital herpes.

HIV+ — treatment modifications

Generally the SAME regimens as HIV-negative: gonorrhea ceftriaxone 500mg IM ×1, chlamydia doxycycline 100mg BID ×7d, syphilis benzathine PCN 2.4 MU IM. Check ART drug interactions (fluoroquinolones, azole antifungals). CSF exam if neurologic symptoms, treatment failure, or tertiary syphilis. More aggressive HSV suppression and syphilis serology follow-up. Screen for all STIs at least annually (every 3-6 months if ongoing risk).

MSM — 3-site testing, LGV, mpox, DoxyPEP

Test all 3 anatomic sites: urethral (urine NAAT/swab), pharyngeal (throat swab NAAT), rectal (rectal swab NAAT). Higher-prevalence conditions: LGV proctitis, M. genitalium, Hepatitis A (recommend vaccination).

Adolescent — confidentiality & consent

In most states, minors can consent to STI diagnosis/treatment without parental consent (state laws vary — check locally). Screen for trafficking indicators; assess relationship safety; partner age difference reporting varies by state. Treatment: same regimens as adults (weight-based dosing if needed). Offer vaccination catch-up (HPV, Hep A, Hep B).

Syndrome-based management algorithm

STI Comprehensive Consult — Syndrome-based empiric treatment — 2024-2025 updates: DoxyPEP, 3× syphilis screening in pregnancy, ceftriaxone monotherapy for GC.

Presenting syndrome? — Select the primary clinical syndrome — multiple syndromes may coexist. Always test all sites of exposure in MSM.

Urethritis vs cervicitis? — Urethritis (male): discharge, dysuria. Cervicitis: mucopurulent discharge, cervical friability. A third real option — recurrent/persistent symptoms → M. genitalium workup — is folded to this node's disclosure (see below) rather than drawn as a flow edge.

Genital ulcer etiology? — HSV: painful vesicles/ulcers. Syphilis: painless, indurated, clean base. Three lower-frequency real options — chancroid, LGV, unclear/empiric — are folded to this node's disclosure rather than drawn as flow edges.

Pelvic/regional syndrome type? — PID, epididymitis/orchitis, proctitis, or pharyngeal infection. Proctitis and pharyngeal are folded to this node's disclosure rather than drawn as flow edges.

Special population screen — Pregnancy, HIV+, MSM, adolescent, sexual assault, and mpox differential — all folded into this node's disclosures (see Special Populations section below).

Urethritis: ceftriaxone + doxycycline — Ceftriaxone 500 mg IM ×1 (1 g if ≥150 kg) + doxycycline 100 mg PO BID ×7 days. Azithromycin no longer routinely co-added.

Cervicitis: ceftriaxone + doxycycline (azithro if pregnant) — Same regimen as urethritis; substitute azithromycin 1 g PO ×1 for doxycycline in pregnancy. Always assess for PID.

PID criteria met? — Any 1 of: cervical motion tenderness, uterine tenderness, adnexal tenderness, plus pelvic/lower abdominal pain.

PID hospitalization criteria met? — Hospitalize if: pregnancy, tubo-ovarian abscess, severe illness (temp >38.5°C, N/V), unable to tolerate oral regimen, no improvement after 72h outpatient, or surgical emergency cannot be excluded.

PID outpatient: CTX + doxy + metronidazole ×14d — Ceftriaxone 500 mg IM ×1 (1 g if ≥150 kg) + doxycycline 100 mg PO BID ×14 days + metronidazole 500 mg PO BID ×14 days (metronidazole now routine, 2021 update). Re-evaluate in 72h.

PID inpatient: IV CTX + doxy + metronidazole — Ceftriaxone 1 g IV q24h + doxycycline 100 mg PO/IV q12h + metronidazole 500 mg IV/PO q12h. Transition to oral after 24-48h improvement; complete 14-day total course. Hospitalize ALL pregnant patients with PID.

First episode vs recurrent HSV? — First episode: more severe, longer duration. Recurrent: milder, often with prodrome. A third real option (frequent recurrences ≥6/yr → direct entry to suppressive therapy) is folded into the merged recurrent/suppressive node below.

HSV first episode: valacyclovir/acyclovir ×7-10d — Valacyclovir 1 g PO BID ×7-10 days or acyclovir 400 mg PO TID ×7-10 days. Start ASAP — most effective within 72h.

HSV recurrent episode + suppressive therapy — Episodic: valacyclovir 500 mg BID ×3d (start within 24h of prodrome). Suppressive (≥6 recurrences/yr, or to reduce transmission): valacyclovir 500 mg daily (1 g if ≥10 episodes/yr) — reduces recurrences 70-80%, transmission ~50%. Also reached directly from first-episode assessment when frequent recurrences are reported.

Syphilis: stage, then benzathine PCN G — Primary/secondary/early latent: 2.4 MU IM ×1. Late latent/tertiary: 2.4 MU IM weekly ×3. Neurosyphilis: aqueous PCN G IV 10-14 days. PCN-allergic non-pregnant: doxycycline 14d (early) or 28d (late). Pregnancy: PCN only, desensitize if allergic.

Epididymitis etiology (age/risk)? — Sexually active <35 or MSM insertive anal: GC/CT (±enteric for MSM). Age >35 or recent instrumentation: enteric bacteria. Rule out torsion (sudden onset, absent cremasteric reflex, high-riding testis, age <25).

Epididymitis (STI/MSM): CTX + doxy ×10d ± levofloxacin — Ceftriaxone 500 mg IM ×1 + doxycycline 100 mg PO BID ×10 days (longer than 7d urethritis course). MSM with insertive anal sex: add levofloxacin 500 mg PO daily ×10 days for enteric coverage.

Epididymitis (enteric): levofloxacin monotherapy — Levofloxacin 500 mg PO daily ×10 days. Not an STI — no partner treatment. Urine culture to guide therapy; add ceftriaxone + NAAT if STI not excluded.

Partner management, test of cure & follow-up — Partners within 60 days evaluated + treated (EPT legal in 47 states + DC). Retest ALL GC/CT patients at 3 months. Test of cure required for pharyngeal GC (7-14d), pregnancy (3-4wk), and treatment failure. Nationally notifiable: syphilis, GC, CT, chancroid, HIV.

Urethral / cervical syndromes — detail

Ceftriaxone: 500 mg IM ×1 (1 g if ≥150 kg) IM — First-line GC coverage. Ceftriaxone monotherapy is standard (2021 update) — azithromycin no longer routinely co-added.

Doxycycline: 100 mg PO BID ×7 days PO — CT coverage. Contraindicated in pregnancy — see azithromycin substitute below.

Azithromycin (pregnancy substitute): 1 g PO ×1 PO — Substitute for doxycycline in pregnancy (doxycycline contraindicated). No matching drug-store STI indication exists — dose sourced from the consult node only.

Recurrent/persistent symptoms → M. genitalium workup (folds sti-mgen-workup)

Consider when: persistent/recurrent urethritis or cervicitis, initial GC/CT treatment failure, or NGU with negative GC/CT. M. genitalium causes 15-25% of NGU. Beta-lactams do NOT work (no cell wall).

Macrolide resistance (US): 44-90%

Resistance status | Regimen

Macrolide-sensitive | Doxy 100mg BID ×7d, then azithromycin 1g day 1, then 500mg daily ×3d (2.5g total)

Macrolide-resistant | Doxy 100mg BID ×7d, then moxifloxacin 400mg daily ×7d

No resistance testing available | Doxy 100mg BID ×7d, then moxifloxacin 400mg daily ×7d

Trichomoniasis & bacterial vaginosis (folds orphaned sti-trichomonas-rx, sti-bv-info — commonly co-diagnosed with cervicitis workups)

Population | Regimen

Women (preferred, 2021 update) | Metronidazole 500mg PO BID ×7 days

Men | Metronidazole 2g PO ×1

Alternative (both sexes) | Tinidazole 2g PO ×1 or secnidazole 2g PO ×1 (avoid tinidazole in pregnancy)

Treatment failure | Metronidazole or tinidazole 2g PO daily ×7 days

Metronidazole + alcohol myth debunked (2021 update): no convincing evidence of a disulfiram-like reaction. Alcohol restriction during treatment is unnecessary.

Regimen | Route/duration

Metronidazole | 500mg PO BID ×7 days

Metronidazole gel 0.75% | Intravaginal daily ×5 days

Clindamycin cream 2% | Intravaginal at bedtime ×7 days

Alternative | Clindamycin 300mg PO BID ×7 days, or secnidazole 2g PO ×1

BV is not technically an STI. Partner treatment traditionally not recommended, but a 2025 RCT found partner treatment reduced recurrence (35% vs 63% at 12 weeks) — consider offering.

PID — outpatient vs inpatient detail

Ceftriaxone: 500 mg IM ×1 (1 g if ≥150 kg) IM

Doxycycline: 100 mg PO BID ×14 days PO

Metronidazole: 500 mg PO BID ×14 days PO — Now routinely added per 2021 update. No matching drug-store PID indication — dose sourced from the consult node only.

Re-evaluate in 72 hours. If no improvement, hospitalize. IUD in place: do NOT remove. Partner treatment within 60 days.

Drug | Dose | Route/frequency

Ceftriaxone | 1 g | IV q24h

Doxycycline | 100 mg | PO/IV q12h

Metronidazole | 500 mg | IV/PO q12h

Transition to oral after 24-48 hours clinical improvement, complete 14-day total course. Hospitalize ALL pregnant patients with PID (substitute azithromycin for doxycycline). TOA may need IR drainage or surgery if not improving.

PID minimum diagnostic criteria: cervical motion tenderness, uterine tenderness, or adnexal tenderness (any 1), plus pelvic/lower abdominal pain. Hospitalization criteria: pregnancy, tubo-ovarian abscess, severe illness, unable to tolerate oral therapy, or no improvement after 72h outpatient therapy — or surgical emergency cannot be excluded.

Genital ulcer syndromes — detail

Scenario | Drug | Dose

First episode | Valacyclovir | 1 g PO BID ×7-10 days

First episode (alt) | Acyclovir | 400 mg PO TID ×7-10 days

Recurrent (start ≤24h of prodrome) | Valacyclovir | 500 mg PO BID ×3 days

Suppressive (≥6 recur/yr) | Valacyclovir | 500 mg PO daily (1 g if ≥10 episodes/yr)

Suppressive therapy — recurrence reduction: 70-80%

Syphilis staging & serology (folds sti-syphilis-staging)

Stage | Presentation | Treatment

Primary | Chancre (painless, indurated) | Benzathine PCN G 2.4 MU IM ×1

Secondary | Rash (palms/soles), mucous patches, condyloma lata | Benzathine PCN G 2.4 MU IM ×1

Early latent (<1yr) | Asymptomatic, positive serology | Benzathine PCN G 2.4 MU IM ×1

Late latent (>1yr/unknown) | Asymptomatic | Benzathine PCN G 2.4 MU IM weekly ×3

Tertiary | Gummas, cardiovascular (rare) | Benzathine PCN G 2.4 MU IM weekly ×3

Neurosyphilis (any stage) | Neurologic symptoms — CSF exam | Aqueous PCN G 18-24 MU/day IV ×10-14 days

PCN allergy (non-pregnant): early — doxycycline 100mg BID ×14 days; late — doxycycline 100mg BID ×28 days; neurosyphilis MUST desensitize, no alternative. Pregnancy: PCN is the only effective treatment — desensitize if allergic. Jarisch-Herxheimer reaction: febrile reaction within 24h of treatment, supportive care, NOT an allergy.

Bicillin shortage (2024-2026): FDA-authorized imported alternatives available (Lentocilin, Extencilline) — prioritize penicillin supply for pregnant patients.

Chancroid, LGV & empiric ulcer coverage (folds sti-chancroid-rx, sti-lgv-rx, sti-ulcer-empiric)

Azithromycin (chancroid): 1 g PO ×1 PO — Haemophilus ducreyi — painful ulcer, purulent base, ragged edges, suppurative inguinal buboes. Alternatives: ceftriaxone 250mg IM ×1, ciprofloxacin 500mg BID ×3d, erythromycin base 500mg TID ×7d. No matching drug-store STI indication — dose sourced from the consult node only.

Doxycycline (LGV): 100 mg PO BID ×21 days PO — Chlamydia trachomatis L1-L3. "Groove sign" inguinal lymphadenopathy; in MSM often presents as severe proctitis. Longer than standard chlamydia course (21d vs 7d). Alternative: erythromycin base 500mg QID ×21d.

When etiology unclear: cover HSV (valacyclovir 1g BID ×7-10d) + syphilis (benzathine PCN G 2.4 MU IM ×1). Add azithromycin 1g ×1 for chancroid coverage if endemic area/travel or suppurative nodes. Test HSV PCR, syphilis serology, and HIV in all patients with genital ulcers.

Epididymitis — MSM add-on & detail

Ceftriaxone: 500 mg IM ×1 IM

Doxycycline: 100 mg PO BID ×10 days PO — Extended to 10 days (vs 7 days for urethritis).

Levofloxacin (MSM add-on): 500 mg PO daily ×10 days PO — Add for enteric coverage if MSM with insertive anal sex. Drug-store's only STI-adjacent indication for levofloxacin specifies 7 days for doxycycline-allergic chlamydia (500mg dose matches, duration differs) — the 10-day duration here is epididymitis-specific, sourced from the consult node.

Supportive care: scrotal elevation, NSAIDs, ice packs. Hospitalize if severe pain suggesting torsion/abscess, systemic symptoms, or diagnostic uncertainty. Rule out torsion — sudden onset, absent cremasteric reflex, high-riding testis, age <25.

Proctitis & pharyngeal GC/CT (folds sti-proctitis, sti-pharyngeal)

Proctitis (MSM focus): etiology GC, CT (incl. LGV), HSV, syphilis — differential includes mpox. Empiric: ceftriaxone 500mg IM ×1 + doxycycline 100mg PO BID ×7 days (extend to 21 days if LGV suspected). HSV proctitis: valacyclovir 1g BID ×7-10d.

Ceftriaxone 500 mg IM ×1 is the ONLY recommended regimen — no reliable oral alternative exists. Test of cure REQUIRED 7-14 days post-treatment for ALL pharyngeal gonorrhea (culture preferred). Treatment failure: ceftriaxone 1g IM + azithromycin 2g PO; report to health department.

Special populations

Pregnancy — screening & drug safety

Routine screening at 1st prenatal visit: HIV, syphilis (RPR/VDRL + treponemal), Hepatitis B, chlamydia, gonorrhea (if <25 or high-risk). ACOG 2024 syphilis screening — now 3×: 1st prenatal visit, 28-32 weeks, and at delivery.

Safe in pregnancy | Contraindicated in pregnancy

Ceftriaxone (all trimesters) | Doxycycline — use azithromycin 1g ×1 instead

Azithromycin (all trimesters) | Fluoroquinolones (moxifloxacin, ciprofloxacin)

Metronidazole (all trimesters, CDC Category B) | Tinidazole — use metronidazole

Acyclovir/valacyclovir | —

Penicillin is the ONLY effective syphilis treatment in pregnancy. If PCN-allergic, MUST desensitize. Vertical transmission prevention: treat syphilis ≥30 days before delivery for best outcomes; HSV suppression from 36 weeks if history of genital herpes.

HIV+ — treatment modifications

Generally the SAME regimens as HIV-negative: gonorrhea ceftriaxone 500mg IM ×1, chlamydia doxycycline 100mg BID ×7d, syphilis benzathine PCN 2.4 MU IM. Check ART drug interactions (fluoroquinolones, azole antifungals). CSF exam if neurologic symptoms, treatment failure, or tertiary syphilis. More aggressive HSV suppression and syphilis serology follow-up. Screen for all STIs at least annually (every 3-6 months if ongoing risk).

MSM — 3-site testing, LGV, mpox, DoxyPEP

Test all 3 anatomic sites: urethral (urine NAAT/swab), pharyngeal (throat swab NAAT), rectal (rectal swab NAAT). Higher-prevalence conditions: LGV proctitis, M. genitalium, Hepatitis A (recommend vaccination).

Doxycycline (DoxyPEP): 200 mg PO within 72 hours post-exposure (max 200mg/24h) PO — For MSM/TGW with a bacterial STI (syphilis, chlamydia, or GC) diagnosed in the past 12 months; shared decision-making. Does NOT replace condoms or HIV PrEP. No matching drug-store DoxyPEP indication — dose sourced from the consult node only.

Organism | Risk reduction

Syphilis | 73-87%

Chlamydia | 70-88%

Gonorrhea | ~50% (resistance concern)

Baseline STI screen; retest every 3-6 months at all exposure sites; reassess need every 3-6 months. NOT recommended for cisgender women, cisgender heterosexual men, or transgender men (insufficient data).

Consider mpox with new genital/perianal skin lesions or proctitis in MSM/transgender women. 41% of mpox patients reported an STI in the prior year; 38% had HIV at diagnosis — test for both. PCR from lesion swabs; supportive care; JYNNEOS vaccination for at-risk MSM.

Adolescent — confidentiality & consent

In most states, minors can consent to STI diagnosis/treatment without parental consent (state laws vary — check locally). Screen for trafficking indicators; assess relationship safety; partner age difference reporting varies by state. Treatment: same regimens as adults (weight-based dosing if needed). Offer vaccination catch-up (HPV, Hep A, Hep B).

Partner management, test of cure & follow-up

Infection | EPT regimen

Chlamydia | Doxycycline 100mg BID ×7 days

Gonorrhea | Cefixime 800mg PO ×1

Trichomonas | Metronidazole 2g PO ×1

Sex partners within 60 days should be evaluated and treated (extend lookback for syphilis based on staging). EPT legal status: 47 states + DC (not Kansas, South Dakota) — most effective for chlamydia; partners should still be evaluated if possible.

Indication | Timing

Pharyngeal gonorrhea (all cases, REQUIRED) | 7-14 days post-treatment, culture preferred

Pregnancy (GC/CT) | 3-4 weeks post-treatment

Treatment failure / persistent symptoms | As clinically indicated

HIV+ with syphilis | RPR titers per schedule below

Reinfection screening — ALL GC/CT patients (not a TOC) | Retest at 3 months

Syphilis follow-up: quantitative RPR/VDRL at 6 and 12 months (24 months for late latent) — fourfold decline expected by 6-12 months; evaluate for neurosyphilis if no decline. The 24-month titer applies only to late latent/tertiary disease, not primary/secondary/early latent.

Nationally notifiable: syphilis (all stages), gonorrhea, chlamydia, chancroid, HIV — check state-specific requirements. Report suspected gonorrhea treatment failures (CDC monitors via GISP).
