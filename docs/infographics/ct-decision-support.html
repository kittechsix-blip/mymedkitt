CT Scan Decision Support

ED clinicians deciding whether to order CT imaging. Covers adult and pediatric head injury (Canadian CT Head Rule, PECARN), cervical spine clearance (Canadian C-Spine Rule), suspected PE (Wells/PERC/D-dimer/CTPA), appendicitis (Alvarado) and renal colic, ankle/knee injury (Ottawa Rules), and special populations (pregnancy, pediatrics, contrast allergy). Not a substitute for clinical judgment — decision rules apply only within their validated inclusion/exclusion criteria.

⚠ Canadian CT Head Rule HIGH-RISK criteria (any one mandates CT): GCS <15 at 2 hours post-injury, suspected open or depressed skull fracture, any sign of basal skull fracture, vomiting ≥2 episodes, age ≥65 — 100% sensitive for neurosurgical intervention.
⚠ PECARN high-risk features mandate CT head: ≥2y — GCS <15/altered mental status OR signs of basilar skull fracture (4.3% ciTBI risk); <2y — GCS <15/altered mental status OR palpable skull fracture (4.4% ciTBI risk).
⚠ Canadian C-Spine Rule Step 1 HIGH-RISK factors (age ≥65, dangerous mechanism, paresthesias in extremities) mandate imaging directly — do NOT proceed to range-of-motion testing.
⚠ High pre-test-probability PE: do NOT use PERC or D-dimer — neither is validated in this population (~37.5% PE prevalence) — proceed directly to CTPA.
⚠ If the patient is on anticoagulants, even minor head injury warrants CT regardless of Canadian CT Head Rule eligibility.
⚠ Do NOT withhold a medically indicated CT from a pregnant patient — CTPA fetal dose (0.01-0.66 mGy) is far below the 50 mGy threshold of concern.
⚠ Choosing Wisely: avoid CT abdomen/pelvis in young (<50), otherwise healthy patients with known stone history presenting with uncomplicated renal colic.
⚠ Choosing Wisely: do not order CT head in patients meeting low-risk decision-rule criteria (CCHR or PECARN negative).

Overview

CT imaging is used in 27 per 100 ED encounters — nearly matching ECG usage. An estimated 103,000 new cancers annually may be attributable to CT imaging in the U.S. This tool applies validated clinical decision rules, prospectively validated in large multicenter studies, to guide CT ordering.

Abdomen — Appendicitis (Alvarado) & Renal Colic

Alvarado score (MANTRELS, max 10)InterpretationAction
0-4UnlikelyDischarge with return precautions: worsening/migrating pain, fever, persistent vomiting, inability to tolerate oral intake
5-6PossibleObservation with serial exams; imaging options — POCUS (91% sensitivity, negative does NOT rule out), CT abd/pelvis (≈10 mSv single phase, most accurate), MRI (preferred in pregnancy). Pediatrics: ultrasound first
7-8ProbableSurgical consult. CT if diagnosis equivocal or presentation atypical
9-10Very probableSurgical consult. CT may not be necessary with classic presentation and high suspicion
Renal colic imaging guidance
Choosing Wisely: avoid CT abdomen/pelvis in young (<50), otherwise healthy patients with known stone history and uncomplicated renal colic symptoms. CT is appropriate when: first episode / no prior stone history, fever/concern for infection, concern for alternative diagnosis, solitary kidney or transplant, AKI or rising creatinine, or persistent symptoms despite treatment. POCUS alternative: identifies hydronephrosis with lower radiation and shorter ED stay, no difference from CT in high-risk patients.

Extremity — Ottawa Ankle & Knee Rules

Ottawa Ankle / Foot RulesCriteria
Ankle X-ray if pain in malleolar zone ANDBone tenderness distal 6cm posterior tibia/tip medial malleolus, OR distal 6cm posterior fibula/tip lateral malleolus, OR unable to bear weight 4 steps
Foot X-ray if midfoot pain ANDTenderness at base of 5th metatarsal, OR tenderness at navicular, OR unable to bear weight 4 steps

Sensitivity 97.6%. Can reduce ankle radiographs by 30-40%. CT is rarely needed — consider only for complex intra-articular fractures (pilon, calcaneus), Lisfranc evaluation when plain films equivocal, or pre-op planning.

Ottawa Knee Rules — X-ray if ANY of
Age ≥ 55 years
Isolated patellar tenderness (no other knee bone tenderness)
Tenderness at head of fibula
Unable to flex knee to 90°
Unable to bear weight 4 steps (immediately + in ED)

Sensitivity 98-100%. Can reduce knee radiographs ~28%. Pittsburgh Knee Rules alternative (specificity 60% vs Ottawa ~48%): X-ray if fall/blunt trauma AND either age <12 or >50, or unable to walk 4 weight-bearing steps. CT rarely needed for acute knee injury (reserve for tibial plateau characterization or complex intra-articular fractures); MRI preferred for ligament/meniscal/soft tissue injury.

Special Populations, Radiation Dose & Documentation

StudyApproximate radiation dose
Head CT≈ 2 mSv (20 CXR equivalents, ~4 months background radiation) — cancer risk ~0.23 per 1,000 patients
C-spine CT≈ 3-4 mSv (~30-40 CXR equivalents)
CTPA≈ 10-15 mSv (~100-150 CXR equivalents)
Abdomen/pelvis CT (single phase)≈ 10 mSv
Pediatrics
Children are several times more radiosensitive than adults; infants are the most radiosensitive age group. Image Gently: child-size the dose, scan only the indicated region, scan once.
Pregnancy
CTPA fetal dose 0.01-0.66 mGy — well below the 50 mGy threshold of concern. Do NOT withhold a medically indicated CT from a pregnant patient. MRI or ultrasound preferred for suspected appendicitis in pregnancy; CT if clinically necessary.

When ordering CT — document: clinical indication, pre-test probability and decision rule result, clinical question, risk-benefit discussion (especially peds/pregnancy/recent CTs), contrast decision and allergy status, alternatives considered. When NOT ordering CT — document: decision rule applied and result, shared decision-making discussion, return precautions, follow-up plan. Document the rule, not just the conclusion — "Canadian CT Head Rule applied: no high-risk or medium-risk criteria met" is stronger than "CT head not indicated."
