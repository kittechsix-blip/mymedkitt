Botulism: Recognition, Antitoxin, and Supportive Care

Adults and infants (<1 year) with suspected botulism (foodborne, wound/IVDU, or infant) in the ED. Botulism is a clinical diagnosis — treatment decisions do not wait for laboratory confirmation. Excludes patients with a confirmed alternative diagnosis (GBS, myasthenia gravis) after workup.

⚠ Botulism is a CLINICAL DIAGNOSIS — do not wait for laboratory or mouse bioassay confirmation (takes 1-4 days) before giving antitoxin.
⚠ Antitoxin neutralizes circulating toxin only — it does NOT reverse toxin already bound to nerve terminals. Give as soon as botulism is clinically suspected, before confirmatory testing, even if diagnosis is uncertain — earlier administration means less ventilator time.
⚠ Infant botulism: do NOT give antibiotics — lysing Clostridium botulinum in the gut releases more toxin and can worsen paralysis. BabyBIG only, never adult antitoxin.
⚠ Airway is the priority — respiratory failure is the leading cause of death in botulism. Intubate early (FVC <12-15 mL/kg or declining, weak cough, bulbar symptoms with aspiration risk) rather than waiting for an emergent crisis.
⚠ Wound botulism: AVOID aminoglycosides and clindamycin — they potentiate neuromuscular blockade and can worsen respiratory failure.

3 · What to do now

Every suspected botulism: call now, admit ICU
Do NOT wait for lab confirmation. Call for antitoxin immediately, notify public health, and admit to ICU with serial FVC monitoring — there is no outpatient management of suspected botulism.
Infant (<1 year): BabyBIG, NOT adult antitoxin
Call the California Infant Botulism Treatment Program: 510-231-7600. Give BabyBIG 50 mg/kg (1 mL/kg) IV over 1-2 hours. Do NOT give antibiotics — lysing C. botulinum releases more toxin.
Wound botulism: antitoxin + debridement + antibiotics
Call CDC: 770-488-7100 for HBAT (1 vial IV) as for any adult case. ALSO required: surgical debridement and metronidazole 500 mg IV q8h (preferred) or penicillin G 3-4 million units IV q4h (alternative). AVOID aminoglycosides/clindamycin — worsen neuromuscular blockade.
Foodborne / unclear-source adult: antitoxin only
Call CDC Emergency Operations Center: 770-488-7100 for HBAT 1 vial IV. Have epinephrine at bedside (~10% hypersensitivity, equine-derived). No antibiotics indicated for foodborne or unclear-source disease.

5 · Diagnostic workup

Clinical diagnosis — treat before confirmation
Botulism is a CLINICAL DIAGNOSIS. Do NOT wait for laboratory confirmation to treat. Mouse bioassay (gold standard) takes 1-4 days — send serum, stool, gastric contents, and wound culture. CBC/BMP/LFTs are typically normal. CSF is normal — this helps rule out GBS (elevated protein).
  • CT/MRI brain — normal in botulism; rules out stroke, brainstem lesion
  • MRI spine — normal; rules out cord compression, transverse myelitis
  • EMG/NCS — low-amplitude CMAPs, incremental response to rapid repetitive stimulation >50 Hz (opposite of MG's decremental response); normal sensory conduction
  • Tensilon (edrophonium) test — minimal/no improvement (vs robust MG response); not recommended as primary diagnostic tool

8 · Public health, disposition & prognosis

Reportable disease — notify immediately
Botulism is a reportable disease. Notify state/local health department and CDC immediately — do not wait for lab confirmation. Botulinum toxin is a Category A bioterrorism agent; save any remaining suspected food (refrigerate, do not freeze); household contacts sharing the food source need evaluation and monitoring for 10 days.
  • ALL suspected botulism → ICU admission; no outpatient management
  • Continuous pulse oximetry, serial FVC monitoring q4h minimum, cardiac monitoring, rapid access to intubation
  • Consult: infectious disease, pulmonology/critical care, neurology, surgery (wound botulism)
  • If no ICU at current facility: transfer emergently, but do not delay antitoxin for transfer; intubate before transport if any airway concern
  • Respiratory: FVC q4h, NIF weaker than -30 cmH2O, single breath count, continuous pulse oximetry, ABG if concern
  • Neurological: serial cranial nerve exams, motor strength, document progression/plateau/improvement
  • Autonomic: continuous telemetry, blood pressure lability, bowel function, urinary output
Prognosis is good with modern ICU care
Mortality <5% with modern ICU care and antitoxin (was 60% in the pre-antitoxin era). Recovery takes 3-12 months — nerve terminals must regenerate; cranial nerve function recovers first, proximal strength before distal. Earlier antitoxin means shorter ventilator course. Most patients recover fully.
