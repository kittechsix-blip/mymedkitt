Neonatal Resuscitation (NRP): Dry, Ventilate, Then Compress

Newborns at delivery in the ED or delivery room, resuscitated by the NRP algorithm. Ventilation is the priority intervention. Excludes established NICU management, congenital-anomaly-specific pathways, and therapeutic-hypothermia protocols (initiated post-resuscitation, not during it).

⚠ Golden 30 seconds: Warm (radiant heater), Dry, Position (sniffing), Stimulate (flick soles/rub back), Clear airway PRN — mouth before nose
⚠ Do NOT delay PPV for meconium suctioning (2025 change) — ventilation is priority; ET suction only if visible thick meconium blocks airway
⚠ PPV is most important intervention — T-piece preferred, C-E grip, PIP 20-25, rate 40-60/min, FiO2 21% for ≥35wk, confirm chest rise
⚠ MR SOPA: Mask adjust, Reposition, Suction, Open mouth, Pressure increase (by 5 cmH2O), Alternative airway (ETT/LMA)
⚠ HR <60 despite confirmed effective ventilation = initiate chest compressions — must secure airway first
⚠ 3:1 ratio (90 compressions + 30 breaths), 2-thumb encircling, lower third sternum, 100% FiO2, establish UVC, prepare epinephrine

1 · Set the scene

~90% of newborns transition with only warmth, drying, and stimulation. ~10% need early support (PPV, supplemental O₂). <1% need full resuscitation — CPR plus medications.[1]

The whole algorithm in one line
Ventilation is the intervention that resuscitates a newborn. Dry and stimulate → if apneic or HR <100, give PPV → only if HR <60 *despite* effective ventilation do you compress → only if HR <60 *despite* compressions do you give epinephrine.

Preterm < 32 weeks — tailor now
Plastic wrap the body (not the face) to prevent heat loss. Start FiO₂ 21–30% and titrate to preductal SpO₂ targets. Temperature is critical — both hypothermia and hyperthermia worsen outcomes. Surfactant and NICU input if <32 weeks.[1][2]
Term / late preterm ≥ 35 weeks
Start PPV at FiO₂ 21% (room air) and titrate up to preductal SpO₂ targets. Maintain normothermia 36.5–37.5 °C.[1]
Meconium + non-vigorous
Do NOT delay PPV to suction (2025 guideline change). Begin PPV as indicated — ventilation is the priority. Endotracheal suction only if visible thick meconium is obstructing the airway.[1][6]
Meconium + vigorous
A vigorous baby through meconium needs no special suctioning — proceed with the normal initial steps.[1][6]

2 · The 3 questions & the Golden 30 seconds

  1. Term gestation? (≥ 37 weeks)
  2. Breathing or crying?
  3. Good muscle tone? (flexed, active)

Yes to all three → stay skin-to-skin, routine care. No to any → move to the radiant warmer and start the initial steps.[1][2]

Golden 30 seconds — W · D · P · S · C
Warm — radiant heater ON, no blankets on top (they block radiant heat). Dry vigorously, remove wet towels. Position — sniffing position, slight neck extension. Stimulate — flick the soles, rub the back. Clear airway PRN — suction mouth then nose ("M before N").[1][2][3]
Cord management (2025 update)DCC · milking

Vigorous term / late preterm: delayed cord clamping 30–60 seconds — improves placental transfusion, iron stores, and hemoglobin. Non-vigorous ≥ 35 weeks: intact cord milking may be reasonable — milk toward the baby 3–4 times over 2 seconds. 28–34 weeks: milking when DCC isn't possible. < 28 weeks: milking is NOT recommended (intraventricular hemorrhage risk). Clamp immediately if the baby needs resuscitation now, or with abruption / cord avulsion.[1][5]

Reassess at 30 seconds — breathing, heart rate (auscultate or palpate the umbilical stump, 6 sec × 10), tone, color. Breathing with HR ≥ 100 → post-resuscitation care. Labored breathing → CPAP. Apnea/gasping or HR < 100 → start PPV.[1][2]

3 · PPV — the most important intervention

  • T-piece resuscitator preferred (2025) — consistent PIP and PEEP; self-inflating bag acceptable if unavailable
  • C-E grip — C fingers seal the mask, E fingers lift the jaw; mask covers mouth and nose, not the eyes
  • PIP 20–25 cmH₂O, rate 40–60/min ("squeeze — release — release")
  • Confirm chest rise and bilateral breath sounds — the rising heart rate is the best sign of effective PPV

Start FiO₂ 21% for ≥ 35 weeks (21–30% for < 35 weeks) and titrate to the preductal SpO₂ targets below.[1]

  2. R — Reposition: sniffing position, shoulder roll for alignment
  3. S — Suction: mouth then nose, clear obstructing secretions
  4. O — Open mouth: open the mouth slightly, jaw thrust if needed
  5. P — Pressure increase: raise PIP by 5 cmH₂O increments, up to 30–40 cmH₂O for chest rise
  6. A — Alternative airway: place an ETT or LMA
Advanced airway & ETT sizingETT / LMA

30-second attempt limit per intubation — ventilate between attempts. Confirm with chest rise, bilateral breath sounds, and a CO₂ detector. An LMA may be the initial interface for ≥ 34 weeks, > 2 kg (2025) but cannot be used for tracheal suctioning. Intubate before compressions.[1][2]

4 · Compressions & epinephrine

Only after effective ventilation
Compressions begin only when HR stays < 60 despite confirmed effective ventilation (chest rise + MR SOPA applied). Secure the airway first — intubate or place an LMA before compressing.[1][2]
  • 2-thumb encircling technique — both thumbs on the lower third of the sternum, just below the nipple line
  • Compress 1/3 of the anteroposterior diameter
  • Ratio 3:1 — "one — two — three — breathe" = 120 events/min (90 compressions + 30 breaths). This differs from pediatric/adult CPR.
  • Increase to 100% FiO₂, establish a UVC (fastest access), and prepare epinephrine

If HR is still < 60 after 60 seconds of coordinated compressions and ventilation, give Epinephrine. IV/IO (preferred): 0.01 mg/kg to 0.03 mg/kg of 1:10,000 (= 0.1–0.3 mL/kg), repeated every 3–5 minutes. Flush with normal saline. IV/IO is always preferred over the endotracheal route (ET 0.05–0.1 mg/kg, absorption unpredictable).[1][2][3]

UVC & volume resuscitationaccess · fluids

Catheterize the single large-lumen vein (not the 2 thick-walled arteries). 5 French term, 3.5 French preterm. Tie umbilical tape at the base, cut the cord 1–2 cm above the skin, prime the catheter with NS, and insert only 2–4 cm until blood aspirates freely — do not advance further (liver injection risk).[1][2]

6 · Post-resuscitation & when to stop

  • APGAR at 1, 5, and 10 minutes
  • Normothermia 36.5–37.5 °C — both hypothermia and hyperthermia worsen outcomes
  • Glucose within 30–60 min of stabilization; treat < 40 mg/dL with D10W 2 mL/kg IV
  • NICU transfer for any neonate requiring PPV or more
  • Therapeutic hypothermia if ≥ 36 weeks with moderate–severe HIE, started within 6 hours — NOT during active resuscitation
When to consider discontinuation20-min rule
No detectable heart rate after 20 minutes of comprehensive resuscitation (effective ventilation, compressions, epinephrine, volume) may be an appropriate time to discuss stopping. Weigh gestational age, duration of no HR, reversible causes, parental wishes, and institutional guidelines. Involve the family and support the team.[1][4]
Do NOT — critical pitfalls4 items
Do NOT delay PPV to suction meconium (2025 change) — ventilation is the priority.
Do NOT start compressions before securing effective ventilation — a poorly ventilated newborn will not respond to compressions.
Do NOT monitor SpO₂ on the left hand or feet — postductal readings run falsely low. Right hand only.
Do NOT advance the UVC beyond 2–4 cm for emergency access — deeper risks hepatic injection.

6 · Post-resuscitation & when to stop

  • **APGAR** at 1, 5, and 10 minutes
  • **Normothermia 36.5–37.5 °C** — both hypothermia and hyperthermia worsen outcomes
  • **Glucose** within 30–60 min of stabilization; treat < 40 mg/dL with D10W 2 mL/kg IV
  • **NICU transfer** for any neonate requiring PPV or more
  • **Therapeutic hypothermia** if ≥ 36 weeks with moderate–severe HIE, started within 6 hours — NOT during active resuscitation

When to consider discontinuation

**No detectable heart rate after 20 minutes** of comprehensive resuscitation (effective ventilation, compressions, epinephrine, volume) may be an appropriate time to discuss stopping. Weigh gestational age, duration of no HR, reversible causes, parental wishes, and institutional guidelines. Involve the family and support the team.{{ref1}}{{ref4}}

Do NOT — critical pitfalls

**Do NOT delay PPV to suction meconium** (2025 change) — ventilation is the priority.

**Do NOT start compressions before securing effective ventilation** — a poorly ventilated newborn will not respond to compressions.

**Do NOT monitor SpO₂ on the left hand or feet** — postductal readings run falsely low. Right hand only.

**Do NOT advance the UVC beyond 2–4 cm** for emergency access — deeper risks hepatic injection.
