Pediatric Skin & Soft Tissue Infections: Classify, Treat, Don't Miss NSTI

Children (neonate through adolescent) presenting to the ED with a skin/soft tissue infection. Covers purulent (abscess), non-purulent (cellulitis/erysipelas), severe/systemic, necrotizing fasciitis, and special-site (periorbital, perianal, neck/Lemierre) presentations. Excludes adult-only dosing and non-skin surgical site infections.

⚠ Pain out of proportion to exam, crepitus, rapidly advancing erythema, hemorrhagic bullae, or systemic toxicity = necrotizing fasciitis until proven otherwise — a surgical emergency. Do not delay emergent surgical consultation or empiric antibiotics (vancomycin + piperacillin-tazobactam + clindamycin within 1 hour) for imaging.
⚠ LRINEC score has poor sensitivity in children (43-60% in validation studies) — a low score does NOT rule out necrotizing fasciitis. Clinical suspicion always overrides the score.
⚠ Toxic-appearing, hemodynamically unstable, or age <6 months with cellulitis/SSTI → admit for IV antibiotics; keep a low threshold for a sepsis workup in neonates and ill-appearing infants.
⚠ Any proptosis, ophthalmoplegia/pain with eye movement, or vision change accompanying lid swelling signals orbital (not preseptal) cellulitis — sight- and life-threatening. Obtain CT orbits/sinuses with contrast and emergent ENT/ophthalmology consultation.
⚠ Adolescent with a sore throat that resolved 4-7 days ago, now with neck pain, fever, and respiratory symptoms (septic pulmonary emboli in >80%) suggests Lemierre syndrome (septic IJ thrombophlebitis) — obtain CT neck with contrast; do not dismiss as simple pharyngitis.

1 · Recognition & classification

Skin and soft tissue infections are among the most common pediatric ED visits, and community-associated MRSA is the dominant organism in many US regions.

Red flags requiring immediate resuscitation: hemodynamic instability/septic appearance, pain out of proportion to exam, crepitus, skin discoloration (dusky/purple/bullae), rapid spread over hours, or hard woody induration extending beyond visible erythema.

Classify EARLY — purulent vs non-purulent vs severe/systemic vs necrotizing drives the entire pathway. Bedside ultrasound improves abscess detection sensitivity to 96-98% over exam alone — use any time the exam is equivocal.

TypeFindingsPathway
PurulentFluctuance, pus, abscess on USI&D pathway
Non-purulentErythema, warmth, swelling, no fluctuanceCellulitis pathway
Severe / systemicFever, toxic appearance, large area, immunocompromisedAdmit + IV abx
NecrotizingPain out of proportion, crepitus, rapid spread, dishwater drainageOR emergently

POCUS technique: high-frequency linear probe (5-12 MHz), 2 orthogonal planes, compare to contralateral side.

Findings: anechoic fluid collection = mature abscess (drainable). "Cobblestoning" (anechoic tracts in subQ fat) = cellulitis, NO abscess. Hyperechoic gas ("snow globe") = NSTI suspicion — get surgery now. Fascial thickening/fluid along fascia = concerning for nec fasc.

Pitfalls: lymph nodes can look like abscesses on B-mode (check hilar Doppler flow); pseudoaneurysm shows turbulent flow; necrotic tumor needs clinical context.

If POCUS shows a true abscess, proceed to the purulent (I&D) pathway below — this equivocal-exam branch feeds back into it.

2 · What to do now

NSTI red flags — surgical emergency
Emergent surgical consult, aggressive fluids, and empiric antibiotics within 1 hour: vancomycin 15 mg/kg IV q6h + piperacillin-tazobactam 100 mg/kg IV q6-8h + clindamycin 10-13 mg/kg/dose IV q6-8h (anti-toxin). ICU admission. Do not delay surgery for imaging or for a LRINEC score.
Severe/systemic — admit + IV antibiotics
Ceftriaxone 50-75 mg/kg IV q24h + clindamycin 10-13 mg/kg/dose IV q6-8h. Add vancomycin 15 mg/kg IV q6h if severe MRSA risk or instability. Blood cultures ×2 before antibiotics. Source control (drainage) urgent.
Purulent — I&D is the mainstay
I&D any fluctuant lesion ≥5 mm. Add antibiotics (TMP-SMX 4-6 mg/kg/dose TMP BID OR clindamycin 10 mg/kg/dose PO TID) for abscess ≥2 cm, multiple lesions, extensive surrounding cellulitis, systemic signs, immunocompromised, age <1 year, or failed prior I&D. Culture the purulent material.
Non-purulent cellulitis — anti-strep coverage
Cephalexin 25-50 mg/kg/day PO divided QID — MRSA coverage is NOT routine for typical cellulitis. Mark the border with skin marker + date, elevate, warm compresses. Add MRSA cover only for penetrating trauma, prior MRSA, concurrent purulent component, or beta-lactam failure.
Special site or atypical — route by anatomy
Periorbital/orbital, perianal/perineal, and neck (Lemierre/deep neck) presentations each follow a distinct pathway — select the special site below in section 8.
Equivocal exam — get bedside ultrasound
Linear probe, 2 planes. Anechoic fluid → treat as purulent (I&D). Cobblestoning only → treat as non-purulent cellulitis. Gas/dirty shadowing → treat as necrotizing fasciitis immediately.

7 · Necrotizing fasciitis detail

Mortality 20–40% even with treatment; doubles with each hour of delay to OR. Suspect with: pain out of proportion (hallmark), rapid progression, disproportionately mild early skin findings, crepitus/hemorrhagic bullae/dusky-to-black discoloration, "wooden" induration beyond visible erythema, systemic toxicity, dishwater-gray exudate, or anesthesia of overlying skin (late finding). Peds risk: varicella superinfection, recent surgery/trauma/IM injections, immunocompromised, neonatal omphalitis.

The LRINEC trap
Original adult validation: LRINEC ≥6 has PPV 92%. But pediatric validation studies show sensitivity only 43–60% — many missed cases scored <6. Canadian Association of General Surgeons (2021) recommends against using LRINEC for decision-making. Clinical suspicion always overrides a low score.

100 mg/kg IV q6-8h

  • ICU admission
  • IVIG 1 g/kg day 1, then 0.5 g/kg days 2–3 if streptococcal toxic shock syndrome suspected
Repeat washout at 24h is the rule
Mortality decreases dramatically when a 2nd-look operation occurs within 24h. Plan for it at the first operation, not as an afterthought.

Orbital cellulitis — CT + IV abx + ENT/ophtho
CT orbits/sinuses with contrast mandatory whenever orbital signs are present or cannot be excluded. IV ceftriaxone 50 mg/kg q24h + vancomycin 15 mg/kg q6h + metronidazole 10 mg/kg q8h (anaerobic, sinus origin). Subperiosteal abscess >10 mm or no improvement at 24-48h → ENT surgical drainage.

Perianal abscess most common in infants <2 years (boys >> girls). Most are superficial (intersphincteric) and amenable to bedside I&D; deep abscesses (ischiorectal, supralevator) need OR drainage. Routine post-I&D antibiotics in infants <2 years. Red flag: older child (>5 years) with recurrent perianal abscess/fistula/skin tags → consider Crohn disease workup (fecal calprotectin, CRP, GI consult).

Lemierre syndrome is septic thrombophlebitis of the internal jugular vein, classically caused by *Fusobacterium necrophorum* oropharyngeal infection. Classic story: adolescent with sore throat 4-7 days ago that "got better," now neck pain (especially along SCM), fever, rigors, dyspnea, pleuritic chest pain — septic pulmonary emboli in >80%. CT neck with contrast for IJ thrombus; CT chest if respiratory symptoms. Blood cultures ×2 (Fusobacterium grows in the anaerobic bottle, may take days). Piperacillin-tazobactam 100 mg/kg IV q6-8h + metronidazole, or meropenem; duration 4-6 weeks IV/PO total.

When in doubt, reconsider the diagnosis
Bilateral cellulitis is almost never bacterial. Failure to respond to 48-72h of appropriate antibiotics should trigger reconsideration of the diagnosis — ultrasound, Doppler, and biopsy are your friends.

9 · Pitfalls — do NOT

8 pitfalls to avoidhigh-yield errors
Do NOT rely on LRINEC to exclude necrotizing fasciitis in a child — pediatric sensitivity is only 43-60%; clinical suspicion overrides the score.
Do NOT delay surgical consultation or empiric antibiotics for imaging when necrotizing fasciitis is clinically suspected.
Do NOT add routine MRSA coverage to a classic non-purulent cellulitis or erysipelas without risk factors — IDSA 2014 recommends empiric anti-streptococcal therapy alone.
Do NOT mistake early cellulitis worsening (24-48h) for treatment failure — mark the border and reassess at the scheduled 48-72h recheck before switching antibiotics.
Do NOT assume preseptal cellulitis when any proptosis, EOM restriction, or vision change is present — treat as orbital and image emergently.
Do NOT dismiss an adolescent with a "resolving" sore throat and new neck pain as simple pharyngitis — consider Lemierre syndrome.
Do NOT skip a Crohn disease workup in an older child (>5 years) with recurrent perianal abscess or fistula.
Do NOT debride pyoderma gangrenosum mistaken for infected ulcer — debridement worsens it; biopsy and immunosuppression are the correct management.

Any drainable collection must be addressed urgently — surgical or IR consult. ICU if septic shock, concern for necrotizing infection, multi-organ involvement, or airway compromise.

7 · Necrotizing fasciitis detail

**Mortality 20–40% even with treatment; doubles with each hour of delay to OR.** Suspect with: pain out of proportion (hallmark), rapid progression, disproportionately mild early skin findings, crepitus/hemorrhagic bullae/dusky-to-black discoloration, "wooden" induration beyond visible erythema, systemic toxicity, dishwater-gray exudate, or anesthesia of overlying skin (late finding). Peds risk: varicella superinfection, recent surgery/trauma/IM injections, immunocompromised, neonatal omphalitis.

Original adult validation: LRINEC ≥6 has PPV 92%. But **pediatric validation studies show sensitivity only 43–60%** — many missed cases scored <6. Canadian Association of General Surgeons (2021) recommends against using LRINEC for decision-making. Clinical suspicion always overrides a low score.

Vancomycin: 15 mg/kg IV q6h IV — Or linezolid 10 mg/kg IV/PO q8h if vancomycin-intolerant. Give within 1 hour of suspicion.

Piperacillin-tazobactam: 100 mg/kg IV q6-8h IV — Or meropenem 20 mg/kg q8h. Broad gram-negative + anaerobic coverage.

Clindamycin (anti-toxin — critical): 10–13 mg/kg/dose IV q6-8h IV — Suppresses streptococcal/staph exotoxin production. Beta-lactams may paradoxically INCREASE toxin release (Eagle effect) — clindamycin must be included.

  • Emergent surgical consultation (general surgery, ortho if extremity, plastics)
  • Aggressive fluid resuscitation (often massive)
  • Empiric antibiotics within 1 hour (vancomycin + piperacillin-tazobactam + clindamycin)
  • ICU admission
  • IVIG 1 g/kg day 1, then 0.5 g/kg days 2–3 if streptococcal toxic shock syndrome suspected

Mortality decreases dramatically when a 2nd-look operation occurs within 24h. Plan for it at the first operation, not as an afterthought.

8 · Special sites & mimics detail

Feature | Preseptal | Orbital

Proptosis | No | Yes

EOM restriction / pain with movement | No | Yes

Vision change | No | Yes

Usual source | Local trauma, sinusitis, conjunctivitis | Ethmoid sinusitis spreading posteriorly

CT orbits/sinuses with contrast mandatory whenever orbital signs are present or cannot be excluded. IV ceftriaxone 50 mg/kg q24h + vancomycin 15 mg/kg q6h + metronidazole 10 mg/kg q8h (anaerobic, sinus origin). Subperiosteal abscess >10 mm or no improvement at 24-48h → ENT surgical drainage.

Perianal abscess most common in infants <2 years (boys >> girls). Most are superficial (intersphincteric) and amenable to bedside I&D; deep abscesses (ischiorectal, supralevator) need OR drainage. Routine post-I&D antibiotics in infants <2 years. **Red flag:** older child (>5 years) with recurrent perianal abscess/fistula/skin tags → consider Crohn disease workup (fecal calprotectin, CRP, GI consult).

Lemierre syndrome is septic thrombophlebitis of the internal jugular vein, classically caused by *Fusobacterium necrophorum* oropharyngeal infection. Classic story: adolescent with sore throat 4-7 days ago that "got better," now neck pain (especially along SCM), fever, rigors, dyspnea, pleuritic chest pain — septic pulmonary emboli in **>80%**. CT neck with contrast for IJ thrombus; CT chest if respiratory symptoms. Blood cultures ×2 (Fusobacterium grows in the anaerobic bottle, may take days). Piperacillin-tazobactam 100 mg/kg IV q6-8h + metronidazole, or meropenem; duration 4-6 weeks IV/PO total.

Mimic | Distinguishing feature

Contact/stasis dermatitis | Bilateral, pruritic > painful, no fever

DVT | Unilateral, risk factors — Doppler

Ruptured Baker cyst | Sudden popliteal/calf pain after activity — US diagnostic

Gout/pseudogout | Adolescents, first MTP/ankle/knee — tap if uncertain

Erythema migrans (Lyme) | Targetoid lesion, tick exposure — doxycycline/amoxicillin 10 days

Pyoderma gangrenosum | Undermined ulcer, IBD-associated — do NOT debride

Bilateral cellulitis is almost never bacterial. Failure to respond to 48-72h of appropriate antibiotics should trigger reconsideration of the diagnosis — ultrasound, Doppler, and biopsy are your friends.

9 · Pitfalls — do NOT

8 pitfalls to avoid

**Do NOT rely on LRINEC to exclude necrotizing fasciitis in a child** — pediatric sensitivity is only 43-60%; clinical suspicion overrides the score.

**Do NOT delay surgical consultation or empiric antibiotics for imaging** when necrotizing fasciitis is clinically suspected.

**Do NOT add routine MRSA coverage to a classic non-purulent cellulitis or erysipelas** without risk factors — IDSA 2014 recommends empiric anti-streptococcal therapy alone.

**Do NOT mistake early cellulitis worsening (24-48h) for treatment failure** — mark the border and reassess at the scheduled 48-72h recheck before switching antibiotics.

**Do NOT assume preseptal cellulitis when any proptosis, EOM restriction, or vision change is present** — treat as orbital and image emergently.

**Do NOT dismiss an adolescent with a "resolving" sore throat and new neck pain as simple pharyngitis** — consider Lemierre syndrome.

**Do NOT skip a Crohn disease workup in an older child (>5 years) with recurrent perianal abscess or fistula.**

**Do NOT debride pyoderma gangrenosum** mistaken for infected ulcer — debridement worsens it; biopsy and immunosuppression are the correct management.
