Gallbladder Disease — Biliary Colic, Cholecystitis, Cholangitis, Choledocholithiasis

Adults ≥18, ED, with suspected or confirmed biliary pathology — biliary colic, acute cholecystitis, cholangitis, or choledocholithiasis. Excludes pediatric biliary disease, gallstone pancreatitis (separate consult), and pregnancy-specific biliary management.

⚠ Grade III cholangitis (Reynolds' pentad — Charcot's triad + hypotension + altered mental status) = septic shock. EMERGENT ERCP within 2-4 hours; if unavailable, immediate percutaneous transhepatic drainage. Delay drainage = death — mortality approaches 50% without emergent drainage.
⚠ Grade III cholecystitis (any organ dysfunction: vasopressor requirement, altered mental status, PaO2/FiO2 <300, Cr >2.0/oliguria, INR >1.5, platelets <100,000) requires ICU-level care. Percutaneous cholecystostomy is first-line source control — DELAY surgery until organ dysfunction resolves; emergency surgery only for perforation with peritonitis.
⚠ Grade II cholangitis requires urgent biliary drainage WITHIN 24 HOURS — mortality 1.7% with timely drainage vs 3.4% if drainage is delayed or withheld.
⚠ Imaging showing complications (gas, perforation, abscess) routes directly to Grade III severe cholecystitis management, bypassing standard Tokyo grading.
⚠ Delayed cholecystectomy (>2 weeks) carries higher morbidity (25-30%) than early surgery (<72h, 15-20%) — avoid delay beyond ~10 days without a specific high-risk indication.

Sphincterotomy + balloon/basket extraction achieves >95% stone clearance. Timing: cholangitis Grade II-III within 24 hours (or emergent); non-emergent within 48-72 hours of diagnosis. Complications occur in 5-10% (post-ERCP pancreatitis 3-5% — rectal indomethacin reduces risk; bleeding 1-2%; perforation 0.5-1%). Cholecystectomy after ERCP (same admission or within 2 weeks) reduces recurrent biliary events (17% → 3%).

Cholecystectomy timing

Early cholecystectomy within 72 hours ("Golden 72") — or even up to 10 days — is safe with no increased operative difficulty and morbidity 15-20%. Delayed surgery (>2 weeks) carries higher morbidity 25-30%, longer stay, higher cost, and recurrent biliary events — avoid if possible.

  • Early surgery: NPO, IV fluids, antibiotics continued to OR, laparoscopic cholecystectomy (subtotal if anatomy unclear); typical discharge POD 0-1.
  • Watch for bile leak (0.5-1%) and bile duct injury (0.3-0.5%) after cholecystectomy.
  • Delayed pathway: percutaneous cholecystostomy bridge, complete antibiotics, optimize comorbidities, interval cholecystectomy 6-8 weeks.
  • Non-surgical: complete 7-14 day antibiotic course, percutaneous cholecystostomy for source control, consider chronic tube if recurrent decline of surgery.

Choledocholithiasis — CBD stone probability

Probability | Criteria | Action

High (>50%) | Visible CBD stone on US/CT, clinical cholangitis, or bilirubin >4 mg/dL + dilated CBD (>6mm) | Proceed directly to ERCP

Intermediate (10-50%) | Dilated CBD (>6mm) without visible stone, bilirubin 1.8-4, abnormal LFTs, age >55, gallstone pancreatitis | Get MRCP/EUS

Low (<10%) | Normal CBD diameter, normal bilirubin, normal LFTs | Proceed to cholecystectomy

Sphincterotomy + balloon/basket extraction achieves >95% stone clearance. Timing: cholangitis Grade II-III within 24 hours (or emergent); non-emergent within 48-72 hours of diagnosis. Complications occur in 5-10% (post-ERCP pancreatitis 3-5% — rectal indomethacin reduces risk; bleeding 1-2%; perforation 0.5-1%). Cholecystectomy after ERCP (same admission or within 2 weeks) reduces recurrent biliary events (17% → 3%).

Cholecystectomy timing

Early cholecystectomy within 72 hours ("Golden 72") — or even up to 10 days — is safe with no increased operative difficulty and morbidity 15-20%. Delayed surgery (>2 weeks) carries higher morbidity 25-30%, longer stay, higher cost, and recurrent biliary events — avoid if possible.

  • Early surgery: NPO, IV fluids, antibiotics continued to OR, laparoscopic cholecystectomy (subtotal if anatomy unclear); typical discharge POD 0-1.
  • Watch for bile leak (0.5-1%) and bile duct injury (0.3-0.5%) after cholecystectomy.
  • Delayed pathway: percutaneous cholecystostomy bridge, complete antibiotics, optimize comorbidities, interval cholecystectomy 6-8 weeks.
  • Non-surgical: complete 7-14 day antibiotic course, percutaneous cholecystostomy for source control, consider chronic tube if recurrent decline of surgery.
