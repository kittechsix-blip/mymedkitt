Testicular Torsion — ED Management

Acute scrotal pain in the ED — all ages (adult, adolescent, pre-pubertal, neonatal). Excludes chronic/non-acute scrotal complaints. Torsion is a time-critical surgical diagnosis; imaging and consult must never delay surgical contact.

⚠ Time is testicle: salvage >90% if detorsed within 6 hours of pain onset, ~50% by 12 hours, near zero by 24 hours.
⚠ TWIST score risk-stratifies before imaging: hard testis (2), swelling (2), nausea/vomiting (1), absent cremasteric reflex (1), high-riding testis (1) — total 0-7.
⚠ TWIST 5-7 (PPV ~93-100%) → urology/OR now; do not wait for ultrasound if the surgeon is willing to operate on clinical findings.
⚠ TWIST 3-4 → STAT Doppler ultrasound AND call urology in parallel; imaging must never delay surgical consult.
⚠ Absent flow / whirlpool sign on Doppler = torsion. Equivocal Doppler in a clinically suspicious patient is a surgical indication, NOT a discharge criterion — false-negative US occurs in spontaneous detorsion and partial torsion.
⚠ Confirmed torsion → page urology immediately, OR-bound; document time of onset, presentation, imaging, and surgical contact.
⚠ Even with full clinical and Doppler resolution after manual detorsion, the patient still needs urgent bilateral orchidopexy — approximately 30% will retort.
⚠ Document time of pain onset → presentation → ultrasound → surgical contact → OR — testicular torsion is one of the highest-litigation diagnoses in urology.
⚠ Age determines the pathway: neonate (<30 days) = extravaginal torsion, salvage near zero but bilateral exploration is still standard; pre-pubertal = intravaginal torsion with a presentation that may be obscured; adolescent = classic presentation.
⚠ Neonatal torsion: firm, non-tender, discolored hemiscrotum. Immediate pediatric urology consult for bilateral exploration even though the affected side is rarely salvageable — contralateral orchidopexy prevents future loss.
⚠ Pre-pubertal torsion: sudden abdominal pain or vomiting without a clear scrotal complaint still requires a testicular exam; the cremasteric reflex is less reliable in young children — keep a lower threshold for OR than in adults.
⚠ Post-traumatic scrotal pain: do not assume the finding is a hematoma — Doppler is essential to rule out testicular rupture (a surgical emergency) and post-traumatic torsion.

▶ What to do now

TWIST 5-7 (high) → straight to OR
PPV ~93-100% for torsion. Urology / pediatric urology consult NOW. Do not wait for ultrasound if the surgeon is willing to operate on clinical findings. IV access, NPO, pain control, antiemetic, consent/labs per surgeon. Manual detorsion while arranging OR.
TWIST 3-4 (intermediate) → Doppler ultrasound

TWIST 0-2 (low) → torsion unlikely, find alternative

Caveat: low TWIST does NOT exclude torsion in classic clinical scenarios (sudden severe pain, vomiting, high-riding testis). When in doubt, get imaging.

Often missed at first newborn exam. Workup: scrotal Doppler US to confirm and rule out alternatives (incarcerated inguinal hernia, neonatal hydrocele, tumor). High medicolegal risk — document exam at birth, time of recognition, and time of surgical contact.

Manual detorsion

Manual detorsion is a temporizing maneuver. It does not replace surgical exploration — even successful detorsion still requires bilateral orchidopexy. Indications: confirmed or strongly suspected torsion with delayed OR access (>30 min), patient cooperative. Contraindications: patient unwilling, severe agitation/non-cooperation, surgeon already in OR or arriving in <15 min.

Atypical presentations

Atypical presentations: intermittent torsion/detorsion, torsion of appendix testis, missed/late torsion (>24h), and post-traumatic torsion.

Pattern: episodes of acute scrotal pain that resolve spontaneously within minutes to hours, may have a "bell clapper" deformity. Safety-net: return immediately for sustained severe pain.

Late presentation (>24h)Orchiectomy likely, contralateral protection, fertility counseling

After 24h the testicle is rarely salvageable. Doppler usually shows no flow with a heterogeneous, enlarged testis. Still consult urology — necrotic testis is a source of infection and can autoimmunize the contralateral testis ("sympathetic orchitis"). Orchiectomy + contralateral orchidopexy is the standard. Counsel family on fertility implications and high probability of orchiectomy.

Possible appendix torsionSupportive care, blue dot sign, normal Doppler

Findings: focal upper-pole tenderness, blue dot sign through scrotal skin, normal cremasteric reflex, normal Doppler flow. Treatment: supportive — NSAIDs, scrotal support, ice. Resolves in 5-10 days. Caveat: if Doppler is at all equivocal or the exam is not classic, treat as torsion until proven otherwise.

Post-traumatic scrotal pain
Trauma can trigger torsion. Do not assume scrotal pain after trauma is just hematoma. Doppler is essential to evaluate for testicular rupture (loss of contour, intratesticular hematoma — surgical emergency), hematocele, or post-traumatic torsion. If Doppler findings unclear or rupture suspected → urology now.

Alternative diagnosis

Common alternatives: Epididymitis (increased flow, gradual onset, dysuria, +cremasteric — see the adult UTI protocol for antibiotics); Torsed appendix testis (focal upper-pole tenderness, blue dot sign, supportive); Orchitis (viral/mumps, bacterial spread); Fournier gangrene (perineum erythema, crepitus, systemic toxicity — emergent surgical consult, broad antibiotics, debridement); Incarcerated hernia (bowel content in scrotum, palpable inguinal mass). Document the alternative dx and treatment plan. Discuss return precautions for new severe pain.

Disposition and follow-up

OR-bound patients: admit to surgery, ED disposition complete. Discharged patients (alternative dx, intermittent torsion pending elective surgery): strict return precautions (any sudden severe pain → return to ED immediately), urology follow-up within 1-2 weeks for elective fixation if intermittent, pain control (NSAIDs ± short opioid course if severe), activity restrictions per urology preference.

  • Time of onset to time of evaluation to time of surgeon contact
  • TWIST score (or rationale for bypassing)
  • Doppler interpretation
  • If discharged: explicit return precautions, ability to follow up, parent/patient verbalized understanding
Pitfalls & medicolegal pearlsTop errors that lead to bad outcomes in testicular torsion
  1. Adolescent with abdominal pain alone — ALWAYS exam the testes; torsion frequently presents as periumbilical or LLQ pain
  2. Assuming epididymitis in a young patient with negative sexual history, no dysuria, sudden onset — think torsion
  3. Trusting a normal Doppler in a high-TWIST patient — false-negative US is real (spontaneous detorsion, partial torsion); clinical exam wins
  4. Delaying urology consult while waiting for imaging — call early, image in parallel
  5. Discharging because pain resolved — intermittent torsion rebounds; refer urology before discharge
  6. Not documenting times — torsion is the #1 urological malpractice diagnosis

Management algorithm

Acute scrotal pain — torsion? — Time is testicle: <6h salvage >90%, ~50% by 12h, near zero by 24h.

TWIST score — 5-finding risk score, 0-7 points, stratifies torsion risk before imaging.

High (5-7) → OR track — PPV 93-100%. Urology now; do not wait for US. Morphine 0.1mg/kg IV, NPO, IV access.

Intermediate (3-4) → Doppler US — STAT Doppler + parallel urology call. Imaging must not delay surgical consult.

Low (0-2) → alternatives, but imaging if any doubt — NPV ~96%. Does NOT exclude torsion in classic scenarios.

Doppler ultrasound result — Sens 86-100%, spec 100% in experienced hands. Look for whirlpool sign, absent flow, heterogeneous parenchyma.

Absent flow / whirlpool → confirmed — Page urology immediately, OR-bound. Document all timestamps — high-litigation diagnosis.

Equivocal → surgical exploration — Equivocal Doppler in a clinically suspicious patient is a surgical indication, NOT a discharge criterion.

Normal flow, alt dx → treat alternative — Epididymitis, torsed appendix testis, orchitis, Fournier gangrene, incarcerated hernia. Document plan + return precautions.
