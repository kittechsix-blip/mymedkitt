Recurrent & Cyclical Vomiting — ED Management

Adults (including pregnant patients) in the ED with recurrent, cyclical, or hyperemetic vomiting. Excludes pediatrics. Hyperemesis gravidarum, cannabinoid hyperemesis syndrome (CHS), gastroparesis, and cyclic vomiting syndrome (CVS) are all diagnoses of exclusion — a can't-miss organic cause (obstruction, DKA, raised ICP, adrenal crisis) must be screened for and treated on its own pathway before a functional syndrome is diagnosed. CHS dosing detail lives in the dedicated CHS consult; this hub recognizes and routes to it, not duplicates it.

⚠ βhCG in every reproductive-age woman before imaging or teratogen exposure.
⚠ Bilious or bloody vomiting is a red flag for obstruction / GI bleed — treat as organic until proven otherwise, NOT a functional syndrome.
⚠ Vomiting can be the presenting sign of DKA, raised ICP, or adrenal crisis — do not anchor on a GI cause.
⚠ Give thiamine 100 mg IV BEFORE any dextrose-containing fluid in any patient with prolonged poor intake — glucose first can precipitate Wernicke encephalopathy.
⚠ CHS: standard antiemetics and opioids typically FAIL — treatment is topical capsaicin, haloperidol/droperidol, benzodiazepines, and cannabis cessation.
⚠ Avoid opioids in gastroparesis and cannabinoid hyperemesis flares — they worsen gastric emptying and perpetuate the cycle.

Stabilize & screen for organic causes

Cyclic vomiting syndrome, cannabinoid hyperemesis, and functional vomiting are diagnoses of exclusion — correct dehydration and electrolyte/acid–base derangement first, then exclude the can't-miss organic causes.

  • βhCG in every reproductive-age woman before imaging or teratogen exposure.
  • Bilious or bloody vomiting is a red flag — think obstruction / surgical cause until proven otherwise.
  • Vomiting can be the presenting sign of DKA, raised ICP, or adrenal crisis — don't anchor on a GI cause.
  • Thiamine before dextrose in any patient with prolonged poor intake — glucose first can precipitate Wernicke.
  • Bilious or hematemesis → obstruction / GI bleed
  • Headache + focal neuro deficit / papilledema → raised ICP, posterior-fossa mass
  • Severe or localized abdominal pain, peritonitis, distension, no flatus → surgical abdomen
  • Shock, hypotension + hyponatremia/hyperkalemia → adrenal crisis
  • Polyuria, Kussmaul, fruity breath → DKA
  • Age >50 with new symptoms, fever, weight loss

Labs: CBC, CMP (glucose, Na, K, Ca, BUN/Cr), lipase, LFTs, VBG + serum ketones + lactate, urine βhCG (all reproductive-age women), UA. Add TSH/cortisol/ammonia when indicated.

ECG — electrolyte effects and a baseline QTc before QT-prolonging antiemetics (ondansetron, haloperidol, droperidol, promethazine).

Resuscitation: isotonic crystalloid; replace K/Mg; give thiamine before any dextrose-containing fluid.

Organic causes excluded. Pick the pathway: pregnant (positive βhCG) → HG · chronic heavy cannabis ± hot-water bathing → CHS · delayed gastric emptying / diabetic / post-surgical → gastroparesis · stereotypical episodes, well in between → CVS.

Red flag → organic workup, not a functional syndrome

Return to the recurrent-vomiting triage only once organic causes are excluded.
HG, tolerating PO → outpatient

Hyperemesis Gravidarum

HG is a diagnosis of exclusion — persistent vomiting not attributable to another cause plus objective markers separating it from ordinary NVP: weight loss >5% of prepregnancy weight, large ketonuria, electrolyte/thyroid/liver derangement. Grade with PUQE (mild <6 · moderate 7–12 · severe ≥13). Rule out molar/multiple gestation, thyrotoxicosis, DKA, pyelonephritis, hepatitis, pancreatitis.

Pyridoxine (B6)
10–25 mg PO q6–8h
PO
Step 1 first-line, ± doxylamine — the only FDA-approved NVP combination. Cap total daily intake at ≤100 mg/day for prolonged use (sensory neuropathy reported below 500 mg/day).
Doxylamine
12.5 mg PO TID
PO
Combined with pyridoxine — the only FDA-approved NVP combination.

Step 2 (antiemetics): Ondansetron, Promethazine, Metoclopramide, Prochlorperazine. Counsel on the ondansetron first-trimester nuance — a small possible signal for cardiac septal/oral-cleft defects if used before 10 weeks.

Isotonic crystalloid or LR, up to ~2 L over 2 h, then titrate to urine output ≥100 mL/h; replace K as needed. IV antiemetic (ondansetron / metoclopramide / promethazine). Admit for persistent intolerance, electrolyte derangement, or weight loss.

Thiamine
100 mg IV once, BEFORE any dextrose-containing fluid
IV
Glucose in a thiamine-deficient patient precipitates Wernicke encephalopathy.
Methylprednisolone
16 mg PO/IV q8h × 3 days, then taper over ~2 weeks (total course ≤6 weeks)
PO/IV
Last-resort for HG refractory to fluids + antiemetics. AVOID before 10 weeks gestation — association with orofacial clefts. Consider enteral (NJ) nutrition before parenteral; monitor for refeeding syndrome.
Thiamine (suspected Wernicke)
500 mg IV q8h × 3 days
IV
Continue thiamine repletion in refractory HG with suspected Wernicke encephalopathy.

Cannabinoid Hyperemesis Syndrome

Recognize the triad: stereotypical episodic vomiting + prolonged heavy cannabis use + relief with cessation. Compulsive hot-water bathing (>41°C) is near-pathognomonic (~92%). Key differentiator: standard antiemetics and opioids typically fail — treatment is topical capsaicin, haloperidol/droperidol (0.05–0.1 mg/kg IV, HaVOC RCT superior to ondansetron), benzodiazepines, and definitive cannabis cessation. Always screen CVS patients for cannabis — cessation can reclassify and cure. Open the full CHS consult for capsaicin technique and QTc handling detail.

Gastroparesis

Delayed gastric emptying without mechanical obstruction. Cardinal symptoms: nausea, vomiting, early satiety, postprandial fullness, bloating. Symptom severity does not correlate with degree of delay. Gold standard: 4-hour solid-phase gastric-emptying scintigraphy (>10% retention at 4 h) — exclude obstruction (EGD/imaging) first. Causes: diabetic, idiopathic (largest group), post-surgical (vagal injury), medication-induced (opioids, GLP-1 agonists, anticholinergics, CCBs).

Dietary modification (small, frequent, low-fat/low-insoluble-fiber), optimize glycemia (hyperglycemia acutely slows emptying), prokinetics + antiemetics. AVOID opioids — they worsen emptying and perpetuate the cycle. Refractory: gastric electrical stimulation, G-POEM; pyloric botox is NOT recommended.

Metoclopramide
5 mg PO/IV TID before meals
PO/IV
BLACK-BOX tardive dyskinesia — limit total duration to ≤12 weeks. First-line prokinetic per ACG 2022.
Erythromycin
3 mg/kg IV q8h (acute flare)
IV
The most potent available prokinetic; best for hospitalized acute gastroparesis. Tachyphylaxis limits chronic use.

Cyclic Vomiting Syndrome

Rome IV: stereotypical episodes of acute vomiting lasting <7 days; ≥3 episodes in the prior year (≥2 in last 6 months) separated by ≥1 week of baseline health; well between episodes. Supportive: personal/family history of migraine. 4 phases: inter-episodic (well) → prodrome (window for abortive tx) → emetic → recovery. Always ask about cannabis — chronic use + hot-water bathing reclassifies to CHS, which is cured by cessation.

Sumatriptan
20 mg intranasal (or 6 mg SC)
IN/SC
Take as early as possible in the prodrome.
Ondansetron
8 mg IV
IV
Aggressive antiemetic backbone during the acute emetic phase. Check baseline QTc; correct K/Mg. Max 16 mg/day.

IV fluids WITH dextrose (e.g., D10-containing crystalloid) + K repletion — aborts the catabolic/ketotic state. Sedation with a benzodiazepine (± diphenhydramine, no formal dose given for this use) to break the cycle.

Aprepitant
125 mg day 1, then 80 mg days 2–3
PO
Second-line NK-1 antagonist for refractory acute episode.
Amitriptyline
Start 10–25 mg qHS, titrate to 75–100 mg/day (≈1–1.5 mg/kg)
PO
First-line prophylaxis for ≥4 episodes/year or severe disease. Baseline ECG for QTc.

Alternatives: topiramate ~100 mg/day, propranolol 20–40 mg BID. Mitochondrial supplements: CoQ10 ~100 mg TID, L-carnitine ~1000 mg BID, riboflavin ~400 mg/day.

Antiemetic Safety & Disposition

  • QTc check before stacking QT-prolongers (ondansetron, haloperidol, droperidol, promethazine, domperidone).
  • Metoclopramide — tardive dyskinesia risk; ≤12 weeks (gastroparesis), ~5 days (HG per EMA).
  • Avoid opioids in CHS and gastroparesis flares.
  • Thiamine before dextrose in any prolonged poor-intake patient.
Discharge / admit
Discharge when tolerating PO with normalized electrolytes and a follow-up plan (OB for HG, GI/neurogastro for gastroparesis/CVS, cessation support for CHS). Admit for refractory vomiting, ongoing electrolyte/acid–base derangement, or an unresolved organic cause.
