Syphilis — ED Evaluation & Treatment

Adults in the ED with suspected or confirmed syphilis — genital/oral lesion, diffuse rash, positive screening test, partner exposure, neurologic/ocular/otic symptoms, or pregnancy. Excludes pediatric/congenital syphilis and the full neurosyphilis CSF workup (see linked Neurosyphilis consult).

⚠ Ocular or otic syphilis = neurosyphilis-level treatment regardless of CSF findings — do NOT delay IV penicillin G for LP results.
⚠ Pregnancy: penicillin is the ONLY acceptable treatment for syphilis — desensitization is MANDATORY if penicillin-allergic; no alternative regimen prevents congenital syphilis.
⚠ Benzathine penicillin G does NOT achieve treponemicidal CSF levels — inadequate for neurosyphilis, ocular syphilis, or otosyphilis.
⚠ Azithromycin is NOT recommended for syphilis — documented macrolide resistance and treatment failures in the US.
⚠ Partner exposure within the preceding 90 days: treat presumptively even if seronegative.
⚠ Jarisch-Herxheimer reaction in pregnancy can precipitate preterm labor — monitor with continuous fetal heart rate monitoring ×24h if viable gestational age; do not delay treatment because of it.
⚠ Treponemal tests (TP-PA, FTA-ABS, EIA, CIA) remain positive for life — cannot distinguish active from past treated infection.
⚠ Doxycycline does not achieve adequate CSF penetration — rule out neurosyphilis before treating late latent/tertiary syphilis with doxycycline.

Syphilis — ED Evaluation Pathway

207,255 total US cases in 2022 (increased 755% from 2012-2022) — 59,233 primary/secondary cases. Known as "the great imitator." Route by presentation: lesion/rash, positive screen, partner notification, neuro/ocular/otic symptoms, or pregnancy. [1][2][4]

Neurosyphilis screen — full ocular/otic/meningeal/late/vascular differentialNeurosyphilis can occur at ANY stage — this is the safety-critical entry gate
Screen for these findings before answering
Neurosyphilis can occur at ANY stage of syphilis infection. Ocular and otosyphilis are clinical diagnoses that require neurosyphilis-level treatment regardless of CSF findings.

Ocular: uveitis (most common), vision loss, floaters, disc edema, retinitis

Otic: hearing loss (conductive or sensorineural), tinnitus, vertigo

Meningeal: headache, stiff neck, photophobia, CN VI/VII/VIII palsies

Late neuro: cognitive decline, personality changes, Argyll Robertson pupils, tabes dorsalis ("foot-slapping" gait), general paresis

Vascular: stuttering stroke-like symptoms from meningovascular disease

Any of the above → answer "yes" and route to the neurosyphilis pathway. [1][6][7]

Risk Factors, Partner Notification & Testing

High-risk populations: MSM — 56% of primary/secondary cases (rate 240× higher than women); HIV co-infection (mutual 2-5× facilitation); persons on PrEP; multiple partners; IVDU; prior STI history. Mandatory co-testing: HIV Ag/Ab (4th gen), GC/CT NAAT, HepB surface Ag/Ab/core Ab, HepC Ab. [1][2][12][17][18]

ED lab orders & point-of-care testing

Standard ED workup: RPR (quantitative, reflects disease activity) + treponemal test (TP-PA or FTA-ABS) if not already reflexed. Co-testing as above. If neurologic symptoms: LP for CSF-VDRL, cell count, protein. Window period: NTTs become reactive 1-4 weeks after chancre appears; TTs seroconvert slightly earlier — very early primary syphilis can be seronegative. [6][16]

Point-of-care testing: Rapid treponemal tests — Sensitivity 85-98%, specificity 95-99%. Still require NTT (RPR) for staging/monitoring; TT positive for life regardless of assay. [19][20]

Neurosyphilis pathway (non-pregnant)

Neurosyphilis in pregnancy
Penicillin is the ONLY acceptable treatment — desensitize if allergic (no ceftriaxone alternative accepted in pregnancy). Continuous fetal monitoring ×24h if viable gestational age (Jarisch-Herxheimer can precipitate preterm labor). [3][11]
Pregnancy — treat by stage

Disposition & Follow-up

Discharge — uncomplicated syphilis. Mandatory: file public health report (all 50 states) + partner notification. Counsel: no sex ×7 days + lesion resolution; return for vision/hearing changes or neuro symptoms. Follow-up: STI clinic 1-2 weeks; RPR at 6, 12, 24 months; HIV result if pending. [5][15]
