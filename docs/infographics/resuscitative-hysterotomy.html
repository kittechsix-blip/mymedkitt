Resuscitative Hysterotomy: 4-Minute Decision to Delivery

Maternal cardiac arrest with gestational age ≥20 weeks (fundus at/above umbilicus) or gestational age unassessable, at the site of arrest in the ED/resuscitation bay. Performed by the senior physician present — do not wait for obstetrics. Excludes confirmed gestational age <20 weeks (fundus below umbilicus) and peri-arrest (not-yet-arrested) optimization only.

⚠ Goal is maternal resuscitation, not fetal rescue — delivery relieves aortocaval compression and restores maternal preload; this is part of the resuscitation itself.
⚠ Do NOT wait for obstetrics and do NOT move the patient to the OR — the senior EM physician performs resuscitative hysterotomy at the bedside, at the site of arrest.
⚠ Manual LUD (two-handed, continuous, up-and-left) is mandatory during arrest — do NOT use tilt/board positioning; tilt degrades chest compression quality.
⚠ Have a scalpel at the bedside within 60 seconds of confirmed arrest; establish continuous manual LUD and start the timer immediately.
⚠ No ROSC by 4 minutes → begin incision now. Goal: fetus delivered by 5 minutes. If already past 4 minutes and only now realizing the need — start immediately regardless of elapsed time.
⚠ No absolute contraindications during arrest and no consent is required — do NOT move to the OR, wait for OB, use sterile prep/drapes, or give anesthesia; continue CPR throughout.
⚠ A scalpel is the only essential tool — do NOT delay incision to wait for an OB tray or additional equipment.
⚠ Uterine atony is near-universal after resuscitative hysterotomy — activate massive transfusion and start uterotonics immediately after ROSC.
⚠ Do NOT give oxytocin as an IV push — infusion only; IV push causes profound hypotension and can re-arrest a post-ROSC patient. Methylergonovine is contraindicated in hypertension/preeclampsia/eclampsia; carboprost is contraindicated in any history of asthma.
⚠ Timekeeper calls out elapsed time every 30 seconds — without a loud, dedicated timekeeper the 4-minute decision point reliably slips past unnoticed.

1 · Recognition & Indications

Resuscitative hysterotomy (RH) — formerly perimortem cesarean delivery — is emergent delivery of the fetus during maternal cardiac arrest. The goal is maternal resuscitation, not fetal rescue: aortocaval decompression after delivery increases cardiac preload by 60-80%, often the single maneuver that achieves ROSC.

Indicated when ALL of the following are true: maternal cardiac arrest, estimated gestational age ≥20 weeks or fundus palpable at/above the umbilicus, and no ROSC within ~4 minutes of high-quality CPR with LUD.

The senior EM physician at bedside decides — do NOT wait for OB. Obstetricians do not routinely perform this outside the OR. Perform at the site of arrest — do NOT move the patient to the OR.

  1. Maternal cardiac arrest — pulselessness requiring CPR
  2. Estimated gestational age ≥20 weeks OR fundus palpable at/above the umbilicus
  3. No ROSC within ~4 minutes of high-quality CPR with LUD

Fundal height is a rapid bedside surrogate for gestational age: below umbilicus → <20 weeks, standard ACLS applies; at umbilicus → ~20 weeks, threshold for RH; above umbilicus → >20 weeks, RH strongly indicated in arrest; fundus at xiphoid → ~36-38 weeks (term).

If unsure, assume ≥20 weeks and proceed. The cost of an unnecessary hysterotomy in a truly pre-viable patient (who is already dying) is minimal. The cost of not performing it in a viable pregnancy is catastrophic.

4 · Optimize Before/During Arrest

LUD IS MANDATORY for every pregnant patient ≥20 weeks with hemodynamic compromise or arrest. Manual LUD (preferred during arrest): one provider stands on the patient's left, two-handed grip, pull the uterus up and to the left, maintain continuously — a dedicated role, not a periodic maneuver.

Avoid tilted-board/30° tilt during arrest — tilting degrades chest compression quality. Manual LUD maintains flat supine positioning for effective CPR.

IV access above the diaphragm (upper extremity or neck, not femoral) — drugs given below the compression point may not reach central circulation. Airway: anticipate difficult (rapid desaturation, mucosal edema, aspiration risk) — video laryngoscopy first-line, smaller ETT (6.5 or 7.0).

Why pregnancy physiology matters — outcomes dataAortocaval compression syndrome, Einav 2012 outcomes

In the supine position after ~20 weeks, the gravid uterus compresses the IVC (reduced venous return, reduced cardiac output up to 30%) and the abdominal aorta. During CPR this makes chest compressions functionally ineffective. Delivery evacuates the uterus, restoring preload within seconds and making CPR effective again.

Outcomes (Einav 2012 systematic review, 94 cases): maternal survival to discharge 54.3%; good neurologic outcome (CPC 1-2) among survivors 78.4%; maternal harm attributable to RH 0%; maternal ROSC documented up to 29 min post-arrest; neonatal survival up to 47 min.

Pre-arrest optimization (if still perfusing)LUD, O2, fluids, cause-finding, scalpel ready

If decompensating but not yet arrested: LUD always, high-flow O2 (maternal SpO2 <95% causes fetal hypoxemia), 1-2L crystalloid bolus unless pulmonary edema, treat reversible causes, call for help (OB/anesthesia/neonatology/blood bank), bedside ultrasound. Have the scalpel at bedside if peri-arrest — do not wait for arrest to prepare.

5 · Arrest Response & The 4-Minute Clock

All at once (parallel, not sequential): call the code, start high-quality CPR, establish continuous manual LUD, start the timer, page OB/neonatology/anesthesia/blood bank/OR, have scalpel at bedside within 60 seconds, treat reversible causes.

Team roles: leader (foot of bed), compressor (rotate q2min), LUD provider (continuous, left side), airway, IV/meds (upper extremity), recorder/timekeeper, neonatal team (warmer on, NRP ready).

ACLS modifications in pregnancySame drugs/energies; LUD, IV siting, airway, 4-min clock differ

Compressions: standard hand position, depth/rate identical (5-6 cm, 100-120/min). Defibrillation: same energies (200J biphasic), standard pad placement, remove fetal monitors before shocking. Drugs: all standard ACLS drugs at standard doses — epinephrine 1 mg IV/IO q3-5 min as usual, no dose adjustments.

What IS different: continuous manual LUD, IV access above the diaphragm, difficult airway anticipation, 4-minute decision point for RH, seek reversible causes (BEAU-CHOPS).

Timekeeper calls out: every 30 seconds; at 2 min — rhythm/pulse check; at 3 min — consider hysterotomy preparation; at 4 min — DECISION POINT, loud and clear; at 5 min — if no ROSC, incision now.

Median time from arrest to RH in registries is ~10 minutes, not 4-5 — because no one tracked it. Use a visible clock; do not rely on memory.

The rule (Katz 1986, refined by SOAP 2014, AHA 2023): if no ROSC at ~4 minutes, begin incision; goal is fetus delivered by 5 minutes — the 4-minute decision / 5-minute delivery rule.

Current evidence: Rose 2015 registry data shows maternal benefit even when RH is performed at 10, 15, even 20+ minutes — later is still better than never. The 4-minute rule is a starting gun, not a finish line: if you are past 4 minutes and have not started, start now regardless of elapsed time.

6 · The Procedure

Do NOT: move to the OR, wait for OB, prep with betadine/drapes/sterile technique, give anesthesia, stop CPR.

Do: perform at the bedside now, continue CPR throughout, continue LUD until the uterus is opened, have the neonatal team at the warmer. Informed consent is not required — this is emergent life-saving surgery. No absolute contraindications during arrest — prior C-section, anticoagulation, infection are not contraindications when the patient is dying.

Minimum equipment: #10 or #20 scalpel — the only essential tool. Everything else (gloves, suction, towels, cord clamps, retractor) is optional and should not delay incision. Neonatal team separately readies: warmer, BVM, suction bulb, pulse oximeter, ETT 2.5/3.0/3.5, epinephrine dosing chart.

StepLandmarks / lengthTechnique
SkinXiphoid to symphysis pubis, 15-20 cmOne firm, deep stroke — skin → fat → fascia → muscle → peritoneum. Do not make repeated small strokes.
UterusMid-fundus, vertical, 10-15 cmShallow 1-2 cm initial cut to avoid fetal laceration; feel for fetal parts/sac; extend with scissors or fingers as a guide.

Vertical (classical), not Pfannenstiel/low-transverse — faster, avoids bladder mobilization, exposes the full uterus, works regardless of placental location. Time from decision to skin incision should be <60 seconds. If the incision meets an anterior placenta, go through it — do not stop.

Cup the fetal head, identify vertex/breech/transverse lie, deliver the presenting part first, gentle fundal pressure from above if needed. Target: fetus delivered within 5 minutes total of maternal arrest. Pass the fetus to the neonatal team and remain focused on the mother.

  1. Two clamps (or hemostats) on the cord, 2-3 cm apart; cut between — no delayed clamping
  2. Gentle cord traction with counter-pressure on the fundus to deliver the placenta
  3. Manual extraction if placenta does not deliver within 1-2 minutes
  4. Do NOT close — pack the open abdomen/uterus with towels or saline gauze
  5. Continue CPR and ACLS medications — watch for ROSC, which often happens here

7 · Neonatal Assessment

Assess in the first 30 seconds: term appearance, tone, breathing or crying. Vigorous (breathing, good tone, crying) → routine care. Any concern → initiate NRP. APGAR at 1 and 5 minutes.

Warm, dry, stimulate. Pulse oximetry (right hand, preductal). NICU evaluation regardless of initial appearance — the neonate was exposed to maternal arrest hypoxia.

  1. Warm, dry, stimulate — do not delay PPV if HR <100
  2. PPV 40-60 breaths/min if HR <100 or apneic — room air (term) or 30% O2 (preterm)
  3. MR-SOPA if PPV inadequate: Mask, Reposition, Suction, Open mouth, Pressure increase, Alternate airway
  4. Chest compressions 3:1 with breaths if HR <60 despite PPV; intubate
  5. Epinephrine 0.01-0.03 mg/kg IV/IO if HR <60 despite PPV + compressions
  6. APGAR at 1, 5, 10 min — continue q5min until ≥7 or age 20 min

8 · Maternal Reassessment & Post-ROSC Care

Reassess the mother at the next pulse check. Delivery has restored preload — this is the moment ROSC most commonly occurs. Check pulse (carotid/femoral), ETCO2 (sudden rise suggests ROSC), rhythm, blood pressure.

Immediate priorities (next 15 minutes): hemostasis (uterine atony is near-universal), activate massive transfusion (expected blood loss 1-2+ L), airway secured, hemodynamic support, identify and treat the cause, prophylactic antibiotics, definitive surgical closure in the OR.

Targeted temperature management 32-36°C for 24 hours — safe in postpartum. Maintain SBP ≥90, MAP ≥65. ICU admission with MFM/ICU/anesthesia co-management.

Calcium Chloride: 1 g IV central line only — Magnesium toxicity arrest; use when central access available (3x more elemental calcium than gluconate).

Timekeeper calls out: every 30 seconds; at 2 min — rhythm/pulse check; at 3 min — consider hysterotomy preparation; at 4 min — DECISION POINT, loud and clear; at 5 min — if no ROSC, incision now.

Median time from arrest to RH in registries is ~10 minutes, not 4-5 — because no one tracked it. Use a visible clock; do not rely on memory.

The rule (Katz 1986, refined by SOAP 2014, AHA 2023): if no ROSC at ~4 minutes, begin incision; goal is fetus delivered by 5 minutes — the 4-minute decision / 5-minute delivery rule.

Current evidence: Rose 2015 registry data shows maternal benefit even when RH is performed at 10, 15, even 20+ minutes — later is still better than never. The 4-minute rule is a starting gun, not a finish line: if you are past 4 minutes and have not started, start now regardless of elapsed time.

6 · The Procedure

Do NOT: move to the OR, wait for OB, prep with betadine/drapes/sterile technique, give anesthesia, stop CPR.

Do: perform at the bedside now, continue CPR throughout, continue LUD until the uterus is opened, have the neonatal team at the warmer. Informed consent is not required — this is emergent life-saving surgery. No absolute contraindications during arrest — prior C-section, anticoagulation, infection are not contraindications when the patient is dying.

Minimum equipment: #10 or #20 scalpel — the only essential tool. Everything else (gloves, suction, towels, cord clamps, retractor) is optional and should not delay incision. Neonatal team separately readies: warmer, BVM, suction bulb, pulse oximeter, ETT 2.5/3.0/3.5, epinephrine dosing chart.

Step | Landmarks / length | Technique

Skin | Xiphoid to symphysis pubis, 15-20 cm | One firm, deep stroke — skin → fat → fascia → muscle → peritoneum. Do not make repeated small strokes.

Uterus | Mid-fundus, vertical, 10-15 cm | Shallow 1-2 cm initial cut to avoid fetal laceration; feel for fetal parts/sac; extend with scissors or fingers as a guide.

Vertical (classical), not Pfannenstiel/low-transverse — faster, avoids bladder mobilization, exposes the full uterus, works regardless of placental location. Time from decision to skin incision should be <60 seconds. If the incision meets an anterior placenta, go through it — do not stop.

Cup the fetal head, identify vertex/breech/transverse lie, deliver the presenting part first, gentle fundal pressure from above if needed. Target: fetus delivered within 5 minutes total of maternal arrest. Pass the fetus to the neonatal team and remain focused on the mother.

  • Two clamps (or hemostats) on the cord, 2-3 cm apart; cut between — no delayed clamping
  • Gentle cord traction with counter-pressure on the fundus to deliver the placenta
  • Manual extraction if placenta does not deliver within 1-2 minutes
  • Do NOT close — pack the open abdomen/uterus with towels or saline gauze
  • Continue CPR and ACLS medications — watch for ROSC, which often happens here

7 · Neonatal Assessment

Assess in the first 30 seconds: term appearance, tone, breathing or crying. Vigorous (breathing, good tone, crying) → routine care. Any concern → initiate NRP. APGAR at 1 and 5 minutes.

Warm, dry, stimulate. Pulse oximetry (right hand, preductal). NICU evaluation regardless of initial appearance — the neonate was exposed to maternal arrest hypoxia.

  • Warm, dry, stimulate — do not delay PPV if HR <100
  • PPV 40-60 breaths/min if HR <100 or apneic — room air (term) or 30% O2 (preterm)
  • MR-SOPA if PPV inadequate: Mask, Reposition, Suction, Open mouth, Pressure increase, Alternate airway
  • Chest compressions 3:1 with breaths if HR <60 despite PPV; intubate
  • Epinephrine 0.01-0.03 mg/kg IV/IO if HR <60 despite PPV + compressions
  • APGAR at 1, 5, 10 min — continue q5min until ≥7 or age 20 min

Epinephrine (neonatal): 0.01-0.03 mg/kg IV/IO — 1:10,000 (0.1 mg/mL). Repeat q3-5 min. IV/IO preferred over ET route. Only after adequate PPV + chest compressions.

8 · Maternal Reassessment & Post-ROSC Care

Reassess the mother at the next pulse check. Delivery has restored preload — this is the moment ROSC most commonly occurs. Check pulse (carotid/femoral), ETCO2 (sudden rise suggests ROSC), rhythm, blood pressure.

Immediate priorities (next 15 minutes): hemostasis (uterine atony is near-universal), activate massive transfusion (expected blood loss 1-2+ L), airway secured, hemodynamic support, identify and treat the cause, prophylactic antibiotics, definitive surgical closure in the OR.

Targeted temperature management 32-36°C for 24 hours — safe in postpartum. Maintain SBP ≥90, MAP ≥65. ICU admission with MFM/ICU/anesthesia co-management.

Norepinephrine: 0.1-0.5 mcg/kg/min IV infusion — Titrate to MAP ≥65 mmHg. Preferred first-line vasopressor post-ROSC.

Uterine atony is near-universal after RH — the uterus has been cut, the patient was in arrest with no myometrial tone, and postpartum hemorrhage is nearly certain.

First-line uterotonics (start all that apply): oxytocin infusion (never IV push), TXA, bimanual uterine massage. Second-line: misoprostol, methylergonovine (not in hypertension/preeclampsia/eclampsia), carboprost (not in asthma). Continue MTP 1:1:1 until hemostasis.

Oxytocin: 20 units in 1L NS at 250 mL/hr IV infusion — Do NOT give IV push — causes profound hypotension and can re-arrest a post-ROSC patient. Start immediately after placental delivery.

Tranexamic Acid (TXA): 1 g IV over 10 minutes — Repeat once at 30 min if bleeding continues. WOMAN trial: reduced death from bleeding (1.5% vs 1.9%, RR 0.81) — must give within 3 hours.

Misoprostol: 800-1000 mcg PR (rectal) or SL — Second-line. No contraindication in HTN/preeclampsia. No contraindication in asthma.

Methylergonovine (Methergine): 0.2 mg IM — May repeat every 2-4 hours, maximum 5 doses (1 mg total). CONTRAINDICATED in hypertension, preeclampsia, eclampsia.

Carboprost (Hemabate): 250 mcg IM q15-90 min — Maximum 8 doses (2 mg total). CONTRAINDICATED in any history of asthma.

Cefazolin (Ancef): 2 grams IV once — 3 g if weight ≥120 kg. Given after ROSC to cover the non-sterile intra-abdominal field.

The aortocaval decompression maneuver did not achieve ROSC. Continue standard ACLS for at least 10 more minutes post-delivery; reassess and treat reversible causes; consider ECMO or open-chest massage if indicated. Pregnant patients have tolerated prolonged arrest with good outcomes — maternal survival reported after >30 min of CPR.

Immediate: transfer to OR for formal surgical closure, continue MTP until hemostasis, arterial + central line, OB/trauma surgery/anesthesia at bedside. Neonate: Level III NICU, therapeutic hypothermia per protocol if HIE criteria met. Maternal ICU: post-cardiac-arrest bundle, TTM 32-36°C for 24 hours, MFM/ICU/anesthesia co-management.
