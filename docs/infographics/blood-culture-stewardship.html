Blood Culture Stewardship: When to Order, How to Draw, How to Interpret

Adults in the ED being evaluated for possible bacteremia. Excludes neonates/pediatric fever workups and inpatient antimicrobial stewardship committee protocols beyond this bedside decision aid.

⚠ Sepsis / septic shock: obtain 2 blood culture sets from 2 separate peripheral venipuncture sites (20-30 mL total) BEFORE antibiotics — but do NOT delay antibiotic therapy to obtain cultures in critically ill patients.
⚠ Do NOT draw blood cultures through central or indwelling lines unless line infection is suspected — contamination rates are significantly higher when drawn through a catheter.
⚠ Order blood cultures only when the pre-test probability of true bacteremia exceeds the contamination rate (~3%) — reflexive ordering drives false positives, unnecessary vancomycin, prolonged antibiotic courses, and 2-4 extra hospital days per false positive.
⚠ Febrile neutropenia (ANC <500): ALWAYS obtain blood cultures — do not skip cultures in this or other severely immunocompromised populations regardless of how low-risk the fever otherwise appears.
⚠ Single-bottle coagulase-negative staphylococci (CoNS) in a clinically stable patient without a central line is most consistent with contamination — do NOT reflexively start vancomycin for this pattern.

8 · Dispositions

Checklist: 2 sets from 2 separate peripheral venipunctures; 20-30 mL total blood volume; chlorhexidine antisepsis, allow to dry; diversion device if available; draw before antibiotics without delaying antibiotics; document time of draw and sites used. If positive: assess true pathogen vs contaminant, repeat cultures if uncertain, adjust antibiotics by susceptibilities.

Document your reasoning: "Blood cultures not obtained given low pre-test probability of bacteremia, clear source amenable to source-specific cultures, and risk of false-positive exceeding clinical benefit." Alternative diagnostics: urine culture (UTI), wound/abscess culture (SSTI), sputum culture/respiratory panel/procalcitonin (CAP). Reconsider if: clinical deterioration, failure to respond to empiric therapy, or new risk factors identified.

Likely true bacteremia — next steps
Continue/adjust antibiotics by susceptibilities; repeat cultures to document clearance (especially *S. aureus*); search for source (POCUS/CT/echo); evaluate for endocarditis if *S. aureus*; ID consultation for persistent or complicated bacteremia. Duration: *S. aureus* bacteremia minimum 2 weeks IV (longer if endocarditis/metastatic foci); gram-negative bacteremia typically 7-14 days depending on source control. Echo indications: all *S. aureus* bacteremia (TTE +/- TEE), prosthetic valve/device, persistently positive cultures, IV drug use.
Likely contaminant
Assess the patient's clinical trajectory: if stable/improving, contamination is more likely; if deteriorating, consider repeat cultures and broader coverage. For isolated CoNS in a single bottle with <3 SIRS criteria and no central line: 77-85% likely contamination — observation without vancomycin is reasonable, with repeat cultures only if clinical concern. Do NOT reflexively start vancomycin for single-bottle CoNS in a stable patient without a central line. Treat CoNS as a true pathogen when: prosthetic valve/device, multiple positive bottles from separate draws, central venous catheter with ≥2 SIRS criteria, or persistent positive cultures despite antibiotics.

11 · Special populations & 2024 shortage

Special populations5 groups
Febrile neutropenia (ANC <500): ALWAYS culture. Also: solid organ transplant within 1 year, hematologic malignancy on chemotherapy, HIV with CD4 <200, chronic high-dose steroids.
ESRD/hemodialysis: higher bacteremia risk from intravascular devices; same initial fluid boluses recommended despite dialysis status; culture if febrile or systemic symptoms.
Elderly: may not mount a typical fever or SIRS response; lower threshold for cultures if altered baseline or functional decline; Shapiro rule still applicable.
Recent antibiotics: urine may be sterile but blood still positive; consider blood cultures with recent antibiotic exposure and concern for missed bacteremia; draw at antibiotic trough.
IV drug use: high risk for *S. aureus* bacteremia and endocarditis; lower threshold for cultures in a febrile patient who uses IV drugs.
2024 shortage & stewardship responsewhy this matters

The June 2024 blood culture bottle shortage (BD Diagnostics shortage, FDA shortage list, CDC Health Advisory) forced conservation strategies. Stewardship protocols reduced blood culture use without increasing sepsis mortality — many institutions reduced cultures 20-40% with no adverse outcomes. IDSA/ASM 2024 guidance: implement diversion devices, train on optimal technique, use clinical decision support for appropriate indications, avoid cultures through indwelling lines.

2 · Ordering algorithm

What is the clinical scenario? — Order blood cultures when pre-test probability of bacteremia exceeds the contamination rate (~3%).

Sepsis / septic shock — always culture — Severe sepsis ~38%, septic shock ~69% pre-test probability of bacteremia. 2 sets from 2 separate peripheral sites, 20-30 mL total, before antibiotics — do NOT delay antibiotics for culture collection. Diversion device reduces contamination ~64%. Do NOT draw through central lines unless line infection suspected.

Febrile, stable — Shapiro score ≥2 or high suspicion? — Shapiro rule: major criteria (temp >39.4C, indwelling catheter, suspected endocarditis) = 3 pts each; minor (temp 38.3-39.3C, age>65, rigors, vomiting, SBP<90, WBC>18k, bands>5%, platelets<150k, Cr>2.0) = 1 pt each. Score >=2 = 19% bacteremia risk. Score <2 = <1% risk.

Specific infection source identified — which one? — False-positive rate often exceeds true-positive rate for CAP, uncomplicated cellulitis, and simple UTI/pyelonephritis.

Community-acquired pneumonia (non-ICU) — Yield 6-9% (range 0.5-14%). Antibiotics broadened based on cultures in only 0.5-1% of cases. Obtain if: ICU-level severe CAP (14-33% yield), immunocompromised, empyema/cavitation on imaging, deterioration despite empiric therapy, chronic liver disease, asplenia, leukopenia.

Cellulitis / skin & soft tissue infection — Bacteremia rate in simple cellulitis <2% — false-positive rate (~3%) exceeds true-positive rate. Obtain if: suspected necrotizing fasciitis/deep space infection, sepsis physiology, severe infection needing surgery, immunocompromised, CVC/surgical site infection, rapidly progressive cellulitis, bite wounds with systemic symptoms. A wound culture is far more useful than blood cultures for guiding SSTI therapy.

UTI / pyelonephritis — Pre-test probability 19-25%, but blood and urine cultures yield the same organism in >97% of cases, and blood cultures changed management in only 2.3% of cases. Obtain if: sepsis/septic shock, urinary obstruction (stone/catheter/stricture), immunocompromised, recent antibiotics, polymicrobial urine culture, hospital-acquired UTI, or no reliable urine sample available.

Other source (GI/surgical/unknown) — high-risk features present? — Most predictive: rigors (likelihood ratio 4.7 for bacteremia), shaking chills. Other risk factors: indwelling vascular device, immunosuppression, recent hospitalization, IV drug use, chronic liver disease, asplenia, prosthetic valves/joints.

High-yield indication confirmed — Endocarditis (3 sets/3 sites, ~95% yield), meningitis/encephalitis (before or with LP), osteomyelitis/septic arthritis (30-50% positive), HAP/CLABSI/surgical site infection, complicated pyelonephritis with systemic toxicity, febrile neutropenia (ANC <500) — always culture, solid organ transplant within 1 year, active chemotherapy.

Blood cultures NOT recommended — Pre-test probability of bacteremia is low (<3-5%); false-positive rate may exceed true-positive rate; result unlikely to change management. Use source-specific cultures instead (urine, wound, sputum). Reconsider if clinical deterioration, failure of empiric therapy, or new risk factors emerge.

Optimal collection technique — 20-30 mL total blood volume (each additional mL increases yield ~3%). Chlorhexidine/alcohol antisepsis, allow to dry. Diversion device reduces contamination ~64%. 2 peripheral venipunctures preferred — never draw only from an indwelling catheter. Draw before antibiotics when possible; if already on antibiotics, draw at trough.

Proceed with blood cultures — 2 sets from 2 separate peripheral venipunctures, 20-30 mL total, chlorhexidine antisepsis, diversion device if available, before antibiotics (without delaying antibiotics). If positive: assess true pathogen vs contaminant, repeat cultures if uncertain, adjust antibiotics by susceptibilities.

11 · Special populations & 2024 shortage

Special populations

Febrile neutropenia (ANC <500): ALWAYS culture. Also: solid organ transplant within 1 year, hematologic malignancy on chemotherapy, HIV with CD4 <200, chronic high-dose steroids.

ESRD/hemodialysis: higher bacteremia risk from intravascular devices; same initial fluid boluses recommended despite dialysis status; culture if febrile or systemic symptoms.

Elderly: may not mount a typical fever or SIRS response; lower threshold for cultures if altered baseline or functional decline; Shapiro rule still applicable.

Recent antibiotics: urine may be sterile but blood still positive; consider blood cultures with recent antibiotic exposure and concern for missed bacteremia; draw at antibiotic trough.

IV drug use: high risk for *S. aureus* bacteremia and endocarditis; lower threshold for cultures in a febrile patient who uses IV drugs.

2024 shortage & stewardship response

The June 2024 blood culture bottle shortage (BD Diagnostics shortage, FDA shortage list, CDC Health Advisory) forced conservation strategies. Stewardship protocols reduced blood culture use without increasing sepsis mortality — many institutions reduced cultures 20-40% with no adverse outcomes. IDSA/ASM 2024 guidance: implement diversion devices, train on optimal technique, use clinical decision support for appropriate indications, avoid cultures through indwelling lines.

12 · Do NOT

Critical pitfalls

Do NOT delay antibiotics to obtain blood cultures in a critically ill patient — draw cultures before antibiotics only when it does not delay therapy.

Do NOT draw blood cultures through central lines unless line infection is suspected — contamination rates are significantly higher.

Do NOT order blood cultures reflexively for uncomplicated CAP, cellulitis, or UTI — the false-positive rate often exceeds the true-positive rate, and results rarely change management (0.5-1% CAP, 2.3% UTI).

Do NOT reflexively start vancomycin for single-bottle CoNS in a stable patient without a central line — 77-85% likely contamination.

Do NOT skip cultures in febrile neutropenia (ANC <500) or other severely immunocompromised patients regardless of how low-risk the fever otherwise appears.
