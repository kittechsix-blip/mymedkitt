Acute PE: Stratify, Reperfuse, Anticoagulate (2026 AHA/ACC)

Adults ≥18 in the ED/ICU with confirmed or high-probability acute pulmonary embolism. Uses the 2026 AHA/ACC 5-category (A–E) system. Excludes pregnancy-specific DOAC choices, pediatrics, and chronic thromboembolic disease — each needs a modified pathway.

⚠ Crashing PE: AVOID intubation and fluid loading. Positive-pressure ventilation and volume both worsen a dilated, failing RV and can precipitate arrest. Use high-flow O₂, start norepinephrine early, and activate PERT immediately.
⚠ Bradycardia (HR <40) is the single most ominous sign — a harbinger of brady-asystolic arrest. Lactate >2 mM adds ~10% absolute mortality; treat these as pre-arrest.
⚠ Give alteplase BEFORE heparin and run UFH with no bolus when thrombolysis is imminent — this reduces bleeding. UFH is preferred over LMWH whenever rapid escalation or reversal may be needed.
⚠ DOACs are contraindicated in pregnancy and severe hepatic impairment (Child-Pugh C). Use LMWH in pregnancy and UFH in severe hepatic/renal disease. In HIT, stop ALL heparin and switch to fondaparinux or argatroban.
⚠ Category D–E is time-critical — mortality exceeds 15% without immediate reperfusion, and VA-ECMO alone carries ~57% mortality unless it bridges to definitive clot removal.

1 · Stratify: the 2026 AHA/ACC A–E system

Confirmed or high-probability acute PE. The 2026 AHA/ACC categories (A–E) replace the old massive / submassive terms. Assess hemodynamics first: SBP <90 (or a ≥40 mmHg drop for >15 min), shock signs, and HR <40 (most ominous). Then get a bedside echo for RV strain plus troponin, BNP, and lactate.[6]

For normotensive patients, use PESI or sPESI to separate Category B from C. Lactate >2 mM = ~10% absolute mortality increase and behaves as a hazard ratio ≈12 for death.[6]

2 · What to do now

Category D–E — resuscitate & reperfuse
Do NOT intubate or fluid-load. High-flow O₂, norepinephrine first-line, push-dose phenylephrine 100–200 mcg for immediate support, activate PERT now. Start UFH with no bolus, then give systemic thrombolysis: alteplase 100 mg IV over 2h, or 50 mg reduced-dose, or 50 mg IV bolus + CPR in arrest.[6][7]
Category C — anticoagulate, monitor, plan
Start UFH (allows rapid escalation), admit to ICU/step-down monitoring, and activate PERT. If ≥3 risk factors (RV dysfunction + elevated biomarkers + lactate >2) and intervention is needed, mechanical thrombectomy is preferred over CDT (PEERLESS). Escalate to reperfusion only if the patient decompensates.[6][8]
Category A–B — anticoagulate, screen for discharge
No advanced therapy. Start enoxaparin or an oral DOAC and screen for outpatient management (Hestia-type criteria). Category A may leave the ED; Category B gets early discharge or a brief observation stay.[4][6]

3 · Crashing PE (Category D–E)

  1. AVOID intubation — positive pressure worsens RV performance and may precipitate arrest. Use high-flow 100% O₂ (a pulmonary vasodilator).
  2. AVOID fluid loading — the RV is already dilated. Give <500 mL only if clearly hypovolemic; diuresis may actually improve hemodynamics.
  3. Start vasopressors early — norepinephrine first-line; push-dose phenylephrine 100–200 mcg for immediate support.
  4. Activate PERT immediately (Class 1, 2026 AHA/ACC) and move toward reperfusion.
