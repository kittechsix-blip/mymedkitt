Oxygen Delivery Systems — Device Selection & Titration

Emergency department patients requiring supplemental oxygen or noninvasive/invasive ventilatory support, spanning Type 1 (hypoxemic) and Type 2 (hypercapnic) respiratory failure — from low-flow nasal cannula through HFNC, CPAP/BiPAP, and pre-intubation bridging.

⚠ COPD / CO2 retainers: target SpO2 88-92% (NOT 94-98%) — over-oxygenation worsens hypercapnia via the Haldane effect
⚠ Non-rebreather mask requires 10-15 L/min to keep the reservoir bag ≥1/3 full — inadequate flow means inadequate O2 delivery
⚠ Nasal cannula max effective flow is 6 L/min (≈44% FiO2) — higher flows do not meaningfully increase FiO2
⚠ HFNC ROX Index <3.85 at 2h = high risk of intubation — consider escalation to NIV or intubation
⚠ Condition-specific SpO2 targets differ (general 92-96%, COPD 88-92%, ARDS 88-95%, COVID-19/ARDS 88-92%, MI/ACS 90-94%, stroke/pregnancy/post-arrest higher) — hyperoxia causes absorptive atelectasis, free-radical damage, and coronary/cerebral vasoconstriction

Initial assessment

Match the oxygen delivery device to physiology. Assess SpO2, respiratory rate, work of breathing (accessory muscle use, retractions, tripoding), mental status, and the underlying diagnosis.

TypeDefinitionCommon causes
Type 1 (Hypoxemic)Low O2, normal/low CO2 — oxygenation problemPneumonia, ARDS, PE, pulmonary edema
Type 2 (Hypercapnic)High CO2 ± low O2 — ventilation problemCOPD, asthma, neuromuscular disease, drug overdose
Do not over-oxygenate CO2 retainers
Over-oxygenation in COPD can worsen hypercapnia. Target SpO2 88-92% in CO2 retainers.

Hypoxemia severity

Assess SpO2 on room air (or current O2) and work of breathing. Bands: Mild 88-94%, Moderate 75-88%, Severe <75% — or COPD/CO2 retainer, which is managed separately regardless of the numeric band.

▶ What to do now

Mild hypoxemia — nasal cannula
Nasal cannula 1-6 L/min is usually sufficient. Above 6 L/min, escalate to a mask or HFNC rather than increasing NC flow further.
Moderate hypoxemia — choose a device
Non-rebreather (bridge therapy), Venturi mask (precise FiO2, COPD), or HFNC (Type 1 respiratory failure, comfort) — choose based on the clinical scenario.
Severe hypoxemia — critical decision
Trial HFNC if alert and tolerating; NIV if Type 2 respiratory failure, CHF, or COPD exacerbation; proceed to intubation if altered, unable to protect the airway, pH <7.20 not improving, hemodynamically unstable, or refractory to maximal non-invasive support.
COPD / CO2 retainer — controlled oxygen
Target SpO2 88-92%. Venturi mask is the most precise (color-coded adapters); nasal cannula 1-2 L/min is acceptable. Consider BiPAP if hypercapnic (pH <7.35, pCO2 >45).

—Estimated FiO2 at selected flow
Above 6 L/min, escalate — do not chase flow
Above 6 L/min, nasal cannula becomes uncomfortable and does not significantly increase FiO2. Escalate to a mask or HFNC. FiO2 estimation assumes normal minute ventilation — fast/deep breathing dilutes inspired O2.

Non-rebreather mask

Flow 10-15 L/min (must keep reservoir bag inflated), FiO2 60-90% (theoretical max ~90%). Typical starting flow: 15 L/min.

  1. Attach reservoir bag to mask.
  2. Occlude mask opening, flush bag with O2 until full.
  3. Place on patient, adjust flow to keep bag ≥1/3 full during inspiration.
  4. Typical starting flow: 15 L/min.
Reservoir bag must stay ≥1/3 full
If the reservoir bag deflates during inspiration, the patient is not getting adequate O2 — increase flow or check connections. Cannot eat/drink/speak easily; maximum ~90% FiO2 due to room-air entrainment. Short-term bridge, not definitive therapy — escalate to HFNC or NIV if SpO2 is not improving.

Venturi mask

Delivers precise, fixed FiO2 regardless of the patient's breathing pattern — a jet of O2 entrains room air through calibrated ports, and total flow exceeds patient demand for a reliable FiO2. Best for COPD, weaning from high-flow O2, and when accurate FiO2 documentation is needed.

High-flow nasal cannula (HFNC)

Flow 20-70 L/min (start 40-50 L/min), FiO2 21-100%, temperature 31-37°C (usually 37°C).

  • Meets inspiratory demand — flow matches/exceeds peak inspiratory flow
  • Dead space washout — flushes nasopharyngeal dead space
  • PEEP effect — ~1 cmH2O per 10 L/min flow (mouth closed)
  • Humidification — improves mucociliary clearance, comfort
  • Reduced work of breathing
—Estimated PEEP effect at selected flow
ROX Index predicts HFNC failure
ROX = (SpO2/FiO2) / RR. ROX ≥4.88 at 2, 6, or 12h → low risk of intubation. ROX <3.85 → high risk of intubation.

NIV: CPAP vs BiPAP

ModePressuresBest For
CPAPSingle pressure (PEEP only)CHF/pulmonary edema, OSA, atelectasis
BiPAPIPAP + EPAPCOPD, hypercapnic failure, Type 2 RF

CPAP delivers continuous positive pressure, recruits alveoli, and reduces preload/afterload — patient controls all breaths. BiPAP adds pressure support (IPAP − EPAP) that augments ventilation and clears CO2; a backup rate can be set for apnea.

CPAP settings

Single continuous pressure recruits collapsed alveoli, reduces venous return (preload), and decreases LV afterload — improves cardiac output in CHF. Initial: 5-10 cmH2O (start 5, titrate up), FiO2 titrated to SpO2 ≥92%.

  1. Start CPAP 5 cmH2O, FiO2 100%.
  2. Titrate CPAP up by 2-3 cmH2O q5-10 min as tolerated.
  3. Typical effective range: 8-12 cmH2O.
  4. Wean FiO2 as SpO2 improves; reassess q15-30 min.
Stop / intubate if
Worsening mental status, unable to tolerate the mask, hemodynamic instability, or no improvement after 1-2 hours.

Titration strategy

Titration depends on the device and the clinical response.

Titrating low-flow oxygen

  • Nasal cannula: start 2 L/min, increase by 1 L/min q5 min. Target SpO2 92-96% (88-92% in COPD). Max effective 6 L/min.
  • Simple mask: minimum 5 L/min (prevents CO2 rebreathing); titrate 5-10 L/min to target. Escalate if need >10 L/min.
  • Non-rebreather: always 10-15 L/min, keep reservoir bag ≥1/3 full; escalate to HFNC/NIV if SpO2 still inadequate at 15 L/min.
  • Weaning: stable SpO2 for 1-2 hours → trial lower flow; discontinue if NC 2 L/min with SpO2 ≥92% on room air.

Titrating HFNC

Increase FiO2 if SpO2 below target; decrease once stable at target. Increase flow if the patient appears hungry for air; most tolerate 50-60 L/min.

  1. First, wean FiO2 to ≤40%.
  2. Then wean flow by 5-10 L/min q1-2h.
  3. When flow <20 L/min and FiO2 <40%, trial nasal cannula.
Escalation triggers
ROX Index <3.85 at 2h or decreasing; RR >30 despite optimization; increasing accessory muscle use; SpO2 <88% on FiO2 100%.

Titrating CPAP

Increase CPAP by 2-3 cmH2O q5-10 min to a typical effective range of 8-12 cmH2O (max 15-20). Once SpO2 >94%, begin weaning FiO2, target 92-96%. Expect a response within 30-60 minutes: decreased RR, improved SpO2, decreased accessory muscle use, diuresis if diuretics given.

  1. Once stable ×1-2h: decrease CPAP by 2 cmH2O q30 min.
  2. When CPAP 5 cmH2O, FiO2 <40%, SpO2 >92% → trial off NIV.
  3. Apply NC 2-4 L/min, observe ×30 min.
Failure / intubation criteria
No improvement after 1-2 hours, worsening mental status, or hemodynamic instability.

Titrating BiPAP

Hypercapnia (pCO2 >45, pH <7.35): increase IPAP by 2 cmH2O q5-10 min, goal increase tidal volume to blow off CO2, max IPAP 20-25 cmH2O. Hypoxemia (SpO2 <88%): increase FiO2 first, then EPAP by 2 cmH2O (max 10-12), which increases PEEP and recruits alveoli. For patient-ventilator dyssynchrony, adjust trigger sensitivity, rise time, or mask fit.

Check ABG at 1-2 hours
pH improving → continue current settings. pH worsening or unchanged → reassess, consider intubation. Wean IPAP before EPAP; trial off when IPAP ≤12, EPAP 5, FiO2 <40%.

Monitoring & assessment

  • All patients: continuous SpO2, RR q15-30 min, work of breathing, mental status.
  • HFNC: calculate ROX Index at 2h, 6h, 12h.
  • NIV: check ABG at 1-2 hours after initiation.
Signs of failure
Worsening tachypnea (RR >30-35), increasing FiO2 requirements, declining mental status, hemodynamic instability, patient fatigue/exhaustion, or a declining ROX Index.

SpO2 targets by condition

ConditionTarget SpO2Rationale
General hypoxemia92-96%Adequate oxygenation without hyperoxia
COPD / CO2 retainer88-92%Avoid suppressing hypoxic drive, Haldane effect
ARDS88-95%Permissive hypoxemia, avoid hyperoxia
MI / ACS90-94%Avoid hyperoxia (may worsen outcomes)
Stroke94-98%Adequate perfusion, avoid hypoxia
Pregnancy>95%Fetal oxygenation
Post-cardiac arrest94-98%Avoid hypoxia and hyperoxia
COVID-19 / ARDS88-92%Permissive hypoxemia per protocols
Hyperoxia harm
Absorptive atelectasis, oxygen free radical damage, coronary/cerebral vasoconstriction, and worse outcomes in MI, stroke, and cardiac arrest.

Escalation decision

  1. NC → Mask → NRB
  2. NRB → HFNC or NIV
  3. HFNC → NIV or Intubation
  4. NIV → Intubation

Intubation criteria

  • Absolute: cardiac or respiratory arrest, GCS ≤8 or rapidly declining, unable to protect airway (secretions, vomiting), upper airway obstruction.
  • Strong: refractory hypoxemia (SpO2 <88% despite maximal NIV/HFNC), severe respiratory acidosis (pH <7.20) not improving on NIV, respiratory fatigue/exhaustion, hemodynamic instability, high work of breathing despite optimization, anticipated deterioration.
Delayed intubation is harmful
Crash intubation carries a higher complication rate, hypoxic arrest risk, and aspiration risk. Intubation is a procedure, not a failure — early controlled intubation beats late emergent intubation.

Match disposition to oxygen requirements. HFNC and NIV require ICU-level monitoring. Ensure SpO2 targets are documented and communicated at handoff.

  • Once stable ×1-2h: decrease CPAP by 2 cmH2O q30 min.
  • When CPAP 5 cmH2O, FiO2 <40%, SpO2 >92% → trial off NIV.
  • Apply NC 2-4 L/min, observe ×30 min.

No improvement after 1-2 hours, worsening mental status, or hemodynamic instability.

Titrating BiPAP

Hypercapnia (pCO2 >45, pH <7.35): increase IPAP by 2 cmH2O q5-10 min, goal increase tidal volume to blow off CO2, max IPAP 20-25 cmH2O. Hypoxemia (SpO2 <88%): increase FiO2 first, then EPAP by 2 cmH2O (max 10-12), which increases PEEP and recruits alveoli. For patient-ventilator dyssynchrony, adjust trigger sensitivity, rise time, or mask fit.

pH improving → continue current settings. pH worsening or unchanged → reassess, consider intubation. Wean IPAP before EPAP; trial off when IPAP ≤12, EPAP 5, FiO2 <40%.

Monitoring & assessment

  • All patients: continuous SpO2, RR q15-30 min, work of breathing, mental status.
  • HFNC: calculate ROX Index at 2h, 6h, 12h.
  • NIV: check ABG at 1-2 hours after initiation.

: High risk of HFNC failure 0-3.85; Intermediate 3.85-4.88; Low risk of HFNC failure 4.88-15

Worsening tachypnea (RR >30-35), increasing FiO2 requirements, declining mental status, hemodynamic instability, patient fatigue/exhaustion, or a declining ROX Index.

SpO2 targets by condition

Condition | Target SpO2 | Rationale

General hypoxemia | 92-96% | Adequate oxygenation without hyperoxia

COPD / CO2 retainer | 88-92% | Avoid suppressing hypoxic drive, Haldane effect

ARDS | 88-95% | Permissive hypoxemia, avoid hyperoxia

MI / ACS | 90-94% | Avoid hyperoxia (may worsen outcomes)

Stroke | 94-98% | Adequate perfusion, avoid hypoxia

Pregnancy | >95% | Fetal oxygenation

Post-cardiac arrest | 94-98% | Avoid hypoxia and hyperoxia

COVID-19 / ARDS | 88-92% | Permissive hypoxemia per protocols

Absorptive atelectasis, oxygen free radical damage, coronary/cerebral vasoconstriction, and worse outcomes in MI, stroke, and cardiac arrest.

Escalation decision

  • NC → Mask → NRB
  • NRB → HFNC or NIV
  • HFNC → NIV or Intubation
  • NIV → Intubation

Intubation criteria

  • Absolute: cardiac or respiratory arrest, GCS ≤8 or rapidly declining, unable to protect airway (secretions, vomiting), upper airway obstruction.
  • Strong: refractory hypoxemia (SpO2 <88% despite maximal NIV/HFNC), severe respiratory acidosis (pH <7.20) not improving on NIV, respiratory fatigue/exhaustion, hemodynamic instability, high work of breathing despite optimization, anticipated deterioration.

Crash intubation carries a higher complication rate, hypoxic arrest risk, and aspiration risk. Intubation is a procedure, not a failure — early controlled intubation beats late emergent intubation.

Disposition

O2 Device | Typical Disposition

NC ≤4 L/min | Floor bed, monitor SpO2

NC 5-6 L/min | Step-down or tele bed

High-flow mask / NRB | ICU or step-down

HFNC | ICU

NIV (CPAP/BiPAP) | ICU

Intubated | ICU

  • SpO2 ≥92% on ≤2 L/min NC (or baseline)
  • Stable vitals for 2-4 hours
  • Ambulate without significant desaturation
  • Underlying cause treated/controlled
  • Follow-up arranged

Match disposition to oxygen requirements. HFNC and NIV require ICU-level monitoring. Ensure SpO2 targets are documented and communicated at handoff.
