Diabetes Management — Triage, Hypoglycemia, DKA/HHS Screen & Disposition

Adults with diabetes in the ED, inpatient, or outpatient setting. Excludes pediatrics. DKA and HHS are screened here but DKA insulin-drip/potassium/fluid detail lives in the dedicated DKA consult — this infographic hands off rather than duplicating that protocol.

⚠ Severe hypoglycemia (Level 3: altered mental status, seizure, loss of consciousness) → IV dextrose or IM/intranasal glucagon immediately — do not wait for confirmatory labs.
⚠ DKA: NEVER stop insulin just because glucose is normalizing — add dextrose instead and continue insulin until the anion gap closes and HCO3 ≥18 mmol/L.
⚠ Euglycemic DKA occurs with SGLT2 inhibitors (glucose 150-250 mg/dL) — check beta-hydroxybutyrate in any SGLT2i user with GI symptoms, even with near-normal glucose.
⚠ HHS: correct glucose SLOWER than DKA (target 50-75 mg/dL/hr) — rapid correction risks cerebral edema; HHS mortality is 10-20%, higher than DKA.
⚠ Insulin pump disconnect (altered mental status, DKA, surgery, malfunction) requires starting alternative insulin within 30 minutes — pump users have no long-acting insulin depot and can develop DKA within 4-6 hours.
⚠ Type 1 diabetics must NEVER have insulin stopped entirely, even if NPO — continue basal insulin to prevent DKA.
⚠ Give thiamine 100 mg IV before or with dextrose in alcoholic/malnourished patients to prevent Wernicke encephalopathy.
⚠ Sulfonylurea-induced hypoglycemia (glipizide, glyburide, glimepiride) can recur for 12-72+ hours due to long half-life and active renal metabolites — requires admission and extended monitoring, not single-dose treatment.

Initial assessment

Triage by point-of-care glucose: glucose <70 mg/dL routes to the hypoglycemia pathway; glucose ≥70 mg/dL routes to DKA/HHS screening. Key questions: Type 1 vs Type 2? On insulin (pump, basal-bolus, premixed)? Recent illness, missed doses, new medications? Last oral intake / NPO status?

  • Altered mental status
  • Kussmaul respirations (deep, rapid breathing)
  • Fruity breath odor (ketones)
  • Signs of dehydration / shock
Clinical context matters
Symptomatic hypoglycemia can occur at a higher glucose in poorly controlled diabetics. Euglycemic DKA (glucose <250 mg/dL) occurs with SGLT2 inhibitors. Elderly/renal patients may develop HHS at lower glucose levels.

DKA/HHS screening detail

2024 ADA/EASD criterionThreshold
D — hyperglycemiaGlucose ≥200 mg/dL OR known diabetes history
K — ketosisBeta-hydroxybutyrate ≥3.0 mmol/L (or ketonuria ≥2+)
A — acidosispH <7.3 AND/OR HCO3 <18 mmol/L

The 2024 consensus removed the anion gap from the diagnostic criteria and uses venous (not arterial) samples — anion gap remains useful for tracking response, not diagnosis. HHS: glucose >600 mg/dL, serum osmolality >320 mOsm/kg, minimal/no ketosis, pH usually >7.3.

Euglycemic DKA (SGLT2 inhibitors)
Empagliflozin, dapagliflozin, canagliflozin can produce full DKA with glucose only 150-250 mg/dL. Check beta-hydroxybutyrate in any SGLT2i user with GI symptoms, even with near-normal glucose.

Go to the DKA consult for fluid resuscitation strategy, insulin drip protocol, potassium management, and transition to subcutaneous insulin. Never stop insulin just because glucose is low — add dextrose instead. Start basal insulin early (within 6-12h) to prevent rebound DKA.

Type & pump assessment detail

Type 1: never stop insulin — will develop DKA. Type 2: can often be managed with oral agents initially. New/unknown: check for DKA, classify with C-peptide and GAD65 antibodies if uncertain.

New diabetes diagnosis: check for DKA (glucose, VBG, ketones), assess symptoms (polyuria, polydipsia, weight loss, fatigue). If stable hyperglycemia, start basal insulin 0.1-0.2 U/kg/day. Do not discharge without glucose monitoring supplies, basic insulin education, follow-up, and hypoglycemia treatment supplies.

  • Document pump type, basal rate(s), insulin-to-carb ratio, correction factor, target glucose
  • Continue self-management — count carbs, bolus for meals, check glucose before meals/bedtime
  • Staff: document glucose in EMR, have backup insulin available, know how to suspend/disconnect in an emergency
  • Target glucose 140-180 mg/dL inpatient
Pump disconnect: 30-minute rule
Must start alternative insulin (IV or SC) within 30 minutes of pump removal — pump users have no long-acting insulin depot; DKA can develop within 4-6 hours.

Worked example: pump basal rate 1 U/hr (24 U/day) + 18 U average daily boluses = 42 U TDD → Basal 21 U glargine SC daily, Bolus 7 U lispro with each meal (50/50 split). Alternative: IV regular insulin drip 0.5-1 U/hr, titrated to glucose.

Admission vs discharge

  • DKA or HHS
  • Severe hypoglycemia with altered mental status, or recurrent hypoglycemia
  • New Type 1 diabetes
  • Significant comorbid illness, or unable to take oral medications
  • Social factors preventing safe discharge
Inpatient glucose management — detailGlucose targets, regimen selection, TDD/basal-bolus math, correction scale, IV insulin drip rates, NPO management.

General inpatient (non-ICU) and ICU target: 140-180 mg/dL. The previous ICU target of 80-110 mg/dL was abandoned after NICE-SUGAR showed increased hypoglycemia and mortality with tight control. Stricter targets (110-140 mg/dL) may suit cardiac surgery/stroke patients; less strict (>180-200 mg/dL) may suit terminal/palliative patients or those with a history of severe hypoglycemia.

Basal-bolus-correction is preferred for most hospitalized diabetics who are eating. IV insulin infusion is for critical illness, NPO >12-24h, DKA/HHS, or perioperative management. Sliding scale alone is NOT recommended — reactive rather than proactive, associated with worse outcomes.

The 0.4-0.5 U/kg/day figure above is the general starting range only. Elderly and renal-impairment patients need a much lower starting TDD (0.2-0.3 U/kg/day) — using the general range in these patients risks insulin overdose and hypoglycemia. Insulin-resistant patients (obese, on steroids) and those on high-dose steroids need a higher starting TDD (0.5-0.6 and 0.6-1.0 U/kg/day respectively).

If already on home insulin: use the patient's home TDD as the starting point rather than a weight-based estimate, and reduce it 20-25% for acute illness with poor oral intake.

Outpatient management — detailHbA1c targets, oral agent selection, insulin initiation math, titration table, regimen adjustment.

HbA1c targets: <7.0% general; <6.5% for young/healthy with low hypoglycemia risk; <8.0% for elderly/limited life expectancy/hypoglycemia risk. Initiate insulin if: A1c >10%, glucose >300 mg/dL with symptoms, Type 1 (always), pregnancy, oral-agent failure, or hospitalization for hyperglycemia.

Metformin
Start 500 mg daily with dinner → 500 mg BID after 1 week → target 1000 mg BID

Maximum 2550 mg/day. Contraindicated eGFR <30; hold if eGFR 30-45; hold 48h before/after IV contrast.

HFrEF or CKD (eGFR 25-60): SGLT2 inhibitor preferred (empagliflozin, dapagliflozin). ASCVD: GLP-1 agonist or SGLT2i. Weight loss desired: GLP-1 agonist; avoid sulfonylureas (weight gain). High hypoglycemia risk: avoid sulfonylureas — metformin, SGLT2i, and GLP-1 agonists have low hypoglycemia risk. SGLT2i carries euglycemic DKA risk — hold before major surgery.

Titrate every 3 days until fasting glucose is at goal. Insulin-to-carb ratio (ICR) = 500 ÷ TDD. Correction factor (rapid insulin) = 1800 ÷ TDD.

Metformin alone at A1c 7-9%: add a second agent. Dual oral therapy at A1c 8-10%: add a third agent, or add basal insulin (often more effective). Basal insulin + orals: optimize basal to fasting goal first, then add mealtime insulin if A1c remains elevated. Red flags: A1c >10% or glucose >300 with symptoms, recent hospitalization for hyperglycemia, new DKA in Type 2 (consider latent autoimmune diabetes).

Reference: IV to SC insulin transitionTiming rule and TDD-from-drip-rate formula for stopping an IV insulin infusion.

Give SC basal insulin 2-4 hours BEFORE stopping the IV drip — stopping without basal coverage causes rapid hyperglycemia/DKA in Type 1. TDD estimate = (average hourly IV rate × 24) × 0.8. Worked example: average rate 2.5 U/hr → TDD 48 U/day → Basal 24 U glargine SC, Bolus 8 U with each meal (if eating). For NPO patients: give the full calculated dose as basal only, add a correction scale, start bolus once eating.

Reference: diabetes discharge planningMedication reconciliation, teach-back education, follow-up timing, red-flag return precautions.
  • Medication reconciliation: clear insulin regimen (names/doses/timing), oral agent adjustments, supplies (insulin, needles/pens, monitor, strips, lancets)
  • Teach-back education: injection technique, glucose monitoring schedule, hypoglycemia recognition/treatment, sick-day rules
  • Follow-up: primary care or endocrinology within 1-2 weeks, A1c in 3 months, diabetes educator referral if new to insulin
  • Return precautions: persistent vomiting, glucose consistently >300 despite medication, signs of DKA, severe hypoglycemia, unable to eat/drink
Reference: perioperative diabetes managementPreoperative, day-of, intraoperative, and postoperative glucose management timeline.
  1. Preop: target 140-180 mg/dL. Hold metformin if contrast/major surgery planned. Hold SGLT2 inhibitors 3-4 days before surgery (DKA risk). Continue basal insulin at 75-100% of usual dose. Hold bolus/oral agents if NPO.
  2. Day of surgery: check morning glucose; give 75-100% of usual basal; hold bolus if NPO; hold oral agents and GLP-1 agonists.
  3. Intraoperative: IV insulin drip for major surgery or poor control; check glucose q1-2h; D5W if glucose <150 and on insulin.
  4. Postoperative: continue IV insulin until eating, transition to SC (basal 2-4h before stopping drip). Do not restart SGLT2 inhibitors until eating well — monitor for euglycemic DKA.

K — ketosis | Beta-hydroxybutyrate ≥3.0 mmol/L (or ketonuria ≥2+)

A — acidosis | pH <7.3 AND/OR HCO3 <18 mmol/L

The 2024 consensus removed the anion gap from the diagnostic criteria and uses venous (not arterial) samples — anion gap remains useful for tracking response, not diagnosis. HHS: glucose >600 mg/dL, serum osmolality >320 mOsm/kg, minimal/no ketosis, pH usually >7.3.

Empagliflozin, dapagliflozin, canagliflozin can produce full DKA with glucose only 150-250 mg/dL. Check beta-hydroxybutyrate in any SGLT2i user with GI symptoms, even with near-normal glucose.

Go to the DKA consult for fluid resuscitation strategy, insulin drip protocol, potassium management, and transition to subcutaneous insulin. Never stop insulin just because glucose is low — add dextrose instead. Start basal insulin early (within 6-12h) to prevent rebound DKA.

DKA IV insulin infusion rate: (computed)

HHS: glucose often >600 mg/dL (can exceed 1000), profound dehydration (average 8-10L deficit), minimal ketosis, mortality 10-20% (higher than DKA). Slower onset than DKA (days to weeks), often elderly with Type 2 diabetes.

Normal Saline 0.9%: 1 L/hr for 1-2 hours, then 250-500 mL/hr IV — Average deficit 8-10L. Switch to 0.45% NS when Na normalizes or is high. Add dextrose when glucose <300.

Type & pump assessment detail

Type 1: never stop insulin — will develop DKA. Type 2: can often be managed with oral agents initially. New/unknown: check for DKA, classify with C-peptide and GAD65 antibodies if uncertain.

New diabetes diagnosis: check for DKA (glucose, VBG, ketones), assess symptoms (polyuria, polydipsia, weight loss, fatigue). If stable hyperglycemia, start basal insulin 0.1-0.2 U/kg/day. Do not discharge without glucose monitoring supplies, basic insulin education, follow-up, and hypoglycemia treatment supplies.

  • Document pump type, basal rate(s), insulin-to-carb ratio, correction factor, target glucose
  • Continue self-management — count carbs, bolus for meals, check glucose before meals/bedtime
  • Staff: document glucose in EMR, have backup insulin available, know how to suspend/disconnect in an emergency
  • Target glucose 140-180 mg/dL inpatient

Must start alternative insulin (IV or SC) within 30 minutes of pump removal — pump users have no long-acting insulin depot; DKA can develop within 4-6 hours.

Worked example: pump basal rate 1 U/hr (24 U/day) + 18 U average daily boluses = 42 U TDD → Basal 21 U glargine SC daily, Bolus 7 U lispro with each meal (50/50 split). Alternative: IV regular insulin drip 0.5-1 U/hr, titrated to glucose.

Admission vs discharge

  • DKA or HHS
  • Severe hypoglycemia with altered mental status, or recurrent hypoglycemia
  • New Type 1 diabetes
  • Significant comorbid illness, or unable to take oral medications
  • Social factors preventing safe discharge

Inpatient glucose management — detail

General inpatient (non-ICU) and ICU target: 140-180 mg/dL. The previous ICU target of 80-110 mg/dL was abandoned after NICE-SUGAR showed increased hypoglycemia and mortality with tight control. Stricter targets (110-140 mg/dL) may suit cardiac surgery/stroke patients; less strict (>180-200 mg/dL) may suit terminal/palliative patients or those with a history of severe hypoglycemia.

Basal-bolus-correction is preferred for most hospitalized diabetics who are eating. IV insulin infusion is for critical illness, NPO >12-24h, DKA/HHS, or perioperative management. Sliding scale alone is NOT recommended — reactive rather than proactive, associated with worse outcomes.

Insulin-naive TDD estimate (general, low end of 0.4-0.5 U/kg/day): (computed)

Population | Starting TDD (insulin-naive)

General | 0.4-0.5 U/kg/day

Elderly / renal impairment | 0.2-0.3 U/kg/day

Insulin-resistant (obese, on steroids) | 0.5-0.6 U/kg/day

High-dose steroids | 0.6-1.0 U/kg/day

The 0.4-0.5 U/kg/day figure above is the general starting range only. Elderly and renal-impairment patients need a much lower starting TDD (0.2-0.3 U/kg/day) — using the general range in these patients risks insulin overdose and hypoglycemia. Insulin-resistant patients (obese, on steroids) and those on high-dose steroids need a higher starting TDD (0.5-0.6 and 0.6-1.0 U/kg/day respectively).

If already on home insulin: use the patient's home TDD as the starting point rather than a weight-based estimate, and reduce it 20-25% for acute illness with poor oral intake.

Insulin Glargine (Basal): 50% of TDD once daily SC at bedtime Subcutaneous — Worked example: 80 kg, TDD 32 U → Basal 16 U glargine. Continue even if NPO (may reduce to 75-80% of usual dose).

Insulin Lispro (Bolus): 50% of TDD divided equally among 3 meals Subcutaneous — Worked example: 16 U bolus total ÷ 3 ≈ 5 U per meal. Hold if NPO or <50% of meal consumed.

Pre-meal glucose (mg/dL) | Correction dose

150-199 | +1 U

200-249 | +2 U

250-299 | +3 U

300-349 | +4 U

>350 | +5 U, notify MD

Correction factor = 1800 ÷ TDD (for rapid insulin). Example: TDD 32 U → CF = 1800/32 = 56, rounded to 50 in this scale. If glucose consistently >180: increase TDD 10-20%. If <100: decrease TDD 10-20%.

Regular Insulin IV Drip: 1-6 U/hr, titrate q1h to target 140-180 mg/dL IV continuous infusion — Prepare 100 U in 100 mL NS = 1 U/mL. Starting rate by glucose: 150-200 → 1-2 U/hr; 200-300 → 2-4 U/hr; >300 → 4-6 U/hr. Add D5W/D10W when glucose <150-200. Give SC basal 2-4 hours before stopping the drip.

Type 1 NPO: MUST continue basal insulin (will develop DKA without it); hold bolus; may reduce basal 20-25% if prolonged NPO. Type 2 NPO: continue basal at 75-100% of usual dose; hold oral agents and GLP-1 agonists; correction-scale insulin PRN. Monitor glucose q4-6h.

Reference: steroid-induced hyperglycemia

Once-daily morning steroids cause a glucose peak 4-8 hours post-dose (afternoon/evening), often with near-normal fasting glucose — NPH given at breakfast covers this pattern well.

NPH Insulin: 0.1-0.2 U/kg in the morning with the steroid dose Subcutaneous — New hyperglycemia on steroids. For known diabetics already on insulin, instead increase TDD 20-50% depending on steroid dose.

10-20 mg | ~20%

20-40 mg | ~40%

>40 mg | ~50-100%

Outpatient management — detail

HbA1c targets: <7.0% general; <6.5% for young/healthy with low hypoglycemia risk; <8.0% for elderly/limited life expectancy/hypoglycemia risk. Initiate insulin if: A1c >10%, glucose >300 mg/dL with symptoms, Type 1 (always), pregnancy, oral-agent failure, or hospitalization for hyperglycemia.

Metformin: Start 500 mg daily with dinner → 500 mg BID after 1 week → target 1000 mg BID PO — Maximum 2550 mg/day. Contraindicated eGFR <30; hold if eGFR 30-45; hold 48h before/after IV contrast.

HFrEF or CKD (eGFR 25-60): SGLT2 inhibitor preferred (empagliflozin, dapagliflozin). ASCVD: GLP-1 agonist or SGLT2i. Weight loss desired: GLP-1 agonist; avoid sulfonylureas (weight gain). High hypoglycemia risk: avoid sulfonylureas — metformin, SGLT2i, and GLP-1 agonists have low hypoglycemia risk. SGLT2i carries euglycemic DKA risk — hold before major surgery.

Outpatient starting basal dose (worked-example midpoint, 0.1-0.2 U/kg/day range): (computed)

Fasting glucose (mg/dL) | Basal adjustment

<70 (hypoglycemia) | Decrease 2-4 U

70-130 (at goal) | No change

130-180 | Increase 2 U

>180 | Increase 4 U

Titrate every 3 days until fasting glucose is at goal. Insulin-to-carb ratio (ICR) = 500 ÷ TDD. Correction factor (rapid insulin) = 1800 ÷ TDD.

Metformin alone at A1c 7-9%: add a second agent. Dual oral therapy at A1c 8-10%: add a third agent, or add basal insulin (often more effective). Basal insulin + orals: optimize basal to fasting goal first, then add mealtime insulin if A1c remains elevated. Red flags: A1c >10% or glucose >300 with symptoms, recent hospitalization for hyperglycemia, new DKA in Type 2 (consider latent autoimmune diabetes).

Reference: IV to SC insulin transition

Give SC basal insulin 2-4 hours BEFORE stopping the IV drip — stopping without basal coverage causes rapid hyperglycemia/DKA in Type 1. TDD estimate = (average hourly IV rate × 24) × 0.8. Worked example: average rate 2.5 U/hr → TDD 48 U/day → Basal 24 U glargine SC, Bolus 8 U with each meal (if eating). For NPO patients: give the full calculated dose as basal only, add a correction scale, start bolus once eating.

Reference: diabetes discharge planning

  • Medication reconciliation: clear insulin regimen (names/doses/timing), oral agent adjustments, supplies (insulin, needles/pens, monitor, strips, lancets)
  • Teach-back education: injection technique, glucose monitoring schedule, hypoglycemia recognition/treatment, sick-day rules
  • Follow-up: primary care or endocrinology within 1-2 weeks, A1c in 3 months, diabetes educator referral if new to insulin
  • Return precautions: persistent vomiting, glucose consistently >300 despite medication, signs of DKA, severe hypoglycemia, unable to eat/drink

Reference: perioperative diabetes management

  • Preop: target 140-180 mg/dL. Hold metformin if contrast/major surgery planned. Hold SGLT2 inhibitors 3-4 days before surgery (DKA risk). Continue basal insulin at 75-100% of usual dose. Hold bolus/oral agents if NPO.
  • Day of surgery: check morning glucose; give 75-100% of usual basal; hold bolus if NPO; hold oral agents and GLP-1 agonists.
  • Intraoperative: IV insulin drip for major surgery or poor control; check glucose q1-2h; D5W if glucose <150 and on insulin.
  • Postoperative: continue IV insulin until eating, transition to SC (basal 2-4h before stopping drip). Do not restart SGLT2 inhibitors until eating well — monitor for euglycemic DKA.
