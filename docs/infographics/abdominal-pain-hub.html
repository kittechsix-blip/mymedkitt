Abdominal Pain Hub: Sort Sick vs Not-Sick First

Adults in the ED with undifferentiated abdominal pain. A triage hub that excludes time-critical catastrophes, then routes to the right deep-dive consult. Excludes trauma, known pregnancy beyond first trimester, and pediatrics — each has its own pathway.

⚠ Sick check FIRST — vitals trend + 4-question screen (pregnancy, pain-out-of-proportion, AAA, chest pain). If unstable, resuscitate in parallel with the workup.
⚠ Age ≥50 + new abdominal / back / flank pain → bedside aorta US NOW. AAA / ruptured AAA until proven otherwise; a non-pulsatile exam does NOT rule it out.
⚠ Pain out of proportion to exam + cardiac / vascular risk → mesenteric ischemia. CTA + vascular consult; a normal lactate does NOT rule it out.
⚠ β-hCG on EVERY reproductive-age female with abdominal pain. Ruptured ectopic kills in minutes.
⚠ Rigid abdomen + free air + sepsis physiology = perforated viscus. Emergent surgical consult; antibiotics within 1 h — do not delay for imaging.
⚠ ECG on every middle-aged-or-older patient with epigastric pain. Inferior MI, aortic dissection, and Boerhaave mimic GI disease.

Deep-dive consults: Aortic Aneurysm · Mesenteric Ischemia · First Trimester Bleeding · Gallbladder Disease · Acute Pancreatitis · Adult Appendicitis · STEMI.

Pancreatitis — Atlanta criteria (2 of 3)
Classic epigastric band-like pain + lipase >3× ULN + consistent imaging. Any 2 of 3 confirm. LR-preferred resuscitation and early enteral nutrition.[7]
Diverticulitis — antibiotic regimen by severity
Outpatient, uncomplicated: ciprofloxacin plus metronidazole × 7-10 days, OR amoxicillin-clavulanate monotherapy alone × 7-10 days. Selected uncomplicated cases may be managed without antibiotics — shared decision.

Complicated / inpatient / immunocompromised: IV antibiotics — ceftriaxone + metronidazole, OR piperacillin-tazobactam. Bowel rest, surgical consult.

Abscess ≥3-4 cm: IR drainage.[9]
Perforated viscus — surgical emergency
Rigid abdomen + free air + sepsis physiology = perforated viscus until proven otherwise (peptic ulcer is the most common etiology). Emergent surgical consult — do not delay for imaging when peritoneal signs, sepsis, and free air are present. Broad-spectrum antibiotics within 1 h: piperacillin-tazobactam 4.5 g IV OR ceftriaxone 2 g IV + metronidazole 500 mg IV. NPO, NG decompression, type & CROSS.[13]
