Eating Disorder Emergencies

Adolescents and adults presenting to the ED with suspected or confirmed eating disorder (anorexia nervosa, bulimia nervosa, or ARFID). Covers recognition, medical-complication triage, MARSIPAN risk stratification, acute stabilization/refeeding protocol, psychiatric risk assessment, and disposition. Excludes detailed pediatric %-median-BMI dosing tables and non-eating-disorder causes of the same electrolyte derangements.

⚠ Medical instability takes precedence over psychiatric disposition — stabilize first.
⚠ AN vital-sign red flags: HR <40 bpm, QTc >460ms (female)/>450ms (male), SBP <90 mmHg, orthostatic drop >20 mmHg SBP or >30 bpm HR, temperature <35.5°C (95.9°F), BMI <13 kg/m² — MEED 2022 / RCPsych CR233.
⚠ QTc >450ms = high risk for arrhythmia and Torsades — correct K+ >3.5 and Mg2+ >2.0 mEq/L BEFORE initiating refeeding.
⚠ K+ <2.5 mEq/L = high risk for arrhythmia; Phos <1.0 mg/dL = critical. Correct magnesium FIRST — hypokalemia is refractory to correction until Mg2+ is repleted.
⚠ Refeeding syndrome: correct K+, phosphate, and Mg2+ BEFORE feeding; give thiamine 100-200 mg IV before carbohydrate loading to prevent Wernicke encephalopathy.
⚠ Order of electrolyte correction: magnesium FIRST (enables K+ repletion), potassium SECOND, phosphate THIRD.
⚠ During refeeding: a phosphate drop >30% means slow the caloric advancement; a new arrhythmia or CHF symptoms means stop and reassess.
⚠ QTc >500 ms or HR <30 bpm is critical — ICU-level monitoring and cardiology consult. Avoid metoclopramide, ondansetron, antipsychotics, and SSRIs during acute malnutrition (all QTc-prolonging).
⚠ A medical emergency (e.g., K+ <2.5, HR <30, glucose <50) may be treated without consent under the emergency doctrine; involuntary psychiatric hold may be needed when a patient lacks capacity and refuses life-saving treatment.

Overview & Recognition

Eating disorders carry the highest mortality of any psychiatric illness. The ED is often the first medical contact for complications. Three main types: Anorexia Nervosa (AN) — restriction, fear of weight gain, distorted body image; Bulimia Nervosa (BN) — binge-purge cycles, often normal weight; ARFID — avoidant/restrictive intake with no body-image disturbance.

  • Bradycardia, hypotension, hypothermia
  • Electrolyte abnormalities (hypokalemia, hypophosphatemia)
  • Syncope, weakness, palpitations
  • Dental erosions, parotid swelling (purging)
  • Severe malnutrition, cachexia

Medical Complications

Bradycardia: adaptive to starvation; HR <40 is high risk for decompensation. QTc prolongation >450ms: caused by hypokalemia, hypomagnesemia, hypocalcemia; can progress to Torsades/sudden death — correct electrolytes BEFORE refeeding. Management: continuous telemetry, correct K+ >3.5 and Mg2+ >2.0, avoid QTc-prolonging drugs; HR<30 or symptomatic → cardiology consult.

Hypokalemia (most common): from vomiting, laxatives, diuretics, starvation. K+ <3.0 mEq/L needs IV replacement; K+ <2.5 mEq/L is high risk for arrhythmia. Hypophosphatemia: often normal/high-normal at presentation, drops precipitously with refeeding (shifts intracellularly) — causes respiratory failure, cardiac dysfunction, rhabdomyolysis, hemolysis; Phos <1.0 mg/dL is critical. Hypomagnesemia: coexists with hypokalemia and makes K+ correction refractory until corrected; Mg2+ <1.5 mEq/L needs IV replacement.

Refeeding red flags
Phosphate drop >30% = slow advancement. New arrhythmia or CHF symptoms = stop and reassess.

Psychiatric Assessment

AN has the highest mortality of any psychiatric disorder — ~20% due to suicide. All patients need a formal suicide-risk assessment. Common comorbidities: major depression (50-75%), anxiety (up to 60%), OCD (25-40%), substance use, personality disorders, PTSD/trauma.

ED-specific risk factors: prior suicide attempts (strongest predictor), purging behaviors (higher risk than restrictive AN), comorbid depression/substance use/personality disorder, recent weight restoration (paradoxically increases risk), treatment dropout, longer illness duration.

  • 1:1 observation
  • Remove means (sharps, cords, medications)
  • Psychiatric consultation
  • Safety plan documentation
  • Involuntary hold if imminent risk

Four components of capacity: understanding, appreciation, reasoning, expression. Severe malnutrition, anosognosia, and depression/anxiety can impair all four. Medical emergency (imminent life threat, e.g. K+ <2.5, HR <30, glucose <50): treat without consent. Not an immediate emergency: formal capacity evaluation + psychiatry consult; if lacking capacity, seek surrogate decision-maker or court order.

Medical criteria supporting involuntary hold: BMI <13 with refusal to eat; life-threatening electrolyte abnormalities; cardiac instability (HR <40, QTc >500); inability to recognize illness severity. Process: document medical necessity, psychiatry consultation, complete state-specific hold paperwork, arrange appropriate level of care, legal review within statutory timeframe.

Disposition

Disposition depends on BOTH medical stability AND psychiatric safety. Levels of care, least to most intensive: outpatient → IOP (3+ days/week) → PHP (daily, home nights) → residential (24h) → inpatient psychiatric → medical hospitalization.

Medical Admission
Telemetry, strict I/Os, daily weights (AM/gowned/post-void), electrolytes q6-12h then daily, glucose q6h, daily ECG until QTc normal, 1:1/close observation around meals, bathroom supervision. Discharge: HR >50, SBP >90, afebrile, QTc <450ms, electrolytes normal without IV support, tolerating oral nutrition, no refeeding syndrome, psychiatric clearance.
Psychiatric Admission
Requires medical clearance: HR >50 bpm, QTc <450ms, electrolytes stable (PO replacement OK), no active refeeding syndrome, able to eat orally. If medically unstable for a psych unit: admit medical with psych consult, transfer once stable.
Outpatient Disposition
Prerequisites: HR >50, SBP >90 without significant orthostatics, QTc <450ms, K+ >3.5/Phos >2.5/Mg2+ >2.0, no active SI, supportive living environment, follow-up committed. Follow-up: PCP within 1 week, eating-disorder specialist within 2 weeks. NEDA Helpline: 1-800-931-2237.

Eating Disorder — Initial Assessment — Highest mortality of any psychiatric illness. ED is often first medical contact. Screen for AN, BN, ARFID.

Predominant presentation? — AN: BMI<18.5, restriction, fear of weight gain. BN: binge-purge, often normal weight. ARFID: nutritional deficiency, no body-image disturbance.

ARFID assessment — Nutritional deficiency without body-image disturbance; same malnutrition complications as AN.

Primary medical complication? — Cardiac instability, electrolyte derangement, refeeding-syndrome risk, or stable.

Cardiac complications — Bradycardia (HR<40 high risk) and QTc prolongation (>450ms) — leading cause of ED death. Correct electrolytes BEFORE refeeding.

Electrolyte derangements — Hypokalemia most common. Correct Mg2+ first, then K+, then prophylactic phosphate.

Refeeding syndrome risk — Carbohydrate reintroduction drives insulin release → electrolytes shift intracellularly → arrhythmia/respiratory failure/death.

MARSIPAN risk level? — High: BMI<13, HR<40, QTc>450, SBP<90, temp<35.5, K+<3.0. Moderate: BMI 13-15. Lower: BMI>15, stable vitals/labs.

Acute stabilization principles — Telemetry, 2 large-bore IVs, cautious fluids, avoid QTc-prolonging drugs (metoclopramide, ondansetron, SSRIs). Do not delay refeeding for mild electrolyte abnormalities.

Correct electrolytes → refeed low & slow → monitor cardiac — Mg2+ first, then K+, then phosphate. Start 5-20 kcal/kg/day by risk tier, advance +200 kcal/day. Telemetry + daily ECG while QTc elevated; avoid QTc-prolonging drugs.

Psychiatric risk? — AN has the highest mortality of any psychiatric disorder (~20% due to suicide). Assess after medical stabilization.

Suicide risk management — 1:1 observation, remove means, psychiatric consultation, safety-plan documentation.

Capacity evaluation / treatment refusal — Four components: understanding, appreciation, reasoning, expression. Severe malnutrition can impair all four.

Involuntary treatment considerations — Criteria: mental illness present, imminent danger, unable to meet basic needs, less-restrictive alternatives insufficient.

Disposition? — Depends on BOTH medical stability and psychiatric safety — MARSIPAN medical criteria + psychiatric admission criteria.

Medical Admission — Telemetry, strict I/Os, daily weights, electrolytes q6-12h, daily ECG until QTc normal. Discharge: HR>50, QTc<450, normal electrolytes without IV support.

Psychiatric Admission — Requires medical clearance: HR>50, QTc<450, stable electrolytes, no active refeeding syndrome.

Phosphate drop >30% = slow advancement. New arrhythmia or CHF symptoms = stop and reassess.

Psychiatric Assessment

AN has the highest mortality of any psychiatric disorder — ~20% due to suicide. All patients need a formal suicide-risk assessment. Common comorbidities: major depression (50-75%), anxiety (up to 60%), OCD (25-40%), substance use, personality disorders, PTSD/trauma.

ED-specific risk factors: prior suicide attempts (strongest predictor), purging behaviors (higher risk than restrictive AN), comorbid depression/substance use/personality disorder, recent weight restoration (paradoxically increases risk), treatment dropout, longer illness duration.

  • 1:1 observation
  • Remove means (sharps, cords, medications)
  • Psychiatric consultation
  • Safety plan documentation
  • Involuntary hold if imminent risk

Four components of capacity: understanding, appreciation, reasoning, expression. Severe malnutrition, anosognosia, and depression/anxiety can impair all four. Medical emergency (imminent life threat, e.g. K+ <2.5, HR <30, glucose <50): treat without consent. Not an immediate emergency: formal capacity evaluation + psychiatry consult; if lacking capacity, seek surrogate decision-maker or court order.

Medical criteria supporting involuntary hold: BMI <13 with refusal to eat; life-threatening electrolyte abnormalities; cardiac instability (HR <40, QTc >500); inability to recognize illness severity. Process: document medical necessity, psychiatry consultation, complete state-specific hold paperwork, arrange appropriate level of care, legal review within statutory timeframe.

Disposition

Disposition depends on BOTH medical stability AND psychiatric safety. Levels of care, least to most intensive: outpatient → IOP (3+ days/week) → PHP (daily, home nights) → residential (24h) → inpatient psychiatric → medical hospitalization.

Telemetry, strict I/Os, daily weights (AM/gowned/post-void), electrolytes q6-12h then daily, glucose q6h, daily ECG until QTc normal, 1:1/close observation around meals, bathroom supervision. Discharge: HR >50, SBP >90, afebrile, QTc <450ms, electrolytes normal without IV support, tolerating oral nutrition, no refeeding syndrome, psychiatric clearance.

Requires medical clearance: HR >50 bpm, QTc <450ms, electrolytes stable (PO replacement OK), no active refeeding syndrome, able to eat orally. If medically unstable for a psych unit: admit medical with psych consult, transfer once stable.

Prerequisites: HR >50, SBP >90 without significant orthostatics, QTc <450ms, K+ >3.5/Phos >2.5/Mg2+ >2.0, no active SI, supportive living environment, follow-up committed. Follow-up: PCP within 1 week, eating-disorder specialist within 2 weeks. NEDA Helpline: 1-800-931-2237.
