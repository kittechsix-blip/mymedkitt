Ingrown Toenail — Partial Nail Avulsion + Phenol Matrixectomy

Adult ED/urgent-care patients with onychocryptosis (ingrown toenail) being considered for partial nail avulsion ± phenol matrixectomy. The tree's own staging system routes Stage 1 (mild, no granulation) to a conservative trial first. Phenol matrixectomy is deferred (avulsion only, refer for definitive care) in diabetes/peripheral arterial disease/poor perfusion, and deferred until inflammation resolves in active cellulitis/spreading infection.

⚠ Phenol is deferred (not performed) in diabetes, peripheral arterial disease, or poor perfusion — risk of delayed healing, ulceration, or necrosis; avulse for symptom relief and refer to podiatry/surgery for definitive matrixectomy
⚠ Phenol is inactivated by blood and tissue fluid — a bloodless digital-tourniquet field is mandatory before applying it
⚠ The digital tourniquet must be removed and reperfusion confirmed BEFORE dressing/discharge — the single most common tourniquet error is forgetting to remove it
⚠ Active cellulitis or spreading infection: treat infection first (drain any abscess) and defer phenol until inflammation resolves
⚠ A retained proximal nail-strip corner guarantees recurrence — the strip must be removed completely including its proximal corner

Stage 3Chronic granulation / hypertrophic nail foldAvulsion + phenol matrixectomy is first-line

Stage 1 — Conservative Trial First

  • Warm soaks 10-20 min BID-TID
  • Lift the lateral nail edge with a wisp of cotton or a strip of dental floss under the corner; replace daily
  • Cut the nail STRAIGHT across, not rounded; do not dig into the corner
  • Wide toe-box footwear
Reassess in 1-2 weeks
If no improvement, recurrent, or granulation/infection develops → partial nail avulsion (± phenol).

In diabetes or peripheral arterial disease, phenol risks delayed healing, ulceration, or necrosis.

- Partial nail avulsion for symptom relief is acceptable for source control
- Do NOT apply phenol
- Refer to podiatry/foot-and-ankle surgery for definitive matrixectomy under controlled conditions
- Optimize glycemic control and document pedal pulses / ABI if vascular disease suspected

Active Infection — Source Control First

Infected — Source Control First
With active cellulitis or abscess:
- Incise/drain any paronychial abscess
- Avulse the offending lateral nail spicule (this is the source — removing it is often curative for the infection)
- Oral antibiotics only if cellulitis extends beyond the nail fold (cover skin flora; consider MRSA per local patterns)
- DEFER phenol until inflammation resolves; reassess for elective matrixectomy if recurrence
- Tetanus per status; return precautions for spreading infection

Step 1 — Digital Block

  1. Prep the toe with chlorhexidine
  2. Insert a 25-27 ga needle at the dorsolateral base of the proximal phalanx; raise a wheal, then advance toward the plantar surface, aspirate, and inject lidocaine
  3. Repeat on the contralateral side of the toe base
  4. Wait 5-10 minutes for full onset; test before incising
Lidocaine 1-2% plain
2-4 mL total (two-point digital block)
Local injection
No epinephrine in traditional teaching. Use the smallest effective volume; max plain dose 4.5 mg/kg.

Step 2 — Tourniquet (Bloodless Field)

Phenol requires a bloodless field
Phenol is INACTIVATED by blood and tissue fluid — a bloodless field is mandatory for an effective matrixectomy.

1. After the block is fully set, apply a digital tourniquet at the base of the toe
2. Set a visible timer the moment the tourniquet goes on. Document the time.
3. Keep total tourniquet time well under 15-20 minutes; never leave it on after the patient departs.

Single most common digital-tourniquet error: forgetting to remove it.

Step 3 — Partial Nail Avulsion

  1. With the field bloodless, slide a Freer elevator (or small hemostat) under the offending lateral edge of the nail plate to free it from the nail bed and the proximal nail fold
  2. Cut a longitudinal strip 3-5 mm wide from the lateral nail, advancing PROXIMALLY all the way under the eponychium to the matrix
  3. Grasp the freed strip with a hemostat and remove it with a steady proximal-and-rotational pull; confirm the complete strip including its proximal corner was removed
  4. Curette/debride any granulation tissue in the lateral fold
Retained corner = guaranteed recurrence
A retained proximal spicule is a guaranteed recurrence — confirm you removed a complete strip including its proximal corner.

Phenol Matrixectomy — Do It?

DecisionWhen
Yes — perform phenol matrixectomyGood perfusion, no diabetes/PVD, bloodless field achieved
No — avulsion only (contraindication)Diabetic/PVD, active infection, or unable to achieve a bloodless field

Step 4 — Phenol Matrixectomy

  1. Ensure the field is dry and bloodless (phenol is neutralized by blood)
  2. Protect the perionychium: apply petrolatum to the surrounding skin so phenol only contacts the matrix groove
  3. Using a tightly-wound cotton-tipped applicator, apply phenol to the exposed lateral matrix (especially the lateral horn) for three 30-second applications (≈90 s total)
  4. Neutralize immediately with copious isopropyl alcohol (or sterile saline) flush after the final application
Protect skin, respect contact time, neutralize
Phenol (~88% liquified) chemically ablates the germinal matrix. Protect surrounding skin and respect contact time to limit chemical burn.

Avulsion Only — Counsel Recurrence

No phenol applied
The lateral strip is removed for symptom relief but the matrix is intact, so the spicule can regrow.

- Recurrence ~10% (vs ~1.4% with phenol)
- Counsel the patient explicitly that the nail may grow back ingrown
- Arrange podiatry/surgery follow-up for definitive matrixectomy if it recurs
- Proceed to dressing + aftercare

Step 5 — Tourniquet Off + Dressing

  1. REMOVE THE DIGITAL TOURNIQUET NOW. Confirm brisk capillary refill returns to the toe before dressing. Document removal time.
  2. Apply a non-adherent dressing (petrolatum gauze or bacitracin-impregnated gauze) over the nail fold
  3. Apply a bulky absorbent toe dressing; tube gauze or a loose wrap. Avoid circumferential tightness
  4. Tetanus prophylaxis per status

Reference — Lateral Nail Fold + Matrix Anatomy (not a flow-tied step)

Key structures for a partial avulsion (background reference — this content has no decision-branch of its own in the source tree; it is linked in-line from the avulsion step):

- Lateral nail fold — the soft-tissue groove where the offending nail edge digs in
- Nail plate — the visible nail; only the offending lateral 3-5 mm strip is removed, NOT the whole nail
- Germinal matrix — sits PROXIMAL, under the proximal nail fold (eponychium); it extends ~5 mm proximal to the visible cuticle and is what phenol must ablate to prevent regrowth
- Lateral horn of the matrix — the corner the phenol most needs to reach; incomplete ablation here is the usual cause of recurrence
