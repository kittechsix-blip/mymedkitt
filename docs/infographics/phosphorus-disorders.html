Phosphorus Disorders — Hypophosphatemia & Hyperphosphatemia

Adults in the ED/inpatient setting with an abnormal serum phosphate. Interpret phosphate together with calcium, magnesium, potassium, renal function, acid-base status, insulin/nutrition exposure, and cell-lysis risk — never as an isolated number. Excludes isolated outpatient CKD-MBD titration, which is a nephrology-coordinated chronic-care process.

⚠ Phos <1 mg/dL or symptomatic hypophosphatemia: use monitored IV phosphate replacement, not oral.
⚠ Choose sodium phosphate if potassium is high or renal function is impaired; choose potassium phosphate only when potassium is low/normal and renal function allows.
⚠ Severe hyperphosphatemia with renal failure, symptomatic hypocalcemia, or tumor lysis syndrome may require urgent dialysis.
⚠ Do not reflexively give calcium for asymptomatic TLS-related hypocalcemia — treat the phosphate and clinical instability instead.
⚠ Refeeding, DKA, or alcohol-use states: monitor phosphate with potassium and magnesium together — phosphate can crash quickly after insulin or nutrition.
