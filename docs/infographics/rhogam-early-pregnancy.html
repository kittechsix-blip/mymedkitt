Early Pregnancy RhoGAM: The 2024 ACOG Practice Change

Rh(D)-negative pregnant patients in the ED/OB triage setting with a potential sensitizing event — bleeding, trauma, invasive procedure, pregnancy loss, or delivery — from first trimester through postpartum. Excludes patients already confirmed Rh(D)-positive with no antibody concern, and does not replace individualized MFM/blood-bank consultation once alloimmunization has occurred.

⚠ Already sensitized (antibody screen positive) — RhoGAM is NOT effective. It prevents alloimmunization, it does not reverse it. Consult MFM/blood bank and begin serial MCA Doppler surveillance for fetal anemia.
⚠ Molar pregnancy, invasive procedures (CVS/amniocentesis/cordocentesis), and abdominal trauma need RhoGAM at ANY gestational age — the 2024 'no routine RhoGAM before 12 weeks' change does NOT apply to these exceptions.
⚠ Give RhoGAM within 72 hours of the sensitizing event (evacuation, procedure, trauma, delivery); a dose given late still provides partial protection up to 28 days postpartum.
⚠ Order Kleihauer-Betke testing after trauma or a sensitizing event beyond 20 weeks gestation — a fetomaternal hemorrhage above the standard dose's 30 mL coverage needs additional vials.
⚠ RhoGAM is not indicated for Rh(D)-positive patients, and cannot be given to prevent an alloimmunization that has already occurred.

2024 ACOG Practice Change

Anti-D immune globulin (RhoGAM) is NO LONGER routinely recommended before 12 weeks gestation for threatened miscarriage, spontaneous abortion, or ectopic pregnancy.[1] Fetal RBCs don't express Rh(D) antigen until ~38 days post-conception (~7–8 weeks GA), and the alloimmunization risk from first-trimester events is <0.1% — RhoGAM still applies to specific high-risk exceptions and is unchanged at 28 weeks and postpartum.

What to do now

Rh(D) positive
RhoGAM is not indicated regardless of clinical scenario. Document Rh-positive status in the chart.
Already sensitized
RhoGAM is NOT effective — it cannot reverse alloimmunization once it has occurred. Consult MFM/blood bank; begin serial MCA Doppler surveillance for fetal anemia (HDFN risk).
No RhoGAM needed
First-trimester threatened miscarriage, spontaneous abortion (including surgical management), or ectopic pregnancy: RhoGAM is not recommended per 2024 ACOG guidance. If this is a weak D patient with genotyping unavailable, still treat as Rh-negative and apply this same guidance.
Molar pregnancy
RhoGAM 300 mcg (1500 IU) IM within 72 hours of evacuation, any gestational age. 50 mcg MICRhoGAM is an acceptable alternative in the first trimester.
Invasive procedure
RhoGAM 300 mcg (1500 IU) IM within 72 hours of CVS, amniocentesis, cordocentesis, or fetal intervention, any gestational age.
Abdominal trauma
RhoGAM 300 mcg (1500 IU) IM within 72 hours, any gestational age. Order Kleihauer-Betke if >20 weeks to size any additional dose.
Significant vaginal bleeding
Not a hard contraindication scenario — clinical judgment applies. Giving RhoGAM 300 mcg (or 50 mcg MICRhoGAM) is reasonable when soaking >1 pad/hour for 2+ hours, hemodynamic changes, symptomatic anemia, or visible clots/tissue.
Routine 28-week prophylaxis
RhoGAM 300 mcg (1500 IU) IM at 28 weeks for all Rh-negative, unsensitized patients — unchanged by 2024 guidance. Repeat antibody screen not required first if the initial screen was negative.
Sensitizing event ≥12 weeks
RhoGAM 300 mcg (1500 IU) IM within 72 hours. Order Kleihauer-Betke if >20 weeks to assess for a larger fetomaternal hemorrhage requiring additional doses.
Postpartum
RhoGAM 300 mcg (1500 IU) IM within 72 hours of delivery if the newborn is Rh-positive (confirm by cord blood). If the 72-hour window is missed, give up to 28 days postpartum for partial protection.

Route: IM (deltoid or gluteal); IV is off-label for emergent massive transfusion. Give within 72 hours of the sensitizing event. MICRhoGAM's 50 mcg dose is acceptable for first-trimester events but is not adequate after 12 weeks — some institutions stock only the standard 300 mcg dose.

Kleihauer-Betke & massive fetomaternal hemorrhage

Order Kleihauer-Betke (KB) after trauma >20 weeks, abruption, or any suspected large fetomaternal hemorrhage (FMH). Vials needed = (fetal blood volume ÷ 30 mL) + 1 vial, where fetal blood volume = % fetal cells × maternal blood volume ÷ 100 (maternal blood volume ≈ 5000 mL, adjust for patient size).

Summary & do-not-miss pitfalls

  • Do NOT assume 'no RhoGAM before 12 weeks' applies to molar pregnancy, invasive procedures, or abdominal trauma — these always need RhoGAM regardless of gestational age.
  • Do NOT expect RhoGAM to help an already-sensitized patient — it does not reverse alloimmunization; consult MFM.
  • Do NOT skip Kleihauer-Betke testing after trauma or a sensitizing event beyond 20 weeks — a large FMH needs more than the standard single dose.
  • Do NOT assume the 72-hour window is a hard stop — a late dose up to 28 days postpartum still offers partial protection.
  • Do NOT give RhoGAM to a confirmed Rh(D)-positive patient.
2024 ACOG bottom line
<12 weeks: no RhoGAM for threatened miscarriage, spontaneous abortion, or ectopic pregnancy. <12 weeks exceptions: molar pregnancy, invasive procedures, abdominal trauma, and clinical-judgment heavy bleeding still need it. ≥12 weeks: standard indications are unchanged — 28-week prophylaxis, any sensitizing event, and postpartum (if newborn Rh-positive).
