Burns: Trauma First, Then the Airway

Adults and adolescents with acute thermal, chemical, electrical, or radiation burns in the ED. A trauma-first teaching overview — the full branching protocol (peds Lund-Browder, Dell-Seton resuscitation, dressing selection) lives in the myMedKitt burns consult.

⚠ Airway is the burn that kills early. Intubate for ANY airway concern — stridor, hoarseness, soot in the nares, or full-thickness facial/neck burns. Airway edema peaks at 12–24 h and only worsens; if you are weighing intubation, do it now.[1]
⚠ Hydrofluoric (HF) acid binds calcium and magnesium → potentially fatal hypocalcemia and hyperkalemia. Treat with calcium gluconate and place the patient on a cardiac monitor.[4][12]
⚠ Elemental metals (Na, K, Li) are water-reactive. Cover with mineral oil and mechanically remove ALL metal FIRST — only then irrigate with water.[4][12]
⚠ Cyanide in enclosed-space fires: lactate >8 mmol/L + altered mental status = treat empirically with hydroxocobalamin. Do not wait for levels.[5]
⚠ Carbon monoxide: pulse oximetry is UNRELIABLE and PaO₂ appears normal. Diagnosis requires co-oximetry.[3]

7 · Escharotomy, transfer & disposition

Escharotomy — do not wait for pulselessness
Circumferential deep burns create a tourniquet as edema builds (typically 4–6 h post-burn). Loss of the Doppler arterial signal is the trigger to incise — pulselessness and paralysis are late findings. Chest: bilateral anterior axillary-line incisions from clavicle to costal margin.[6][7][8][9][14][15]
ABA burn-center transfer criteria10 criteria
  1. Partial-thickness burns >10% TBSA
  2. Burns to face, hands, feet, genitalia, perineum, or major joints
  3. Full-thickness (3rd degree) burns of any size
  4. Electrical burns, including lightning
  5. Chemical burns
  6. Inhalation injury
  7. Burns in patients with significant comorbidities
  8. Burns with concomitant trauma where the burn is the greater risk
  9. Burns in children at hospitals without pediatric burn capability
  10. Burns requiring social, emotional, or rehabilitative intervention (suspected abuse, psychiatric, long-term rehab)
Pre-transfer checklist
Secure the airway BEFORE transport if any inhalation concern. Continue LR and document the rate + total volume. Place a Foley and record UOP. Clean, dry dressings only — no wet dressings during transport (hypothermia).
First-aid pitfalls — do NOT4 items
Do NOT delay intubation for a facial/airway burn — edema peaks at 12–24 h and the window closes fast.
Do NOT irrigate elemental-metal burns with water — cover with mineral oil and remove the metal first.
Do NOT neutralize acid or alkali burns — the exothermic reaction adds thermal injury. Irrigate with water.
Do NOT use ice or bolus crystalloid to 'catch up' — ice causes frostbite; catch-up boluses drive fluid creep.

7 · Escharotomy, transfer & disposition

Circumferential deep burns create a tourniquet as edema builds (typically 4–6 h post-burn). Loss of the Doppler arterial signal is the trigger to incise — pulselessness and paralysis are late findings. Chest: bilateral anterior axillary-line incisions from clavicle to costal margin.[6][7][8][9][14][15]

ABA burn-center transfer criteria

  • Partial-thickness burns >10% TBSA
  • Burns to face, hands, feet, genitalia, perineum, or major joints
  • Full-thickness (3rd degree) burns of any size
  • Electrical burns, including lightning
  • Chemical burns
  • Inhalation injury
  • Burns in patients with significant comorbidities
  • Burns with concomitant trauma where the burn is the greater risk
  • Burns in children at hospitals without pediatric burn capability
  • Burns requiring social, emotional, or rehabilitative intervention (suspected abuse, psychiatric, long-term rehab)

Secure the airway BEFORE transport if any inhalation concern. Continue LR and document the rate + total volume. Place a Foley and record UOP. Clean, dry dressings only — no wet dressings during transport (hypothermia).

First-aid pitfalls — do NOT

Do NOT delay intubation for a facial/airway burn — edema peaks at 12–24 h and the window closes fast.

Do NOT irrigate elemental-metal burns with water — cover with mineral oil and remove the metal first.

Do NOT neutralize acid or alkali burns — the exothermic reaction adds thermal injury. Irrigate with water.

Do NOT use ice or bolus crystalloid to 'catch up' — ice causes frostbite; catch-up boluses drive fluid creep.
