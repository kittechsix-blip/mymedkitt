Acute Aortic Dissection: Rate Down, Pressure Down, Classify, Cut

Adults ≥18 with suspected acute aortic dissection in the ED. Excludes pediatrics, pregnancy-specific protocols, chronic (>14-day) dissection, and traumatic aortic injury — each needs a modified pathway.

⚠ ACS mimic is lethal. Coronary-ostial involvement can produce a true STEMI pattern — thrombolytics or full anticoagulation in an unrecognized dissection can be fatal. Painless dissection (6%) can present as stroke, syncope, or heart failure alone.
⚠ Anti-impulse order matters: lower HR to <60 bpm with a beta-blocker FIRST, then bring SBP to 100–120 mmHg. A vasodilator given before rate control causes reflex tachycardia that raises aortic wall stress and can propagate the dissection.
⚠ Hypotension is a grave sign — rupture, tamponade, or acute severe AR. Do NOT delay the OR; pericardiocentesis may precipitate complete rupture and is reserved for tamponade with imminent arrest.
⚠ Type A is a surgical emergency — 1–2% per hour mortality and ~50% at 1 week without surgery. Stroke is NOT a contraindication to repair.
⚠ Complicated Type B (malperfusion, rupture, refractory pain or HTN) needs emergent TEVAR; open surgery carries 30–40% mortality.
⚠ Mesenteric malperfusion is the most lethal branch complication — emergent intervention. For spinal-cord ischemia maintain MAP >80–90 mmHg and consider CSF drainage.

1 · Recognize & risk-stratify

The lesion
An intimal tear lets blood dissect into the aortic media, splitting a true and false lumen. Stanford Type A involves the ascending aorta (surgical); Type B does not (usually medical). Untreated Type A kills at 1–2% per hour.[1]

Classic: sudden, maximal-at-onset tearing chest or interscapular back pain. Red-flag exam: pulse deficit or an SBP differential >20 mmHg between arms, focal neuro deficit, or a new diastolic (AR) murmur.[3]

Do not be fooled
6% are painless and present as stroke, syncope, or heart failure. Coronary-ostial extension mimics STEMI — anticoagulate or lyse an unrecognized dissection and the patient can die.[3]

High-risk conditionMarfan / connective-tissue disease, family history, known aneurysm or aortic-valve disease, prior aortic manipulation
High-risk painChest/back/abdominal pain that is abrupt, severe, and tearing/ripping
High-risk examPulse deficit or SBP differential >20 mmHg, focal neuro deficit, aortic-regurgitation murmur, hypotension/shock
D-dimer rules OUT only the low-risk
ADD-RS 0–1 with D-dimer <500 ng/mL has >99% NPV (ADvISED) — dissection is essentially excluded. D-dimer is not helpful if ADD-RS ≥2, symptoms >24 h, or intramural hematoma is suspected — go straight to CTA.[6]

2 · Anti-impulse therapy (rate first, then pressure)

Two sequential targets: HR <60 bpm with a beta-blocker, THEN SBP 100–120 mmHg (MAP ~60–75) with a vasodilator. Add IV opioid (morphine or fentanyl) for pain to blunt the catecholamine surge. Rate control first — a vasodilator alone triggers reflex tachycardia that raises wall stress.[9]

Hypotensive / shock — this overrides everything
Hypotension means rupture, tamponade, or acute severe AR until proven otherwise. Resuscitate with blood products, add a vasopressor (norepinephrine or phenylephrine) only as needed, and move to the emergent OR — do NOT delay surgery. Pericardiocentesis is reserved for tamponade with imminent arrest and may precipitate complete rupture.[10]
Stable — start esmolol first
Start Esmolol at a 500 mcg/kg load over 1 min, then titrate the infusion (50–200 mcg/kg/min) to HR <60 before adding any vasodilator. Ultra-short half-life (~9 min) makes it easy to titrate and to stop if going to the OR. Labetalol 20 mg IV then 20–80 mg q10min (max 300 mg) is an equally valid combined alpha/beta-blocker alternative, especially in a stable patient.[9]
Beta-blocker contraindicated — use diltiazem
If beta-blockade is contraindicated (severe AR, high-grade AV block, decompensated HF, asthma, cocaine), substitute Diltiazem: 0.25 mg/kg IV bolus, then 0.35 mg/kg if rate control is inadequate.[9]

3 · Confirm & classify

CTA is first-line
CTA chest/abdomen/pelvis (sensitivity 95–100%, specificity 98–100%) is the ED test of choice — it shows extent, branch involvement, and complications. Send labs in parallel (type & crossmatch 6 units, troponin, lactate) but do not delay imaging for labs. A normal chest X-ray does NOT rule out dissection.[8]

On imaging, classify by whether the ascending aorta is involved: ascending = Type A; not = Type B, then decide if the Type B is complicated. Intramural hematoma (no flap, >5 mm wall thickening) is a dissection variant and is managed the same way.[1]

4 · Type-specific disposition

Type A → emergent surgery
Ascending involvement is a surgical emergency: call cardiac surgery now, keep the patient NPO, continue anti-impulse therapy, secure blood products, and place arterial + central lines. If no on-site surgeon, transfer emergently surgeon-to-surgeon. Untreated mortality ~50% at 1 week.[10]
Type B uncomplicated → medical + ICU
No malperfusion, rupture, refractory pain, or refractory HTN: manage medically in the ICU with IV anti-impulse therapy, serial imaging, and transition to a lifelong oral beta-blocker targeting SBP <130 and HR <60.[11]
Type B complicated → TEVAR
Malperfusion (mesenteric, renal, limb, spinal), rupture, refractory pain, or refractory HTN: emergent vascular surgery, TEVAR first-line (open surgery 30–40% mortality). For spinal-cord protection maintain MAP >80–90; mesenteric ischemia is the most lethal territory.[11]
ED-while-awaiting-OR checklist5 items
  • ICU-level monitoring in the ED with continuous arterial BP.
  • Maintain HR <60 and SBP 100–120; serial neuro exams.
  • Serial lactate and abdominal exams for malperfusion.
  • Blood products immediately available; keep NPO.
  • Document time of onset, pulses in all four limbs, and neuro baseline.

6 · Do NOT

Critical pitfalls

**Do NOT anticoagulate or lyse a suspected dissection** presenting as STEMI or stroke — coronary-ostial or carotid involvement mimics both.

**Do NOT give a vasodilator before beta-blockade** — reflex tachycardia raises dP/dt and propagates the tear.

**Do NOT let a normal CXR or normal D-dimer reassure you** when ADD-RS ≥2 — image with CTA.

**Do NOT delay the OR** for a hypotensive Type A to place a central line or drain a pericardial effusion — surgery is the resuscitation.
