Serotonin Syndrome: Clonus, Cool, Cyproheptadine

Adults ≥18 in the ED with suspected serotonin syndrome after serotonergic drug exposure. Excludes pediatrics and pregnancy — each needs specialist input. Severe cases warrant toxicology consultation; this tool does not replace it.

⚠ Stop ALL serotonergic agents immediately — SSRIs, SNRIs, MAOIs, tramadol, linezolid, methylene blue, MDMA, dextromethorphan.
⚠ Benzodiazepines are first-line for agitation: lorazepam 2-4 mg IV q15-30min PRN (or diazepam 5-10 mg IV q15min). They also reduce muscle activity and thermogenesis.
⚠ Cyproheptadine 12 mg PO initial, then 2 mg q2h (max 32 mg/24 hours) for moderate-severe cases.
⚠ Aggressive cooling for hyperthermia >41°C (evaporative cooling + cold IV fluids). Antipyretics do NOT work — the heat is muscular, not hypothalamic — and physical restraints WORSEN hyperthermia (isometric struggle raises heat production); sedate, don't restrain.
⚠ Intubate with paralysis (rocuronium/vecuronium) if severe rigidity or temp >41°C. Avoid succinylcholine — rhabdomyolysis raises hyperkalemia risk.
⚠ Bromocriptine (or any dopaminergic agent) is contraindicated in serotonin syndrome. It is the NMS-specific antidote — giving it for a misdiagnosed SS would worsen serotonergic excess.

1 · Recognize the triad

Serotonergic excess — the classic triad
Altered mental status (agitation, confusion) + autonomic dysfunction (hyperthermia, tachycardia, diaphoresis, mydriasis) + neuromuscular excitation (clonus, hyperreflexia, tremor).[1][2]

Clonus is the hallmark — a hyperkinetic state, not rigid. Onset is typically within 24 hours (often within 6 hours) of a drug start, dose increase, or interaction. Lower-extremity findings dominate; check for ankle clonus >3 beats.[1]

OpioidsTramadol, fentanyl, meperidine
RecreationalMDMA, cocaine, amphetamines
InteractionsSSRI + triptan, SSRI + MAOI, DXM

2 · Hunter Criteria (the diagnosis)

Hunter Serotonin Toxicity Criteria
More accurate than Sternbach: sensitivity 84%, specificity 97%. Requires exposure to a serotonergic agent AND at least one finding cluster below.[4]
Finding clusterMeets criteria?
Spontaneous clonusYes
Inducible clonus + agitation OR diaphoresisYes
Ocular clonus + agitation OR diaphoresisYes
Tremor + hyperreflexiaYes
Hypertonia + temp >38°C + ocular OR inducible clonusYes

No serotonergic exposure → serotonin syndrome is excluded; work up an alternative diagnosis (anticholinergic, sympathomimetic, sepsis, thyroid storm, heat stroke).[1][5]

6 · The pathway

Suspected serotonin syndrome — Serotonergic excess causing AMS, autonomic dysfunction, neuromuscular excitation — onset within 24h of drug change, clonus is hallmark.

Serotonergic drug exposure? — Hunter Criteria REQUIRE exposure. Check SSRIs/SNRIs/MAOIs/tramadol/MDMA/linezolid.

Take full medication history — Rx, OTC/supplements (DXM, St. John's Wort), recreational drugs; check new starts, dose changes, interactions.

Alternative diagnosis — Anticholinergic, sympathomimetic, sepsis, meningitis, thyroid storm, heat stroke, malignant hyperthermia.

Apply Hunter Criteria — 84% sensitive, 97% specific. Serotonergic agent + clonus/tremor/hyperreflexia/hypertonia cluster.

Meets Hunter Criteria? — Exposure + one qualifying finding cluster.

Differentiate SS from NMS & mimics — Onset speed, muscle tone (hyperkinetic vs rigid), drug class, pupils, CK.

SS or NMS? — SS: hours, clonus, hyperreflexia, mydriasis. NMS: days, lead-pipe rigidity, bradyreflexia.

NMS pathway (dantrolene / bromocriptine) — Bromocriptine is contraindicated in serotonin syndrome.

Severity? — Mild (alert, temp <38.5), moderate (agitation, temp 38.5-40), severe (temp >40, rigidity, seizures, rhabdo).

Mild: stop agents, benzos, observe 6-12h — IV fluids, cardiac monitoring; most resolve in 24-72 hours.

Moderate: high-dose benzos + cyproheptadine + cooling — Cyproheptadine 12 mg load then 2 mg q2h (max 32 mg/24 hours). Avoid restraints and antipyretics.

Severe: airway, paralysis, aggressive cooling — Avoid succinylcholine. Rocuronium/vecuronium; cyproheptadine via NG; no-ceiling benzos.

Discharge with return precautions — If resolving after 6-12h, stable vitals, tolerating PO; psych follow-up.

Admit to telemetry — Continue benzos + cyproheptadine q6h 24-48h; tox and psych consults; MAOIs may take up to 1 week.

Admit to ICU — Temp >40, intubation, paralysis, hemodynamic instability, rhabdo with renal failure, DIC.

Serotonin syndrome managed — Most resolve in 24-72 hours; MAOI-related cases longest.
