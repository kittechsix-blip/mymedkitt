Acetaminophen Overdose: Level, Nomogram, NAC

Adults and adolescents in the ED with known or suspected acetaminophen (paracetamol) overdose. Weight-based doses cap at 100 kg. Pediatric-specific thresholds, modified-release tablets sold outside the US, and definitive transplant decisions require toxicology / poison-control input.

⚠ NAC is nearly 100% effective within 8 hours of ingestion — determine the ingestion pattern and get a level moving so the antidote is not delayed.[2]
⚠ When in doubt, give NAC. It has minimal side effects and no absolute contraindication for acetaminophen toxicity — pregnancy is NOT a contraindication.[6]
⚠ NEVER permanently stop NAC for a reaction. Anaphylactoid reactions are histamine-mediated, not IgE allergy — slow or pause, treat, and resume.[11]
⚠ Check an APAP level at 4 hours post-ingestion and plot it on the Rumack-Matthew nomogram (150 mcg/mL treatment line).[4]
⚠ Psychiatric evaluation is mandatory before discharge for every intentional ingestion.[1]

1 · Recognize & classify the ingestion

Why this matters
Acetaminophen is the most common cause of acute liver failure in the US and UK, hidden in >600 OTC and prescription products (Tylenol, Percocet, Vicodin, NyQuil, Excedrin, Fioricet). NAC is a near-perfect antidote if given early — the whole algorithm exists to get it started in time.[1][3]

Mechanism: ~90% of a therapeutic dose is glucuronidated/sulfated to nontoxic metabolites; ~5% is oxidized by CYP2E1 to NAPQI, which glutathione neutralizes. In overdose, conjugation saturates, glutathione is depleted, and NAPQI accumulates → centrilobular hepatocyte necrosis. NAC replenishes glutathione and detoxifies NAPQI.[16]

Threshold
Therapeutic dose10–15 mg/kg per dose (max 4 g/day; 2 g/day in chronic alcohol use)
Toxic single ingestion>150 mg/kg or >7.5 g total in adults
Massive ingestion>30 g (or >0.5 g/kg if <60 kg)
Three ingestion patterns — they are NOT managed the same
1. Acute single ingestion → nomogram applies (see §2). 2. Repeated supratherapeutic (multiple doses spanning >24 h) → nomogram does NOT apply; use the (ALT)(APAP) product. 3. Unknown / unreliable history → draw APAP + ALT now and start NAC empirically if either is abnormal.[6][17]

2 · Level & the Rumack-Matthew nomogram

For an acute single ingestion, draw a serum APAP level at 4 hours post-ingestion (or immediately if already >4 h) and plot it on the Rumack-Matthew nomogram. The US treatment line is 150 mcg/mL at 4 h; the high-risk line is 300 mcg/mL at 4 h.[4][6]

  • APAP level (repeat q4–6h if treating)
  • AST/ALT, INR/PT, bilirubin
  • BMP (creatinine, glucose, bicarbonate), lactate
  • Salicylate level — coingestion screen
  • Lipase, CBC, β-hCG
Repeated / unknown ingestion — how the rules changeno nomogram
Repeated supratherapeutic — use the (ALT)(APAP) product
The nomogram has no single ingestion time to plot. Treat if ALT is elevated, APAP is detectable, or the (ALT)(APAP) product >10,000 mcg/mL × IU/L; hepatotoxicity is very unlikely when the product is <1,500.[17]
Unknown / unreliable history
Cannot apply the nomogram. Draw APAP + AST/ALT + INR + BMP + lactate + salicylate now. If APAP is detectable OR ALT is elevated → start NAC empirically. When in doubt, give NAC — labs will guide de-escalation while it runs.[2][6]

4 · Massive overdose & extracorporeal removal

Recognize massive overdose
>30 g ingested (or >0.5 g/kg if <60 kg), or an APAP level above the 300 line. Classic tell: early lactic acidosis + altered mental status within 12 h from mitochondrial dysfunction — *before* liver damage. Standard NAC neutralizes NAPQI 1:1, so the dose must scale with the amount ingested.[8][14]

Not a massive overdose
Standard 3-bag NAC (§3) is sufficient. Reassess the level and the stop criteria (§6) — no rate increase, fomepizole, or dialysis needed.
Massive overdose — escalate now
Keep bag 1 (150 mg/kg) and bag 2 (50 mg/kg) standard, increase the bag-3 rate, add fomepizole, and evaluate for hemodialysis. Loop in toxicology / poison control.

High-dose NAC (Hendrickson 2019). Keep bags 1–2 standard; scale the bag-3 infusion rate to severity: above 300 line → 12.5 mg/kg/hr (2×); above 450 → 18.75 mg/kg/hr (3×); above 600 → 25 mg/kg/hr (4×). 6 · Stop criteria, hepatic failure & disposition

Stop NAC only when ALL four are met
1. APAP <10 mcg/mL (undetectable) 2. INR <2 3. AST/ALT normal, at baseline, or down >25–50% from peak 4. Clinically well. If any criterion is unmet → repeat bag 3 (100 mg/kg over 16 h) indefinitely.[6][13]
Established hepatic failure
Continue IV NAC indefinitely — repeat bag 3 until the liver clearly improves AND the APAP level is zero. NAC gives a proven 28% mortality benefit even in APAP-induced fulminant hepatic failure (Keays 1991). Do NOT let the infusion stop.[5][10]
