Acetaminophen Overdose: Nomogram to NAC to Transplant Referral

Adults in the ED with acute, repeated-supratherapeutic, or unknown-pattern acetaminophen (paracetamol) ingestion. The Rumack-Matthew nomogram applies ONLY to a single acute ingestion with a known/estimated time — it does NOT apply to repeated/chronic ingestion, which is evaluated with the (ALT)(APAP) product instead. Excludes non-acetaminophen causes of acute liver failure and pediatric-specific weight-estimation nuances beyond the standard mg/kg calculator.

⚠ NEVER permanently stop NAC for an anaphylactoid reaction. These are histamine-mediated, NOT true IgE allergy — hold up to 1 hour, treat with diphenhydramine ± epinephrine, then resume. Liver failure from stopping NAC is far more dangerous than the reaction itself.
⚠ Do NOT delay NAC to give activated charcoal. Give both concurrently when both are indicated — charcoal is contraindicated with an unprotected airway/altered mental status, caustic coingestant, or GI perforation/obstruction.
⚠ Pregnancy is NOT a contraindication to NAC. It is safe and beneficial; delayed treatment increases risk of miscarriage/fetal death. IV NAC is preferred in pregnancy.
⚠ The Rumack-Matthew nomogram does NOT apply to repeated/chronic supratherapeutic ingestion — there is no single ingestion time to plot. Use the (ALT)(APAP) product instead.
⚠ Massive overdose (>300 line or >30 g) can cause early lactic acidosis and altered mental status from mitochondrial dysfunction BEFORE liver injury appears. Standard NAC dosing may be inadequate — escalate to high-dose NAC, fomepizole, and hemodialysis evaluation.
⚠ Psychiatric evaluation is MANDATORY before discharge for all intentional ingestions.

1 · Recognize & risk-stratify

Why NAC works
Acetaminophen is the most common cause of acute liver failure in the US/UK, found in >600 OTC/Rx products (Tylenol, Percocet, Vicodin, NyQuil, Excedrin, Fioricet). Normal metabolism: 90% glucuronidation/sulfation → nontoxic. ~5% is oxidized by CYP2E1 → NAPQI (toxic), neutralized by glutathione. In overdose, conjugation saturates → NAPQI accumulates → glutathione depletes → hepatocellular necrosis. NAC is a glutathione precursor — nearly 100% effective when given within 8 hours of ingestion.[1][2][3]

  • Unknown/unreliable history — conflicting statements, unknown timing, or insufficient detail.

2 · Level, timing & the nomogram

Rumack-Matthew nomogram (acute ingestion only)
Draw serum APAP level at 4 hours post-ingestion (or immediately if >4 h since ingestion). Treatment line: 150 mcg/mL at 4h (US uses this line; original Rumack line was 200). High-risk line: 300 mcg/mL at 4h (massive overdose). If drawn <4h, an undetectable concentration after >2h typically excludes significant ingestion — repeat at ≥4h to confirm.[4][6]

Labs to order: APAP level, AST/ALT, INR/PT, BMP (Cr, glucose, bicarb), lipase, bilirubin, CBC, salicylate level, lactate, VBG/ABG.[1]

Extended-release / coingestant — a single level is unreliable
Opioid/anticholinergic coingestants and ER tablets delay peak absorption. If a 4-12h level is above the treatment line → treat regardless. If a 4-24h level is <10 mcg/mL → no further levels/treatment. If below the line but >10 mcg/mL → redraw in 4-6 hours; treat if the second level is above the line at its time point. Modified-release tablets (outside the US) are more unpredictable — consult toxicology.[6][13]
Nomogram does NOT apply to repeated ingestion
Repeated supratherapeutic ingestion often presents with hepatotoxicity without early Stage 1 symptoms (nausea/vomiting) — may present directly with RUQ pain and elevated transaminases. "Alcohol-Tylenol Syndrome": chronic alcohol + chronic acetaminophen causes hepatotoxicity at doses generally considered safe (<4 g/day), via CYP2E1 induction plus glutathione depletion.[6][17]
(ALT)(APAP) productInterpretation
>10,000 mcg/mL × IU/LStrongly associated with hepatotoxicity
<1,500 mcg/mL × IU/LHepatotoxicity very unlikely

Treat if ANY: ALT elevated above normal · APAP level detectable · (ALT)(APAP) product >10,000 · clinical concern despite labs (risk factors present).[6][17]

Unreliable history — draw labs now, treat empirically
Unreliable = insufficient detail for dose/time, conflicting statements, or symptoms/labs inconsistent with the stated history. Draw APAP level + AST/ALT + INR + BMP + lactate + salicylate level now. If APAP is detectable OR ALT is elevated → start NAC empirically. When in doubt → give NAC — minimal side effects, potentially life-saving.[2][6]

4 · Massive overdose — high-dose NAC, fomepizole, dialysis

Massive overdose — definition & presentation
Massive overdose: >30 grams ingested (or >0.5 g/kg if <60 kg), OR APAP level above the 300 line. Clinical presentation: early lactic acidosis + altered mental status within 12 hours — mitochondrial dysfunction, BEFORE liver damage occurs. Standard NAC may be INADEQUATE — NAC neutralizes NAPQI in a 1:1 molar ratio, so the dose must scale with the amount of acetaminophen ingested. Activate all interventions together.[8][14][16]

5 · Hepatic failure & transplant referral

Continue NAC indefinitely
Repeat Bag 3 (100 mg/kg over 16 hours) until the patient recovers or dies. NAC provides benefit even in established hepatic failure — proven 28% mortality benefit in an RCT of patients with acetaminophen-induced fulminant hepatic failure (Keays 1991). Do NOT allow the infusion to stop until the liver is clearly improving AND the APAP level is zero.[5][10]

King's College criteria met — transplant referral now
Contact the transplant center now. Continue IV NAC indefinitely alongside transplant workup.
Criteria not yet met — reassess and stay ahead
Reassess trend serially. Still contact the transplant center early for any severe liver injury — do not wait for lactate >3.5 or phosphate >3.75 at 48-96 hours to escalate discussion.
  • Coagulopathy: FFP only if ACTIVE bleeding — it obscures INR trending, the single best prognostic marker.
  • Cerebral edema: elevate HOB 30°, hypertonic saline 23.4% for herniation, mannitol 0.5-1 g/kg, avoid stimulation, maintain normothermia.
  • Hypoglycemia: D10W continuous infusion, glucose monitoring q1-2h.
  • Renal failure (10-25% of patients, >50% with hepatic failure): supportive care, dialysis PRN — renal recovery generally occurs if the patient survives.
  • Infection: low threshold for broad-spectrum antibiotics — hepatic failure patients are immunocompromised.

ICU — hepatic failure/transplant — Coagulopathy, encephalopathy, acidosis, renal failure, or massive OD with instability.

Admit — ward, complete NAC — Continue protocol, labs q6-12h, reassess stopping criteria each set. Psych eval for intentional ingestion.

Discharge — criteria met — Normal AST/ALT/INR/Cr, below treatment line or low (ALT)(APAP) product, asymptomatic. Psych eval MANDATORY for intentional ingestion.

8 · Disposition & pitfalls

Disposition | Who | Key actions

Discharge | Below treatment line or low-risk chronic, normal labs, asymptomatic, 4-6h observation complete | Counsel max 4 g/day (2 g/day with chronic alcohol). Psych eval MANDATORY for intentional ingestion.

Admit — ward | NAC ongoing (non-massive), elevated LFTs, unreliable history with ongoing concern | Labs q6-12h, reassess stopping criteria each set. Escalate to ICU if hepatic failure develops.

ICU | Hepatic failure (coagulopathy, encephalopathy, pH <7.3, renal failure), massive OD, King's criteria/transplant, dialysis required | Continue NAC indefinitely. Contact transplant center early. Poison Control 1-800-222-1222.

Critical pitfalls

**Do NOT give FFP prophylactically in hepatic failure** unless there is active bleeding — it obscures INR trending, the single best prognostic marker.

**Do NOT assume oral NAC absorption is reliable if activated charcoal was given simultaneously** — charcoal decreases oral NAC absorption; use IV NAC when both are indicated.

**Do NOT dismiss early lactic acidosis or altered mental status in a massive overdose as unrelated** — it reflects mitochondrial dysfunction BEFORE liver injury and signals the need for high-dose NAC, fomepizole, and dialysis evaluation.

**Do NOT wait for full King's College criteria to contact the transplant center** — early discussion is warranted for any severe liver injury (encephalopathy, pH <7.3, INR >3, renal failure).

**Do NOT trust an undetectable level drawn <4 hours as reassuring** without a confirmatory level at ≥4 hours — delayed absorption (ER formulation, coingestant) can falsely lower an early level.
