ED patients with a wound requiring a tetanus prophylaxis decision, or suspected/confirmed active tetanus. Adult and pediatric. Excludes routine well-child immunization scheduling (not an ED task).

⚠ Active tetanus requires 3000-6000 units TIG IM (NOT the 250 unit prophylaxis dose)

Tetanus — ED approach

Tetanus is caused by *Clostridium tetani* neurotoxin (tetanospasmin), which irreversibly binds spinal cord inhibitory interneurons, producing unopposed muscle contraction. Mortality is 10-20% even with modern ICU care, higher in the elderly and neonates. Toxin binding is IRREVERSIBLE — recovery requires new synapse formation over weeks to months. Two ED scenarios: (1) wound prophylaxis — the Tdap/TIG decision below; (2) active tetanus — a clinical diagnosis requiring ICU (see Active Tetanus Management).

  • Wound — need prophylaxis decision → use the algorithm below
  • Suspected active tetanus (trismus, spasms, rigidity) → see Active Tetanus Management
  • Special population (pregnancy, neonate, IVDU, immunocompromised) → see Special Populations

▶ What to do now

Clean wound, incomplete/unknown vaccination
Give Tdap 0.5 mL IM today (deltoid). TIG NOT needed. Refer to PCP to complete the primary series (dose 2 at 4-8 weeks, dose 3 at 6-12 months after dose 2).
Clean wound, booster overdue (>10y)
Give Tdap 0.5 mL IM booster. TIG NOT needed. Tdap preferred over Td if the patient has never received Tdap as an adult.
Clean wound, protected
No Tdap/Td needed. No TIG needed. Standard wound care only — clean, irrigate, debride devitalized tissue if present.
Tetanus-prone wound, incomplete/unknown vaccination
Give BOTH: TIG 250 units IM (500 units for large/heavily contaminated wounds) AND Tdap 0.5 mL IM — DIFFERENT injection sites, never mix in the same syringe. Refer to PCP to complete the primary series.
Tetanus-prone wound, booster overdue (>5y)
Give Tdap 0.5 mL IM booster only — TIG NOT needed. Note the more aggressive 5-year interval for tetanus-prone wounds (vs 10 years for clean wounds).
Tetanus-prone wound, protected
No Tdap/Td needed. No TIG needed. Focus on wound care — thorough irrigation and debridement of devitalized tissue is what actually prevents tetanus in an adequately-immunized patient.

Wound care for tetanus prevention

  • Debridement — remove ALL devitalized tissue; spores survive in necrotic tissue
  • Copious irrigation — high pressure for contaminated wounds, reduces spore/bacterial load
  • Closure — clean wounds OK for primary closure; contaminated wounds consider delayed closure; puncture wounds leave open
  • Antibiotics — NOT routinely required for tetanus prevention; do NOT replace TIG
  • Foreign bodies — remove if possible; retained FB increases tetanus risk

Active tetanus — clinical recognition

Clinical tetanus is a CLINICAL DIAGNOSIS — there is no confirmatory lab test and culture is rarely positive. Classic presentation: trismus (lockjaw, often first symptom), risus sardonicus, opisthotonus, dysphagia, diffuse muscle rigidity, reflex spasms triggered by stimuli. Incubation is typically 3-21 days; shorter incubation predicts worse prognosis. Spatula test (touch posterior pharynx): positive = reflex jaw spasm/bite — Sensitivity 94%, Specificity 100%.

Active tetanus — ICU management cascade

  1. AIRWAY — intubate EARLY for generalized tetanus; do not wait for respiratory failure
  2. NEUTRALIZE UNBOUND TOXIN — TIG 3,000-6,000 units IM as soon as possible (does not reverse bound toxin)
  3. ELIMINATE SOURCE — metronidazole (preferred over penicillin) + aggressive wound debridement
  4. CONTROL SPASMS — benzodiazepines first-line; may need massive doses
  5. MANAGE AUTONOMIC INSTABILITY — magnesium sulfate infusion; avoid beta-blockers
  6. SUPPORTIVE CARE — ICU admission, nutrition, DVT prophylaxis, dark/quiet environment
Intubate early
Trismus limits mouth opening and spasms complicate laryngoscopy — laryngospasm can be sudden and fatal. RSI with succinylcholine is acceptable (no hyperkalemia risk as in burns). Consider awake fiberoptic if severe trismus; have a surgical airway ready. Cephalic tetanus requires ICU admission with early airway control.

Avoid beta-blockers
Autonomic instability (labile BP, tachy/bradyarrhythmias, hyperpyrexia) is the major cause of ICU death in tetanus. Beta-blockers can cause severe hypotension or asystole from unopposed alpha stimulation — case reports of sudden death with propranolol. Alternatives: morphine, clonidine, labetalol (combined alpha/beta) if a beta-blocker is truly needed.

  • Early enteral nutrition — hypermetabolic state, high caloric needs
  • DVT prophylaxis — LMWH/heparin + SCDs, high risk from immobility
  • Pressure ulcer prevention — frequent repositioning
  • Expected course 3-6 weeks ICU for generalized tetanus
Vaccination does not confer immunity
Natural infection does NOT confer immunity — the toxin dose causing disease is too small to trigger an immune response. Recovered patients must complete the full 3-dose primary series (dose 1 now, dose 2 at 4-8 weeks, dose 3 at 6-12 months after dose 2) before or at first follow-up.

Prophylaxis decision algorithm

Tetanus — ED approach — Two ED scenarios: wound prophylaxis (this diagram) vs active tetanus/special populations (see other sections). Toxin binding is irreversible.

Classify the wound — Tetanus-prone: puncture, contaminated with dirt/feces/saliva, crush, burns, frostbite, devitalized tissue, >6h old, missile injuries, compound fractures, IVDU sites. Clean/minor: superficial, <6h, uncontaminated.

Animal/human bite — Bite wounds are tetanus-prone (oral flora contamination + puncture + crush + devitalized tissue). Also consider rabies and antibiotic prophylaxis.

Clean wound: vaccination history? — TIG is never indicated for clean, minor wounds regardless of vaccination status.

Tetanus-prone wound: vaccination history? — Both vaccine AND TIG may be indicated. TIG indicated if <3 doses/unknown history, or immunocompromised regardless of history.

Give Tdap today — no TIG — Tdap 0.5 mL IM. Refer PCP for series completion (dose 2 at 4-8wk, dose 3 at 6-12mo after dose 2).

Give Tdap booster — no TIG — Antibody levels decline over time; 10-year interval standard for clean wounds.

Protected — standard wound care only — ≥3 doses + last booster ≤10 years = adequately protected. No vaccine, no TIG.

Give TIG + Tdap — DIFFERENT sites — TIG 250 units IM (500 units for large/contaminated) AND Tdap 0.5 mL IM. Must be different injection sites — never mix in same syringe (TIG antibodies inactivate the toxoid).

TIG administration details — Deltoid or anterolateral thigh, opposite side from Td/Tdap. Passive immunity lasts ~4 weeks. Do NOT give if severe allergic reaction to human Ig products or IgA deficiency with anti-IgA antibodies.

Give Tdap booster only — no TIG — ≥3 doses + last >5 years = booster needed. 5-year interval for tetanus-prone wounds (more aggressive than the 10-year clean-wound interval).

Protected — focus wound care — ≥3 doses + last ≤5 years = adequately protected. No vaccine, no TIG.

Wound care: debride + irrigate — Remove ALL devitalized tissue — spores survive in necrotic tissue; aerobic wound bed prevents germination. Antibiotics do NOT replace TIG.

Discharge — verify care + follow-up — Confirm wound care, Tdap/TIG given if indicated, series-completion plan documented, immunization record provided.

Special populations

Population | Key modification

Pregnancy | Tdap recommended for ALL pregnant women, optimal 27-36 weeks gestation — protects newborn via passive antibody transfer. Tdap and TIG both safe in pregnancy; do not withhold.

Neonate | Onset day 3-14 of life, inability to suck is first sign. NICU mandatory; TIG 3,000-6,000 units IM; metronidazole IV; aggressive spasm control. Mortality 60-80%. Prevention = maternal vaccination.

IVDU | Lower threshold for TIG — even apparently "clean" injection sites can be contaminated. Assume incomplete vaccination unless documented. Look for occult abscess.

Immunocompromised | May not respond to vaccine (HIV low CD4, transplant, chemo, chronic steroids). Lower threshold for TIG with tetanus-prone wounds; some experts give TIG regardless of vaccination history if CD4 <200 or high-dose steroids >2 weeks.
