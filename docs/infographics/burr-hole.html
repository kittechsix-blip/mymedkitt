Emergency Burr Hole Craniostomy

Emergency physicians facing a patient who will die of brain herniation before neurosurgical intervention can be reached. Last-resort burr hole craniostomy for CT-confirmed epidural or subdural hematoma with herniation signs. Excludes GCS 3 with bilateral fixed/dilated pupils (non-survivable), intraparenchymal hemorrhage without an extra-axial component, and posterior fossa lesions.

⚠ LAST RESORT procedure — outcomes worsen significantly if drainage is delayed >70-90 min from herniation signs
⚠ Transfer with aggressive medical management is almost always preferred over non-neurosurgeon drilling, even if neurosurgery is 2-3 hours away
⚠ Contraindicated: GCS 3 with bilateral fixed/dilated pupils (non-survivable), intraparenchymal hemorrhage without extra-axial component, posterior fossa lesion
⚠ CLUTCH drill bit is the critical safety feature — auto-disengages at the inner table. Do NOT stop and restart mid-drill; re-engagement is difficult
⚠ DO NOT tamponade the wound — loose dressing only; tight packing increases ICP
⚠ Maximum 2 burr holes total — do not 'hunt' for hematoma with multiple holes

Overview

Rarely performed, potentially life-saving evacuation of an expanding extra-axial hematoma (epidural or subdural) when neurosurgery is unavailable. This is a LAST RESORT procedure. The question: will the patient herniate and die before reaching a neurosurgeon?

Last resort only
Outcomes worsen significantly if drainage occurs >70-90 min after herniation signs. If neurosurgery is available within 2 hours, favor aggressive medical management + transport.

Procedure algorithm

This map covers the full emergency burr hole procedure. See the complete consult in myMedKitt for full reference detail and citations.

Critical actions

Use CLUTCH drill bit — auto-disengages at inner table, prevents brain injury.
Stay ≥3 cm lateral to midline — avoids sagittal sinus.
Ipsilateral to dilated pupil — correct side in >85% of cases.
DO NOT tamponade bleeding — loose dressing only (tight packing increases ICP).
This is a BRIDGE, not a cure — patient still requires definitive neurosurgical craniotomy.
Time-critical: outcomes significantly worsen if drainage >70-90 min from herniation signs.
If no blood found — DO NOT explore further, close and transport immediately.

Indications & contraindications

  • CT-confirmed epidural or subdural hematoma
  • AND midline shift on imaging
  • AND GCS <8 or rapidly deteriorating
  • AND unilateral fixed/dilated pupil (anisocoria)
  • AND neurosurgery unavailable within 2 hours
  1. Ipsilateral pupil dilation (CN III compression)
  2. Contralateral hemiparesis (cerebral peduncle)
  3. Cushing's triad: HTN + bradycardia + irregular respirations

Without CT: severe head trauma + unilateral fixed/dilated pupil, or obvious skull fracture (especially temporal) — clinical suspicion may suffice when CT is unavailable.

  • GCS 3 with bilateral fixed/dilated pupils — non-survivable injury
  • Neurosurgery available within a reasonable timeframe — transfer is almost always preferable
  • Clinical improvement with medical management — patient stabilizing, transport instead
  • Intraparenchymal hemorrhage without an extra-axial component — burr hole will not help
  • Posterior fossa lesion — requires suboccipital approach, high brainstem/cerebellar risk

  • Head of bed 30°, head midline
  • Target EtCO₂ 35–40 mmHg (normocapnia)
  • Brief hyperventilation (EtCO₂ 30–35) only for acute pupil dilation or impending herniation, max 5–10 min
  • Avoid hypoxia (SpO₂ >94%), hypotension (MAP >80), hyperthermia, hyperglycemia

Transfer with aggressive medical management is almost always preferred — even 2–3 hours to neurosurgery may be better than non-neurosurgeon drilling. Continuous osmotherapy, neuro checks q15min, and telemedicine consult during transport.

Indications to turn back
New bilateral fixed pupils, loss of all brainstem reflexes, or cardiac arrest without ROSC.

Temporal point (2 FB above/anterior to tragus, above zygomatic arch): 75% of adult EDH occur here — MMA territory, thin bone. Frontal point: 10 cm above supraorbital ridge, mid-pupillary line. Parietal point: 4 FB posterior/superior to EAC, over the parietal eminence.

Danger zones
Sagittal sinus: stay ≥3 cm lateral to midline. Asterion: overlies transverse/sigmoid sinus in 61–66%. Pterion: thin bone but the MMA runs deep.

CT-guided is always preferred. Without CT: (1) ipsilateral to the dilated pupil — correct side in >85% of extra-axial lesions; (2) if both pupils dilated, side of first dilation or obvious trauma; (3) if pupils equal, side of trauma/skull fracture; (4) contralateral hemiparesis suggests the hematoma is on the opposite side. False localizing signs (Kernohan's notch) occur in 15% — CT is essential when available.

Equipment

  • Drill: Hudson-Brace manual, pneumatic/electric craniotome, or in extremis EZ-IO with 25mm needle
  • Drill bit: 14mm perforator with CLUTCH mechanism (critical); matchstick/cutting ball bit as backup
  • Instruments: scalpel, Weitlaner retractor, Penfield dissectors, hooks, Leksell rongeur
  • Hemostasis: bipolar electrocautery, bone wax, Gelfoam/Surgicel, suction
  • Medications: local anesthetic with epinephrine; cefazolin 2g or ceftriaxone 2g IV

The clutch bit auto-disengages when it penetrates the inner table — it detects the resistance drop and prevents plunging into brain tissue. This is what allows non-neurosurgeons to perform the procedure.

  1. Position perpendicular to the skull
  2. Apply firm, steady downward pressure
  3. Drill continuously until the spindle stops — do NOT stop and restart
  4. Irrigate with saline during drilling
  1. Perforator bit through the outer table only — stop at decreased resistance (diploe)
  2. Switch to a blunt/ball burr and gently enlarge through the inner table with irrigation
  3. Stop immediately when dura is visible
  4. Never continue with the perforator through the inner table

Absolute minimum: cutting instrument, any drill with a bit, scalp retraction, irrigation, cleanest available dressing. Improvised drill/retractor acceptable. EZ-IO 25mm needle technique described by Marc Grossman (EMCrit) — case reports only. Without a clutch drill, cortical injury risk is HIGH; proceed only if the patient will certainly die otherwise.

Pre-procedure preparation

  • Airway: intubate, sedate, paralyze; C-spine immobilization if trauma; target EtCO₂ 35–40, SpO₂ >94%
  • Osmotherapy: start mannitol or hypertonic saline now (see doses above)
  • Positioning: supine, head elevated, rotated 30–40° off midline (operative side up); mark midline and incision site BEFORE draping
  • Site prep: shave 5cm strip, chlorhexidine/betadine, local anesthetic with epinephrine
  • Antibiotics: cefazolin 2g IV or ceftriaxone 2g IV (see doses above)
  • Telemedicine: connect with neurosurgeon if available

Incision, exposure, drilling

3–4 cm vertical incision directly to bone. For the temporal approach: 2 FB above/anterior to the tragus, above the zygomatic arch (avoid the superficial temporal artery). Scalp bleeding is impressive but not life-threatening — apply pressure/clips/cautery and keep moving; focus on the brain, not the scalp.

Clear periosteum from bone (2–3 cm clear area), insert a self-retaining retractor, and verify the landmark against anatomy/CT before drilling. If a fracture is present, drill adjacent to — not through — it.

  1. Position the drill perpendicular to the skull
  2. Apply firm, steady downward pressure; assistant stabilizes the head
  3. Irrigate with saline throughout — prevents thermal injury, clears bone dust
  4. Drill continuously — do NOT stop and restart (clutch re-engagement is difficult)
  5. Feel for clutch engagement: resistance (outer table) → easier (diploe) → spindle stops (inner table)
  6. Remove the drill straight up — do not angle, can tear dura

Evacuation of hematoma

After completing the hole, assess what's visible: dark blood under pressure (EDH), bluish dura (SDH), normal gray pulsating dura (wrong location), or brain tissue (dural tear — stop).

  1. Allow spontaneous drainage under pressure — do not suction aggressively
  2. Gentle saline irrigation to liquefy and evacuate clot
  3. Gentle low suction — NEVER suction brain tissue; stop if dura bulges into the hole
  4. Blunt hook to break up and remove accessible clot only — do not explore beyond the visible area
Incomplete evacuation is expected

  2. Make a small cruciate (+) incision with a #15 blade — brain is immediately deep to dura
  3. Drain with gentle irrigation — acute SDH is often clotted and may not drain well
Do NOT

Possible explanations: wrong location, intraparenchymal (not extra-axial) hematoma, CT misinterpreted, or false localizing signs. Recommended: close and transport rather than explore with multiple random holes.

Consider only with strong clinical suspicion and a different anatomically sensible location — e.g. temporal→parietal, frontal→temporal, or opposite side only if clinical signs suggest it. Maximum 2 burr holes total — if both are negative, close and transport.

  3. Irrigate gently with saline; remove visible bone fragments
  4. Cover with saline-soaked gauze to keep brain moist
  5. Close scalp loosely over the defect and transport immediately

Hemostasis & extending to craniectomy

Bleeding sourceTreatment
Middle meningeal artery (arterial)Bipolar electrocautery; bone wax into foramen spinosum
Bone edge (diploe oozing)Bone wax, packed firmly into the edge
Venous/dural surface oozingGelfoam or Surgicel with gentle pressure — do not pack tightly against brain
Scalp bleedingElectrocautery to individual vessels, Raney clips; resolves with closure

Consider extending to craniectomy if drainage is inadequate, a large clot won't evacuate, arterial bleeding needs direct visualization, or brain is herniating through the defect. This significantly increases complexity and complication risk — only if truly necessary.

  1. Second burr hole 3–4 cm from the first, along the planned line
  2. Connect holes with a Leksell/Kerrison rongeur, protecting dura with a Penfield #4 — work slowly, do not tear dura
  3. Remove the bone bridge to create a 4–6 cm window
  4. Evacuate under direct vision with bipolar hemostasis
  5. Save the bone flap (may be replaced) or leave as decompressive craniectomy — neurosurgeon decides at definitive surgery

Wound closure

LOOSE dressing only — tight packing increases ICP and can cause secondary brain injury. If hemostasis achieved: irrigate, approximate scalp loosely (nylon/staples), non-adherent gauze, no pressure dressing. If ongoing hemorrhage: leave the retractor in place, cover with saline-moistened gauze, transport with the wound open. If brain is herniating through the defect, close the scalp immediately, consider additional osmotherapy, and transport urgently.

Overall major complication rate: approximately 2.5%.

Post-procedure care

This is a BRIDGE, not a cure — the patient still requires definitive neurosurgical craniotomy, ICU-level care, and ongoing ICP management.

Cefazolin (continuing): 2 g IV q8h IV — Meningitis prophylaxis, continued post-procedure.

Ceftriaxone (continuing, alternative): 1 g IV q24h IV — Alternative continuing meningitis prophylaxis.

Levetiracetam: 500 mg IV/PO BID IV/PO — Seizure prophylaxis for supratentorial injuries.

  • Head elevated 30°, midline
  • MAP >80–90 mmHg
  • SpO₂ >94%
  • Normocapnia (EtCO₂ 35–40)
  • Normothermia
  • Glucose 140–180 mg/dL
  • Serial neuro checks q15min: GCS, pupil size/reactivity, motor response
  • Watch for: declining GCS, new/worsening anisocoria, new seizure, Cushing's triad (late sign)
  • If deteriorating: brief hyperventilation (EtCO₂ 30–35, max 5–10 min) and/or additional osmotherapy
  • Notify receiving facility — may need OR on arrival
  • Pre-procedure: time of injury, GCS/pupil progression with times, CT findings, time neurosurgery unavailable, medical management given
  • Consent: emergency procedure under implied consent — life-threatening, no neurosurgical alternative, expected outcome without intervention is death
  • Procedure: time of incision, burr hole location(s), equipment used, findings (EDH/SDH/volume), complications, hemostasis
  • Post-procedure: neurologic response to decompression, current GCS/pupils, antibiotics given, transfer destination/ETA
