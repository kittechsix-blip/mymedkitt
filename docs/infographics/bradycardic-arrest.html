Bradycardic Arrest: Pace First, Then Fix the Cause

Adults in cardiac arrest from asystole or extreme bradycardia (<20 bpm) in the ED / resuscitation bay. Excludes pediatrics (use PALS bradycardia), pregnancy-specific arrest, and stable symptomatic bradycardia with a pulse (managed medically as a non-arrest bradycardia, not this arrest protocol).

⚠ Transcutaneous pacing FIRST for organized extreme bradycardia — do not wait for medications. Set the pacer rate 60-80 bpm and increase mA until electrical capture.
⚠ Asystole is non-shockable — do NOT defibrillate. Start high-quality CPR and give epinephrine ASAP; TCP rarely captures in true asystole.
⚠ Electrical capture is not enough — confirm mechanical capture with a palpable pulse or POCUS contractility before trusting the pacer.
⚠ Hyperkalemia: give calcium gluconate 3 g IV over 10 minutes for a wide-QRS bradycardic arrest — minimal downside even before labs return.
⚠ Hypothermia exception: defer pacing and active-rewarm to >30°C first — the myocardium is irritable below 30°C. Not dead until warm and dead.
⚠ Re-arrest: resume CPR immediately, re-check pacing capture, and reassess the reversible causes — something was missed or undertreated.

1 · Recognize the rhythm

What is bradycardic arrest?
Extreme bradycardia (<20 bpm) or asystole causing pulselessness. Two very different pathways hide inside one flat-looking monitor: asystole (CPR-first, non-shockable) vs extreme bradycardia with an organized wide-QRS escape (pace-first). Classify before you act.[1]
Confirm before you call asystole
Confirm the rhythm in 2 leads — a lead disconnect or low monitor gain mimics a flat line. True asystole = no electrical activity in any lead.[1]

2 · What to do right now

Set the toggles above to your patient. Exactly one first move shows — precedence is hypothermia → asystole → organized bradycardia. Modifiers (hyperkalemia, conscious sedation) stack on top.

Hypothermia first — do NOT pace yet
Core temp <30°C: the myocardium is irritable and pacing can trigger refractory VF. Active-rewarm to >30°C first — warm IV fluids, forced-air, lavage, or ECMO rewarming — then re-enter the pacing pathway. Not dead until warm and dead.[10]
Asystole → CPR + epinephrine, do NOT defibrillate
Asystole is a non-shockable rhythm. High-quality CPR immediately, give Epinephrine 1 mg IV/IO ASAP and repeat q3-5 min, and hunt aggressively for reversible causes. TCP rarely captures in true asystole — spikes without capture.[2]
Organized extreme bradycardia → pace NOW
Transcutaneous pacing immediately — don't wait for medications. Rate 60-80 bpm, start at 0 mA and increase until electrical capture (usually 50-100 mA). Run Epinephrine infusion 2-10 mcg/min as the bridge while you set up.[8]
Modifier: hyperkalemia suspected
Wide QRS + peaked T in a bradycardic arrest is hyperkalemia until proven otherwise. Give Calcium Gluconate 3 g IV over 10 minutes now — treat empirically, minimal downside even before labs return — then bicarb + insulin/dextrose.[2]
Modifier: conscious patient — sedate for TCP
Transcutaneous pacing is extremely painful. A conscious patient needs sedation — see the weight-based ketamine / fentanyl doses in section 3.

3 · Transcutaneous pacing & capture

  1. Set rate 60-80 bpm.
  2. Start at 0 mA and increase until electrical capture (usually 50-100 mA).
  3. Use AP pad position — anterior left parasternal, posterior left infrascapular.
  4. Run Epinephrine infusion 2-10 mcg/min as the bridge while pacing is set up.
Confirm capture — both kinds
Electrical capture: a pacer spike followed by a wide QRS — each spike makes a QRS, not just artifact. Mechanical capture: a palpable pulse AND/OR contractility on POCUS. Electrical capture alone is not capture.[9]
  • Increase mA up to maximum output.
  • Reposition pads — try AP if you started anterolateral.
  • Ensure skin contact — shave chest hair, dry the skin.
  • If still no electrical capture at max output: move to medications while arranging transvenous pacing.

Conscious patient — sedate first (TCP is extremely painful). Enter weight above; dissociative-dose Ketamine is first-line, with Fentanyl as an analgesic adjunct.

5 · Reversible causes (find & fix)

Pace the rhythm, but the patient does not survive unless you fix the cause. Reassess after every intervention — narrowing QRS, rising rate, and return of pulse are your response markers.

7 · Disposition, ROSC & TOR

Post-Cardiac Arrest Care: 12-lead ECG to find the cause (STEMI, hyperK pattern, tox), continue pacing — do not pull TCP until TVP or a stable intrinsic rhythm, targeted temperature management per protocol, serial K / lactate / troponin / gas, and cardiology for permanent-pacer evaluation.[2]

Termination of resuscitation (TOR)

Consider TOR when ETCO2 <10 mmHg after 20 min of high-quality CPR, no reversible cause is identified, cardiac standstill on POCUS, and no response to pacing/meds/cause-directed therapy. EXCEPTION — hypothermia: rewarm to >30°C before declaring futility. See TOR Rules.[3]

Critical pitfalls — do NOT

Do NOT defibrillate asystole — it is non-shockable. CPR + epinephrine.

Do NOT trust electrical capture alone — confirm a pulse or POCUS contractility.

Do NOT delay pacing for atropine — atropine is an adjunct and fails in infranodal block.

Do NOT pace a <30°C heart — rewarm first; it is irritable and can go to refractory VF.
