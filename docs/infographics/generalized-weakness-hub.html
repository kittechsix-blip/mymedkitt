Generalized Weakness: Sick Check First, Then Localize

Adults presenting to the ED with generalized/undifferentiated weakness needing initial localization and disposition. Hands off to disease-specific myMedKitt consults (sepsis, stroke/ICH, GBS/myasthenia, electrolyte, DKA/adrenal/thyroid, chest pain/ACS, toxicology) once a lane is identified — this hub does not replace those pathways.

⚠ 5 DO NOT MISS on every weakness patient: neuromuscular respiratory failure (GBS, myasthenic crisis, botulism) — single-breath count, NIF/FVC; hyperkalemia/severe electrolyte derangement — ECG now; ACS/occult sepsis as an atypical presentation in elderly/diabetic patients; stroke/cord compression — localize asymmetry, sensory level, bowel/bladder; adrenal crisis/profound hypoglycemia/tox (beta-blocker, CCB, digoxin) — glucose, cortisol context, medication review.
⚠ Generalized weakness is a trap complaint. True weakness needs localization — CNS, cord/root, NMJ, muscle, metabolic, or shock — before it is labeled benign.
⚠ Shock: older adults may present with isolated weakness and no fever. Hypotension, tachypnea, confusion, or rigors should move weakness into a resuscitation lane — treat physiology first.
⚠ Neurovascular: generalized weakness can hide a focal deficit when the patient is tired, aphasic, neglectful, or unable to walk safely. If the exam localizes, activate the stroke/bleed workflow.
⚠ Neuromuscular: normal SpO2 does not clear neuromuscular respiratory failure. Trend ventilation and bulbar safety. Two cheap moves that prevent embarrassing misses: actually look for a tick, and check the potassium.
⚠ Electrolyte: ECG-dangerous electrolyte disorders are treated before complete diagnostic certainty — do not wait for the full lab panel when ECG shows dangerous potassium physiology.
⚠ Endocrine: fatigue plus hypotension/hypoglycemia/hyponatremia after steroid exposure or adrenal disease is adrenal crisis until proven otherwise — correct the life-threatening physiology while the endocrine workup runs.
⚠ Cardiac/anemia/bleeding: MI, dysrhythmia, GI bleed, and severe anemia often present as weakness in older adults, diabetics, and patients with limited symptom reporting.
⚠ Toxicologic: urine drug screen rarely explains weakness — ECG, glucose, acid-base, and medication list change care faster.
⚠ Disposition: persistent true weakness is not a discharge diagnosis until dangerous localization is excluded. Do not discharge inability to walk, bulbar symptoms, respiratory weakness, or persistent unexplained weakness.

1 · Sick check first

First 60 seconds: is this true motor weakness, fatigue, dizziness, pain-limited movement, or altered mental status? Assess airway/breathing (voice strength, single-breath count if neuromuscular concern, work of breathing, aspiration risk), vitals trend (fever, hypotension, tachy/brady, hypoxia, hypo/hyperthermia), fingerstick glucose now, ECG early (hyperkalemia/hypokalemia, ischemia, dysrhythmia, QRS/QTc toxicity), quick neuro localization (face/arm/leg asymmetry, speech, gaze, pupils, reflexes, gait if safe), and a medication/toxin scan (sedatives, beta-blocker/CCB, insulin/sulfonylurea, statin, diuretic, lithium, alcohol/withdrawal).[1][2][3]

  1. Neuromuscular respiratory failure (GBS, myasthenic crisis, botulism) — single-breath count, NIF/FVC.
  2. Hyperkalemia / severe electrolyte derangement — ECG now.
  3. Acute coronary syndrome / occult sepsis — weakness as an atypical presentation in elderly/diabetic patients.
  4. Stroke / cord compression — localize: asymmetry, sensory level, bowel/bladder.
  5. Adrenal crisis / profound hypoglycemia / tox (beta-blocker, CCB, digoxin) — glucose, cortisol context, medication review.
Generalized weakness is a trap complaint
Stabilize physiology and localize before labeling it benign. Glucose, ECG, potassium, and vital-sign trajectory come before broad weakness labs.[1]

3 · Shock / sepsis / volume problem

Treat physiology first
Open Sepsis Management when infection physiology is plausible. Next 5 minutes: monitor, IV/IO, glucose, ECG, lactate if ill, cultures if they do not delay antibiotics, crystalloid if hypoperfused, pressor early if persistent hypotension, POCUS heart/lungs/IVC when physiology is unclear. Weakness with shock is not a benign chief complaint.

4 · Neurovascular weakness

If the exam localizes, activate the stroke/bleed workflow
Open Acute Ischemic Stroke, ICH, or Altered Mental Status Hub as appropriate. Next 5 minutes: glucose corrected/confirmed, last-known-well, NIHSS-style focused exam, CT/CTA/perfusion per stroke workflow, BP strategy by diagnosis, anticoagulant/reversal history.

5 · GBS / myasthenia / neuromuscular respiratory risk

Normal SpO2 does not clear neuromuscular respiratory failure.
Open Guillain-Barre Syndrome or Myasthenia Gravis. Next 5 minutes: assess bulbar symptoms, cough, secretion handling, neck flexion, single breath count or bedside respiratory mechanics if available, cardiac monitoring for dysautonomia, avoid premature discharge even if pulse ox is normal. Danger signs: rapidly progressive weakness, inability to walk, facial/bulbar weakness, autonomic instability, weak cough, rising CO2, or fatigue with repeated exam. Trend ventilation and bulbar safety.

6 · Electrolyte paralysis / weakness

ECG-dangerous electrolyte disorders are treated before complete diagnostic certainty.
Open Sodium Disorders, Calcium Disorders, Phosphorous Disorders, or Magnesium Disorders. Next 5 minutes: ECG first, BMP/Mg/Phos/Ca, renal function, CK if rhabdo concern. Treat unstable ECG hyperkalemia immediately with calcium. Severe symptomatic hyponatremia with seizure/coma follows hypertonic saline pathway. Severe hypoK/hypoPhos/hypoMg can present as profound weakness or respiratory failure. Do not wait for the full lab panel when ECG shows dangerous potassium physiology.

7 · Endocrine / metabolic weakness

Adrenal crisis until proven otherwise
Open DKA, Adrenal Insufficiency, Thyroid Disorders, or Heat Stroke when the phenotype fits. Next 5 minutes: VBG/chemistry/ketones for DKA/HHS, potassium before insulin, temperature, TSH/free T4 if thyroid storm/myxedema clues, empiric hydrocortisone if adrenal crisis is plausible with shock/hypoglycemia/hyponatremia. Fatigue plus hypotension/hypoglycemia/hyponatremia after steroid exposure or adrenal disease is adrenal crisis until proven otherwise. Correct the life-threatening physiology while the endocrine workup runs.

8 · Cardiac / dysrhythmia / anemia / bleeding

Weakness can be the only symptom
Open Chest Pain Hub, STEMI, NSTEMI, or A-Fib RVR when symptoms or ECG point cardiac. Next 5 minutes: ECG, troponin if ischemia possible, orthostatics if safe, CBC, rectal/bleeding history when anemia/GI bleed plausible, POCUS when shock or dyspnea coexists. MI, dysrhythmia, GI bleed, and severe anemia often present as weakness in older adults, diabetics, and patients with limited symptom reporting.

9 · Toxicologic / medication weakness

ECG and acid-base beat a urine drug screen
Open TCA Toxidrome, Calcium Channel Blocker Overdose, Salicylate Toxicity, Toxic Alcohols, or CO Toxicity when clues fit. Next 5 minutes: ECG for QRS/QTc/bradycardia, glucose, VBG/anion gap/osm gap, acetaminophen/salicylate levels when ingestion possible, co-oximetry if exposure risk, medication reconciliation, poison center early. Urine drug screen rarely explains weakness — ECG, glucose, acid-base, and medication list change care faster.

10 · Rescue bundle, reassess, imaging

Default bundle when no killer has declared itself.

  • Glucose, ECG, monitor if ill.
  • CBC, CMP, Mg, Phos, Ca, CK when myopathy/rhabdo possible.
  • UA/CXR/cultures only when infection clues exist; do not shotgun without exam signal.
  • Orthostatic vitals and ambulation test when safe.
  • Medication reconciliation, recent illness, tick/travel, exertion, dialysis, insulin/sulfonylurea, diuretics.
  • Focused neuro exam repeated after fluids, glucose correction, analgesia, or rest.
A repeat exam is the key test in vague weakness
Reassess in 30-60 minutes: ability to stand/walk, symmetric strength, reflexes, bulbar/respiratory symptoms, vitals, ECG/lab changes. Worsening or inability to walk is not benign weakness.

Image by localization and physiology. Do not image vague weakness reflexively. Image the diagnosis you are willing to act on.

13 · Do NOT

Critical pitfalls

Do NOT skip glucose, ECG, and potassium before broad weakness labs — these come first on every generalized-weakness patient.

Do NOT let a normal SpO2 reassure you in suspected GBS/myasthenia/botulism — trend ventilation (NIF/FVC, single-breath count) and bulbar safety instead.

Do NOT wait for the full lab panel when ECG shows dangerous potassium physiology — treat unstable hyperkalemia immediately.

Do NOT dismiss weakness in an afebrile older adult — isolated weakness with hypotension, tachypnea, confusion, or rigors is shock/sepsis until excluded.

Do NOT rely on urine drug screen to explain weakness — ECG, glucose, acid-base, and medication reconciliation change management faster.

Do NOT miss adrenal crisis — fatigue plus hypotension/hypoglycemia/hyponatremia after steroid exposure or known adrenal disease is adrenal crisis until proven otherwise.

Do NOT image vague weakness reflexively — image the diagnosis you are willing to act on.

Do NOT discharge inability to walk, bulbar symptoms, respiratory weakness, or persistent unexplained weakness — these require localization before disposition.
