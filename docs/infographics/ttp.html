TTP: Recognize, Score, Exchange

Adults ≥18 with suspected acquired/immune TTP (MAHA + thrombocytopenia) in the ED, inpatient, or ICU setting, including pregnancy-associated presentations. Excludes pediatric TTP and congenital (Upshaw-Schulman) TTP, which need ADAMTS13-replacement dosing outside this scope. DIC and HUS are differentiated as off-ramps — their independent management is not detailed further here.

⚠ Do NOT transfuse platelets — "adding fuel to the fire." Contraindicated except for CNS or life-threatening hemorrhage; may precipitate sudden death, MI, or stroke.
⚠ PLASMIC 6-7 → initiate TPE within 4-8 hours. Do NOT wait for the ADAMTS13 result to start plasma exchange when clinical suspicion is high.
⚠ Draw ADAMTS13 BEFORE any plasma exposure (FFP or TPE). Plasma contains ADAMTS13 and falsely elevates the level.
⚠ Normal PT / PTT / fibrinogen distinguishes TTP from DIC — abnormal coags mean DIC, which is NOT treated with plasma exchange.
⚠ FFP 30 mL/kg is a bridge only if TPE is delayed >4-6 hours — it is never a substitute for plasma exchange.
⚠ HELLP resolves within 48-72 hours of delivery; TTP continues to deteriorate postpartum without TPE — if not improving by 72h, treat as TTP.

1 · Recognize the emergency

MAHA + thrombocytopenia = suspect TTP
Untreated mortality is >90%; with prompt plasma exchange it falls to <10%.[3] TTP is a clinical diagnosis — treat on suspicion, do not wait for confirmatory testing.

Microangiopathic hemolytic anemia (MAHA): schistocytes on smear, elevated LDH, undetectable haptoglobin, elevated indirect bilirubin, negative direct Coombs.[1][2] Thrombocytopenia is usually severe (<30 ×10⁹/L). Coagulation studies (PT/PTT/fibrinogen) are normal — that is what separates TTP from DIC.

Not MAHA — consider alternatives
Without confirmed MAHA + thrombocytopenia, TTP is unlikely. Thrombocytopenia without hemolysis → ITP, HIT, drug-induced, bone marrow failure. Hemolysis without schistocytes → AIHA, PNH, G6PD deficiency.
ADAMTS13
ADAMTS13 activity <10% is diagnostic of TTP.[2][4] Draw it before any plasma (FFP or TPE) — plasma contains ADAMTS13 and falsely elevates the result. The send-out result takes 2-7 days; do NOT wait for it to treat.

PLASMIC 0-4 has 98% negative predictive value for TTP. Consider HUS, DIC, drug-induced TMA (quinine, ticlopidine, cyclosporine, gemcitabine), HELLP, or malignant hypertension instead.

Two fast discriminators
Normal PT/PTT/fibrinogen rules out DIC (DIC is not treated with plasma exchange).[6] Cr <2 mg/dL favors TTP over HUS.[9]
DIC — different pathway, no TPE
DIC is NOT treated with plasma exchange. Treat the underlying cause (sepsis, malignancy, OB emergency) and transfuse only for bleeding: FFP if PT >1.5× normal and bleeding, platelets if <50k and bleeding (or <20k prophylactic), cryoprecipitate if fibrinogen <100 mg/dL.[6]
HUS — different pathway, no TPE
STEC-HUS: bloody diarrhea → severe AKI; antibiotics may worsen it (increased toxin release); supportive care ± dialysis, usually self-limited. Atypical HUS: complement-mediated, recurrent — treated with eculizumab. ADAMTS13 is normal (>10%) in HUS.[9]
Pregnancy: TTP vs HELLP
HELLP resolves within 48-72 hours of delivery; TTP continues to deteriorate postpartum without TPE. HELLP ADAMTS13 is low but >10% (average 31%); TTP is <10%.[8] Fetal loss >40% if maternal TTP is untreated, improved with prompt treatment.[16] Recombinant ADAMTS13 (Adzynma) is FDA-approved for CONGENITAL TTP only — NOT for acquired/immune TTP, which remains TPE + steroids + caplacizumab.[17]
