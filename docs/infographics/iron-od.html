Iron Overdose: Recognize, Chelate, Dispose

Any age with an acute iron-containing product ingestion (prenatal vitamins, children's chewables, ferrous sulfate/gluconate/fumarate) in the ED. Excludes chronic transfusion-related iron overload (different chelation regimen, not modeled here).

⚠ Elemental iron >60 mg/kg is potentially lethal — toxicity depends on ELEMENTAL iron content (ferrous sulfate 20%, gluconate 12%, fumarate 33%), not total pill weight; ingestions >200-250 mg/kg can be fatal.
⚠ Stage 2 (6-24 hours) is a deceptive 'latent phase' — apparent clinical improvement while cellular toxicity progresses. Resolution of GI symptoms does NOT mean resolution of toxicity; do not discharge or downgrade care based on symptom improvement alone.
⚠ Severe ingestion (>60 mg/kg elemental iron) needs large-bore IV access x2, aggressive fluid resuscitation, type & screen, and early deferoxamine anticipation — these patients need ICU admission.
⚠ Serum iron >500 mcg/dL = severe toxicity — start deferoxamine and anticipate ICU admission.
⚠ Deferoxamine is indicated at serum iron >500 mcg/dL OR severe symptoms (shock, altered mental status, metabolic acidosis, significant GI bleeding) regardless of level — treat the patient, not just the number.
⚠ Activated charcoal does NOT adsorb iron and is NOT effective GI decontamination — use whole bowel irrigation instead.
⚠ Do NOT use 'vin rosé' urine color to guide or stop deferoxamine therapy — it is unreliable.
⚠ TIBC is falsely elevated in acute iron overdose — do NOT use TIBC to guide management.

1 · Recognition & elemental iron

Iron is a common pediatric poisoning — prenatal vitamins and children's chewables look like candy. Toxicity depends on elemental iron content, not total pill weight. Free iron causes direct GI mucosal injury and systemic toxicity via free-radical formation, lipid peroxidation, and mitochondrial dysfunction leading to metabolic acidosis and cardiovascular collapse.

2 · What to do now

<20 mg/kg — observation only
No GI decontamination and no labs required if asymptomatic. Observe 6 hours from ingestion; discharge if remains asymptomatic.
20-60 mg/kg — evaluate and monitor
Draw serum iron at 4-6 hours, abdominal X-ray, CBC/BMP/LFTs/coags/lactate. Watch closely for Stage 1 GI symptoms.
>60 mg/kg — aggressive treatment
Large-bore IV x2, aggressive fluid resuscitation, type & screen, early deferoxamine anticipation. Call Poison Control (1-800-222-1222). ICU admission expected.
Unknown amount — treat as significant
Draw serum iron at 4-6 hours, abdominal X-ray, full labs. When in doubt, assume significant ingestion and manage aggressively.
Start deferoxamine now
Level >500 mcg/dL OR severe symptoms (shock, AMS, acidosis, significant GI bleeding). Start at 5 mg/kg/hr, titrate to 15 mg/kg/hr.
Observe without deferoxamine
Level <500 mcg/dL AND asymptomatic/mild symptoms. IV fluids, antiemetics, serial labs q4-6h, admit for observation — the latent phase can still progress.
Whole bowel irrigation indicated
Pills/fragments visible on X-ray, sustained-release formulation, or large ingestion. Activated charcoal does NOT work — use GoLYTELY via NG tube.

4 · Diagnostic workup & classic stages

Serum iron level is the key diagnostic test. Draw at 4-6 hours post-ingestion (peak absorption) — earlier levels may underestimate toxicity. TIBC is NOT useful in acute poisoning — it is falsely elevated; do not use TIBC to guide management.

Serum iron (4-6h)Interpretation
<350 mcg/dLUsually minimal symptoms
350-500 mcg/dLModerate toxicity
>500 mcg/dLSevere toxicity — deferoxamine indicated
Stage & timingFindings
Stage 1 (0-6h) — GI phaseNausea, vomiting, diarrhea, abdominal pain, GI hemorrhage
Stage 2 (6-24h) — Latent phaseApparent improvement — 'quiescent period', DO NOT BE FOOLED
Stage 3 (12-48h) — Shock phaseCardiovascular collapse, profound metabolic acidosis, coagulopathy
Stage 4 (2-4 days) — HepatotoxicityHepatic necrosis, hypoglycemia, worsening coagulopathy
Stage 5 (2-6 weeks) — GI scarringGastric outlet obstruction, pyloric stenosis, intestinal strictures

5 · GI decontamination

Activated charcoal does NOT adsorb iron — it is NOT effective. Whole bowel irrigation (WBI) with GoLYTELY is the mainstay for radiopaque pills or large ingestions.

GoLYTELY (PEG-electrolyte solution)
Pediatric: 500 mL/hr (25 mL/kg/hr, max 500 mL/hr); Adult: 1-2 L/hr
NG tube
Continue until clear rectal effluent AND no pills on repeat abdominal X-ray. Contraindicated with unprotected airway, bowel obstruction, ileus, or hemodynamic instability.
—Pediatric WBI rate (25 mL/kg/hr, capped at 500 mL/hr)
Endoscopic removal
For large pill masses, bezoars, or failed WBI. Iron tablets can coalesce into hard concretions. Consult GI and/or surgery early.

6 · Deferoxamine therapy

Deferoxamine chelates free iron, forming water-soluble ferrioxamine excreted renally. Enter weight above to size the infusion.

—Starting infusion rate (5 mg/kg/hr, minimizes hypotension)
—Titrated target rate (15 mg/kg/hr, max 6 g/24 hr)
Deferoxamine
Start 5 mg/kg/hr, titrate to 15 mg/kg/hr
IV continuous infusion
Maximum dose: 6 grams per 24 hours in adults. Increase by 5 mg/kg/hr every 30-60 min as BP tolerates.
Deferoxamine (no IV access)
90 mg/kg IM (max 1 g per injection site)
IM, may repeat q4-6h
Only temporary until IV access obtained — less reliable absorption. Convert to IV as soon as possible.
Duration & monitoring
Continue until clinically improved AND acidosis resolved AND iron normalized AND GI symptoms resolved — typical duration 24-48 hours. Do NOT use 'vin rosé' urine color to stop therapy (unreliable). Watch for hypotension (most common), ARDS with infusion >24h, and Yersinia infection (deferoxamine is a bacterial growth factor).

7 · Rescue therapies

For severe, refractory iron poisoning despite deferoxamine and supportive care.

Exchange transfusion
Pediatric massive ingestion or refractory shock not responding to deferoxamine + fluids + pressors. Requires large-bore central access; consult hematology/transfusion medicine.
Hemodialysis
Removes ferrioxamine complex (NOT free iron). Indicated for renal failure, ARDS from prolonged deferoxamine, or severe refractory acidosis. Not a substitute for deferoxamine — continue chelation during dialysis.
Supportive care
Aggressive crystalloid for GI losses, blood products for coagulopathy/GI bleeding, norepinephrine first-line for refractory shock, D10W for hypoglycemia (hepatic failure), bicarbonate for pH <7.1.

8 · Disposition

Observe & discharge (6 hours)
Elemental iron <20 mg/kg OR serum iron <350 mcg/dL, asymptomatic, no pills on X-ray. Discharge after 6 hours if remains asymptomatic with normal mental status. Return precautions: vomiting, bloody stool, abdominal pain, lethargy. Poison Control: 1-800-222-1222.
Admit for monitoring
Any symptomatic patient, serum iron 350-500 mcg/dL, elemental iron 20-60 mg/kg, ongoing GI decontamination, or unreliable history with concerning features. Serial iron levels q4-6h; escalate to ICU if deferoxamine required, acidosis develops, or hemodynamic instability occurs.
ICU admission
Deferoxamine therapy required, serum iron >500 mcg/dL, or elemental iron >60 mg/kg with acidosis/instability. Continuous deferoxamine, aggressive resuscitation, serial labs q4h. Consult Poison Control, toxicology, and nephrology (if considering dialysis).
Other pitfalls4 items

Do NOT wait for symptoms to worsen before drawing a serum iron level — draw at 4-6 hours post-ingestion regardless of symptom status.

Other pitfalls

**Do NOT rely on a negative abdominal X-ray to exclude ingestion** — liquid, chewable, or dissolved tablets may not be radiopaque.

**Do NOT wait for symptoms to worsen before drawing a serum iron level** — draw at 4-6 hours post-ingestion regardless of symptom status.

**Do NOT discharge based on TIBC** — it is falsely elevated in acute iron overdose and is not a reliable marker.

**Do NOT stop deferoxamine based on urine color alone** — absence of 'vin rosé' urine does not mean chelation isn't working.
