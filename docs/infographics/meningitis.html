Meningitis & Encephalitis: Antibiotics Within 1 Hour

Adults and adolescents in the ED with suspected acute bacterial meningitis or encephalitis. Excludes neonates (age-based dosing differs) and confirmed non-infectious causes. Weight-based doses assume normal renal/hepatic function — adjust per pharmacy.

⚠ Untreated bacterial meningitis is approximately 100% fatal — give empiric antibiotics + dexamethasone within 1 HOUR of suspicion. Never delay for LP or CT.
⚠ Give dexamethasone WITH or up to 15–20 min BEFORE the first antibiotic dose.
⚠ STOP dexamethasone immediately if Listeria or Cryptococcus is identified — corticosteroids worsen outcomes for both.
⚠ CT before LP only if: GCS ≤12, papilledema, new focal deficit, uncontrolled seizure, or severe immunocompromise with new seizures — otherwise proceed directly to LP.
⚠ Add Ampicillin 2 g IV q4h for age ≥50, pregnant, or immunocompromised patients — cephalosporins do not cover Listeria monocytogenes.
⚠ For ANY encephalitis feature (focal deficit, seizure, personality change), add Acyclovir 10 mg/kg IV q8h — untreated HSV encephalitis mortality is ~70%.

Equivocal? Treat as bacterial
When CSF results are equivocal, treat as bacterial: 6% of culture-proven ABM cases have no elevated WBC, 10% have lymphocyte-predominant differentials, and 25% of Listeria cases show normal or near-normal CSF.[1][14]

3 · Empiric antibiotics + dexamethasone (within 1 hour)

Time to empiric antibiotics + dexamethasone: 1 hour

Give empiric antibiotics within **1 hour** of suspicion.{{ref1}} Delayed antibiotics correlate with disease progression and increased mortality.{{ref19}}{{ref20}}

Give dexamethasone WITH or up to **15–20 min** BEFORE the first antibiotic dose.{{ref1}}{{ref21}}

Category | Core regimen | Key addition

Immunocompetent <50y | Ceftriaxone 2 g IV q12h + Vancomycin + Dexamethasone | —

≥50y / pregnant / immunocompromised | Ceftriaxone + Vancomycin + Dexamethasone | + Ampicillin 2 g IV q4h (Listeria)

Post-neurosurgical / healthcare-associated | Vancomycin + Meropenem 2 g IV q8h (or Cefepime) | No proven dexamethasone benefit

HIV/AIDS subacute (suspected crypto) | Amphotericin B + Flucytosine → Fluconazole | ALSO empiric Ceftriaxone + Vancomycin + Ampicillin pending CSF; NO corticosteroids

Ceftriaxone: 2 g IV q12h — Higher-dose regimen for consistent CNS penetration. In the suspected-cryptococcal pathway, given empirically alongside antifungals until CSF results exclude bacterial meningitis.

Vancomycin (meningitic dosing): 1050 IV q8–12h — Target trough 15–20 mcg/mL; adjust for renal function.

Ampicillin (Listeria coverage): 2 g IV q4h — Age ≥50, pregnant, or immunocompromised — cephalosporins do not cover Listeria monocytogenes. Listeria meningitis mortality 17–30%. Also given empirically in the suspected-cryptococcal pathway pending CSF results.

Meropenem (or Cefepime alternative): 2 g IV q8h — Broad gram-negative + Pseudomonas coverage for post-neurosurgical, shunt/drain, CSF leak, or basilar-skull-fracture-associated meningitis. Combine with vancomycin for MRSA.

Amphotericin B liposomal (cryptococcal induction): 280 IV daily × 1 week — Calculator uses the 4 mg/kg/day upper bound of the 3–4 mg/kg/day induction range. Combine with flucytosine; ID consultation required.

Flucytosine (cryptococcal induction): 1750 PO q6h × 1 week — 25 mg/kg PO q6h. Always combine with amphotericin B. Follow with fluconazole 400–800 mg/day PO for consolidation.

Dexamethasone (adjunctive): 10.5 IV q6h × 2–4 days — Give WITH or up to 15–20 min BEFORE the first antibiotic dose. Not part of the empiric regimen for healthcare-associated/post-neurosurgical meningitis — the consult's nosocomial regimen omits dexamethasone (evidence is for community-acquired, largely pneumococcal disease).

Corticosteroids are associated with **worsened outcomes** in cryptococcal meningitis{{ref1}} and a nonsignificant trend toward worse outcomes in Listeria meningitis{{ref21}} — discontinue immediately if either is identified.

7 · Do NOT

Critical pitfalls

**Do NOT delay antibiotics for CT or LP** — give empiric antibiotics + dexamethasone within 1 hour of suspicion.

**Do NOT give the first antibiotic dose without dexamethasone** in suspected bacterial meningitis — it must be WITH or up to 15–20 min BEFORE.

**Do NOT continue corticosteroids if Listeria or Cryptococcus is identified** — associated with worsened outcomes.

**Do NOT skip Ampicillin** in patients ≥50, pregnant, or immunocompromised — cephalosporins do not cover Listeria.

**Do NOT withhold Acyclovir** for any encephalitis feature pending PCR — untreated HSV mortality is 70%.

**Do NOT rely on Kernig/Brudzinski signs** to rule out meningitis — sensitivity is only 2%.
