Meningitis & Encephalitis: Antibiotics Within 1 Hour

Adults and adolescents in the ED with suspected acute meningitis or encephalitis. Excludes neonates (age-based dosing differs) and confirmed non-infectious causes. Weight-based doses assume normal renal/hepatic function — adjust per pharmacy.

⚠ Untreated = 100% mortality — abx within 1 HOUR
⚠ Antibiotics + Dexamethasone within 1 HOUR of suspicion - do NOT delay for LP or CT
⚠ Abx + dexa BEFORE CT — never delay for diagnostics
⚠ Delayed abx = mortality — dexa WITH or BEFORE 1st dose
⚠ Acyclovir 10mg/kg q8h — HSV 70% vs 9% treated
⚠ STOP dexamethasone immediately if Listeria or Cryptococcus identified — steroids worsen these outcomes

1 · Recognize — the 1-hour clock starts at suspicion

Why the clock matters
Untreated bacterial meningitis approaches 100% mortality; even treated, mortality is 14–25%.[1][5] Herpes simplex encephalitis killed 70% untreated → ~9% with acyclovir.[7][15] Every hour of antibiotic delay is associated with higher mortality.[19][20]

Screen for 4 features: headache, fever, neck stiffness, altered mental status. The classic triad appears in only 44–66% of cases, but at least 1 of the 4 is present in 99–100%.[3][5]

The rule-out (use it deliberately)
Absence of ALL 4 (headache, fever, neck stiffness, AMS) effectively rules out meningitis/encephalitis (~99% NPV).[9] Kernig/Brudzinski are only 2% sensitive — a negative exam never rules it in or out.[13]

2 · What to do NOW — set the patient, get the exact move

Toggle the patient's state above. Exactly one instruction below lights up — focal/encephalitis overrides everything, then HIV-subacute, then Listeria risk, then standard.

NOW: Encephalitis pathway
Start Acyclovir 10 mg/kg IV q8h immediately AND full empiric bacterial coverage. Focal deficits mean parenchymal involvement — get CT before LP, but give antibiotics + dexamethasone BEFORE CT. HSV mortality 70% → 9% with acyclovir.[7][8]
NOW: Suspected cryptococcal (HIV, subacute)
Send serum cryptococcal antigen (99.7% sens)[17] and measure opening pressure. After ID consult, induction is Amphotericin B 4 mg/kg/day IV + Flucytosine 25 mg/kg PO q6h.[22] Still start empiric bacterial coverage until crypto is confirmed.
NOW: Add Listeria coverage
Age ≥50, pregnant, or immunocompromised → add Ampicillin 2 g IV q4h to Ceftriaxone 2 g q12h + Vancomycin 20 mg/kg + Dexamethasone 0.15 mg/kg. L. monocytogenes mortality 17–30%.[1][24]
NOW: Standard immunocompetent adult
Ceftriaxone 2 g IV q12h + Vancomycin 20 mg/kg IV + Dexamethasone 0.15 mg/kg IV q6h, all within 1 hour. Covers S. pneumoniae and N. meningitidis.[1][24]

4 · Workup — CT only when it changes the LP

CT before LP — only if
GCS ≤12, papilledema, focal deficit, new seizure, or severe immunocompromise.[12][18] Otherwise LP directly. Post-LP herniation is rare (0.1% deteriorated within 1 h).[18] Antibiotics never wait for imaging.
  1. Draw blood cultures before antibiotics (66% yield in bacterial meningitis).
  2. Give antibiotics + dexamethasone within 1 hour — do not wait for the LP.
  3. Collect 4 tubes (up to 15 mL): (1) cell count, (2) protein/glucose/lactate/PCR, (3) Gram stain/culture/antigen, (4) cell count to compare for traumatic tap.
  4. Send serum procalcitonin — >0.25–0.5 ng/mL is 90–95% sensitive, 98–100% specific for bacterial.
