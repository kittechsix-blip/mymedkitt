Major Joint Dislocation: Vessel First, Reduce Fast, Recheck After

Adults with a suspected or confirmed hip, knee, or ankle dislocation in the ED. Excludes pediatric physeal/epiphyseal injuries, chronic/recurrent dislocation, and prosthetic joint dislocation — each needs a modified pathway.

⚠ Knee dislocation = popliteal artery injury until proven otherwise. Popliteal artery injury occurs in 20-40% of knee dislocations. Reduce immediately — do not wait for OR. Ischemia time is critical: a 6-8 hour window for limb salvage. Hard signs (absent pulse, expanding hematoma) go straight to OR; soft signs (ABI 0.8-0.9) get CTA then decide; a normal post-reduction exam still needs serial ABI ×24-48h.[2][6]
⚠ Ankle skin tenting is an emergency. Reduce immediately without waiting for X-ray or full sedation setup — delay risks skin necrosis, conversion to an open injury, and compartment syndrome.[1][8]
⚠ Open ankle fracture-dislocation: give IV antibiotics as soon as possible — ideally within 1 hr of injury, never delay past 3 hr. Cefazolin 2 g IV (3 g if weight ≥120 kg) for all grades; clindamycin 900 mg IV for PCN allergy; add piperacillin-tazobactam 4.5 g IV for Gustilo III or heavy contamination (preferred over aminoglycosides).[9]
⚠ Any absent or diminished pulse after reduction, at any joint, is a vascular emergency. STAT vascular surgery consult. Do not delay definitive care for imaging if the limb is clearly ischemic — ischemia time >6 hours carries a high amputation risk.[2][3]
⚠ Posterior hip dislocation is time-critical. Reduce within 6 hours to minimize avascular necrosis risk (10% overall, higher if delayed).[4][5]

2 · Algorithm

Which joint is dislocated? — ABCs + trauma survey first. Document pulses, sensation, motor, and skin BEFORE any reduction attempt.

Hip dislocation — which type? — Posterior 90% (flexed, adducted, internally rotated). Anterior 10% (extended, abducted, externally rotated). Sciatic nerve injury in 10-20% with posterior.

Obtain AP pelvis + lateral hip X-ray — Confirms direction and identifies acetabular/femoral head fracture. Do NOT delay reduction for CT if neurovascularly compromised.

Reduce within 6 hours — Allis or Stimson maneuver — AVN risk 10% overall, higher if delayed >6h. Post-reduction: pulse check, AP pelvis X-ray, CT scan, knee exam.

Reduce — inline traction + external rotation/adduction — Reverse Allis technique. Watch for femoral artery/vein/nerve injury. Post-reduction: femoral nerve check, AP pelvis X-ray, CT, monitor for compartment syndrome.

Knee dislocation — vascular status? — Popliteal artery injury in 20-40% of knee dislocations. Check popliteal pulse, ABI, and peroneal nerve immediately.

Reduce NOW — popliteal artery emergency — Ischemia time critical — 6-8 hour window. Hard signs (absent pulse, expanding hematoma) → OR. Soft signs (ABI 0.8-0.9) → CTA then decide. Vascular surgery STAT.

Reduce with inline traction (technique by direction) — Post-reduction: pulse check, ABI, posterior splint at 20° flexion, serial ABI q4h ×24-48h. CTA for all knee dislocations.

Spontaneously reduced — treat as knee dislocation — Gross multi-planar instability + hyperextension/dashboard history = high suspicion. Serial ABI q4h ×24-48h, CTA recommended, admit for observation.

Ankle dislocation — skin/wound status? — Usually a fracture-dislocation. Any skin break near the joint = open until proven otherwise.

Reduce immediately — skin tenting — Do NOT wait for X-ray or full sedation setup. Local hematoma block + reduce if sedation not immediately available.

Open fracture-dislocation protocol — Photograph, remove gross contamination, sterile dressing, reduce, splint. Antibiotics ASAP — within 1 hr ideally, never >3 hr. OR for I&D within 6-24h.

Reduce with inline traction (direction-specific) — Post-reduction: pulse check, posterior splint at 90°, elevate above heart, X-ray, CT if articular concern.

Post-reduction status? — Checklist: pulse, sensation, motor, skin, splint, X-ray — compare to the pre-reduction exam.

Vascular emergency — STAT vascular surgery — Ischemia >6h = high amputation risk. CTA if stable; do not delay OR if clearly ischemic. Prepare for fasciotomy, vascular repair, or possible amputation.

Reduction preparation — consent, sedation, equipment — Procedural sedation (propofol, ketamine, etomidate), regional block, or intra-articular lidocaine (hip). Monitoring, suction/airway, fluoro available.

Failed reduction — emergent OR — Splint in position of stability, continuous NV monitoring, NPO. Emergent ortho consult ± vascular.

Admit or discharge? — All major joint dislocations need admission or close follow-up — discharge is the rare exception.

Admit — NV checks q4h, ortho ± vascular — Serial ABI q4h ×24-48h for knee. CT for hip. NPO + IV antibiotics continued for open injuries.

Discharge — NWB, ortho follow-up NEXT DAY — Only if reduction confirmed on X-ray, normal NV exam before AND after, reliable patient, next-day ortho follow-up CONFIRMED.
