SVT: Stabilize, Convert, Dispo

Adults with a regular narrow-complex tachycardia in the ED — AVNRT, AVRT, atrial tachycardia, atrial flutter, and WPW-associated SVT including pre-excited atrial fibrillation. Excludes confirmed ventricular tachycardia and pediatrics (pediatric adenosine dosing is noted in myMedKitt but not modeled here).

⚠ Unstable SVT (hypotension, altered mental status, chest pain, pulmonary edema) → immediate synchronized cardioversion; do not delay for vagal maneuvers or adenosine.
⚠ WPW + pre-excited AF (irregular, wide, often >200 bpm): AVOID adenosine, diltiazem/verapamil, beta-blockers, and digoxin — AV-nodal blockers can precipitate ventricular fibrillation. Use synchronized cardioversion or procainamide.
⚠ Diltiazem/verapamil and beta-blockers are also contraindicated in WPW with atrial fibrillation, decompensated heart failure, and SBP <90.
⚠ Carotid sinus massage: never bilateral simultaneous massage; avoid with carotid bruits, recent TIA/stroke, or MI within 3 months — stroke risk ~1 in 1,000.

4 · Vagal maneuvers

  1. Patient sitting upright at 45°
  2. Blow into a 10 mL syringe for 15 seconds (~40 mmHg pressure)
  3. Immediately lie the patient flat
  4. Elevate the legs to 45° for 15 seconds
  5. Return to sitting and check the rhythm
Modified Valsalva (REVERT trial) converts 43% vs 17% with standard Valsalva (NNT 3.8) — zero cost, no adverse effects. Can repeat 2-3 times.
  1. Patient supine, neck extended, head turned away
  2. Firm circular pressure below the angle of the jaw for 5-10 seconds
  3. Try the RIGHT side first, wait 1 minute, then LEFT if needed
  4. Never bilateral simultaneous massage
Carotid sinus massage converts 20-40% of AVNRT but carries a stroke risk of 1 in 1,000. Avoid with carotid bruits, recent TIA/stroke, or MI within 3 months.

8 · WPW considerations

WPW baseline ECG: short PR interval (<120 ms), delta wave, wide QRS. Orthodromic AVRT (90%) is narrow-complex and generally safe for AV-nodal blockers; pre-excited AF is wide, irregular, often >200 bpm, and dangerous.

Orthodromic AVRT — management
Vagal maneuvers, adenosine, and diltiazem/metoprolol are acceptable — the accessory pathway is only conducting retrograde. If the rhythm later goes into AF, the accessory pathway becomes dangerous — keep procainamide available and monitor closely. Refer for EP study + ablation.
Pre-excited AF — safe treatments
Synchronized cardioversion (100-200J) if unstable. If stable: procainamide or ibutilide (1 mg IV over 10 min, may repeat once). All WPW + AF requires cardiology consult and admission.

9 · Disposition

Discharge if
Known SVT with similar prior episodes, stable throughout, no ischemic ECG change or troponin concern, reliable follow-up, no WPW. Teach modified Valsalva; cardiology follow-up in 1-2 weeks; EP referral for ablation (curative >95%).
Admit if
First episode, required multiple agents, hemodynamic compromise during the episode, structural heart disease, elevated troponin with ACS concern, WPW identified, or unreliable follow-up. Telemetry, check TSH and electrolytes, cardiology consult; EP referral if WPW.
Other pitfalls3 items
Do NOT push adenosine slowly — its half-life is ~10 s; a slow push or missing flush will fail to convert.
Do NOT discharge a first-episode SVT or any WPW without cardiology follow-up and EP consideration.
Do NOT combine an IV beta-blocker with a calcium channel blocker for rate control — risk of synergistic hypotension and bradycardia.

8 · WPW considerations

WPW baseline ECG: short PR interval (<120 ms), delta wave, wide QRS. Orthodromic AVRT (90%) is narrow-complex and generally safe for AV-nodal blockers; pre-excited AF is wide, irregular, often >200 bpm, and dangerous.

Vagal maneuvers, adenosine, and diltiazem/metoprolol are acceptable — the accessory pathway is only conducting retrograde. If the rhythm later goes into AF, the accessory pathway becomes dangerous — keep procainamide available and monitor closely. Refer for EP study + ablation.

Synchronized cardioversion (100-200J) if unstable. If stable: procainamide or ibutilide (1 mg IV over 10 min, may repeat once). All WPW + AF requires cardiology consult and admission.

Procainamide: 20-50 mg/min IV to max 17 mg/kg — Slows accessory-pathway conduction. Stop if hypotension occurs or QRS widens >50%.

Procainamide max total load (17 mg/kg): 1190 mg

9 · Disposition

Known SVT with similar prior episodes, stable throughout, no ischemic ECG change or troponin concern, reliable follow-up, no WPW. Teach modified Valsalva; cardiology follow-up in 1-2 weeks; EP referral for ablation (curative >95%).

First episode, required multiple agents, hemodynamic compromise during the episode, structural heart disease, elevated troponin with ACS concern, WPW identified, or unreliable follow-up. Telemetry, check TSH and electrolytes, cardiology consult; EP referral if WPW.

Other pitfalls
