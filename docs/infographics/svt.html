SVT: Stabilize, Convert, Dispo

Adults ≥18 in the ED with a regular narrow-complex tachycardia (AVNRT, AVRT, atrial tachycardia, or flutter). Excludes pediatrics, pregnancy, and confirmed ventricular tachycardia — each needs a separate pathway.

⚠ Unstable SVT (hypotension, AMS, chest pain, pulmonary edema) → synchronized cardioversion
⚠ Modified Valsalva (REVERT): 43% conversion vs 17% standard (NNT 3.8)
⚠ Adenosine: 6mg rapid IV push → 12mg if no response; NOT for WPW with pre-excited AF
⚠ WPW with AF: AVOID adenosine, diltiazem, beta-blockers → use procainamide or cardiovert
⚠ Diltiazem 15-20mg IV over 2 min preferred for rate control (98% success)

3 · Vagal → adenosine → cardiovert

Modified Valsalva (REVERT) — first line
Blow into a 10 mL syringe for 15 s at 45°, then immediately lie flat and elevate the legs for 15 s. 43% conversion vs 17% with standard Valsalva (NNT 3.8), zero cost, no adverse effects.[3]

Carotid sinus massage is an alternative (20–40% success in AVNRT) but carries a stroke risk of 1 in 1,000. Never bilateral; avoid with carotid bruit, recent TIA/stroke, or MI <3 months.[9]

Adenosine
6mg → 12mg
rapid IV push (1–2 s) + 20 mL flush
First 6mg; repeat 12mg if no response in 1–2 min. Use a proximal (antecubital) IV; single-syringe (drug + 18 mL NS) method converts more reliably. Warn the patient about transient flushing and a few seconds of asystole.
Do NOT push adenosine in pre-excited AF
Irregular pre-excited AF is present — adenosine can accelerate accessory-pathway conduction into VF. Cardiovert or give procainamide instead.[8]

Read the response: abrupt termination → reentrant AVNRT/AVRT; transient slowing that unmasks saw-tooth waves → atrial flutter; no change → reconsider flutter, VT, or dose/technique.

6 · Disposition & pitfalls

Discharge if
Known SVT with similar prior episodes, stable throughout, no chest pain or ischemic ECG change, no troponin concern, reliable follow-up, no WPW. Teach modified Valsalva; cardiology in 1–2 weeks; EP referral for ablation (curative >95%).[1]
Admit if

6 · Disposition & pitfalls

Known SVT with similar prior episodes, stable throughout, no chest pain or ischemic ECG change, no troponin concern, reliable follow-up, **no WPW**. Teach modified Valsalva; cardiology in 1–2 weeks; EP referral for ablation (curative >95%).{{ref1}}

First episode, required multiple agents, hemodynamic compromise during the episode, structural heart disease, elevated troponin with ACS concern, or WPW identified. Telemetry, check TSH and electrolytes, cardiology consult.{{ref1}}

Critical pitfalls

**Do NOT give AV nodal blockers in pre-excited AF** (adenosine, diltiazem, beta-blockers, digoxin) — they can drive VF.

**Do NOT delay cardioversion in a truly unstable patient** to try vagal maneuvers or adenosine.

**Do NOT push adenosine slowly** — its half-life is ~10 s; a slow push or missing flush will fail.

**Do NOT discharge a first-episode SVT or any WPW** without cardiology follow-up and EP consideration.
