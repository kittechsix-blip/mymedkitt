STEMI: ECG Diagnosis to Reperfusion

Adults ≥18 in the ED or prehospital setting with suspected acute coronary syndrome. STEMI is an ECG diagnosis — do NOT wait for troponin. Excludes stable chronic coronary disease, pediatrics, and pregnancy-specific management.

⚠ 12-lead ECG within 10 minutes of first medical contact. STEMI is an ECG diagnosis — a negative troponin does NOT rule it out. 8.4% of ACS presents WITHOUT chest pain.
⚠ Activate the cath lab immediately for confirmed STEMI — goal FMC-to-device ≤90 min (local) / ≤120 min (transfer). Do not wait on labs.
⚠ Inferior STEMI → get right-sided leads. If RV involvement (STE ≥1 mm in V4R): AVOID nitrates and diuretics — they drop preload and can cause hemodynamic collapse. RV infarct is volume-dependent.
⚠ Do NOT give morphine unless pain is refractory — it delays P2Y12 absorption and is associated with increased mortality.
⚠ AIVR after reperfusion is benign — a sign of successful reperfusion. Do NOT treat with antidysrhythmics; suppressing it can cause hemodynamic collapse.
⚠ Prasugrel is contraindicated with prior stroke/TIA and has no benefit at age >75 y or weight <60 kg.

1 · Recognize — STEMI is an ECG diagnosis

12-lead ECG within 10 minutes
Obtain a 12-lead ECG within 10 minutes of first medical contact. STEMI is an ECG diagnosis — do NOT delay for troponin, and a negative troponin does not rule it out.[4]
  • Chest pain + diaphoresis carries the highest likelihood ratio for STEMI.[1]
  • Radiation to both arms — 96% specific but only 11% sensitive.[2]
  • 8.4% of ACS presents WITHOUT chest pain — dyspnea, diaphoresis, nausea, or syncope may be the only clue.[3]

Classic presentation: chest pain/pressure ± radiation to arms, jaw, or back, with diaphoresis, nausea/vomiting, or dyspnea. Anginal equivalents dominate in women and the elderly.[28][29]
