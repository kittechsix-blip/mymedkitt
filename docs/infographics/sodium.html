Sodium Disorders: Symptoms Drive Speed, Not the Number

Adults ≥18 in the ED/inpatient setting with hypo- (Na⁺ <135) or hypernatremia (Na⁺ >145). Excludes pediatrics, pregnancy, and dialysis-dependent ESRD — each needs a modified correction strategy.

⚠ Severe symptomatic hyponatremia (seizure, coma, respiratory distress) → 3% saline 100-150 mL IV bolus NOW. Peripheral access is acceptable — do NOT delay for a central line. Raise Na⁺ by only 4-6 mEq/L to reverse cerebral edema, then STOP.
⚠ Cap Na⁺ correction at ≤10 mEq/L per 24 h (≤8 if high ODS risk). Overcorrection causes osmotic demyelination syndrome (ODS) — often irreversible. K⁺ repletion raises Na⁺ too: 40 mEq KCl ≈ +1 mEq/L Na⁺, so count it toward the daily total.
⚠ Correct hypernatremia SLOWLY: ≤10-12 mEq/L per 24 h with enteral free water or D5W. Rapid correction of a chronic (>48 h) elevation risks cerebral edema.
⚠ Treat the cause, not the number. Symptom severity — not the sodium value alone — sets the urgency of treatment.

Two doors from one lab
Na⁺ <135 mEq/L → hyponatremia (excess water relative to sodium). Na⁺ >145 mEq/L → hypernatremia (almost always a free-water deficit). The workup, dangers, and correction pace differ — pick the branch first.[7]

2 · Severe hyponatremia — 3% saline now

Severe/moderate symptoms → treat NOW
Seizure, obtundation, coma, respiratory arrest, or vomiting/confusion: give 3% Hypertonic Saline 100-150 mL IV bolus over 10-20 min, repeat up to 2 more times (3 total) if symptoms persist. Peripheral access is fine.[14]
Mild / asymptomatic → no emergent 3% saline
Subtle cognitive change, gait instability, or an incidental lab finding: do NOT bolus hypertonic saline. Move to the ordered diagnostic workup below and correct slowly.[1]

Target: raise Na⁺ by 4-6 mEq/L in the first 1-2 h — enough to stop seizures and reverse cerebral edema, then STOP. Each 100 mL bolus raises Na⁺ ≈2 mEq/L in a 70 kg adult.[8]

Hypernatremia is almost always a free-water deficit — inadequate intake or excessive loss. Assess volume and urine output, then quantify the deficit to guide replacement volume.[10]

Polyuria >3 L/day → diabetes insipidus
Give a DDAVP 2 mcg IV trial: urine osm rises >50% → central DI (replace with Desmopressin (DDAVP) 2 mcg IV q8-12h). No rise → nephrogenic DI (DDAVP ineffective — stop the offending agent; lithium is the top medication cause; use a thiazide + amiloride).[12]

Iatrogenic (volume-overloaded) hypernatremia from NS/NaHCO₃ loads: give Furosemide 20-40 mg IV to make hypotonic urine, replace the urine volume with D5W, and switch maintenance from NS to D5W.

5 · Correction limits & monitoring

Overcorrection → osmotic demyelination (ODS)
Standard risk: ≤10 mEq/L in the first 24 h (≤18 in 48 h). High ODS risk (Na⁺ ≤105, alcohol use disorder, liver disease, malnutrition, hypokalemia): ≤8 mEq/L in 24 h. Correct just enough to stop symptoms (4-6 mEq/L), then slow down.[15]

7 · Do NOT

Critical pitfalls

**Do NOT chase the number over symptoms.** An asymptomatic Na⁺ of 118 gets a slow workup; a seizing Na⁺ of 125 gets 3% saline.

**Do NOT exceed 10 mEq/L in 24 h (8 if high risk).** Overcorrection causes osmotic demyelination — count K⁺ repletion toward the total.

**Do NOT forget the glucose correction.** A measured Na⁺ of 118 at glucose 600 is really ~126 — treat the osmole, not the sodium.

**Do NOT correct chronic hypernatremia fast.** Keep it ≤10-12 mEq/L per 24 h with free water/D5W to avoid cerebral edema.
