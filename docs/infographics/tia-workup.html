TIA Workup: Stratify, Image, Disposition

Adults ≥18 in the ED with resolved focal neurologic symptoms suspected of TIA. Excludes ongoing deficits (treat as acute stroke — activate stroke pathway), pediatrics, and pregnancy.

⚠ Fingerstick glucose immediately (rule out hypoglycemia mimic)
⚠ 12-lead ECG ASAP (detect atrial fibrillation in ~7%)
⚠ MRI with DWI when feasible (distinguish TIA from minor stroke)
⚠ CTA head/neck to identify large vessel stenosis/dissection
⚠ Load DAPT if non-cardioembolic (ASA 162-325 mg + clopidogrel 300-600 mg)
⚠ Start high-intensity statin immediately (atorvastatin 80 mg)

1 · What counts as a TIA

Tissue-based definition (AHA 2009, reaffirmed 2023)
Acute focal neurologic dysfunction in a single vascular territory, rapid resolution, and no infarction on DWI-MRI. Resolved symptoms + positive DWI = ischemic stroke, not TIA.[2]
This is time-critical
90-day stroke risk after TIA is up to 17.8%, with ~50% occurring in the first 2 days. Do not delay stratification, imaging, or treatment.[2]

Up to 60% of TIA referrals are mimics. Sudden onset, negative symptoms (weakness, numbness, vision loss), and a single vascular territory favor true TIA; gradual spread and positive phenomena favor a mimic.

Prefer the Canadian TIA Score
The 13-variable Canadian TIA Score (validated 2021) discriminates better than ABCD2 (AUC 0.70 vs 0.60) and incorporates ECG and imaging findings. Use it as the primary tool where available; ABCD2 remains a fast bedside anchor.[4]

3 · High-risk features that override any score

Admit regardless of ABCD2 or Canadian score if any of these are present — the score is not the ceiling on risk.

  • Crescendo TIA (>1 event within 7 days) — unstable plaque / ongoing embolization
  • Large-vessel stenosis ≥50% (carotid or intracranial)
  • Atrial fibrillation — needs anticoagulation, not DAPT
  • Acute infarct on DWI — this is a stroke, not a TIA
  • Known hypercoagulable state or identified cardioembolic source
  • Fluctuating or recurrent symptoms

4 · ED workup

  1. STAT fingerstick glucose — rule out hypoglycemia mimic
  2. 12-lead ECG ASAP — atrial fibrillation is found in ~7%
  3. Labs: CBC, BMP, coags, lipid panel, HbA1c
  4. Non-contrast CT head — rule out hemorrhage / mass
  5. MRI with DWI — distinguishes true TIA from minor stroke
  6. CTA head/neck — identify stenosis ≥50% or dissection
  7. Cardiac evaluation — telemetry now, echo per etiology
CT is not enough
Non-contrast CT has only 16–30% sensitivity for acute infarct. 30–50% of clinical 'TIAs' have infarct on DWI and are actually strokes. Get MRI-DWI when feasible.[3]
