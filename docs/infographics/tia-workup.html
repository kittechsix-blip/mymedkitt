TIA Workup: Risk-Stratify, Find the Etiology, Treat Fast

Adults presenting to the ED with a suspected transient ischemic attack (TIA) or minor ischemic stroke (NIHSS ≤3). Excludes hemorrhagic stroke, moderate-to-large ischemic stroke (NIHSS >3), and pediatric patients — each needs a different pathway.

⚠ High-risk features override any risk score. Crescendo TIA (>1 in 7 days), symptomatic carotid/intracranial stenosis ≥50%, atrial fibrillation, acute infarct on DWI, or a known hypercoagulable state mandate admission regardless of ABCD2 or Canadian TIA Score.
⚠ Atrial fibrillation requires anticoagulation, NOT dual antiplatelet therapy (DAPT). Giving DAPT for a cardioembolic source increases bleeding without stroke-prevention benefit.
⚠ Do NOT rely on ABCD2 alone to identify patients safe for discharge (ACEP 2016 Level B) — it does not account for stroke mechanism (large-vessel or cardioembolic).
⚠ Speed of assessment matters more than setting. 90-day stroke risk after TIA is up to 17.8%, with about 50% occurring in the first 2 days — do not delay workup because symptoms have resolved.

1 · Presentation, mimics & risk stratification

TIA = transient focal neurologic deficit attributable to a single vascular territory, with no infarction on DWI-MRI. Resolved symptoms + negative DWI = true TIA; resolved symptoms + positive DWI = ischemic stroke, not TIA. 90-day stroke risk is up to 17.8%, with about 50% occurring in the first 2 days.[1][2]

Migraine with auraGradual spread (5-20 min), positive phenomena (scintillations, tingling), headache follows
Seizure / Todd'sConvulsive activity, tongue bite, postictal confusion, gradual recovery
HypoglycemiaRapidly correctable, autonomic symptoms, altered consciousness
SyncopeLoss of consciousness, diffuse (not focal), prodrome, rapid recovery
VestibularIsolated vertigo, positional, Dix-Hallpike positive, no other neuro signs
TGAIsolated memory dysfunction, repetitive questioning, no motor deficits

Up to 60% of referrals are mimics
60% of TIA clinic referrals turn out to be mimics. Red flags against TIA: gradual onset or spread, positive phenomena, non-anatomic distribution, loss of consciousness, preceding headache.[1][3]
When in doubt, treat as TIA
Even if a mimic is suspected, still perform basic workup (glucose, ECG, CT head) if any vascular risk factor is present, this is a first episode, or the diagnosis cannot be confirmed — the consequences of missing a TIA outweigh over-evaluation.[1][3]

Canadian TIA Score is preferred over ABCD2 (AUC 0.70 vs ABCD2 0.60, validated in 7,607 patients). ACEP 2016 (Level B) warns against using ABCD2 alone to identify patients safe for discharge — it does not account for stroke mechanism (large-vessel or cardioembolic).[4][3]

2 · ED workup

  1. Fingerstick glucose immediately — rules out the hypoglycemia mimic.
  2. 12-lead ECG ASAP — detects atrial fibrillation in about 7% of TIA/stroke patients.
  3. Non-contrast CT head — rules out hemorrhage; low sensitivity (~16-30%) for acute infarct alone.
  4. MRI with DWI when feasible — distinguishes true TIA (negative) from minor stroke (positive); 30-50% of clinically-diagnosed "TIAs" have an infarct on DWI.
  5. CTA head/neck — identifies large-vessel stenosis, occlusion, or dissection; about 50% of TIA patients have abnormal vascular imaging.
  6. Labs — CBC, BMP, coagulation panel, lipid panel, HbA1c.

5 · One-glance pathway

Suspected TIA / minor stroke — TIA = transient focal neurologic deficit, single vascular territory, resolving, DWI negative. 90-day stroke risk up to 17.8%, ~50% in first 2 days.

Presentation atypical / mimic suspected? — Up to 60% of TIA clinic referrals are mimics — migraine with aura, seizure/Todd's, hypoglycemia, syncope, vestibular, TGA, anxiety. Red flags for mimic: gradual onset, positive phenomena, bilateral symptoms, LOC, preceding headache.

Basic workup if any risk factor, first episode, or uncertain — Even if mimic suspected, still perform glucose + ECG + CT head + basic neuro exam if any vascular risk factor, first episode, or diagnosis uncertain. When in doubt, treat as TIA.

ED workup: glucose+ECG, CT/MRI-DWI, CTA head/neck, labs — STAT glucose + ECG, then CT head, MRI-DWI when feasible, CTA head/neck, labs (CBC, BMP, coags, lipids, HbA1c).

Suspected etiology? — TOAST classification: large artery atherosclerosis (15-25%), cardioembolism (20-30%), small vessel (20-25%), other (5%), cryptogenic (25-40%). Etiology determines anticoagulation vs DAPT vs DAPT+revascularization workup.

Anticoagulation (apixaban preferred) — NOT DAPT — Afib requires anticoagulation, NOT DAPT. Apixaban preferred (lowest bleeding risk). Can start immediately for TIA without infarct; delay 1-14 days for minor-to-large stroke per the '1-2-3-4 day rule'.

DAPT + urgent CEA/CAS eval (within 2 weeks) — Symptomatic stenosis ≥50% needs CEA/CAS within 2 weeks. CEA preferred over CAS if within 7 days. Start DAPT while awaiting intervention.

DAPT: non-cardioembolic pathway (small vessel/crypto/dissection) — Small vessel, cryptogenic, dissection, or other cardioembolic (PFO) sources follow the DAPT pathway (CHANCE/POINT protocol).

All: high-intensity statin + BP <130/80 long-term — All patients: atorvastatin 80 mg started immediately, and BP control — permissive acutely (unless >220/120), target <130/80 long-term.

High-risk feature present? — High-risk features override any risk score and mandate admission: crescendo TIA, symptomatic stenosis ≥50%, atrial fibrillation, DWI-positive infarct, known hypercoagulable state, or inability to complete outpatient workup within 48-72 hours.

Admit — Admit to stroke/neurology service. Telemetry, neuro checks q4h, complete remaining workup, DVT prophylaxis unless anticoagulated, arrange specialty follow-up.

23-hour ED observation — Complete CTA/MRI, continuous telemetry, TTE, neurology consult, initiate secondary prevention. Discharge if 24h telemetry negative, imaging reassuring, and rapid TIA clinic follow-up arranged.

Discharge + rapid TIA clinic <48h — Discharge with DAPT/anticoagulation loaded, high-intensity statin started, rapid TIA clinic follow-up within 24-48 hours, and BE FAST return precautions given in writing.

6 · Do NOT

Critical pitfalls

**Do NOT give DAPT for a cardioembolic (Afib) source** — it increases bleeding without stroke-prevention benefit. Afib needs anticoagulation.

**Do NOT rely on ABCD2 alone to identify safe discharges** (ACEP 2016 Level B) — it does not account for mechanism (large-vessel, cardioembolic).

**Do NOT delay treatment because symptoms resolved** — 90-day stroke risk is up to 17.8%, with ~50% in the first 2 days.

**Do NOT discharge without a rapid TIA clinic follow-up within 24-48 hours** — if unavailable, admit or observe instead.

**Do NOT continue DAPT beyond 21 days** without reassessing — duration beyond 21 days increases bleeding without additional stroke-prevention benefit.

**Do NOT skip DVT prophylaxis on admission** unless the patient is already anticoagulated.
