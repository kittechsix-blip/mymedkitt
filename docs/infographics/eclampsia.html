Eclampsia: Magnesium First, Then Blood Pressure

Pregnant patients ≥20 weeks gestation OR up to 4 weeks postpartum presenting to the ED with a new tonic-clonic seizure or severe hypertension. Excludes known epilepsy and non-pregnancy seizure etiologies, which need a different workup.

⚠ Call OB stat, left lateral position, high-flow O2, two large-bore IVs, fetal monitoring
⚠ Magnesium 4-6g IV over 15-20min — first-line for eclamptic seizures over benzodiazepines
⚠ Treat SBP ≥160 or DBP ≥110 within 30-60 min — hemorrhagic stroke is leading cause of death
⚠ Loss of DTRs at 9-12 mg/dL, respiratory arrest at 15 mg/dL — antidote: calcium gluconate 1g IV
⚠ If seizing on adequate mag: additional 2g bolus, then lorazepam — consider non-eclamptic etiology
⚠ Magnesium sulfate 4-6 g IV bolus over 15-20 min, then 1-2 g/hr infusion

1 · Recognize

Definition
Eclampsia = a new tonic-clonic seizure in a patient with preeclampsia, at ≥20 weeks gestation or up to 4 weeks postpartum, with no other cause.[1] Seizures are self-limited but signal a hypertensive emergency to the brain.
It arrives without warning
20-38% of eclampsia occurs WITHOUT prior preeclampsia or hypertension, and 20% have no prodrome at all.[2] A first seizure in any pregnant or recently-pregnant patient is eclampsia until proven otherwise.

Headache66-83%Severe, frontal/occipital, unrelieved by acetaminophen
Visual changes27-44%Scotomata, blurring, photophobia, rare cortical blindness
Epigastric / RUQ pain12-25%Hepatic involvement — think HELLP

  • Platelets <100,000/µL

  • Visual disturbances

2 · What to do right now

Set the toggles above to your patient. Exactly one instruction below is highlighted — magnesium toxicity overrides everything, then active seizure, then severe hypertension.

Magnesium toxicity — STOP and reverse
Stop the magnesium infusion. Give calcium gluconate 1 g (10 mL of 10%) IV over 3-5 min — works in 1-3 min. Support ventilation. Recheck level and reflexes.[5]
Active seizure — magnesium first
Magnesium sulfate 4-6 g IV over 15-20 min now. Left lateral decubitus, high-flow O₂, protect the airway, do NOT restrain. Magnesium beats benzodiazepines and phenytoin for eclampsia.[4]
Severe hypertension — treat within 30-60 min
Labetalol 20 mg IV (escalate 40/80/80) or hydralazine 5-10 mg IV. Target 140-150/90-100 — avoid precipitous drops. Untreated severe HTN causes hemorrhagic stroke, the leading cause of eclampsia death.[6]
No seizure, no severe HTN — prophylaxis & monitor
If severe features are present, start magnesium prophylaxis: 4 g IV load then 1-2 g/hr (NNT 50-100). Serial BP, HELLP labs q6-12h, continuous fetal monitoring, OB co-management.[4]

6 · Stabilize, HELLP & disposition

**H**emolysis (LDH >600), **E**levated **L**iver enzymes (AST/ALT >2× normal), **L**ow **P**latelets (<100,000/µL). May occur without hypertension. **Delivery is the treatment.**{{ref3}}

Fetal bradycardia and late decelerations are expected during/after a seizure and usually recover in 10-20 min. **Allow 15-30 min of intrauterine resuscitation** (left lateral, O₂, cautious fluids) before an emergent section — unless abruption, cord prolapse, or a Category III tracing with no recovery.{{ref7}}

Disposition | Criteria

ICU / high-risk L&D | Refractory seizures or BP, respiratory failure, HELLP with DIC, multi-organ dysfunction, AMS >30 min

L&D with close monitoring | Seizures controlled on magnesium, BP controlled, stable mother, reassuring fetus

Critical pitfalls — do NOT

**Do NOT reach for a benzodiazepine first** — magnesium is first-line and reduces recurrent seizures better than diazepam or phenytoin.

**Do NOT ignore SBP ≥160 / DBP ≥110** — treat within 30-60 min to prevent hemorrhagic stroke.

**Do NOT miss magnesium toxicity** — loss of DTRs then respiratory arrest; stop the drip and give calcium gluconate.

**Do NOT stop watching postpartum** — most maternal deaths occur postpartum; continue magnesium 24-48h.
