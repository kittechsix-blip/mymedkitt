Eclampsia: Seizure Control, BP Management, and Delivery

Pregnant or postpartum patients (≥20 weeks gestation through 4 weeks postpartum) presenting with an eclamptic seizure or severe preeclampsia in the ED. Excludes pediatric and non-pregnant seizure etiologies — eclampsia is a diagnosis of exclusion once other causes (epilepsy, ICH, PRES, venous thrombosis) have been considered.

⚠ Eclampsia is a diagnosis of exclusion — 20-38% occurs WITHOUT a prior preeclampsia diagnosis or hypertension, and 20% has NO warning symptoms.
⚠ Never drop MAP by more than 25% in the first hour of BP treatment — precipitous drops risk uteroplacental hypoperfusion.
⚠ Magnesium toxicity: stop the infusion if deep tendon reflexes are absent or RR <12/min — calcium gluconate is the antidote.
⚠ Persistent severe hypertension or seizures refractory to magnesium are indications for delivery — delivery is the definitive treatment, not BP or seizure control alone.

Severe hypertension = SBP ≥160 mmHg OR DBP ≥110 mmHg sustained ≥15 minutes. Treat within 30-60 minutes — hemorrhagic stroke is the leading cause of eclampsia death. Target BP 140-150/90-100 mmHg; never drop MAP more than 25% in the first hour.

4 · Stabilization

  • SpO2 target >94%, aspiration precautions
  • Two large-bore IVs, cautious fluids (avoid pulmonary edema)
  • Foley catheter, strict I/O
  • Serial CBC, LFTs, LDH, creatinine, magnesium level q6-12h
HELLP syndrome (10-20% of eclampsia)
Hemolysis (LDH >600, schistocytes) + Elevated Liver enzymes (AST/ALT >2× normal) + Low Platelets (<100,000). Management is supportive care, blood products for coagulopathy, and delivery.

Fetal bradycardia and late decelerations are common during/after seizure and usually resolve within 10-20 minutes — allow up to 15-30 minutes for fetal recovery rather than rushing to emergent cesarean.

5 · Delivery & disposition

Delivery is the definitive treatment. Target delivery within 12-24 hours of eclampsia diagnosis. Cesarean is NOT mandatory — vaginal delivery is acceptable with a favorable cervix, reassuring fetal status, and stable maternal condition.

ICU / high-risk L&D
Refractory seizures, refractory BP requiring continuous infusion, HELLP with DIC, respiratory failure, or multi-organ dysfunction → arterial line, continuous magnesium infusion, multidisciplinary team for urgent delivery.
L&D with close monitoring
Seizures controlled on magnesium, BP controlled, stable maternal status, reassuring fetal status → continuous fetal monitoring, mag infusion, BP checks q15-30min, prepare for delivery within 12-24 hours.

7 · Do NOT

Critical pitfalls

Do NOT use sublingual nifedipine — unpredictable absorption, can cause severe hypotension.

Do NOT delay treatment of severe hypertension beyond 30-60 minutes — hemorrhagic stroke is the leading cause of eclampsia mortality.

Do NOT drop BP too fast — avoid a MAP fall of more than 25% in the first hour; it risks uteroplacental hypoperfusion.

Do NOT give magnesium if reflexes are absent — check DTRs before every bolus and ensure urine output >25 mL/hr.

Do NOT rush to emergent cesarean during an active seizure or the immediate postictal period — allow 15-30 minutes for fetal recovery first.

Do NOT proceed with neuraxial anesthesia if platelets are below 70,000 without hematology/anesthesia input.
