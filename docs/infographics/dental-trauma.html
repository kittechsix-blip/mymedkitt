Dental Trauma — Comprehensive ED Management

ED evaluation and initial management of acute dental trauma in patients of any age — crown fractures (Ellis I-III), luxation injuries (concussion through intrusive luxation), alveolar fractures, and tooth avulsion — covering both primary (baby) and permanent dentition. Scope is ED-level assessment, stabilization, and antibiotic/splinting decisions; definitive care (pulp capping, root canal, orthodontic/surgical repositioning) requires dental or oral surgery follow-up.

⚠ Ellis III (pulp exposure) is a DENTAL EMERGENCY — exposed pulp is viable only 3-6 hours; apply calcium hydroxide and arrange same-day dental follow-up.
⚠ Permanent tooth avulsion is TIME CRITICAL — reimplant within 60 minutes; store the tooth in milk or HBSS if reimplantation is delayed.
⚠ NEVER reimplant primary (baby) teeth — risk of damaging the permanent tooth bud.
⚠ Handle an avulsed tooth by the CROWN only — never touch, scrub, or dry the root.
⚠ Intrusive luxation >3mm requires surgical repositioning — do NOT attempt to force eruption.
⚠ Alveolar fracture is treated as an open fracture — requires antibiotics and rigid/semi-rigid splinting.
⚠ Doxycycline is NOT recommended under age 12 (IADT 2020) — risk of permanent tooth discoloration; use amoxicillin or clindamycin instead.

Ellis fracture management

White, chalky chip, smooth or rough edges, no temperature sensitivity, no bleeding. File/smooth rough edges with an emery board or fine file if causing lip irritation. Return if pain develops (may indicate deeper injury).

Yellow/ivory dentin visible, sensitive to air/cold/touch, may have slight bleeding from dentinal tubules. Irrigate with saline, apply calcium hydroxide paste, cover with glass ionomer cement, dental wax, or an aluminum foil barrier. Soft diet, avoid extreme temperatures.

Luxation management

Pain to percussion, no mobility, no displacement, normal X-ray. Observation only, soft diet × 2 weeks, monitor for color change (sign of necrosis). No splinting required. Dental check if symptoms persist.

Increased mobility (wiggles), normal position, may have gingival bleeding, tender to percussion. Tooth appears longer than adjacent teeth, very loose, elevated from socket. Reposition with gentle finger pressure back into socket, verify occlusion, flexible splint × 2 weeks. Tooth angled forward (labially) or backward (palatally/lingually), usually locked in position due to alveolar bone impaction, occlusion disturbed, high-pitched percussion sound. Alveolar fracture

Multiple teeth move TOGETHER as a segment, palpable bone step deformity, malocclusion, gingival bleeding. Imaging: periapical, occlusal, panorex, ± CBCT. Closed reduction under local anesthesia before swelling — reposition teeth first if luxated, then reduce bone. Splint (rigid or semi-rigid) × 3-4 weeks. Tetanus update if indicated. Complex fractures may need ORIF. Oral surgery referral same day for definitive splinting.

Avulsion management

Complete displacement from socket — TIME CRITICAL. First confirm primary vs. permanent tooth. NEVER reimplant primary teeth — damages the permanent tooth bud.

Risks of reimplanting primary teeth: damage to the underlying permanent tooth bud, ankylosis interfering with eruption, infection. Reassure parents this is the correct approach — control bleeding with gauze pressure, soft diet × 2-3 days, ice for swelling. Assess for associated injuries (alveolar fracture, embedded fragment) — X-ray if needed. Counsel family that a space maintainer may be needed if the tooth is lost early.

Do NOT reimplant primary teeth
Reimplanting a primary tooth risks damaging the underlying permanent tooth bud (20-30% risk). Monitor for damage to the permanent successor at dental follow-up 1-2 weeks.
  1. Locate the tooth — if not found, CXR to rule out aspiration.
  2. Handle by CROWN only — NEVER touch, scrub, or dry the root.
  3. If dirty, rinse briefly (<10 seconds under cold running water) — do NOT scrub or use chemicals.
  4. If not reimplanting immediately, store in an optimal medium (see table).

  1. Rinse the socket with saline (remove clot gently).
  2. Rinse the tooth root briefly (<10 sec cold water).
  3. Insert the tooth into the socket with gentle pressure.
  4. Have the patient bite on gauze to stabilize.
  5. Verify correct orientation (facial surface forward) and check occlusion.
  6. If resistance is encountered, do not force — suction gently to clear clot, or refer to a dentist if still difficult.

Splinting (IADT 2020): flexible splint × 2 weeks for avulsion, regardless of dry time. Flexible splint × 4 weeks if there is an associated alveolar/marginal bone fracture. If splinting materials are unavailable, have the patient bite on gauze and refer to dental ASAP.

Antibiotics strongly recommended (IADT 2020). Tetanus update if >5 years since last dose or dirty wound. Mouth care: chlorhexidine 0.12% rinse BID × 1 week, soft foods only, no chewing on the reimplanted tooth. Long-term: ~50% survive 5+ years even with optimal care. Return precautions: increased looseness, fever, worsening pain.

Assess for associated injuries (alveolar fracture, embedded fragment) — X-ray if needed. Counsel family that a space maintainer may be needed if the tooth is lost early.

Reimplanting a primary tooth risks damaging the underlying permanent tooth bud (20-30% risk). Monitor for damage to the permanent successor at dental follow-up 1-2 weeks.

  • Locate the tooth — if not found, CXR to rule out aspiration.
  • Handle by CROWN only — NEVER touch, scrub, or dry the root.
  • If dirty, rinse briefly (<10 seconds under cold running water) — do NOT scrub or use chemicals.
  • If not reimplanting immediately, store in an optimal medium (see table).

Medium | Duration | Notes

HBSS | Up to 24h | GOLD standard

Cold milk | Up to 6h | Excellent, accessible

Saliva | <30 min | Place in buccal vestibule

Saline | <1h | Acceptable

Water | <5 min | AVOID — hypotonic

  • Rinse the socket with saline (remove clot gently).
  • Rinse the tooth root briefly (<10 sec cold water).
  • Insert the tooth into the socket with gentle pressure.
  • Have the patient bite on gauze to stabilize.
  • Verify correct orientation (facial surface forward) and check occlusion.
  • If resistance is encountered, do not force — suction gently to clear clot, or refer to a dentist if still difficult.

Splinting (IADT 2020): flexible splint × 2 weeks for avulsion, regardless of dry time. Flexible splint × 4 weeks if there is an associated alveolar/marginal bone fracture. If splinting materials are unavailable, have the patient bite on gauze and refer to dental ASAP.

Antibiotics strongly recommended (IADT 2020). Tetanus update if >5 years since last dose or dirty wound. Mouth care: chlorhexidine 0.12% rinse BID × 1 week, soft foods only, no chewing on the reimplanted tooth. Long-term: ~50% survive 5+ years even with optimal care. Return precautions: increased looseness, fever, worsening pain.

Amoxicillin (adult): 500 mg PO TID × 7 days PO — IADT 2020 first-line (all ages). Amoxicillin/penicillin preferred over tetracyclines.

Amoxicillin (peds): 25 mg/kg/day divided TID PO — Per-dose (TID) amoxicillin for the entered weight, based on 25 mg/kg/day divided TID.

Clindamycin (adult, PCN allergy): 300 mg PO TID × 7 days PO — For penicillin allergy.

Clindamycin (peds, PCN allergy): 10 mg/kg/day divided TID PO — Per-dose (TID) clindamycin for the entered weight, based on 10 mg/kg/day divided TID.

Doxycycline (age ≥12 ONLY): 100 mg PO BID × 7 days PO — Anti-resorptive option. IADT 2020 does NOT recommend tetracyclines under age 12 — risk of permanent tooth discoloration.

Dental follow-up within 24-48 hours is mandatory. Root canal typically needed within 2-4 weeks for mature (closed-apex) teeth.
