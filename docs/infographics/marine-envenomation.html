Marine Envenomation: Identify the Creature, Match the Antidote

Adults and children with a marine sting or bite presenting to the ED. Excludes freshwater envenomation and non-marine terrestrial snakebite (see the terrestrial-envenomation consult).

⚠ Box jellyfish (Chironex fleckeri) can cause cardiac arrest within minutes of the sting — begin CPR immediately if arrest occurs; prolonged resuscitation has succeeded and is justified.
⚠ Irukandji syndrome causes delayed (30-120 min) catecholamine storm — severe hypertension, tachycardia, impending doom — that can progress within 4 hours to toxic cardiomyopathy, cardiogenic shock, or intracerebral hemorrhage. No antivenom exists.
⚠ Stonefish is the most potent fish venom — pain described as "the worst pain imaginable"; rare systemic effects include hypotension and cardiac dysfunction. Antivenom exists for severe cases.
⚠ Blue-ringed octopus (tetrodotoxin) causes progressive ascending paralysis and respiratory failure while the patient remains conscious throughout — no antivenom exists; prepare for intubation (typical duration 2-5 days).
⚠ Cone snail (conotoxin) causes rapid-onset paralysis, hypotension, and respiratory failure — no antivenom exists; apply pressure immobilization and prepare for cardiorespiratory support.
⚠ Sea snake envenomation causes delayed (30 min-2 hr) myotoxicity and neurotoxicity — ptosis, dysphagia, non-rigid paralysis, rhabdomyolysis, respiratory failure. Observe ALL suspected bites a minimum of 8 hours even if initially asymptomatic.
⚠ Every marine antivenom (box jellyfish, stonefish, sea snake) carries anaphylaxis risk — administer only in a monitored setting with epinephrine drawn up at bedside; stop the infusion immediately if a reaction occurs. Serum sickness can occur 4-14 days later.
⚠ If an antivenom reaction occurs: stop the infusion immediately, maintain the airway, and give epinephrine 0.3-0.5mg IM (anterolateral thigh) FIRST — before diphenhydramine or methylprednisolone.
⚠ Do NOT use vinegar on bluebottle/Portuguese man-of-war — vinegar triggers additional nematocyst discharge in Physalia; it is only correct for true cnidarians like box jellyfish. Do NOT use freshwater or ice on any jellyfish sting (freshwater causes nematocyst discharge).

3 · Cnidarian (jellyfish) envenomation

Box jellyfish (Chironex fleckeri): long visible tentacles (up to 3m), whip-like marks, immediate severe pain, can cause cardiac arrest within minutes.

Irukandji (Carukia barnesi): tiny jellyfish, minimal initial sting, delayed systemic symptoms 30-120 min later, impending doom + hypertension.

Bluebottle/Portuguese man-of-war (Physalia): blue tentacles, painful but rarely life-threatening. Do NOT use vinegar — causes nematocyst discharge.

Other jellyfish: most cause local pain and dermatitis only, rarely life-threatening.

  1. Remove from water, call for help
  2. Douse with vinegar for 30 seconds (neutralizes undischarged nematocysts)
  3. Pick off remaining tentacles with forceps — safe for rescuer after vinegar
  4. Apply ice packs for analgesia
  5. Begin CPR if arrest — prolonged resuscitation may still succeed

Severe complications within 4 hours: toxic cardiomyopathy, cardiogenic shock or pulmonary edema, intracerebral hemorrhage (uncontrolled HTN). No antivenom available — supportive care only. Symptoms typically resolve within 12 hours.

7 · Disposition

Discharge criteria: pain controlled with oral analgesics, no systemic symptoms after 3-4 hour observation, wound care instructions provided, tetanus prophylaxis complete, antibiotics prescribed if indicated.

Admit if: severe muscle cramping; respiratory distress or paralysis; hypotension or arrhythmias; antivenom administration; Irukandji syndrome with ongoing symptoms; blue-ringed octopus or cone snail (respiratory monitoring); penetrating trauma requiring OR exploration.

Wound care: keep clean and dry, watch for infection signs, complete antibiotic course if prescribed, follow up 48-72h for deep punctures. Return precautions: signs of infection, worsening pain after initial improvement, new systemic symptoms (breathing/swallowing difficulty), fever.

Levofloxacin
750mg PO daily
Alternative outpatient regimen to ciprofloxacin or doxycycline. Covers Staph, Strep, Vibrio species.

ICU admission: antivenom administration (anaphylaxis risk), respiratory failure/intubation, cardiac arrhythmias or arrest, blue-ringed octopus envenomation (even if stable), severe Irukandji syndrome.

Floor admission: ongoing IV analgesia, observation for delayed systemic symptoms, IV antibiotics for severe wound infection, penetrating trauma post-surgery.

Surgical consultation: penetrating chest/abdomen/neck, retained foreign body near joints/vessels, hand wounds with retained urchin spines, compartment syndrome concerns.

Poison Control: Australia 13 11 26, US 1-800-222-1222.

Do NOT apply freshwater to any jellyfish sting — causes nematocyst discharge; use seawater or vinegar (for true cnidarians) instead.
Do NOT use ice or cold packs on stonefish/fish stings — worsens pain and prolongs toxin activity; heat is the correct treatment for heat-labile venoms.
Do NOT delay CPR in box jellyfish arrest — prolonged resuscitation has succeeded; do not presume futility early.
