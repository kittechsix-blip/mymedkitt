Bleeding AV Fistula / Graft — ED Management

Patients with a hemodialysis arteriovenous (AV) fistula or graft presenting with access-site bleeding — active/brisk hemorrhage, post-dialysis needle-site oozing, a high-risk access lesion (scab, ulcer, aneurysm/pseudoaneurysm, exposed graft, or infection), or bleeding reported as already controlled before arrival.

⚠ Apply precise focal pressure directly over the bleeding access hole; do not rely on bulky circumferential wraps that hide ongoing bleeding or occlude the whole access.
⚠ Do not use the access arm for a BP cuff, venipuncture, or IV access unless this is a life-over-limb/access emergency.
⚠ Use tourniquet/BP cuff only as a life-saving bridge when hemorrhage is uncontrolled or focal pressure cannot be maintained — it is not first-line for routine oozing.
⚠ Do not discharge spontaneous bleeding, scab, ulcer, necrotic skin, infection, exposed graft, or aneurysm/pseudoaneurysm without urgent vascular/access evaluation — temporary hemostasis is not definitive treatment.
⚠ Resuscitate and reverse anticoagulation adjunctively, but do not delay mechanical source control to do so.
⚠ After hemostasis, confirm thrill/bruit and distal perfusion before disposition.

Bleeding AV Fistula / Graft

Dialysis access bleeding can rapidly become fatal. Treat brisk bleeding from an AV fistula or graft as arterialized venous hemorrhage until controlled.

  • PPE; expose the access fully
  • Call vascular surgery/IR early for spontaneous, recurrent, aneurysmal, infected, or uncontrolled bleeding
  • Apply precise focal pressure directly over the bleeding point
  • Do not use the access arm for BP cuff, venipuncture, or IV access unless this is a life-over-limb/access emergency
  • If shock or life-threatening hemorrhage: resuscitate in parallel and escalate to tourniquet/BP cuff bridge if focal pressure fails

▶ What to do now

Emergent vascular control
Ongoing bleeding despite pressure/adjuncts, tourniquet required, shock/transfusion, or exposed/infected/ruptured graft. Do not discharge after temporary hemostasis.
Urgent access evaluation
High-risk lesions need urgent specialty evaluation even if bleeding stops. Vascular/IR/nephrology consult before discharge; avoid cannulation over the lesion; admit/transfer if urgent evaluation cannot occur safely as outpatient.
Disposition
Discharge only truly low-risk simple puncture bleeds — stable vitals, intact thrill/bruit and distal perfusion, no lesion/infection, no transfusion/tourniquet/reversal, dialysis unit follow-up arranged.

Management algorithm

This is the high-yield spine. See the full protocol for complete node text and citations.

Active hemorrhage — control + resuscitate

  1. One gloved finger or thumb directly over the bleeding hole; use folded gauze, hemostatic gauze, or a hard focal pressure device if available.
  2. Hold uninterrupted pressure for 10-20 min. Do not repeatedly peek.
  3. Elevate arm if feasible.
  4. Avoid bulky circumferential wraps that hide ongoing bleeding or occlude the entire access.
  5. If pressure fails or cannot be maintained during transfer/resuscitation → tourniquet/BP cuff bridge.
  • Large-bore IV/IO away from access arm
  • CBC, BMP/K, PT/INR, aPTT, fibrinogen, type/cross
  • MTP if shock or ongoing major hemorrhage
  • Assess anticoagulants/antiplatelets and last dialysis heparin
  • Call vascular surgery/IR/nephrology now

Response to focal pressure

After uninterrupted focal pressure for 10-20 minutes: bleeding controlled at a simple site is observed; persistent bleeding gets topical/local hemostasis; uncontrolled bleeding or an unstable patient needs the tourniquet/BP cuff bridge; any aneurysm, ulcer, scab, infection, or exposed graft is a high-risk lesion regardless of whether bleeding stopped.

Needle-site oozing

  • Direct focal pressure over the hole for 10-20 min
  • Confirm no expanding hematoma
  • Review dialysis heparin exposure and anticoagulants
  • Check platelet count/coags if prolonged, recurrent, or unexpected
  • After hemostasis: confirm thrill/bruit and distal perfusion
Do not send out unreassessed
Do not place a tight circumferential wrap and send the patient out without reassessment. Prolonged post-dialysis bleeding may signal stenosis, anticoagulation excess, or aneurysm/pseudoaneurysm risk.

Local hemostatic adjuncts

If focal pressure slows but does not stop a localized puncture-site bleed: add a hemostatic dressing if available (chitosan, kaolin, oxidized cellulose, gelatin/thrombin, or equivalent local protocol product). If the source is a single superficial needle hole and vascular help is not immediately available, consider a superficial figure-of-eight or purse-string suture around the skin puncture only — avoid deep bites into the fistula/graft. Confirm thrill/bruit and distal perfusion after any suture/compression.

Tourniquet / BP cuff bridge

  • Brisk hemorrhage with shock or impending shock
  • Spurting/pulsatile bleeding not immediately controlled by direct focal pressure
  • Bleeding cannot be controlled during transfer, procedure setup, or resuscitation
  • Need to free hands for airway/vascular access/MTP while vascular control is mobilized
  • Place proximal enough to stop inflow if using a commercial tourniquet
  • BP cuff option: inflate above systolic pressure and titrate to hemostasis
  • Document application time
  • Reassess every few minutes; transition back to focal pressure/vascular control as soon as feasible
  • Confirm distal perfusion and access thrill when loosened/removed if bleeding remains controlled
Priority is survival
Access thrombosis and limb ischemia are real risks, but uncontrolled access hemorrhage can be fatal within minutes. Tourniquet is not first-line for routine oozing.

  • Hemoglobin acceptable or stable for clinical context
  • Thrill/bruit present after hemostasis

Disposition

  • Simple needle-site bleeding, fully controlled after observation
  • Stable vitals and no recurrent bleeding
  • No high-risk access lesion or infection
  • Thrill/bruit and distal perfusion intact
  • No need for transfusion, tourniquet, reversal, or suture for structural lesion
  • Dialysis unit/nephrology follow-up arranged; access site restrictions communicated
Return precautions
Return immediately for recurrent bleeding, expanding swelling, loss of thrill, hand ischemia symptoms, fever/drainage, syncope, or bleeding through dressing.
Admit/transfer criteria
Admit/transfer for spontaneous bleeding, recurrent/prolonged bleeding, high-risk skin/aneurysm/graft findings, anticoagulation complications, anemia/shock, tourniquet use, transfusion, or inability to obtain urgent access follow-up.
