Video-Assisted Flexible Endoscopic Intubation (VAFEI)

Emergency department patients requiring intubation via combined video laryngoscopy + flexible endoscopy (VAFEI) — anatomically distorted/difficult airways (angioedema, Ludwig angina, oropharyngeal abscess/hematoma, laryngeal or orofacial trauma, airway tumors, prior head/neck surgery or radiation), intrinsic difficult-airway features (micrognathia, trismus, morbid obesity, failed 3-3-2 rule, Mallampati IV, prior difficult intubation), or scenarios such as 'scared to paralyze', failed VL intubation with preserved oxygenation, cervical spine immobility with a predicted difficult airway, or need for continuous visualization in laryngeal trauma. Covers awake/breathing, RSI-then-flexible-endoscope-rescue, and ketamine-only (KOBI) approaches.

⚠ Maximum lidocaine dose for topicalization is 7 mg/kg — calculate before starting
⚠ ALWAYS have a paralytic drawn and ready — laryngospasm can occur even with low-dose ketamine
⚠ VL operator suctions aggressively — a soiled airway obscures both the VL and FE views
⚠ The right speed is SLOWER than your instincts — do not rush tube passage
⚠ Confirm carina visualization through the scope before railroading the ETT
⚠ Waveform capnography is mandatory for confirmation — no capnography means intubation is not confirmed
⚠ Do NOT force the ETT past the cords — forceful advancement can cause tracheal injury
⚠ If the patient deteriorates during an attempt, abort and move to a rescue airway

Overview

VAFEI combines video laryngoscopy to expose the airway with flexible endoscopy to guide tube delivery — VL clears a path through the mouth and lifts the epiglottis, FE provides continuous visualization through the cords, and no nasal route is needed. Maintains spontaneous ventilation; two operators give two sets of eyes; works when VL alone fails to pass the tube.

Reported success rates: awake flexible endoscopic intubation 92% first-pass (nasal), 57% (oral). Combining VL with the flexible scope addresses the oral-route challenge by using VL to guide the scope.

Critical actions across this consult
VAFEI preserves spontaneous breathing — ideal for the "can't paralyze" airway. Calculate the 7 mg/kg max lidocaine dose before topicalization. Always have a paralytic drawn and ready. The VL operator suctions aggressively. The right speed is slower than your instincts. Confirm carina visualization before railroading the ETT.

Procedure — step by step

  1. VL insertion: VL operator inserts the blade and obtains a laryngeal view, maintains position with the LEFT hand, keeps suction ready in the RIGHT hand — "sets the table" for the FE operator
  2. FE insertion: FE operator looks INTO the patient's mouth (direct vision), advances the scope to the tip of the VL blade under direct view, then looks at the VL SCREEN to guide the scope to the cords
  3. Cord passage: spray lidocaine on the cords via the FE channel (if awake), advance through the cords using whichever screen view is better, continue until the CARINA is visualized
  4. Tube delivery: FE operator holds the scope steady, an assistant (or the VL operator) railroads the ETT over the scope until the tip is visible on the FE screen, positioned just proximal to the carina

Positioning (per the source diagram): VL operator at the patient's left shoulder, FE operator behind the head, patient semi-Fowler, split-screen monitor above the bed.

Key pearl
"The right speed is SLOWER than your instincts." The VL operator suctions aggressively — a soiled airway obscures both views.

Railroading the ETT

SexETT size
Women7.0-7.5 (women)
Men7.5-8.0 (men)
  1. FE operator holds the scope steady at the carina
  2. Assistant advances the ETT over the scope
  3. Rotate the ETT 90° counterclockwise if there is resistance at the cords
  4. Advance until the cuff passes the cords (visible on the FE screen)
  5. Continue until the ETT tip is visible just above the carina
Tube hang-up at the cords
Common problem — the ETT catches on the arytenoids. Solutions: rotate 90° counterclockwise (bevel posterior), withdraw slightly and re-advance, jaw thrust by the VL operator, or use a smaller ETT (6.5-7.0).

After tube placement: withdraw the FE while watching ETT position, confirm the ETT is 2-3 cm above the carina, inflate the cuff, attach to the ventilator, confirm with waveform capnography.

Intubation successfulConfirm and move to post-intubation checklist
  • Soiled airway: VL operator suctions aggressively (suction stays in the right hand at all times); clear the pharynx before FE insertion
  • Fogging: warm the scope in warm water before use; anti-fog solution on the lens; wipe the lens on mucosa gently
  • Poor VL view: reposition to more sniffing position, external laryngeal manipulation (BURP), different blade geometry, suction aggressively
  • Poor FE view: withdraw scope slightly and clean lens, suction through the FE channel, advance/withdraw to find a better angle, use the VL view to guide if the FE view is poor
If secretions cannot be cleared
Consider aborting the attempt → BVM → alternative approach.
  • Awake patient: more topical lidocaine to the cords via the FE channel, wait 30-60 seconds for effect, ask the patient to take a deep breath, advance during inspiration when the cords open
  • Cord adduction (KOBI/dissociated): ketamine can increase muscle tone — may need a small dose of paralytic; wait for a breath, advance during inspiration
  • Mechanical obstruction (tumor, mass, edema): try a smaller scope if available; may need a surgical airway
Laryngospasm
STOP attempting to advance, withdraw the scope slightly, give more lidocaine to the cords if possible. If persistent, give the drawn paralytic: rocuronium 1.2 mg/kg or succinylcholine 1.5 mg/kg.
  1. Rotate the ETT 90° counterclockwise — most effective; turns the bevel posteriorly, slides past the arytenoids
  2. Withdraw and re-advance: pull back 1-2 cm, re-advance with rotation
  3. Jaw thrust by the VL operator to open the glottic inlet
  4. Try a smaller ETT (6.5 or 6.0) — easier passage, can upsize later
In the trachea
If the tube is hitting the carina or right mainstem: withdraw the scope/tube as a unit, reposition with the scope just above the carina. Do NOT force — forceful advancement can cause tracheal injury.
  1. STOP the procedure
  2. Remove the FE and VL
  3. BVM ventilate with 100% O2
  4. Reassess airway patency
Escalation ladder
If oxygenating: allow recovery to SpO2 >95%, consider apneic oxygenation (nasal cannula 15 L/min), reattempt when stable. If cannot ventilate: reposition, two-person BVM, place a supraglottic airway (LMA/iGel), consider FE intubation through the SGA. If cannot oxygenate: CICO → surgical airway (cricothyrotomy).

Intubation successful — post-procedure

  • Confirmation: waveform capnography confirmed, bilateral breath sounds, ETT depth appropriate (21-25 cm), ETT secured
  • Immediate care: sedation/analgesia initiated, ventilator settings adjusted, OG/NG tube placed if indicated, CXR ordered for position confirmation
  • Documentation: technique/attempts/view, ETT size and depth, medications used, complications if any
  • Disposition: ICU admission for ongoing ventilation, notify the receiving team, consider early extubation planning if appropriate

STOP attempting to advance, withdraw the scope slightly, give more lidocaine to the cords if possible. If persistent, give the drawn paralytic: rocuronium 1.2 mg/kg or succinylcholine 1.5 mg/kg.

  • Rotate the ETT 90° counterclockwise — most effective; turns the bevel posteriorly, slides past the arytenoids
  • Withdraw and re-advance: pull back 1-2 cm, re-advance with rotation
  • Jaw thrust by the VL operator to open the glottic inlet
  • Try a smaller ETT (6.5 or 6.0) — easier passage, can upsize later

If the tube is hitting the carina or right mainstem: withdraw the scope/tube as a unit, reposition with the scope just above the carina. Do NOT force — forceful advancement can cause tracheal injury.

  • STOP the procedure
  • Remove the FE and VL
  • BVM ventilate with 100% O2
  • Reassess airway patency

If oxygenating: allow recovery to SpO2 >95%, consider apneic oxygenation (nasal cannula 15 L/min), reattempt when stable. If cannot ventilate: reposition, two-person BVM, place a supraglottic airway (LMA/iGel), consider FE intubation through the SGA. If cannot oxygenate: CICO → surgical airway (cricothyrotomy).

Intubation successful — post-procedure

  • Confirmation: waveform capnography confirmed, bilateral breath sounds, ETT depth appropriate (21-25 cm), ETT secured
  • Immediate care: sedation/analgesia initiated, ventilator settings adjusted, OG/NG tube placed if indicated, CXR ordered for position confirmation
  • Documentation: technique/attempts/view, ETT size and depth, medications used, complications if any
  • Disposition: ICU admission for ongoing ventilation, notify the receiving team, consider early extubation planning if appropriate
