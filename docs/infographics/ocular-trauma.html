Ocular Trauma: Irrigate, Shield, Decompress, Classify

Adults and children presenting to the ED with any acute eye trauma — chemical, open-globe/penetrating, blunt/closed-globe, lid/cornea/superficial FB, or pediatric mechanisms. Excludes non-traumatic ophthalmologic emergencies (acute angle-closure glaucoma, central retinal artery occlusion, endophthalmitis without trauma) and post-surgical eye complications.

⚠ Suspected open globe: rigid Fox shield, NPO, IV vancomycin + ceftazidime, and NO pressure on the globe — no tonometry, no lid retraction, no eye drops, no Seidel test, no ultrasound.
⚠ Chemical burn: IRRIGATE AT TRIAGE before any other exam — minimum 1-2 L LR/NS until pH 7.0-7.4 sustained for 5 minutes off irrigation. Alkali burns may need 4-6 L+.
⚠ Orbital compartment syndrome (proptosis + IOP >40 + vision loss): lateral canthotomy + cantholysis NOW — do NOT wait for CT, ophthalmology, or transfer. 90-120 minute vision-loss window; this is a bedside ED procedure.
⚠ Hyphema: screen Black, Mediterranean, Middle Eastern, or Indian patients for sickle trait/disease. Sickle hyphema = AVOID carbonic anhydrase inhibitors (acetazolamide, methazolamide, dorzolamide) and mannitol — both worsen sickling in the anterior chamber.
⚠ Pediatric blunt orbital trauma + persistent N/V or limited up-gaze → rule out white-eyed blowout fracture (WEBOF) with entrapment — surgical emergency, often with minimal external findings. Surgical repair within 24-48 hr to prevent muscle fibrosis.
⚠ Pediatric multilayered, multiquadrant retinal hemorrhages extending to the ora serrata, without a credible accidental mechanism = abusive head trauma until proven otherwise — mandatory ophthalmology dilated exam and child protection consult.
⚠ NEVER give succinylcholine in a confirmed or suspected open globe — the fasciculations raise IOP and can extrude contents; use rocuronium.
⚠ NEVER order MRI if a metallic intraocular foreign body is possible — CT first; a magnetic IOFB will move and shred intraocular tissue.
⚠ NEVER patch a contact-lens-related corneal abrasion — Pseudomonas infection risk (Cochrane).
⚠ NEVER discharge a patient with topical anesthetic (tetracaine/proparacaine) for home use — corneal melt, ring infiltrate, and vision loss are documented; ACEP 2024 reaffirmed this.
⚠ Document visual acuity (best-corrected if possible) on every patient, every visit — it is the medicolegal anchor and the required input for OTS prognostic scoring.
⚠ Never prescribe a topical steroid drop without ophthalmology approval — appropriate only as an ED bridge when ophthalmology access is delayed.

1 · Triage & Universal Rules

Ocular trauma ranges from corneal abrasion (benign) to open globe (vision-threatening, time-critical). The first 90 seconds of triage drive everything — identify the dominant injury pattern before any exam maneuver that could pressurize the eye.[1][2][3]

#Never-do
1Tonometry (Tono-Pen, Goldmann) if open globe suspected
2Succinylcholine in confirmed/suspected open globe — use rocuronium
3MRI if metallic IOFB possible — CT first
4Carbonic anhydrase inhibitor (acetazolamide/methazolamide) or mannitol in sickle hyphema
5Patch a contact-lens-related corneal abrasion — Pseudomonas risk
6Discharge a patient with topical tetracaine/proparacaine for home use
7Prescribe topical steroid drops without ophthalmology approval
8Delay irrigation for chemical burn — irrigate at triage, exam later
Document visual acuity on every patient
Standard sequence: Snellen at 20 ft (best-corrected if possible) → walk closer → count fingers → hand motion → light perception (with projection) → no light perception. Pinhole improves a refractive cause. VA is the medicolegal anchor and the required OTS input — do not skip it even if the patient is uncomfortable; topical anesthetic for the exam only is appropriate.[4]

2 · Chemical Burns — Irrigate First

  1. Topical anesthetic (proparacaine) — single drop, allows tolerance of irrigation.
  2. Place Morgan lens (or wide-bore IV tubing) — both eyes if bilateral.
  3. Irrigate with 1-2 L LR or NS minimum (alkali burns may need 4-6 L+).
  4. Sweep upper and lower fornices to remove particulate (cement, lime).
  5. Continue until pH 7.0-7.4 sustained for 5 minutes after irrigation stopped.
  6. Recheck pH at 5 and 30 min off irrigation — alkali rebounds.
Alkali penetrates deeper than acid
Alkali (lye, ammonia, drain cleaner, cement, fertilizer) saponifies cell membranes and tracks into the anterior chamber, damaging limbal stem cells and intraocular structures — can perforate. Acid coagulates proteins, forming a barrier (except hydrofluoric acid, which behaves like alkali). After irrigation: document VA, slit lamp with fluorescein, assess limbal ischemia (white "porcelain" limbus) — the single most important prognostic finding.

3 · Open Globe / IOFB / Penetrating

Findings that strongly suggest open globe: peaked/teardrop/eccentric pupil, visible full-thickness laceration, prolapsed uvea, 360° subconjunctival hemorrhage + chemosis, hyphema with poor fundus view, severe vision loss out of proportion to exam, or high-velocity mechanism (grinding metal, lawn mower, BB, nail gun). CT orbits without contrast (1 mm cuts, axial + coronal) looks for globe deformity, intraocular air, and IOFB. MRI is contraindicated until a metallic IOFB is excluded.

Exam rules — open globe vs notdo/don't

If open globe suspected: no tonometry, no lid retraction, no eye drops, no Seidel test, no ultrasound, no MRI. Rigid Fox shield, NPO, IV vancomycin + ceftazidime, antiemetic, CT orbits without contrast, ophthalmology to OR.

If open globe NOT suspected: slit lamp with fluorescein (Seidel test, abrasion mapping), tonometry (defer if any doubt), dilated fundus exam, lid eversion if FB sensation persists.

  • Rigid Fox shield (NOT a pressure patch — pressure extrudes contents).
  • NPO — assume OR within hours.
  • NO eye drops of any kind (cycloplegics, abx, dilators).
  • IV antibiotics: vancomycin 15-20 mg/kg + ceftazidime 1-2 g (covers Bacillus cereus, gram-negatives).
  • Tetanus update if indicated.
  • Antiemetic (ondansetron 4-8 mg IV) — vomiting → Valsalva → extrusion.
  • Pain control with IV opioid (fentanyl).
  • Head of bed 30°; strict bedrest, no straining; dim lights.
  • CT orbits without contrast, 1 mm cuts.
  • Ophthalmology to OR — goal <24 hr from injury.
  • If intubation needed: rocuronium, NOT succinylcholine.

4 · Closed-Globe Blunt Trauma

Open globe excluded. Sort by dominant finding: hyphema, orbital compartment syndrome, orbital wall fracture, pediatric WEBOF, or other posterior-segment/anterior findings.

Standard management (non-sickle)
Bedrest with HOB 30-45°, rigid Fox shield, topical cycloplegic (atropine 1% BID), topical steroid (prednisolone acetate 1% q2-4h), antifibrinolytic (TXA or aminocaproic acid — dosing below), avoid ASA/NSAIDs, daily ophthalmology follow-up. IOP target <24 mmHg. Surgical AC washout threshold: IOP >50 ×5 days or >35 ×7 days or ≥80% AC. Re-bleed risk highest day 2-5.
Sickle hyphema — its own emergency
AC has low pH/pO2 → sickled RBCs → trabecular blockade → IOP spike → optic nerve ischemia. AVOID carbonic anhydrase inhibitors (acetazolamide, methazolamide, dorzolamide, brinzolamide) and mannitol — both worsen sickling. Use timolol 0.5% BID, brimonidine 0.2% TID, or prostaglandin analogs (latanoprost qHS if no significant inflammation) for IOP control instead. Surgical AC-washout threshold is lower: IOP >24 ×24 hours or any optic nerve compromise — vs. IOP >50 ×5 days in non-sickle patients. Confirm with sickle prep + Hb electrophoresis (prep alone has false negatives in trait).

  1. Anesthetize — 1-2% lidocaine with epi SC into lateral canthus (or proceed dry if catastrophic).
  2. Hemostat crush — clamp lateral canthus horizontally 60-90 sec.
  3. Canthotomy — straight scissors, horizontal cut lateral canthus to orbital rim (~1-1.5 cm).
  4. Inferior cantholysis — strum and cut the inferior crus of the lateral canthal tendon. This is the actual decompression; canthotomy alone is inadequate. Add superior cantholysis if IOP not relieved.
  5. Check IOP after — should drop to <30. If not, extend cantholysis.
  6. After: topical abx ointment, urgent ophthalmology/oculoplastics, ice, reverse anticoagulation if applicable, CT orbits to confirm cause.

Diagnosis: any 2 of proptosis, IOP >40 mmHg, vision loss/RAPD, resistance to retropulsion, severe orbital pain, limited/absent EOM. Common causes: retrobulbar hemorrhage post-trauma, orbital cellulitis with abscess, anticoagulation + minor trauma.

CT orbits without contrast (1 mm coronal + axial) is gold standard. Document VA, EOM, infraorbital (V2) sensation; check for entrapment and retrobulbar hemorrhage. Nasal precautions (no nose-blowing — orbital emphysema risk). Amoxicillin-clavulanate 875 mg PO BID ×7-10 days if treating for sinus communication (institution-dependent). Ice + HOB 30°. Oculoplastics/ENT follow-up 7-10 days; surgical indications include entrapment with positive forced duction, diplopia >2 weeks, large (>50%) floor fracture.

WEBOF and pediatric AHT — both surgical/child-protection emergencies2 distinct patterns
White-eyed blowout fracture (WEBOF)
Pediatric trapdoor orbital-floor fracture with inferior rectus entrapment — bone springs back, minimal external signs ("white eye"). Triad: restricted up-gaze + persistent N/V (oculocardiac reflex) + bradycardia. CT orbits 1 mm coronal cuts looking for trapdoor fragment or "missing rectus" sign — often a subtle finding, not a large fracture line. Management: antiemetics, HOB 30°, ice, oculoplastics same-day, surgical repair within 24-48 hr to prevent muscle fibrosis.
Pediatric abusive head trauma (AHT) — eye findings
Multilayered, multiquadrant retinal hemorrhages extending to the ora serrata, without a credible accidental mechanism, = AHT until proven otherwise. Macular schisis is highly specific for shaking. Workup: mandatory dilated fundus exam by ophthalmology, CT/MRI brain, skeletal survey if <2 yo, coagulation studies, and child protection team consult per institution.
Other closed-globe findings — posterior segment, iritis, subconjunctival hemorrhage, normal exam4 patterns

Posterior segment: vitreous hemorrhage (floaters, dim red reflex, B-scan only if globe closed) — usually self-clears in weeks. Retinal detachment (flashing lights, curtain, RAPD if macula off) — surgical emergency. Commotio retinae (gray-white opacification 24-48h) — usually self-limited. Choroidal rupture — late CNV risk. Traumatic optic neuropathy (vision loss + RAPD, no visible injury) — CT to rule out optic canal fracture; steroids controversial (CRASH trial).

Traumatic iritis: anterior uveitis peaking 24-72h post-injury. Consensual photophobia is pathognomonic. Treat with cycloplegic + topical steroid (ophtho input ideal) + oral analgesic + sunglasses + ophtho follow-up 24-48h. Red flags for urgent referral: hypopyon, significant IOP elevation, worsening at 48h.

Subconjunctival hemorrhage (isolated): painless, normal VA, sectorial, sclera visible posteriorly → reassurance + tears, resolves 1-2 weeks. Concerning features (360° hemorrhage + chemosis, bullous, pain out of proportion, decreased VA, high-velocity mechanism) → work up as possible occult open globe.

Globe structurally normal: discharge with cool-then-warm compresses, NSAID/acetaminophen, HOB elevation 48h, eye protection for sport, return precautions (vision change, new floaters/flashes, severe pain, photophobia, diplopia).

5 · Lid / Cornea / Superficial FB

ED can repair: superficial lacs lateral to the medial canthus, parallel to the lid margin, not involving the margin, levator, canalicular system, or tarsal plate. Technique: 6-0 nylon or fast-absorbing gut, lidocaine 1% with epi (NOT bupivacaine within 2 mm of the lid margin), tetanus update, erythromycin ointment BID, sutures out 5-7 days.

Oculoplastics required: lid margin involvement, medial 1/3 (canalicular system — needs silicone tube intubation), levator involvement (post-septal fat visible, new ptosis), tarsal plate full-thickness, tissue avulsion, through-and-through with conjunctival involvement. Always assess the globe before/after closure — a lid lac may hide an open globe.

Corneal abrasion, FB removal, and UV keratitis3 patterns
Corneal abrasion
Fluorescein uptake under cobalt blue; "ice rink sign" = subtarsal FB, evert the lid.

Non-contact-lens: topical abx (erythromycin QID or polymyxin/trimethoprim QID) + oral analgesic + topical NSAID (ketorolac 0.5% QID, max 24-48h) + cycloplegic only if photophobic. Follow-up 24h if large, 48-72h if small.

Contact-lens wearer (Pseudomonas risk): moxifloxacin 0.5% q2h ×24h then QID, or ciprofloxacin 0.3% q1-2h. Do NOT patch. Discontinue contacts, mandatory 24h follow-up.

Never discharge with topical anesthetic (tetracaine/proparacaine) for home use — corneal melt, ring infiltrate, vision loss documented.

FB removal: ED candidates are superficial corneal/conjunctival, single-piece, accessible. Refer if penetrating/questionable depth, central visual axis, multiple FBs, or a rust ring you can't finish. Technique: irrigation → moistened swab → 27-30G needle tangential approach on slit lamp (bevel up, scoop motion). Never use a needle without a slit lamp. Re-stain after removal, tetanus update, 24-48h follow-up if rust ring or central cornea.

UV/welder's flash keratitis: bilateral, painful, photophobic, 6-12h after exposure. Diffuse punctate epithelial keratitis on fluorescein. Treat with topical abx + cycloplegic + oral analgesic + cool compresses; resolves 24-72h. Same never-tetracaine-home rule applies.

6 · Pediatric-Specific Patterns

Children are not small adults in ocular trauma — three patterns to know:

1. White-eyed blowout fracture — see Blunt Trauma section.

2. Abusive head trauma eye findings — see Blunt Trauma section.

3. Cooperation challenges — distraction, gentle papoose, parent-on-lap exam. Topical anesthetic single-use for exam only. Sedation only with anesthesia + ophtho support, NOT PO sedation in an unstable airway. Exam under anesthesia if exam impossible and concern persists.

Other peds patterns: toy projectiles (bow/arrow, BB, paintball) carry a high IOFB rate; bungee cords carry a high open-globe rate. Always ask about timing, mechanism, witnesses, eye protection.

Admit / OR / transfer
Admit: open globe → OR, OCS post-cantholysis monitoring, severe chemical burn, hyphema in sickle disease or large grade III-IV, pediatric AHT (+ child protection), endophthalmitis, pediatric WEBOF. Transfer: cantholyze first if OCS unrelieved; irrigate first if chemical burn unirrigated — never transfer either unstabilized. 9 · Do NOT

Critical pitfalls (also see the persistent warnings above)4 items
Do NOT pressurize a suspected open globe — no tonometry, no lid retraction, no ultrasound, no eye drops, no Seidel test.
Do NOT delay chemical-burn irrigation for history or exam — irrigate first, always.
Do NOT wait for CT, ophthalmology, or transfer to perform lateral canthotomy + cantholysis in orbital compartment syndrome — the vision-loss window is 90-120 minutes.
Do NOT miss pediatric WEBOF — minimal external findings with restricted up-gaze + persistent N/V + bradycardia is a surgical emergency, not a benign contusion.

  • IV antibiotics: vancomycin 15-20 mg/kg + ceftazidime 1-2 g (covers Bacillus cereus, gram-negatives).
  • Tetanus update if indicated.
  • Antiemetic (ondansetron 4-8 mg IV) — vomiting → Valsalva → extrusion.
  • Pain control with IV opioid (fentanyl).
  • Head of bed 30°; strict bedrest, no straining; dim lights.
  • CT orbits without contrast, 1 mm cuts.
  • Ophthalmology to OR — goal <24 hr from injury.
  • If intubation needed: rocuronium, NOT succinylcholine.

Vancomycin (open-globe prophylaxis) — low end: 15 mg/kg IV IV — = computed low-end dose at current weight. Combine with ceftazidime.

Vancomycin (open-globe prophylaxis) — high end: 20 mg/kg IV IV — = computed high-end dose at current weight.

Ceftazidime (open-globe prophylaxis): 1-2 g IV IV — Flat adult dose in this consult — not weight-based.

Ondansetron (antiemetic): 4-8 mg IV IV — Prevents Valsalva/extrusion from vomiting.

Material | Concern

Metallic (most common) | High siderosis risk if iron-containing; magnetic vs non-magnetic guides extraction

Glass / inert | May be left in place if posterior and visually quiet

Organic (wood, plant) | High endophthalmitis risk — urgent removal

Copper | Sterile chalcosis, severe inflammation

CT orbits without contrast, thin cuts (1 mm) is primary imaging. B-scan US only if globe confirmed closed. MRI contraindicated until metallic IOFB excluded. IOFB endophthalmitis rates run ~10-30% without antibiotics, <5% with timely surgery + antibiotics.

OTS — Ocular Trauma Score

Kuhn 2002 prognostic tool: sum initial VA score (NLP=60, LP-HM=70, 1/200-19/200=80, 20/200-20/50=90, ≥20/40=100) minus penalties for globe rupture (-23), endophthalmitis (-17), perforating injury (-14), retinal detachment (-11), afferent pupillary defect (-10).

OTS category (sum) | % NLP at 6 mo | % ≥20/40 at 6 mo

1 (≤44) | 74% | —

2 (45-65) | 28% | —

3 (66-80) | 2% | 11%

4 (81-91) | — | 41%

5 (92-100) | — | 94%

Use for prognostic counseling only, not triage — shield + OR are warranted regardless of OTS.

4 · Closed-Globe Blunt Trauma

Open globe excluded. Sort by dominant finding: hyphema, orbital compartment syndrome, orbital wall fracture, pediatric WEBOF, or other posterior-segment/anterior findings.

Grade | Anterior-chamber blood

Microhyphema | RBCs only, no layered blood

I | <1/3 AC layered

II | 1/3-1/2 AC layered

III | >1/2 but not full

IV ("eight-ball") | Full AC — worst prognosis

Bedrest with HOB 30-45°, rigid Fox shield, topical cycloplegic (atropine 1% BID), topical steroid (prednisolone acetate 1% q2-4h), antifibrinolytic (TXA or aminocaproic acid — dosing below), avoid ASA/NSAIDs, daily ophthalmology follow-up. IOP target <24 mmHg. Surgical AC washout threshold: IOP >50 ×5 days or >35 ×7 days or ≥80% AC. Re-bleed risk highest day 2-5.

AC has low pH/pO2 → sickled RBCs → trabecular blockade → IOP spike → optic nerve ischemia. AVOID carbonic anhydrase inhibitors (acetazolamide, methazolamide, dorzolamide, brinzolamide) and mannitol — both worsen sickling. Use timolol 0.5% BID, brimonidine 0.2% TID, or prostaglandin analogs (latanoprost qHS if no significant inflammation) for IOP control instead. Surgical AC-washout threshold is lower: IOP >24 ×24 hours or any optic nerve compromise — vs. IOP >50 ×5 days in non-sickle patients. Confirm with sickle prep + Hb electrophoresis (prep alone has false negatives in trait).

Tranexamic acid (hyphema): 25 mg/kg PO TID PO — = computed dose at current weight. Reduces re-bleed (~30% incidence day 2-5).

Aminocaproic acid (Amicar) — alternative to TXA (hyphema): 50 mg/kg PO q4h × 5 days PO — = computed per-dose amount at current weight.

  • Anesthetize — 1-2% lidocaine with epi SC into lateral canthus (or proceed dry if catastrophic).
  • Hemostat crush — clamp lateral canthus horizontally 60-90 sec.
  • Canthotomy — straight scissors, horizontal cut lateral canthus to orbital rim (~1-1.5 cm).
  • Inferior cantholysis — strum and cut the inferior crus of the lateral canthal tendon. This is the actual decompression; canthotomy alone is inadequate. Add superior cantholysis if IOP not relieved.
  • Check IOP after — should drop to <30. If not, extend cantholysis.
  • After: topical abx ointment, urgent ophthalmology/oculoplastics, ice, reverse anticoagulation if applicable, CT orbits to confirm cause.

Diagnosis: any 2 of proptosis, IOP >40 mmHg, vision loss/RAPD, resistance to retropulsion, severe orbital pain, limited/absent EOM. Common causes: retrobulbar hemorrhage post-trauma, orbital cellulitis with abscess, anticoagulation + minor trauma.

CT orbits without contrast (1 mm coronal + axial) is gold standard. Document VA, EOM, infraorbital (V2) sensation; check for entrapment and retrobulbar hemorrhage. Nasal precautions (no nose-blowing — orbital emphysema risk). Amoxicillin-clavulanate 875 mg PO BID ×7-10 days if treating for sinus communication (institution-dependent). Ice + HOB 30°. Oculoplastics/ENT follow-up 7-10 days; surgical indications include entrapment with positive forced duction, diplopia >2 weeks, large (>50%) floor fracture.

WEBOF and pediatric AHT — both surgical/child-protection emergencies

Pediatric trapdoor orbital-floor fracture with inferior rectus entrapment — bone springs back, minimal external signs ("white eye"). Triad: restricted up-gaze + persistent N/V (oculocardiac reflex) + bradycardia. CT orbits 1 mm coronal cuts looking for trapdoor fragment or "missing rectus" sign — often a subtle finding, not a large fracture line. Management: antiemetics, HOB 30°, ice, oculoplastics same-day, surgical repair within 24-48 hr to prevent muscle fibrosis.

Multilayered, multiquadrant retinal hemorrhages extending to the ora serrata, without a credible accidental mechanism, = AHT until proven otherwise. Macular schisis is highly specific for shaking. Workup: mandatory dilated fundus exam by ophthalmology, CT/MRI brain, skeletal survey if <2 yo, coagulation studies, and child protection team consult per institution.

Other closed-globe findings — posterior segment, iritis, subconjunctival hemorrhage, normal exam

Posterior segment: vitreous hemorrhage (floaters, dim red reflex, B-scan only if globe closed) — usually self-clears in weeks. Retinal detachment (flashing lights, curtain, RAPD if macula off) — surgical emergency. Commotio retinae (gray-white opacification 24-48h) — usually self-limited. Choroidal rupture — late CNV risk. Traumatic optic neuropathy (vision loss + RAPD, no visible injury) — CT to rule out optic canal fracture; steroids controversial (CRASH trial).

Traumatic iritis: anterior uveitis peaking 24-72h post-injury. Consensual photophobia is pathognomonic. Treat with cycloplegic + topical steroid (ophtho input ideal) + oral analgesic + sunglasses + ophtho follow-up 24-48h. Red flags for urgent referral: hypopyon, significant IOP elevation, worsening at 48h.

Subconjunctival hemorrhage (isolated): painless, normal VA, sectorial, sclera visible posteriorly → reassurance + tears, resolves 1-2 weeks. Concerning features (360° hemorrhage + chemosis, bullous, pain out of proportion, decreased VA, high-velocity mechanism) → work up as possible occult open globe.

Globe structurally normal: discharge with cool-then-warm compresses, NSAID/acetaminophen, HOB elevation 48h, eye protection for sport, return precautions (vision change, new floaters/flashes, severe pain, photophobia, diplopia).

5 · Lid / Cornea / Superficial FB

ED can repair: superficial lacs lateral to the medial canthus, parallel to the lid margin, not involving the margin, levator, canalicular system, or tarsal plate. Technique: 6-0 nylon or fast-absorbing gut, lidocaine 1% with epi (NOT bupivacaine within 2 mm of the lid margin), tetanus update, erythromycin ointment BID, sutures out 5-7 days.

Oculoplastics required: lid margin involvement, medial 1/3 (canalicular system — needs silicone tube intubation), levator involvement (post-septal fat visible, new ptosis), tarsal plate full-thickness, tissue avulsion, through-and-through with conjunctival involvement. Always assess the globe before/after closure — a lid lac may hide an open globe.

Corneal abrasion, FB removal, and UV keratitis

Fluorescein uptake under cobalt blue; "ice rink sign" = subtarsal FB, evert the lid.

Non-contact-lens: topical abx (erythromycin QID or polymyxin/trimethoprim QID) + oral analgesic + topical NSAID (ketorolac 0.5% QID, max 24-48h) + cycloplegic only if photophobic. Follow-up 24h if large, 48-72h if small.

Contact-lens wearer (Pseudomonas risk): moxifloxacin 0.5% q2h ×24h then QID, or ciprofloxacin 0.3% q1-2h. Do NOT patch. Discontinue contacts, mandatory 24h follow-up.

Never discharge with topical anesthetic (tetracaine/proparacaine) for home use — corneal melt, ring infiltrate, vision loss documented.

FB removal: ED candidates are superficial corneal/conjunctival, single-piece, accessible. Refer if penetrating/questionable depth, central visual axis, multiple FBs, or a rust ring you can't finish. Technique: irrigation → moistened swab → 27-30G needle tangential approach on slit lamp (bevel up, scoop motion). Never use a needle without a slit lamp. Re-stain after removal, tetanus update, 24-48h follow-up if rust ring or central cornea.

UV/welder's flash keratitis: bilateral, painful, photophobic, 6-12h after exposure. Diffuse punctate epithelial keratitis on fluorescein. Treat with topical abx + cycloplegic + oral analgesic + cool compresses; resolves 24-72h. Same never-tetracaine-home rule applies.

6 · Pediatric-Specific Patterns

Children are not small adults in ocular trauma — three patterns to know:

1. White-eyed blowout fracture — see Blunt Trauma section.

2. Abusive head trauma eye findings — see Blunt Trauma section.

3. Cooperation challenges — distraction, gentle papoose, parent-on-lap exam. Topical anesthetic single-use for exam only. Sedation only with anesthesia + ophtho support, NOT PO sedation in an unstable airway. Exam under anesthesia if exam impossible and concern persists.

Other peds patterns: toy projectiles (bow/arrow, BB, paintball) carry a high IOFB rate; bungee cords carry a high open-globe rate. Always ask about timing, mechanism, witnesses, eye protection.

7 · One-glance Algorithm

Ocular trauma — dominant injury pattern? — The first 90 seconds of triage drive everything. Eight Never-Dos apply across every branch.

Chemical splash/burn — IRRIGATE NOW — Irrigate BEFORE VA, BEFORE history, BEFORE exam. Time-to-irrigation is the strongest predictor of outcome.

Open globe / penetrating injury suspected? — Peaked/teardrop pupil, full-thickness laceration, prolapsed uvea, 360° SCH+chemosis, hyphema with poor view, out-of-proportion vision loss, or high-velocity mechanism.

Blunt/closed-globe trauma — sub-triage — Open globe excluded. Sort by dominant finding.

Lid laceration / superficial cornea / FB sensation — Sub-triage by injury type.

Pediatric trauma (any mechanism) — Children are not small adults — WEBOF and AHT patterns are easy to miss.

Stage severity (Roper-Hall / Dua) — Key driver: limbal-ischemia clock-hours.

Open globe confirmed → management bundle — Rigid Fox shield, NPO, no drops, no tono, no lid retraction, no US, no MRI until metal excluded. IV vanc + ceftaz. Rocuronium not sux.

IOFB on imaging — CT thin cuts; B-scan only if globe confirmed closed; MRI contraindicated until metal excluded. Organic FB = highest endophthalmitis risk.

Hyphema (blood in anterior chamber) — Grade the hyphema. Sickle screen every Black/Mediterranean/Middle Eastern/Indian patient.

Orbital compartment syndrome → cantholysis NOW — Proptosis + IOP >40 + vision loss (any 2 of 6 signs). 4-step lateral canthotomy + inferior cantholysis at bedside.

Orbital wall fracture suspected — CT 1mm coronal + axial. Check entrapment, V2 sensation, retrobulbar hemorrhage.

Posterior segment / iritis / SCH / normal globe — Vitreous heme, retinal detachment, commotio retinae, choroidal rupture, traumatic optic neuropathy, traumatic iritis, subconjunctival hemorrhage, or normal exam.

Lid laceration — repair zones — ED can repair superficial lateral lacs. Oculoplastics for margin, medial 1/3 (canaliculus), levator, tarsus full-thickness, avulsion.

Corneal abrasion / FB / UV keratitis — Contact-lens wearer = fluoroquinolone, NEVER patch. NEVER discharge with topical anesthetic.

WEBOF or AHT concern — White-eyed blowout fracture (up-gaze restriction + N/V + bradycardia) or abusive head trauma (multilayered retinal hemorrhage to ora serrata).

Mild/moderate burn — outpatient bundle — Roper-Hall I-III / Dua I-IV. Escalating medication bundle by grade.

Severe burn — admit, multi-specialty — Roper-Hall III-IV / Dua V-VI. AMG/PROKERA, oculoplastics, guarded prognosis.

8 · Disposition & Consult Tiers

Tier | Conditions

1 — Immediate (page now) | Open globe/IOFB → OR; orbital compartment syndrome → cantholysis now; severe chemical burn; pediatric WEBOF; retinal detachment (macula off/imminent); endophthalmitis; severe lid avulsion/canalicular injury; pediatric AHT eye findings

2 — Same-day (~24h) | Hyphema (any grade); moderate chemical burn; traumatic iritis; lid lac with margin/levator involvement; posterior segment findings

3 — Outpatient (24-72h) | Mild corneal abrasion (contact lens = mandatory 24h); superficial ED-closed lid lac; subconjunctival hemorrhage with concerning features; persistent UV keratitis; orbital fracture without entrapment

  • Specific diagnosis and grade/severity, current VA each eye.
  • Medications: name, dose, frequency, duration, technique (drops vs ointment).
  • Eye shield instructions (sleep with shield ×24h after blunt trauma even if minor).
  • HOB elevation 30° ×48h if hyphema or contusion.
  • Activity restrictions (no contact sports/heavy lifting if hyphema; no swimming with abrasion).
  • Return precautions: new/worsening vision loss, uncontrolled pain, new floaters/flashes, curtain across vision, new diplopia, increasing redness/discharge/fever, hyphema re-bleed.
  • Specific follow-up (who/where/when) and patient teach-back.
  • Lower threshold to admit if anticoagulated with any orbital/intraocular bleed, or sickle cell + hyphema (admit).

Admit: open globe → OR, OCS post-cantholysis monitoring, severe chemical burn, hyphema in sickle disease or large grade III-IV, pediatric AHT (+ child protection), endophthalmitis, pediatric WEBOF. Transfer: cantholyze first if OCS unrelieved; irrigate first if chemical burn unirrigated — never transfer either unstabilized. Provide imaging, last VA/IOP/meds, eye shield in place, antiemetic on board for open globe.

9 · Do NOT

Critical pitfalls (also see the persistent warnings above)

Do NOT pressurize a suspected open globe — no tonometry, no lid retraction, no ultrasound, no eye drops, no Seidel test.

Do NOT delay chemical-burn irrigation for history or exam — irrigate first, always.

Do NOT wait for CT, ophthalmology, or transfer to perform lateral canthotomy + cantholysis in orbital compartment syndrome — the vision-loss window is 90-120 minutes.

Do NOT miss pediatric WEBOF — minimal external findings with restricted up-gaze + persistent N/V + bradycardia is a surgical emergency, not a benign contusion.
