Hypertension in Pregnancy — ED Management

Pregnant and up-to-6-weeks-postpartum patients ≥20 weeks gestation (or postpartum) presenting with hypertension, in the ED/L&D setting. Excludes active eclamptic seizure (see the Eclampsia consult) and chronic HTN diagnosed/managed purely outpatient without acute presentation.

⚠ Severe-range BP (≥160 SBP OR ≥110 DBP) sustained ≥15 min → treat within 30–60 min. Stroke, not seizure, is the leading cause of maternal death from preeclampsia.
⚠ First-line severe-range options: IV labetalol, IV hydralazine, or PO nifedipine IR — do not stack full doses of two agents in parallel.
⚠ Active seizure → go directly to the Eclampsia consult; start magnesium load and treat severe BP in parallel while navigating there.
⚠ Magnesium sulfate for seizure prophylaxis in preeclampsia with severe features — 4–6 g IV load, 1–2 g/hr maintenance through 24 h postpartum. NOT routinely given for preeclampsia without severe features.
⚠ Magnesium toxicity antidote: calcium gluconate 1 g (10 mL of 10%) IV over 3–5 min. Stop the magnesium infusion and support ventilation.
⚠ Refractory severe hypertension (≥2 first-line agents at max dose) is a strong indication for delivery — bridge with a continuous infusion, do not delay obstetric involvement.
⚠ HELLP: 15–20% present WITHOUT hypertension or proteinuria — do not anchor on normal BP to exclude it. Watch for hepatic subcapsular hematoma (mortality 60–80%) with RUQ pain + falling Hgb.
⚠ Severe features are defined WITHOUT requiring proteinuria (~25% of severe preeclampsia has none) — do not withhold the severe-features diagnosis or magnesium while awaiting a protein/creatinine ratio.
⚠ Postpartum risk persists to 6 weeks — 55% of postpartum preeclampsia occurs in patients with no antepartum preeclampsia. BP peaks 3–6 days postpartum; recheck BP 3–7 days after discharge.

▶ What to do now

Severe-range BP confirmed — treat now
Treat within 30–60 minutes. Target 140–150/90–100 mmHg — do not chase normal BP, and do not drop MAP >25% in the first hour. Choose IV labetalol, IV hydralazine, or PO nifedipine IR.
Refractory severe HTN — infusion + delivery planning
≥2 first-line agents failed at max dose. Start nicardipine (5 mg/hr, titrate 2.5 mg/hr q5–15min, max 15 mg/hr) or clevidipine (1–2 mg/hr, double q90sec, max 21 mg/hr). Refractory severe HTN is a strong indication for delivery — involve MFM/anesthesia immediately. Avoid nitroprusside (fetal cyanide toxicity >4h).
Severe features present — magnesium + expedite delivery
Control severe-range BP, start magnesium sulfate seizure prophylaxis, labs q6–12h, continuous fetal monitoring, steroids if 23–34 wk and delivery expected within 7 days. Deliver at 34 0/7 weeks after stabilization (earlier for instability).
Suspected HELLP
LDH >600, AST/ALT >2× ULN, platelets <100,000/µL. 15–20% present without HTN or proteinuria. Transfuse platelets <20K regardless, or <50K if bleeding/cesarean planned. Delivery is the only definitive treatment.
No severe features — classify and schedule delivery
Chronic HTN, gestational HTN, preeclampsia without severe features, and superimposed preeclampsia without severe features are managed with oral antihypertensives (target <140/90) and scheduled delivery at 37–39 weeks depending on category — magnesium is NOT routinely given.

Management algorithm

Hypertension in Pregnancy — Recognition — ~10% of pregnancies. Non-severe SBP 140–159/DBP 90–109 ×2 ≥4h apart; severe-range SBP≥160/DBP≥110. Preeclampsia = HTN≥20wk + proteinuria or end-organ. Severe features (any one) override subtype: severe-range BP, plt<100K, LFTs>2×ULN, Cr>1.1 or doubled, pulm edema, new HA/visual sx — no proteinuria required (~25% severe PE has none).

Presenting scenario? — Triage: active seizure, severe-range BP, non-severe HTN needing workup, postpartum HTN, or a future-pregnancy aspirin-prophylaxis question.

Active seizure → Eclampsia consult — Active eclamptic seizure management lives in the dedicated Eclampsia consult. Start magnesium load 4–6 g IV over 15–20 min and treat severe-range BP in parallel while navigating there.

Severe-range BP — act within 30–60 min — Severe-range = SBP≥160 or DBP≥110, persistent ≥15 min. Target 140–150/90–100 mmHg. Do not drop MAP >25% in first hour. Stroke (not seizure) is the #1 preeclampsia death cause.

Workup: labs + fetal assessment — CBC+smear, Cr, AST/ALT/LDH, uric acid, UA+protein/Cr, T&S. Continuous EFM ≥23–24wk, growth/AFI/UA-Doppler US. Betamethasone/dexamethasone if 23–34wk and delivery expected ≤7 days. Do not require proteinuria to diagnose severe PE.

Postpartum hypertension — Most maternal PE deaths occur postpartum. BP peaks 3–6 days postpartum. 55% of postpartum PE has no antepartum PE. Risk window to 6 weeks. Same acute BP protocol; continue magnesium ×24h if severe features. BP recheck 3–7 days postpartum.

Choose first-line severe-BP agent — All three are ACOG-endorsed first-line: IV labetalol, IV hydralazine, PO nifedipine IR. Choose by access, contraindications, prior response.

Labetalol 20→40→80→80 mg IV q10min (max 300 mg) — 20 mg IV → wait 10 min → 40 mg → wait 10 min → 80 mg → wait 10 min → 80 mg. Max cumulative 300 mg. Infusion alt 1–2 mg/min. Avoid in asthma, bradycardia <60, HF, heart block >1°.

Hydralazine 5–10 mg IV q20min (max 30 mg) — 5–10 mg IV → wait 20 min → 10 mg → wait 20 min → switch to labetalol 20 mg IV. Max cumulative 30 mg. Pre-load 500 mL NS if volume-depleted. Reflex tachycardia; avoid in CAD.

Nifedipine IR 10→20→20 mg PO q20min (max 50 mg) — 10 mg PO → wait 20 min → 20 mg → wait 20 min → 20 mg → switch to labetalol IV. Max 50 mg short-term. NEVER sublingual. Safe with magnesium.

BP response after escalation? — Controlled (<160/110) → transition to maintenance. Still severe at max dose → switch agent. Refractory (≥2 agents failed) → continuous infusion.

Switch agent (labetalol ↔ hydralazine ↔ nifedipine) — Do not stack full doses of two agents in parallel. If BP drops <140/90 suddenly: stop infusion, left lateral position, 250–500 mL NS cautiously, watch fetal tracing.

Refractory: nicardipine or clevidipine infusion — Nicardipine 5 mg/hr, titrate 2.5 mg/hr q5–15min, max 15 mg/hr. Clevidipine 1–2 mg/hr, double q90sec, max 21 mg/hr (avoid egg/soy allergy). AVOID nitroprusside (fetal cyanide toxicity >4h). Refractory = strong indication for delivery.

Classify the disorder — Severe features override everything — if present, manage as severe regardless of subtype.

Chronic / gestational / PE-no-severe / superimposed (no severe) — Oral agents (labetalol, nifedipine XL, methyldopa), target <140/90 (CHAP). Magnesium NOT routine. Deliver 37 0/7wk (gestational, PE-no-severe, superimposed-no-severe) or 38 0/7–39 6/7wk (chronic, well-controlled).

Severe-range BP protocol

Labetalol: 20 mg IV → 40 mg IV → 80 mg IV → 80 mg IV, q10min, max 300 mg cumulative IV — Fastest onset (2–5 min). Avoid in asthma, bradycardia <60, decompensated HF, heart block >1°. If uncontrolled at max dose, switch to hydralazine or start infusion 1–2 mg/min.

Hydralazine: 5–10 mg IV → 10 mg IV, q20min, max 30 mg cumulative IV — Slower onset (10–20 min), may cause reflex tachycardia. Pre-load 500 mL NS if volume-depleted. Avoid in CAD.

Nifedipine IR: 10 mg PO → 20 mg PO → 20 mg PO, q20min, max 50 mg short-term PO — Use when IV access delayed/unavailable. NEVER sublingual — unpredictable absorption. Safe with magnesium sulfate.

Nicardipine: Start 5 mg/hr IV, titrate 2.5 mg/hr q5–15min, max 15 mg/hr IV infusion — Preferred if beta-blocker contraindicated. ICU-level monitoring, arterial line preferred.

Clevidipine: Start 1–2 mg/hr IV, double every 90 sec to target, max 21 mg/hr IV infusion — Ultra-short half-life (~1 min) — most precise titration. Contraindicated in egg/soy allergy.

Fetal cyanide toxicity risk with use beyond 4 hours — avoid when possible in refractory severe hypertension.
