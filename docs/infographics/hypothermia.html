Accidental Hypothermia: Recognition, Rewarming, and Hypothermic Arrest

Adults and adolescents with accidental (environmental) hypothermia — ED, prehospital, and wilderness/transport settings. Covers recognition/classification, severity-based rewarming, and hypothermic cardiac arrest decisions. Does not cover therapeutic/induced hypothermia (targeted temperature management) protocols — the source tree explicitly distinguishes accidental from induced hypothermia and only cites induced hypothermia as a comparison data point.

⚠ "No one is dead until they are WARM and dead." Do not declare death in a hypothermic patient based on prolonged down time, fixed/dilated pupils, or muscular rigidity alone — these are NOT contraindications to resuscitation.
⚠ Hard contraindications to starting/continuing resuscitation: chest wall frozen solid and incompressible; obvious lethal injury (decapitation, truncal transection); decomposition; avalanche burial >35 min WITH airway packed with snow AND asystole; or confirmed K+ >12 mEq/L (rule out hemolysis artifact first).
⚠ Potassium thresholds gate resuscitation decisions: <10 mEq/L → proceed with aggressive resuscitation; 10-12 mEq/L → poor prognosis, calculate HOPE score; >12 mEq/L → futile, no documented survivors.
⚠ Handle hypothermic patients extremely gently below 28°C — physical manipulation/rough movement can precipitate VF ("cardiac time bomb"). Move horizontally; minimize movement during exam, transport, and airway management.
⚠ Defibrillation by core temp: <30°C → attempt a maximum of 1-3 shocks, then DEFER further attempts until rewarmed above 30°C (severely hypothermic myocardium is refractory); 30-35°C → resume standard defibrillation attempts; >35°C → standard ACLS.
⚠ Drug therapy by core temp: <30°C → WITHHOLD all vasopressors and antiarrhythmics (hepatic metabolism markedly reduced — drugs accumulate to toxic levels on rewarming); 30-35°C → DOUBLE dosing intervals (e.g., epinephrine q6-10 min, not q3-5 min); ≥35°C → standard ACLS dosing.
⚠ Transport hypothermic arrest to an ECMO center even if it is not the closest hospital. Maintain continuous CPR (mechanical CPR device preferred) through loading and transport. Apply heated blankets to the TRUNK only — do NOT rewarm extremities first; cold blood return from the extremities causes afterdrop and arrhythmias.
⚠ Terminate resuscitation only when: ROSC is not achieved AND core temp ≥32°C with persistent asystole, OR K+ is confirmed >12 mEq/L during resuscitation, OR another hard contraindication is identified. Do NOT terminate based on prolonged down time alone, fixed/dilated pupils, muscular rigidity, or multiple failed defibrillations while the patient is still cold.
⚠ VA-ECMO rewarms at 6-10°C/hour — the fastest available method. Monitor K+ frequently during rewarming (shifts back out of cells) and anticipate reperfusion injury and coagulopathy correction as temperature normalizes.

1 · Recognition & Classification

Definition: Core temperature <35°C (95°F) due to environmental exposure.

"No one is dead until they are WARM and dead."

Remarkable survivals documented:
- Lowest survivor (accidental hypothermia): 11.8°C
- Lowest survivor (induced hypothermia, comparison only): 4.0°C
- Longest CPR with intact neuro survival: 6 hours 52 minutes

Key principle: Hypothermia is PROTECTIVE if it occurs BEFORE cardiac arrest. Cold brain = reduced metabolic demand = prolonged tolerance to ischemia.

Accurate core temperature is essential — guides every decision below.

Preferred: esophageal probe (gold standard, intubated patients), rectal probe (acceptable, lags during rapid changes), bladder probe (acceptable).

Do NOT use: oral, axillary, temporal/forehead, or tympanic thermometry — all unreliable in hypothermia.

If no core temp is available, use the Swiss Staging System based on clinical signs.

  • Measure core temperature with an esophageal or rectal probe
  • Remember: "No one is dead until they are WARM and dead"
  • Continue CPR during rewarming (may need hours of CPR) if the patient is in arrest

4 · Mild hypothermia (32-35°C)

Swiss Stage HT-I: Alert, shivering.

Remove wet clothing, warm dry environment, warm blankets (passive rewarming), warm oral fluids if alert and protecting airway. Add active external warming if slow progress.

Rewarming rate: 0.5-2°C/hour with passive measures.

Monitoring: core temp q30-60 min, cardiac monitor, mental status.

5 · Moderate hypothermia (28-32°C)

Swiss Stage HT-II: Altered consciousness, shivering stopped. High arrhythmia risk — handle gently.

Remove wet clothing (cut off, avoid excessive movement), horizontal positioning, cardiac monitoring (anticipate bradycardia, AF), active external rewarming (forced air preferred), warm IV fluids (40-43°C crystalloid), heated humidified O2.

Rewarming rate: 1-3°C/hour.

Avoid: rough handling, cold IV fluids, rewarming extremities before trunk.

  • Remove wet clothing and prevent further heat loss immediately
  • Give warm IV fluids (40-42°C) via a fluid warmer
  • Start active rewarming with a Bair Hugger, warm blankets, heated humidified O2

ECG changesSinus bradycardia (universal); prolonged PR/QRS/QT; Osborn (J) waves below 32°C; atrial fibrillation common; VF risk increases as QRS widens
CoagulopathyPlatelet dysfunction + enzyme inhibition below 35°C; standard PT/aPTT run at 37°C — will not detect hypothermic coagulopathy; primary treatment is rewarming
ElectrolytesCooling causes hypokalemia (intracellular shift) and hyperglycemia; rewarming causes hyperkalemia (K+ shifts back out); do NOT aggressively replace K+ during hypothermia

ICU if: arrhythmias, hemodynamic instability, requiring invasive rewarming, or significant comorbidities.

Telemetry if: stable hemodynamics, responding to active external rewarming, no significant arrhythmias.

Workup: toxicology screen, TSH/cortisol, glucose, infection workup, social assessment.

6 · Severe/profound hypothermia (<28°C)

Swiss Stage HT-III: Unconscious, vital signs present. EXTREME VF RISK — handle as a "cardiac time bomb."

Minimize all movement, horizontal positioning only, continuous cardiac monitoring, secure airway if GCS ≤8 (RSI acceptable, ketamine preferred), active internal rewarming required.

If hemodynamically stable: warm IV fluids + heated O2 + forced air warming, prepare for ECMO if available.

If hemodynamically unstable: ECMO strongly preferred; if no ECMO, thoracic + peritoneal lavage.

ECMO transfer indicated when: core temp <28°C, hemodynamically unstable, not responding to standard rewarming, or high risk of imminent arrest.

ECMO provides the fastest rewarming (6-10°C/hour) plus circulatory support and the ability to continue support if arrest occurs.

7 · Hypothermic cardiac arrest

Check pulse for 30-45 seconds — pulse may be very slow and difficult to detect.

Do NOT resuscitate if: chest wall frozen solid (incompressible); obvious lethal injuries (decapitation, truncal transection); decomposition; avalanche burial >35 min WITH airway packed with snow AND asystole; or K+ >12 mEq/L (no survivors).

These are NOT contraindications: muscular rigidity; fixed/dilated pupils; prolonged down time; very low temperature.

Critical question: did hypothermia occur BEFORE or AFTER cardiac arrest? Hypothermia BEFORE arrest is protective → aggressive resuscitation indicated. Arrest BEFORE hypothermia is not protective → standard futility criteria apply. Rule out hemolysis artifact before using K+ for termination.

<10 mEq/LProceed with aggressive resuscitation
10-12 mEq/LPoor prognosis, calculate HOPE score
>12 mEq/LFutile — no documented survivors

Criteria met for termination/non-initiation: chest wall frozen and incompressible, OR obvious lethal injury, OR decomposition, OR avalanche >35 min + snow-packed airway + asystole, OR K+ >12 mEq/L (confirmed, non-hemolyzed).

Documentation: specific contraindication and clinical reasoning. Family communication: despite remarkable survival stories in hypothermia, specific criteria indicate resuscitation would not be successful in this case.

Start CPR immediately if no hard contraindications.

CPR modifications: standard compression rate and depth; mechanical CPR strongly preferred for prolonged resuscitation/transport; intermittent CPR acceptable if continuous is not possible (unique to hypothermia).

Gentle handling: physical manipulation can precipitate VF — move patient horizontally, avoid rough movements.

Rhythm check: VF/VT → attempt defibrillation per the table below. Asystole/PEA → continue CPR, focus on rewarming.

ECMO/extracorporeal rewarming is the gold standard for hypothermic cardiac arrest.

Survival benefit: NNT = 2 (40-90% mortality reduction in appropriate patients).

ECMO indicated if: core temp <30-32°C; no confirmed death before cooling; K+ <12 mEq/L; no unsurvivable injuries; HOPE score suggests reasonable survival. Calculate HOPE score before the transport decision.

8 · ECMO transport & rewarming

Transport to the ECMO center EVEN IF NOT the closest hospital.

Pre-transport: calculate HOPE score (≥10% survival → proceed); obtain K+ (must be <12 mEq/L); activate the ECMO team at the receiving center; apply a mechanical CPR device if available.

During transport: continuous CPR (do not stop for loading/unloading); mechanical CPR preferred for consistent quality; horizontal positioning — avoid orthostatic stress; gentle handling — rough movement can precipitate VF; begin rewarming with warm IV fluids and heated blankets to the TRUNK only.

  • HOPE score calculated — document survival probability
  • K+ confirmed <12 mEq/L — non-hemolyzed sample
  • ECMO team notified — ETA and patient status
  • Mechanical CPR applied — LUCAS, AutoPulse, or similar
  • Core temp probe in place — continuous monitoring
  • Warm IV fluids running — 40-43°C crystalloid
  • Heated blankets to trunk — avoid extremities (afterdrop risk)
  • Patient secured horizontally
  • Team briefed — continuous CPR, gentle handling

During transport, continuous CPR is critical — but intermittent is acceptable if continuous is impossible. Monitor for VF conversion (pause briefly to check rhythm) and ROSC (check pulse q5 min during CPR pauses).

If ROSC during transport: continue rewarming, standard post-arrest care, still transport to the ECMO center (may need ECMO for rewarming or cardiogenic shock).

Arriving at the ECMO center: handoff to the ECMO team with HOPE score, K+, down time, and CPR quality — cannulation proceeds under ongoing CPR.

VA-ECMO rewarming protocol: rewarms at 6-10°C/hour (fastest available method). Target core temp 32-34°C initially, then slow to 36-37°C.

During rewarming: monitor K+ frequently (shifts out of cells during rewarming); watch for reperfusion injury; anticipate coagulopathy correction as temp normalizes; gradual weaning as cardiac function recovers.

Post-ECMO: targeted temperature management per post-arrest protocols; neuro-prognostication delayed ≥72 hours; many patients recover excellent neurological function.

9 · Non-ECMO invasive rewarming

When ECMO is unavailable, use aggressive invasive techniques.

Thoracic lavage (most effective non-ECMO method): rewarming rate 3-6°C/hour. Bilateral chest tubes — anterior tube for instillation, posterolateral tube for drainage.

Peritoneal lavage: rewarming rate 3-4°C/hour, 40-42°C crystalloid, continuous or intermittent drainage.

Combine with: warm IV fluids (40-43°C), heated humidified O2, active external warming to the trunk.

  1. Bilateral chest tube insertion — anterior tube (2nd-3rd ICS MCL) for instillation, posterolateral tube (5th ICS MAL) for drainage
  2. Warm crystalloid (LR or NS) to 42°C using a fluid warmer or microwave
  3. Instill 300-500 mL via the anterior tube, clamp 15 minutes, then unclamp and drain via the posterior tube — repeat cycle
  4. Alternative: continuous flow with a two-tube system per hemithorax for higher flow rates
  5. Monitor core temp, chest tube output, and lung sounds throughout

Continue CPR until: ROSC achieved, OR core temp ≥32°C with persistent asystole (termination is permitted), OR K+ confirmed >12 mEq/L during resuscitation, OR another contraindication identified.

"Warm and dead" = 32°C minimum.

Do NOT terminate based on: prolonged down time alone, fixed/dilated pupils, muscular rigidity, or multiple failed defibrillations while still cold.

Documentation: core temp at termination, total resuscitation time, reason for termination.

10 · Disposition

ICU admission for: post-arrest care (TTM protocols), required ECMO/mechanical support, significant organ dysfunction, persistent arrhythmias.

Monitoring: delayed neuro-prognostication (≥72 hours post-arrest), serial K+ (rewarming hyperkalemia), coagulation parameters, renal function (rhabdomyolysis risk).

Workup underlying cause: intoxication (EtOH, drugs), infection/sepsis, endocrine (hypothyroid, adrenal insufficiency), trauma, social factors (homelessness, elder abuse).

Remarkable recoveries are possible — many hypothermia survivors have excellent neurological outcomes.

30-35°C | DOUBLE intervals — epinephrine q6-10 min (not q3-5 min)

≥35°C | Standard ACLS dosing

ECMO/extracorporeal rewarming is the gold standard for hypothermic cardiac arrest.

Survival benefit: NNT = 2 (40-90% mortality reduction in appropriate patients).

ECMO indicated if: core temp <30-32°C; no confirmed death before cooling; K+ <12 mEq/L; no unsurvivable injuries; HOPE score suggests reasonable survival. Calculate HOPE score before the transport decision.

8 · ECMO transport & rewarming

Transport to the ECMO center EVEN IF NOT the closest hospital.

Pre-transport: calculate HOPE score (≥10% survival → proceed); obtain K+ (must be <12 mEq/L); activate the ECMO team at the receiving center; apply a mechanical CPR device if available.

During transport: continuous CPR (do not stop for loading/unloading); mechanical CPR preferred for consistent quality; horizontal positioning — avoid orthostatic stress; gentle handling — rough movement can precipitate VF; begin rewarming with warm IV fluids and heated blankets to the TRUNK only.

  • HOPE score calculated — document survival probability
  • K+ confirmed <12 mEq/L — non-hemolyzed sample
  • ECMO team notified — ETA and patient status
  • Mechanical CPR applied — LUCAS, AutoPulse, or similar
  • Core temp probe in place — continuous monitoring
  • Warm IV fluids running — 40-43°C crystalloid
  • Heated blankets to trunk — avoid extremities (afterdrop risk)
  • Patient secured horizontally
  • Team briefed — continuous CPR, gentle handling

During transport, continuous CPR is critical — but intermittent is acceptable if continuous is impossible. Monitor for VF conversion (pause briefly to check rhythm) and ROSC (check pulse q5 min during CPR pauses).

If ROSC during transport: continue rewarming, standard post-arrest care, still transport to the ECMO center (may need ECMO for rewarming or cardiogenic shock).

Arriving at the ECMO center: handoff to the ECMO team with HOPE score, K+, down time, and CPR quality — cannulation proceeds under ongoing CPR.

VA-ECMO rewarming protocol: rewarms at 6-10°C/hour (fastest available method). Target core temp 32-34°C initially, then slow to 36-37°C.

During rewarming: monitor K+ frequently (shifts out of cells during rewarming); watch for reperfusion injury; anticipate coagulopathy correction as temp normalizes; gradual weaning as cardiac function recovers.

Post-ECMO: targeted temperature management per post-arrest protocols; neuro-prognostication delayed ≥72 hours; many patients recover excellent neurological function.

9 · Non-ECMO invasive rewarming

When ECMO is unavailable, use aggressive invasive techniques.

Thoracic lavage (most effective non-ECMO method): rewarming rate 3-6°C/hour. Bilateral chest tubes — anterior tube for instillation, posterolateral tube for drainage.

Peritoneal lavage: rewarming rate 3-4°C/hour, 40-42°C crystalloid, continuous or intermittent drainage.

Combine with: warm IV fluids (40-43°C), heated humidified O2, active external warming to the trunk.

  • Bilateral chest tube insertion — anterior tube (2nd-3rd ICS MCL) for instillation, posterolateral tube (5th ICS MAL) for drainage
  • Warm crystalloid (LR or NS) to 42°C using a fluid warmer or microwave
  • Instill 300-500 mL via the anterior tube, clamp 15 minutes, then unclamp and drain via the posterior tube — repeat cycle
  • Alternative: continuous flow with a two-tube system per hemithorax for higher flow rates
  • Monitor core temp, chest tube output, and lung sounds throughout

Continue CPR until: ROSC achieved, OR core temp ≥32°C with persistent asystole (termination is permitted), OR K+ confirmed >12 mEq/L during resuscitation, OR another contraindication identified.

"Warm and dead" = 32°C minimum.

Do NOT terminate based on: prolonged down time alone, fixed/dilated pupils, muscular rigidity, or multiple failed defibrillations while still cold.

Documentation: core temp at termination, total resuscitation time, reason for termination.

10 · Disposition

ICU admission for: post-arrest care (TTM protocols), required ECMO/mechanical support, significant organ dysfunction, persistent arrhythmias.

Monitoring: delayed neuro-prognostication (≥72 hours post-arrest), serial K+ (rewarming hyperkalemia), coagulation parameters, renal function (rhabdomyolysis risk).

Workup underlying cause: intoxication (EtOH, drugs), infection/sepsis, endocrine (hypothyroid, adrenal insufficiency), trauma, social factors (homelessness, elder abuse).

Remarkable recoveries are possible — many hypothermia survivors have excellent neurological outcomes.
