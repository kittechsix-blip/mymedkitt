Neck Trauma: Hard Signs, Zones, and the OR-Now Decision

Adults with penetrating or blunt neck trauma in the ED. Excludes isolated cervical spine fracture management (covered elsewhere) and pediatric-specific dosing.

⚠ Hard signs of hemorrhage or airway compromise mean OR now, not CT. Active arterial bleeding, expanding/pulsatile hematoma, bruit/thrill, hemoptysis/hematemesis, air bubbling through the wound, massive subcutaneous emphysema, shock unresponsive to fluids, or stroke/lateralizing neuro signs — CT delays definitive care.
⚠ Do NOT probe the wound or remove an impaled object. Control hemorrhage with direct pressure or Foley-balloon tamponade; do NOT clamp blindly. Secure the airway (awake intubation preferred) BEFORE CT. Activate trauma surgery STAT, blood bank/MTP, and OR standby.
⚠ Transcervical injury (crosses midline) carries the highest risk — bilateral carotid/vertebral involvement, combined aerodigestive tract injury, and a very low threshold for OR.
⚠ Airway management in neck trauma is high-risk for failed/false passage. Avoid blind nasotracheal intubation, avoid multiple attempts (worsens edema), and avoid paralysis without a backup plan. Prefer awake fiberoptic or video laryngoscopy; escalate to surgical airway (cricothyrotomy) for failed oral attempts, expanding hematoma, complete obstruction, or unstable laryngeal fracture.
⚠ Blunt cerebrovascular injury (BCVI) and vascular injury on CTA carry high stroke risk — antiplatelet/anticoagulation decisions must balance bleeding risk (ICH, solid-organ injury) with vascular/neurosurgery consultation; neuro checks q1h.

1 · Primary survey & mechanism

Immediate assessment: airway (stridor, voice change, dysphagia), breathing (expanding hematoma, tracheal deviation), circulation (active bleeding, pulse deficit), neuro (lateralizing signs, altered mental status). Mechanism is penetrating (stab, GSW, impalement) or blunt (MVC, strangulation, sports). The critical question: is the patient hemodynamically stable?

Unstable — hard signs present
Secure the airway BEFORE CT (awake intubation if possible; prepare for surgical airway; avoid blind nasal attempts). Control hemorrhage with direct pressure or Foley-balloon tamponade — do NOT probe the wound or remove an impaled object. Activate trauma surgery STAT, blood bank (consider MTP), and OR team standby. Proceed to OR for uncontrolled hemorrhage, expanding hematoma, uncontrolled airway compromise, or shock despite resuscitation.

Immediate surgical exploration
While preparing: secure airway (awake intubation preferred), two large-bore IVs, Type & crossmatch 4+ units, activate MTP if hemorrhaging. Direct pressure for hemorrhage — manual compression or Foley catheter balloon tamponade. Do NOT clamp blindly. Do NOT remove impaled objects. Notify trauma surgery, vascular surgery, ENT (if airway involvement), anesthesia (difficult airway). No CT if hard signs present — will delay definitive care.

5 · CTA imaging & results

CTA protocol: neck AND chest (especially Zone I), include circle of Willis for Zone III, arterial-phase timing. Evaluates vascular injury (pseudoaneurysm, dissection, occlusion), active extravasation, trajectory, associated fractures. Sensitivity >95% for significant vascular injury (may miss small intimal injuries). If CTA positive or indeterminate, consider conventional angiography or endovascular treatment. CTA is less sensitive for esophageal injury — consider esophagoscopy/esophagram if high suspicion.

Vascular injury on CTA
Operative repair for active hemorrhage, expanding pseudoaneurysm, or major vessel transection. Endovascular (covered stent) for Zone I/III access or A-V fistula. Observation with anticoagulation for small intimal flaps, minor dissections, non-flow-limiting injuries. Consult vascular surgery, IR, trauma surgery. Stroke risk is high with carotid injuries — neuro checks q1h.
Aerodigestive tract injury
Laryngotracheal: hoarseness/stridor/subcutaneous emphysema, laryngoscopy/bronchoscopy; small tears observed + antibiotics, large tears surgically repaired. Esophageal (most commonly missed neck injury, high morbidity if delayed): water-soluble contrast swallow first, then thin barium if negative, esophagoscopy. Combined injuries have worse prognosis, need operative repair with a tissue flap. Antibiotics: broad coverage for anaerobes + GNR — Unasyn or Zosyn.
Negative CTA — observation
Can consider discharge if ALL of: no hard or soft signs, normal CTA, normal swallow, no voice changes, reliable patient. Most recommend a 24-hour observation period with serial neck exams instead. Return immediately for voice changes, difficulty swallowing, increasing neck swelling, fever, or coughing blood. Wound care: primary closure if clean and <6h; delayed closure if contaminated; antibiotics not routine.
Indeterminate CTA — further workup
Vascular: conventional angiography (gold standard), MRA if no contraindication, or repeat CTA in 24-48h. Esophagus: water-soluble contrast swallow, esophagoscopy. Larynx/trachea: direct laryngoscopy, bronchoscopy. Spine: CT c-spine if not included, MRI for ligamentous injury. When in doubt: observe with serial exams, repeat imaging in 24h, low threshold for endoscopy.

6 · Blunt neck trauma

Mechanisms: MVC (seatbelt, steering wheel), strangulation/hanging, direct blow (assault, sports), clothesline injury. Key concerns: blunt cerebrovascular injury (BCVI), laryngotracheal injury, cervical spine injury, esophageal injury (rare). Signs of laryngeal injury: hoarseness/aphonia, stridor, subcutaneous emphysema, anterior neck tenderness, loss of laryngeal landmarks.

Strangulation
Manual, ligature, or hanging. Immediate concerns: airway edema delayed up to 24-36h, BCVI, laryngeal fracture, aspiration. Exam: petechiae (conjunctival, facial, oral), ligature marks, neck tenderness, voice changes, neurologic deficits. Workup: CTA neck (BCVI screening), laryngoscopy if voice change/stridor, CT c-spine if appropriate. Admit for any airway symptoms, altered mental status, positive imaging, high-risk mechanism, or forensic/safety concerns.

Antiplatelet (ASA 325mg) or anticoagulation, balancing bleed risk (ICH, solid-organ injury). Neuro/vascular surgery consult.

7 · Airway management

Anticipate a difficult airway
Awake technique preferred if time allows; prepare for surgical airway; avoid paralysis if possible. Order of preference: (1) awake fiberoptic intubation, (2) video laryngoscopy (awake or sedated), (3) DSI then intubation, (4) surgical airway (cricothyrotomy). Avoid blind nasotracheal (false-passage risk), avoid multiple attempts (worsens edema), avoid paralysis without a backup plan. Surgical airway indications: failed oral attempts, expanding hematoma, complete obstruction, laryngeal fracture with instability. Tracheostomy is preferred over cricothyrotomy if the laryngeal injury is below the cricoid and time/expertise allow.

8 · Disposition

Admit ICU
Airway precautions (intubation/cric at bedside), neuro checks q1h (if vascular injury), serial neck exams q4h, NPO (potential OR), blood products available. If vascular injury: antiplatelet or anticoagulation per consult, SBP goal <140 if pseudoaneurysm, stroke protocol if deficit. If aerodigestive injury: NPO, IV antibiotics, speech therapy eval, surgical planning. Consults: trauma surgery, vascular surgery (if vascular injury), ENT (if laryngeal injury), IR PRN.
Admit floor — observation
Indications: negative CTA but concerning mechanism, soft signs present, need for serial exams, social/safety concerns. Monitoring: serial neck exams q4-6h, neuro checks if any BCVI concern, swallow eval before PO. Antibiotics not routine for clean wounds; Augmentin or Unasyn if contaminated. Disposition criteria: no new symptoms x24h, normal swallow, no expanding hematoma, follow-up arranged. Follow-up: trauma clinic 1-2 weeks, earlier if new symptoms.
Discharge — superficial injury
Discharge criteria (ALL must be met): superficial wound only (not through platysma), no hard or soft signs, negative CTA (if obtained), normal voice and swallow, reliable patient, clear follow-up. Wound care: clean/irrigate, primary closure if <6h, clean, tetanus update if needed. Return precautions: voice change, difficulty swallowing, neck swelling, increasing pain, fever, coughing/spitting blood, weakness or numbness. Follow-up: PCP or trauma clinic in 3-5 days, suture removal 5-7 days.
