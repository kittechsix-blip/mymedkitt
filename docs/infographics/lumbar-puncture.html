Lumbar Puncture — Indications, Technique, CSF Interpretation

Adults ≥18 undergoing diagnostic or therapeutic lumbar puncture in the ED. Covers indication triage, CT-before-LP and coagulation safety gates, positioning/needle/approach selection, opening-pressure measurement, CSF interpretation, and complication management. Excludes pediatric/neonatal weight-based dosing pathways and image-guided (fluoroscopy/IR) LP, which is referenced but not detailed here.

⚠ CT BEFORE LP if: age >60, immunocompromised, history of CNS disease, new-onset seizure, altered mental status (GCS <15), focal neurological deficit, or papilledema.
⚠ DO NOT delay antibiotics for CT or LP in suspected bacterial meningitis — give empiric antibiotics within 60 minutes of presentation.
⚠ DO NOT perform LP if CT shows significant midline shift, loss of basal/quadrigeminal cisterns, posterior fossa mass, or obstructive hydrocephalus — risk of herniation; get neurosurgery involved instead.
⚠ Coagulation thresholds: platelets >40,000/mm³ and INR <1.5 are considered safe — spinal hematoma is rare even with mild coagulopathy.
⚠ Use ATRAUMATIC (pencil-point) needles — PDPH ~4% vs 15-30% with cutting (Quincke) needles.
⚠ Opening pressure MUST be measured in LATERAL DECUBITUS with legs extended — sitting position falsely elevates or invalidates the reading.
⚠ Xanthochromia with BILIRUBIN present = definitive SAH (requires >9-10 hours to develop) — RBC clearing tube-to-tube alone is unreliable.
⚠ Conus medullaris ends at L1 — perform LP at L3-L4 or L4-L5 to avoid spinal cord injury.

1 · Indications & anatomy

Lumbar puncture is a bedside procedure to obtain cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) for diagnosis or to measure/reduce intracranial pressure. The conus medullaris ends at L1; LP is performed at L3-L4 or L4-L5 to avoid spinal cord injury.

  • Infectious: bacterial/viral/TB/fungal meningitis, encephalitis, neurosyphilis
  • Vascular: SAH (CT-negative but high clinical suspicion)
  • Inflammatory: MS (oligoclonal bands), GBS (albuminocytologic dissociation), autoimmune encephalitis
  • Neoplastic: leptomeningeal carcinomatosis, CNS lymphoma
  • Other: idiopathic intracranial hypertension (IIH), normal pressure hydrocephalus (NPH)
  • IIH: high-volume tap (30-50 mL) for immediate symptom relief
  • NPH: "tap test" — remove 40-50 mL, assess gait/cognition improvement
  • Intrathecal medications: chemotherapy, antibiotics, baclofen
Safe level
Conus medullaris ends at L1. Perform LP at L3-L4 or L4-L5 to avoid cord injury — below this level lies only the mobile cauda equina.

2 · What to do now — by indication

Suspected bacterial meningitis — antibiotics FIRST
Bacterial meningitis is a time-critical emergency. Order of operations: blood cultures ×2, empiric antibiotics immediately (door-to-antibiotic <60 minutes), CT head if indicated, LP when safe, then adjust antibiotics on CSF results. See empiric dosing below.
CT-negative SAH workup
LP indicated when CT head is negative but thunderclap headache/high clinical suspicion remains. CT sensitivity for SAH is 98-100% within 6 hours of onset; LP is most useful 12+ hours after headache onset to allow xanthochromia to develop.
IIH / pseudotumor cerebri
Diagnostic AND therapeutic. Opening pressure ≥25 cm H₂O (adults) supports the diagnosis. Therapeutic high-volume tap: remove 30-50 mL, target closing pressure 10-15 cm H₂O. Must exclude a mass lesion before LP.
Other diagnostic indication
MS, GBS, NPH tap test, or neoplastic workup — proceed directly to CT-before-LP criteria assessment.

Discuss indication, alternatives, risks (PDPH 10-30%, back pain 35%, bleeding, infection, nerve injury, herniation rare), and benefits (diagnosis and/or treatment) before proceeding.

10 · Positioning

Lateral decubitus setupRequired for opening pressure
  1. Patient lies on side, spine parallel to bed edge, perpendicular to floor
  2. Knees drawn toward chest, neck flexed, spine NOT rotated (shoulders/hips stacked)
  3. For OP measurement: legs extended (not flexed), bed completely flat, patient relaxed (no straining/Valsalva)
Sitting position setupWider interspaces, cannot measure OP
  1. Patient sits on bed edge, feet supported, leans forward over bedside table ("cat stretch")
  2. Head flexed, chin to chest, arms resting on table/pillow
  3. Wider interspinous spaces, higher first-attempt success, better for obese patients
  4. CANNOT accurately measure opening pressure — use lateral decubitus if OP is needed

12 · Approach & landmarks

Tuffier's line (connecting the iliac crests) crosses the spine at the L4 vertebral body or L4-L5 interspace. Landmark accuracy is poor in ~30% of patients — consider ultrasound guidance (87% vs 50% first-attempt success) if landmarks are unclear.

Consider paramedian if
Failed midline attempts, calcified ligaments (age >50), severe degenerative changes, cannot flex spine, kyphosis/scoliosis, or obesity with poor landmarks. Paramedian has 30-40% higher first-attempt success than midline.
Midline layer-by-layer detailLayers, angle, depth
  1. Skin → subcutaneous tissue → supraspinous ligament → interspinous ligament
  2. Ligamentum flavum (distinct "pop") → epidural space
  3. Dura mater (second "pop") → arachnoid → subarachnoid space (CSF)
  4. Angle needle 15° cephalad toward the umbilicus; depth 4-8+ cm depending on body habitus
Paramedian 3-step guideL5-S1, 2cm lateral/caudal, 30-45°
  1. Locate the L5-S1 interspace (iliac crest line crosses at L4-L5; target one level caudal)
  2. Mark entry point: 2 cm lateral to midline, 2 cm caudal from interspace center
  3. Trajectory: 30-45° cephalad and toward midline — soft tissue only until dura, no ligamentous resistance

13 · Opening pressure

  1. BEFORE withdrawing any CSF, attach 3-way stopcock and manometer column to needle hub
  2. Patient relaxed in lateral decubitus, legs extended, head neutral, bed flat
  3. Wait for the fluid column to stop oscillating with respirations, then read at the meniscus

Traumatic tap vs true SAH
RBC clearing tube-to-tube is UNRELIABLE (partial clearing occurs in both traumatic tap and SAH). Xanthochromia with bilirubin present is the key — definitive for SAH but requires >9-10 hours to develop. <2000 RBC/µL + no xanthochromia excludes SAH (100% sensitive) if LP is >12h post-headache.

16 · Complications & disposition

No complications
Routine post-procedure care: ambulation is fine, encourage hydration, PRN analgesia. Mild back soreness (35%) is expected and transient.
PDPH — positional headache
Bilateral frontal/occipital headache, worse upright, better supine, usually within 5 days. Conservative first-line: bed rest, hydration, caffeine 300-500 mg PO, NSAIDs/acetaminophen. If refractory: epidural blood patch, 15-20 mL autologous blood, success rate 75-95%.
Failed LP
Reassess landmarks (consider ultrasound, 87% vs 50% first-attempt success), try a different interspace, switch to paramedian approach (30-40% higher success), or use fluoroscopy-guided LP via IR. Consider stopping after 3-4 failed attempts.
Other rare complicationsHematoma, herniation, infection, nerve injury
Epidural/spinal hematoma: incidence 0.20-0.23% — back pain, progressive weakness, urinary retention → urgent MRI spine, emergent neurosurgery. Brain herniation: risk with mass lesion/elevated ICP — emergent neurosurgery, ICP management. Infection (iatrogenic meningitis): usually operator oral flora — prevent with sterile technique/mask. Nerve root irritation: transient radicular pain — withdraw and redirect needle.
  • Bacterial meningitis: ICU admission, IV antibiotics, dexamethasone
  • Viral meningitis: usually supportive care; may discharge stable patients
  • SAH confirmed: neurosurgery consult, angiography, ICU
  • IIH: neurology follow-up, acetazolamide, weight-loss counseling
  • Uncomplicated LP discharge: return for severe positional headache, leg weakness, numbness, urinary issues, or fever

1 | 1-2 mL | Cell count, differential

2 | 1-2 mL | Protein, glucose

3 | 2-3 mL | Gram stain, culture, PCR

4 | 1-2 mL | Repeat cell count (compare to tube 1)

Diagnostic LP: **8-15 mL** total. CSF regenerates ~20 mL/hour. Safe to remove up to **30-40 mL** in adults.

  • Replace stylet before withdrawing needle (reduces PDPH)
  • Withdraw needle smoothly, apply pressure with sterile gauze
  • Small bandage over site; ambulation is fine (bed rest NOT proven to prevent PDPH)

15 · CSF analysis & interpretation

Parameter | Normal (adults)

Appearance | Clear, colorless

WBC | 0-5 cells/µL

RBC | 0 cells/µL

Protein | 15-45 mg/dL

Glucose | 40-85 mg/dL

CSF:Serum glucose | >0.6

Lactate | <25 mg/dL

Condition | WBC | Type | Protein | Glucose

Bacterial | 100-10,000+ | PMN | 100-500+ | <40 (low)

Viral | 5-1000 | Lymph | Normal-150 | Normal

TB | 100-500 | Lymph | 100-500 | <45 (low)

Fungal | 20-500 | Lymph | 50-500 | Low

SAH | Variable | RBC+WBC | Elevated | Normal

GBS | 0-10 | — | 45-200+ | Normal

MS | 0-50 | Lymph | Normal-100 | Normal

CSF:blood glucose ratio **<0.36**; lactate **>35 mg/dL**; Gram stain positive in **60-90%**.

ADA **>10 IU/L**; glucose ratio **<0.5**; AFB smear sensitivity only **20-40%**.

Any ONE of: glucose **<34 mg/dL**, protein **>220 mg/dL**, WBC **>2,000/µL**, or neutrophils **>1,180/µL**.

RBC clearing tube-to-tube is UNRELIABLE (partial clearing occurs in both traumatic tap and SAH). **Xanthochromia with bilirubin present is the key** — definitive for SAH but requires **>9-10 hours** to develop. <2000 RBC/µL + no xanthochromia excludes SAH (100% sensitive) if LP is >12h post-headache.

16 · Complications & disposition

Routine post-procedure care: ambulation is fine, encourage hydration, PRN analgesia. Mild back soreness (35%) is expected and transient.

Bilateral frontal/occipital headache, worse upright, better supine, usually within 5 days. Conservative first-line: bed rest, hydration, caffeine 300-500 mg PO, NSAIDs/acetaminophen. If refractory: **epidural blood patch, 15-20 mL** autologous blood, success rate **75-95%**.

Reassess landmarks (consider ultrasound, 87% vs 50% first-attempt success), try a different interspace, switch to paramedian approach (30-40% higher success), or use fluoroscopy-guided LP via IR. Consider stopping after 3-4 failed attempts.

Other rare complications

**Epidural/spinal hematoma:** incidence **0.20-0.23%** — back pain, progressive weakness, urinary retention → urgent MRI spine, emergent neurosurgery. **Brain herniation:** risk with mass lesion/elevated ICP — emergent neurosurgery, ICP management. **Infection (iatrogenic meningitis):** usually operator oral flora — prevent with sterile technique/mask. **Nerve root irritation:** transient radicular pain — withdraw and redirect needle.

  • Bacterial meningitis: ICU admission, IV antibiotics, dexamethasone
  • Viral meningitis: usually supportive care; may discharge stable patients
  • SAH confirmed: neurosurgery consult, angiography, ICU
  • IIH: neurology follow-up, acetazolamide, weight-loss counseling
  • Uncomplicated LP discharge: return for severe positional headache, leg weakness, numbness, urinary issues, or fever
