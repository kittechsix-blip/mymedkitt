Headache Hub: Sick-Check First, Then the Cocktail

Adults ≥18 presenting to the ED with headache. Sort sick vs not-sick first, exclude the time-critical mimics, then treat benign-pattern headache. Excludes pediatrics and isolated post-LP headache management; each can't-miss diagnosis (SAH, meningitis, AACG, CVST, dissection, GCA, CO) has its own deep-dive consult.

⚠ Thunderclap = SAH until proven otherwise. Non-contrast CT is ~98% within 6 h of onset; if onset >6 h or CT is negative, do an LP for xanthochromia.
⚠ Fever + meningismus or immunocompromise = empiric antibiotics within 60 minutes of suspicion. Do NOT wait for imaging or LP — CT-before-LP does not delay the empiric dose.
⚠ Painful red eye + halos + mid-dilated pupil = acute angle-closure glaucoma. Do NOT dim the room lights and do NOT give mydriatic drops — both worsen the angle closure.
⚠ CO toxicity: standard pulse oximetry reads CO-Hgb as oxyhemoglobin and is FALSELY NORMAL. Use co-oximetry; put the patient on 100% O₂ via non-rebreather while the disposition is decided.
⚠ Age ≥50 + new headache + jaw claudication / scalp tenderness / vision change = GCA. Start empiric high-dose steroid BEFORE the biopsy — vision loss is preventable, not reversible.
⚠ Any NEW neurologic finding during the rescue pathway stops it. Re-image and return to the time-critical exclusions — do not keep dosing the cocktail.
⚠ Avoid opioids and butalbital for benign-pattern headache — they worsen recurrence and drive medication-overuse headache.

1 · Sick check first

Walk into the room. Before any framework, sort sick vs not-sick: gestalt, vitals trend, a quick neuro screen, a quick eye look, and the skin. If the patient is unstable, resuscitate in parallel — the hub can wait.[1][7]

The 5 do-not-miss killers
SAH (thunderclap / worst-ever / exertional) · bacterial meningitis / encephalitis (fever + meningismus + altered) · CNS venous sinus thrombosis (progressive HA, hypercoagulable, papilledema, seizure) · CO poisoning (co-exposure, clustered sick contacts) · acute angle-closure glaucoma / GCA (red painful eye + halos, or jaw claudication + scalp tenderness in older adults).[1][7]

2 · Bedside triage — set the state

Flip the toggles above for what you actually see at the bedside. Exactly one next-5-minutes instruction shows below (precedence: SAH → meningitis → glaucoma → CO → benign), and the pathway diagram highlights the matching branch.

Thunderclap → SAH workup now
IV access, monitor, BP control if SBP >160 (target 140-160 with labetalol or nicardipine), neuro recheck q15 min. Non-contrast CT now — sensitivity is ~98% within 6 h; if onset >6 h or CT negative, LP for xanthochromia. Positive CT → neurosurgery. Open SAH.[5]
Fever + meningismus → antibiotics now
Empiric antibiotics within 60 minutes of suspicion — ceftriaxone 2 g IV + vancomycin 15-20 mg/kg IV (add ampicillin 2 g IV if age <3 mo or >50 y). Do NOT wait for imaging or LP. Open Meningitis / Encephalitis.[7]
Red eye + halos → AACG
Emergent ophthalmology, measure IOP now, start medical IOP-lowering. 🛑 Do NOT dim the room lights and 🛑 do NOT give mydriatic drops — both worsen angle closure. Open AACG.[7]
CO setting → co-oximetry + 100% O₂
Standard SpO₂ is falsely normal — get co-oximetry. Place on 100% O₂ via non-rebreather (cuts CO-Hgb half-life from ~5 h on room air to ~80 min) regardless of the measured level. Open CO Toxicity.[9]
No red flags → benign-pattern pathway
Pattern matches prior benign headache (migraine, tension, undifferentiated). Proceed to the rescue cocktail in section 4 — but keep scanning: any new neurologic finding sends you back here.

Thunderclap → SAH. Ottawa SAH Rule for risk-out; non-contrast CT sensitivity is ~98% within 6 h and drops sharply after, so a later or negative CT means LP for xanthochromia. While transitioning: BP target 140-160 if SBP >160, neuro recheck q15 min.[5][6]

Focal deficit / AMS / seizure — image first
CT non-contrast for hemorrhage or mass effect; add contrast or MRI if infectious or tumor suspected. Open the deep-dive that fits — SAH if thunderclap onset too, ICH for spontaneous bleed, the stroke pathway for tPA/EVT + NIHSS, meningitis/encephalitis for fever + neuro signs, or status epilepticus for active/recent seizure. Next 5 minutes: IV access, monitor, fingerstick glucose, consider RSI if GCS ≤8, reverse anticoagulation early if ICH is suspected on an anticoagulant.[7]

Fever + meningismus / immunocompromised. Empiric antibiotics target within 60 minutes of suspicion — ceftriaxone 2 g IV plus vancomycin 15-20 mg/kg IV, add ampicillin 2 g IV at the age extremes. CT-before-LP is indicated for AMS / focal deficit / papilledema / immunocompromise / recent seizure / age >60 — but it does NOT delay the empiric dose.[7]

Red eye + halos → AACG
Emergent ophthalmology, measure IOP now, start medical IOP-lowering (timolol + brimonidine + dorzolamide + oral acetazolamide + IV mannitol if severe). 🛑 Do NOT dim the room lights. 🛑 Do NOT give mydriatic drops. 🛑 Do NOT discharge without ophthalmology disposition. If the pupil is normal or miotic with autonomic features (lacrimation, ptosis, rhinorrhea) plus restlessness, think cluster headache instead, not AACG.[7]
Pregnancy / postpartum — broaden the differential
Work up in parallel based on findings: pre-eclampsia (BP ≥140/90 or rising, plus proteinuria or end-organ signs — magnesium prophylaxis, delivery is definitive), CVST (papilledema, seizure, focal deficit — postpartum hypercoagulability peaks ~3 weeks out, MRV), RCVS (recurrent thunderclap + vasoactive trigger — CTA/MRA, nimodipine per neuro), pituitary apoplexy (sudden severe HA + visual field defect + ophthalmoplegia), and cervical artery dissection (neck pain + Horner). Imaging: non-contrast CT is acceptable for life-threats; MRI without gadolinium is preferred when available. Migraine analgesia in pregnancy: acetaminophen first-line — avoid ergots and valproate.[8]
Neck pain + Horner — suspect dissection
CTA neck (carotid + vertebral) is the practical first study in most EDs; MRA plus fat-suppressed neck MRI is the reference standard if available and the patient is stable. Vertebral artery dissection presents as posterior headache + neck pain ± posterior-circulation stroke symptoms — a different posture than carotid. Ask about recent chiropractic manipulation, MVC, strenuous exertion, persistent cough, forceful vomiting, fibromuscular dysplasia, or connective tissue disease. The antithrombotic decision is made under the stroke/neuro consult.[7]
Postpartum + papilledema/seizure/deficit — CVST
MRV is the workhorse; CT venogram is acceptable when MRV is unavailable. Think CVST when postpartum (especially 1–3 weeks out), hypercoagulable (factor V Leiden, OCPs + smoking, malignancy, antiphospholipid, dehydration), papilledema with HA, seizure with HA, or focal deficit + HA without an arterial stroke pattern. Treatment is therapeutic anticoagulation (UFH or LMWH) even when a hemorrhagic component is present on imaging, plus neurology + hematology consult.[7]
GCA — steroid before biopsy
Age ≥50 + new HA + jaw claudication / scalp tenderness / vision change / PMR. Stat ESR + CRP, then treat in parallel — do NOT wait for biopsy. No visual symptoms: prednisone 60 mg PO daily. Visual symptoms or jaw claudication: methylprednisolone 1 g IV daily × 3 days, then prednisone 60 mg PO.[7]
CO — co-oximetry, not SpO₂
Standard pulse oximetry reads CO-Hgb as oxyhemoglobin — falsely normal. Place on 100% O₂ via non-rebreather (half-life ~5 h on room air → ~80 min) and continue regardless of the measured CO-Hgb pending co-oximetry. Pregnancy and symptomatic kids get hyperbaric consideration at lower thresholds.[9]

Cluster phenotype (severe unilateral periorbital + ipsilateral autonomic + can't sit still): place on 100% O₂ via NRB at 12-15 L/min — diagnostic and therapeutic, ~78% abort within 15 min. If not aborted by 7 min, add SQ sumatriptan after the contraindication screen.[3]

Trigeminal neuralgia — screen before carbamazepineHLA-B*1502
Electric-shock V2/V3 face pain triggered by touch / chewing / cold wind. 🛑 **Before the first carbamazepine or oxcarbazepine dose, screen HLA-B*1502 ancestry** (Han Chinese, Thai, Vietnamese, Filipino, Malay, Indonesian) — positive means avoid both and bridge with gabapentin or baclofen. Atypical features (age <40, bilateral, V1-only, sensory deficit) need MRI with FIESTA/CISS sequences first. Open Trigeminal Neuralgia.[10]

Post-traumatic HA. Acute (<7 days) with any red flag — LOC, GCS drop, focal deficit, repeated emesis, anticoagulation, age >65, severe mechanism — apply the Canadian CT Head Rule and image with non-contrast CT; reverse anticoagulation early if ICH is suspected. Chronic (>3 months) without progressive features: conservative management, neuro/sports-medicine follow-up. Post-LP HA: hydration + caffeine + analgesia, blood patch if unresponsive beyond 24 h. Spontaneous CSF leak: orthostatic HA + tinnitus + nausea without an LP history — MRI brain with contrast ± cisternogram.[7]

4 · Rescue cocktail — benign pattern

No red flags, pattern matches prior benign headache. Standard parenteral cocktail — antidopaminergic + akathisia prophylaxis + NSAID + magnesium + a fluid bolus:

  • Prochlorperazine 10 mg IV over 2 min — the antidopaminergic workhorse
  • Diphenhydramine 25 mg IV — akathisia prophylaxis (DO NOT SKIP; prochlorperazine is the worst offender)
  • Ketorolac 15-30 mg IV — NSAID component (cap 15 mg if age >65, renal disease, or low body weight)
  • Magnesium 1-2 g IV over 15 min — adjunct
  • 1 L normal saline bolus
  • Metoclopramide 10 mg IV if prochlorperazine is unavailable

Enter age and toggle renal disease / low body weight above → capped ketorolac dose:

—Ketorolac IV (auto-capped)
Do NOT reach for opioids or butalbital
Opioid and butalbital combinations worsen recurrence and drive medication-overuse headache — they are not part of the benign-pattern cocktail.[2]
Partial response → second rounddexamethasone reduces recurrence

Repeat prochlorperazine (or switch to metoclopramide 10 mg IV if akathisia despite diphenhydramine). Add dexamethasone 10 mg IV — cuts 24–72 h recurrence by ~30% (NNT ~9). Add valproate 500–1000 mg IV over 10–15 min if not pregnant / no liver disease. Repeat the 1 L NS bolus if dehydrated; ED obs 4–6 h with a planned reassessment.[2]

Refractory after a second round
Greater occipital nerve block at the bedside (5–15 min onset) — open Greater Occipital Nerve Block. Or DHE IV (Raskin protocol: metoclopramide 10 mg IV first, then DHE 0.5–1 mg IV slow push). DHE is absolutely contraindicated in pregnancy, CAD, uncontrolled HTN, or recent triptan.[2]

6 · Disposition & do-NOT

  1. Pain reduced to an acceptable level (typically ≤3/10 or back to baseline)
  2. Tolerating PO
  3. Orthostatic vitals normal (prochlorperazine + diphenhydramine + ketorolac can drop BP)
  4. No new neurologic findings on recheck
  5. Suicide screen if cluster, TN, chronic migraine, or mood disorder + chronic pain
  6. Written return precautions: first-or-worst severity, new neuro symptom, fever or stiff neck, persistent vomiting, vision change
  7. Follow-up arranged
Critical pitfalls4 items
Do NOT skip diphenhydramine. It is akathisia prophylaxis for the antidopaminergic — prochlorperazine is the worst offender.
Do NOT keep dosing the cocktail through a new neurologic finding. Stop, image, and return to the time-critical exclusions.
Do NOT give opioids or butalbital for benign-pattern headache — they worsen recurrence and drive medication-overuse headache.
Do NOT wait for a biopsy to start steroid in suspected GCA — vision loss is preventable, not reversible.
