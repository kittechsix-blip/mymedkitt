Precipitous Delivery: Assess, Prepare, Deliver, Prevent Hemorrhage

Adults in active/imminent labor presenting to the ED before planned arrival at a delivery unit. Assumes vertex (head-down) presentation confirmed or presumed. Excludes breech and other malpresentations (separate consult), multiple gestation specifics, and shoulder dystocia management (separate consult).

⚠ Page OB and Pediatrics STAT immediately — do not delay, even if delivery seems far off. Assign a minimum of 4 people (delivering provider, nurse for mom, nurse for baby, second provider for baby) and turn on the neonatal warmer NOW.
⚠ Active labor is an emergency medical condition under EMTALA. If delivery is imminent, the hospital must deliver the baby regardless of OB availability. Antenatal transfer (pregnant patient in a vehicle) is always safer than neonatal transfer (newborn in an ambulance).
⚠ Tight nuchal cord: double-clamp and cut between the clamps, then deliver the baby PROMPTLY — once the cord is cut the baby has no placental circulation.
⚠ Shoulder delivery — traction only along the long axis of the fetal neck. Pulling at an angle risks brachial plexus injury. If the head delivers but retracts against the perineum ("turtle sign") → this is shoulder dystocia, an obstetric emergency requiring specific maneuvers.
⚠ NEVER force the placenta. Aggressive cord traction risks uterine inversion — a life-threatening emergency. If the placenta has not delivered within 30 minutes, this is a retained placenta requiring manual removal by OB.
⚠ Oxytocin — do NOT give IV push (can cause profound hypotension). Give as 20 units in 1L NS (or LR) IV at 250 mL/hr, or 10 units IM if no IV access, after the placenta delivers.

  1. During contractions: deep breath at start, bear down and push 10 sec x 3, rest between
  2. When crowning: tell the patient to STOP pushing, breathe/pant/blow through contractions
  3. Reassure throughout — many ED precipitous deliveries are terrified patients

5 · Stage 2 — delivering the baby

Delivering the head
One hand on the perineum with a towel — gentle upward pressure on the fetal chin. Other hand on the occiput — gentle counter-pressure. Goal: slow, controlled delivery. Let the uterus do the work — do NOT pull. Routine nose/mouth suctioning is no longer recommended (NRP 9e) — only suction if the airway is visibly obstructed.

Immediately after the head delivers, sweep a finger around the baby's neck feeling for the umbilical cord. Nuchal cord occurs in 20-30% of deliveries and is usually manageable.

Shoulder delivery — long-axis traction only
Anterior shoulder: gentle downward traction, guide under the pubic symphysis. Posterior shoulder: gentle upward traction. Traction only in the long axis of the fetal neck — off-axis risks brachial plexus injury. "Turtle sign" (head retracts against perineum) = shoulder dystocia — an obstetric emergency; see the Shoulder Dystocia consult.
Cord clamping and cutting
Do NOT drop the baby — place skin-to-skin on mom's abdomen and dry vigorously. Clamp the cord in two places ~3 cm apart (first clamp ~3 cm from the baby's abdomen), cut between. Deferred cord clamping (at least 60 sec) for vigorous newborns not needing resuscitation; clamp/cut immediately if resuscitation is needed. Non-sterile delivery → clean the cord stump with antiseptic.

8 · Do NOT

Critical pitfalls

**Do NOT give oxytocin IV push** — can cause profound hypotension. Infuse 20 units in 1L NS at 250 mL/hr, or 10 units IM if no IV access.

**Do NOT force the placenta** — aggressive traction risks uterine inversion, a life-threatening emergency.

**Do NOT pull off-axis on the fetal shoulders** — traction must stay in the long axis of the neck or risk brachial plexus injury.

**Do NOT delay delivery once a tight nuchal cord is cut** — the baby has no placental circulation after the cord is cut.

**Do NOT routinely suction the nose and mouth** at delivery of the head (NRP 9e) — only suction if the airway is visibly obstructed.

**Do NOT delay calling OB/Peds** or wait to see if delivery is truly imminent before paging — page STAT and turn on the warmer immediately.
