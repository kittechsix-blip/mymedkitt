CHF Exacerbation: SCAPE vs Volume Overload

Adults ≥18 in the ED with acute decompensated heart failure. Excludes pediatrics, pregnancy, and primary right-heart / pulmonary-hypertension crises — each needs a modified pathway. The pivot is SCAPE (afterload/redistribution) vs FOPE (true volume overload) vs cardiogenic shock.

⚠ SCAPE is a vasodilator emergency, not a volume problem. Simultaneous BiPAP + high-dose NTG — patient should be out of the water by 10 minutes.
⚠ In SCAPE, avoid these: No beta-blockers, no morphine (MIMO trial: increased arrest), no routine diuretics in SCAPE.
⚠ Cold & wet = cardiogenic shock: dobutamine for SBP 80-100, NE first for SBP <80 — milrinone for HFpEF or BB toxicity.
⚠ If SCAPE fails BiPAP + max NTG: Refractory SCAPE: ketamine for RSI (not propofol), have vasopressor ready — now cardiogenic shock.
⚠ Respiratory rescue: NIV (BiPAP/CPAP) 10-15 cm H₂O for severe respiratory distress.
⚠ Volume overload (FOPE): Furosemide 40-80 mg IV (or 2× home dose) within 30 minutes.

1 · The pivot: SCAPE vs FOPE vs shock

One question changes everything
Acute decompensated heart failure splits three ways, and the treatments are opposite. SCAPE (Sympathetic Crashing Acute Pulmonary Edema) is an afterload/redistribution crisis — treat with vasodilation, not diuresis. FOPE (Fluid Overload Pulmonary Edema) is true volume overload — treat with diuretics. Cardiogenic shock (cold & wet) needs inotropes/pressors first.[1]

Why SCAPE spirals
Sympathetic surge → vasoconstriction → massive afterload → LV can't eject → acute backward failure → pulmonary edema → hypoxia → more sympathetic surge. Break the loop with vasodilation and positive-pressure ventilation.[1][3]

DO NOW: cardiogenic shock (cold & wet)
IV access, monitor, labs (lactate, troponin, BNP), arterial line if available. Gentle or no diuretics — they worsen hypotension. Start an inotrope/pressor by pressure: SBP 80-100 → dobutamine; SBP <80 → norepinephrine first, then add inotrope. Toggle HFpEF / BB toxicity if milrinone is the better inodilator. See section 5.[2]
DO NOW: SCAPE — vasodilate and ventilate
Simultaneously: start CPAP 8-10 (titrate up to 15-20) or BiPAP 12/6 → 20-24/10-12, AND high-dose nitroglycerin — bolus 400-800 mcg/min ×2-2.5 min, then infusion 100-200 mcg/min titrated to 400+ mcg/min. No routine diuretics. See section 3.[1][3]
DO NOW: volume overload (FOPE) — diurese
Warm & wet is the most common ADHF profile. Give an IV loop diuretic at 1-2.5× the home oral dose (40-80 mg IV if diuretic-naïve) within 30 minutes, add NTG if SBP >100, and reassess urine output at 4-6 hours. See section 4.[2][4]

3 · SCAPE pathway

  1. BiPAP/CPAP now: CPAP 8-10 cm H₂O or BiPAP 12/6, titrate up aggressively (goal CPAP 15-20 or BiPAP 20-24/10-12).
  2. High-dose nitroglycerin: bolus 400-800 mcg/min ×2-2.5 min, then infusion 100-200 mcg/min → titrate to 400+ mcg/min. No ceiling dose in SCAPE.
  3. Reassess at 10-15 minutes — flash is fast both ways.
SCAPE: what to AVOID
Beta-blockers (impair pump function — contraindicated), morphine (MIMO trial stopped early for harm — increased arrest and shock), and routine diuretics (many SCAPE patients are intravascularly depleted). Diurese only after vasodilation is working and only with true volume overload.[1]
Refractory SCAPE → prepare to intubate
Failing BiPAP + max NTG: push NTG to 800+ mcg/min, rule out pneumothorax / PE / tamponade, and set up for RSI with ketamine (less hypotension than propofol) with a vasopressor ready. This is now cardiogenic-shock territory.[1]

4 · Volume overload (FOPE): diuretic strategy

Furosemide (IV loop diuretic)
1-2.5× home oral dose IV
IV
Diuretic-naïve: start 40-80 mg IV. Home dose >80 mg: give 1-2× that as IV. Add NTG 10-20 mcg/min if SBP >100 to speed diuresis.
  • Urine output >100-150 mL/hr by 6 hours.
  • Urine spot sodium >50-70 mEq/L at 2 hours.
  • Daily weight loss 0.5-1 kg/day.
  • DOSE trial: bolus vs infusion — no significant difference.
Diuretic resistance (UOP <50 mL/hr)sequential nephron blockade

Double the IV furosemide dose (may need 160-200 mg IV) or switch to an infusion 5-10 mg/hr.

Add Metolazone 2.5-5 mg PO 30 min BEFORE the loop diuretic — sequential nephron blockade. Watch for hypotension and hypokalemia; monitor K⁺.[4]

Before discharge / do-NOT list

**Do NOT diurese SCAPE by reflex** — most are euvolemic or dry; vasodilate first.

**Do NOT give morphine in acute pulmonary edema** — MIMO trial: increased arrest.

**Do NOT push aggressive IV fluids in volume overload** — worsens pulmonary edema.

Hold ACE-I/ARB during acute cardiorenal AKI; a transient creatinine rise during effective diuresis (permissive worsening renal function) is acceptable.

**Before discharge:** address the precipitant, optimize GDMT (ACE-I/ARNI, beta-blocker once euvolemic, MRA, SGLT2i), cardiology follow-up within 7 days, daily-weight education.{{ref5}}
