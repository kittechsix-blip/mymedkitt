Acute Decompensated Heart Failure: SCAPE vs Volume Overload vs Shock

Adults ≥18 in the ED with acute decompensated heart failure (ADHF). Excludes pediatrics and isolated chronic stable HF without acute decompensation. The pivot is SCAPE (afterload/redistribution crisis) vs FOPE (true volume overload) vs cardiogenic shock — the first two require opposite initial treatments.

⚠ Beta-blockers are absolutely contraindicated in SCAPE — they impair pump function during the sympathetic-surge afterload crisis.
⚠ Avoid morphine in SCAPE. MIMO trial: significantly more cardiovascular serious adverse events (cardiac arrest, cardiogenic shock) vs midazolam.
⚠ Do not give routine diuretics in SCAPE unless there is confirmed true volume overload — many SCAPE patients are intravascularly depleted, and diuresis can worsen the crisis.
⚠ Cold & Dry (pure cardiogenic shock without congestion) is hypovolemic — DO NOT give diuretics. Cautious fluid boluses first, then inotropes.
⚠ If intubation is unavoidable in SCAPE or refractory cardiogenic shock: ketamine is the preferred induction agent. AVOID propofol (profound hypotension) and have a vasopressor ready.
⚠ NT-proBNP rule-in thresholds are age-stratified. An unqualified >900 pg/mL cutoff misses acute HF in patients >75 with levels 900-1800 pg/mL.

SCAPE is NOT volume overload — afterload crashes fluid into the lungs. Break the sympathetic spiral with NIV + high-dose nitrates simultaneously.[1][3]

Non-invasive ventilation

5 · Renal Assessment & Disposition

AKI staging (cardiorenal syndrome is common in ADHF): Stage 1: Cr 1.5-1.9x baseline; Stage 2: Cr 2-2.9x baseline; Stage 3: Cr >=3x baseline or >4.0. Transient Cr rise is acceptable if diuresis is effective and the patient is improving; hold ACE-I/ARB during the acute episode.[2][4]

Severe AKI — consider RRT
Stage 2-3 AKI with ADHF is high risk. RRT indications: K+ >6.5 unresponsive to medical therapy, severe metabolic acidosis pH <7.15, volume overload refractory to diuretics, uremic symptoms (encephalopathy, pericarditis). RRT options: CVVH/CVVHD if hemodynamically unstable, intermittent HD if stable, or ultrafiltration for pure volume removal. Consult nephrology early.[2][4]

Mild AKI: continue diuretics — Continue diuretics if volume overloaded — permissive worsening renal function is acceptable.

Severe AKI: consider RRT — Stage 2-3 AKI: consider RRT if K+ >6.5, pH <7.15, or refractory volume overload.

Disposition + address precipitant + GDMT — ICU for shock / NTG drip / respiratory failure; telemetry for good diuretic response. Always address the precipitant and optimize GDMT.

7 · Do NOT

Critical pitfalls

**Do NOT give beta-blockers or morphine in SCAPE** — beta-blockers impair pump function; morphine's MIMO trial showed more cardiac arrest and cardiogenic shock.

**Do NOT reflexively diurese SCAPE** — most patients are euvolemic or dry; vasodilate first.

**Do NOT give diuretics in Cold & Dry** — the patient is hypovolemic; cautious fluids then inotropes.

**Do NOT push aggressive IV fluids in volume overload** — worsens pulmonary edema.

**Do NOT use an unqualified NT-proBNP >900 cutoff** — use age-stratified thresholds or acute HF will be missed in older patients.

**Do NOT discharge without addressing the precipitant and optimizing GDMT** — medication non-adherence, ACS, arrhythmia, and infection are the common drivers of relapse.
