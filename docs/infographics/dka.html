DKA: Fluids First, Then Insulin

Adults ≥18 in the ED with confirmed diabetic ketoacidosis. Excludes pediatrics, pregnancy, and dialysis-dependent ESRD — each needs a modified protocol.

⚠ Check K⁺ before insulin. If K⁺ <3.3 mEq/L, HOLD insulin and replete potassium first — insulin drives K⁺ intracellularly and can precipitate fatal hypokalemia.
⚠ Never stop insulin for a low glucose. Add dextrose and continue the drip — stopping insulin lets ketoacidosis recur even after glucose normalizes.
⚠ Euglycemic DKA is real. SGLT2-inhibitor patients present at glucose 150–300 mg/dL; a normal glucose does not exclude DKA.

4 · One-glance pathway

Confirmed DKA

Balanced crystalloid 500 mL–1 L bolus — Then 150–200 mL/hr. Reassess HR, BP, urine output q1–2h. Caution in HF / ESRD — use POCUS.

K⁺ ≥ 3.3? — Insulin drives K⁺ into cells. Below 3.3 mEq/L, giving insulin can cause fatal hypokalemia.

Hold insulin · replete K⁺ first — Replete to K⁺ ≥3.3, then start the drip.

Insulin 0.1 units/kg/hr — No bolus needed. Add KCl if K⁺ 3.3–5.3.

Glucose ≤ 300? — The transition point where iatrogenic hypoglycemia happens.

Continue NS 150–200 mL/hr — Switch the moment glucose hits 300.

Drop & split: ½ NS + D10W — Cut NS in half, add D10W at equal rate. Keep insulin running.

5 · Do NOT

Critical pitfalls

**Do NOT give insulin before checking K⁺.** Below 3.3 mEq/L it can be fatal.

**Do NOT stop insulin for a low glucose** — add dextrose and continue.

**Do NOT continue plain NS after glucose <300** — that is the iatrogenic-hypoglycemia trap.

**Do NOT resolve on glucose alone** — use β-OHB / anion-gap closure.
