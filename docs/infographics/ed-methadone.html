ED Methadone: 72-Hour Rule, Verification, Dosing, QTc, Disposition

Adults in the ED being considered for methadone — established OTP patients needing guest dosing, opioid-withdrawal patients being considered for ED-initiated methadone under the 72-hour rule, patients with an unverifiable methadone history, or inpatients on methadone maintenance. Excludes pediatric patients and outpatient OTP dose titration (OTP-managed, not an ED decision).

⚠ Never give a patient's claimed OTP dose without verification — call the OTP directly; if unable to verify, give a conservative 30 mg PO regardless of claimed dose (may add 10 mg after 2-4h if withdrawal persists, max 40 mg Day 1).
⚠ QTc >= 500 ms or a Torsades history is a contraindication to methadone — avoid or get cardiology consult; obtain an ECG before dosing if dose will exceed 100 mg/day or other QTc risk factors are present.
⚠ Do NOT combine methadone with benzodiazepines or other sedative-hypnotics — additive respiratory depression (FDA black box warning).
⚠ Methadone respiratory depression is DELAYED (peak effect 3-4 hours after a PO dose) and PROLONGED (half-life 8-59 hours) with no ceiling effect — observe the first dose a minimum of 2-4 hours.
⚠ If respiratory depression occurs: titrate naloxone to respiratory rate, NOT full reversal — full reversal precipitates severe withdrawal; a continuous infusion (0.25-0.5 mg/hour) is often needed, and monitoring continues a minimum of 8-12 hours after the last methadone dose.
⚠ Methadone toxicity/overdose requires prolonged observation (minimum 8-12 hours, often 24+ hours) — do NOT discharge early; delayed toxicity is common. Torsades de Pointes: magnesium sulfate 2g IV, overdrive pacing/isoproterenol if refractory, discontinue methadone and other QT-prolonging drugs.
⚠ Do NOT abruptly discontinue methadone in a hospitalized or pregnant patient — precipitates severe withdrawal, AMA risk, and (in pregnancy) fetal distress/preterm labor risk.
⚠ If the patient is not currently enrolled in the claimed OTP or the dose cannot be verified, do NOT give high-dose methadone without verified tolerance — risk of fatal respiratory depression; treat as a new initiation (max 30 mg) or use buprenorphine instead.
⚠ Cannot discharge without a documented OTP referral and appointment within 72 hours under 21 CFR 1306.07(b).
⚠ Methadone is lethal to opioid-naive individuals, including children, at a single adult dose — safe storage counseling and a naloxone kit are mandatory before discharge.

1 · Recognition & the 72-Hour Rule

Methadone is a full mu-opioid agonist used for OUD maintenance. Outside a licensed Opioid Treatment Program (OTP), the 72-hour rule (21 CFR 1306.07(b)) lets any DEA-registered ED provider administer or dispense up to a 3-day supply of methadone for emergency withdrawal management while arranging OTP linkage. The 2023 DEA rule update allows dispensing take-home doses, not just administration.

6 · Monitoring & QTc risk

  • Respiratory rate: q15-30 min x 2h, then q1h x 2h
  • SpO2: continuous or q30 min
  • Sedation: assess response to verbal/tactile stimuli
  • COWS score: before and 2-4 hours after dosing
  • First dose observation minimum 2-4 hours; guest dosing (verified) minimum 30 minutes
  • Red flags: RR < 10, SpO2 < 92%, excessive sedation, slurred speech/ataxia

Obtain an ECG if: dose > 100 mg/day, combining with other QT-prolonging drugs, cardiac disease/syncope/arrhythmia history, family history of long QT, or electrolyte abnormalities (hypoK, hypoMg). ECG NOT required for a single dose <= 40 mg in an otherwise healthy patient, a known stable maintenance dose without prior QTc issues, or guest dosing at verified usual dose.

7 · Respiratory depression & overdose

Respiratory depression is delayed and prolonged
Peak effect 3-4 hours after PO dosing. Half-life 8-59 hours. No ceiling effect (unlike buprenorphine). If it occurs: stimulate patient, support airway, give naloxone 0.04-0.4 mg IV titrated to RR — NOT full reversal (precipitates severe withdrawal, likely AMA). Infusion 0.25-0.5 mg/hour may be needed. Monitor at least 8-12 hours after the last dose.
Methadone toxicity / overdose
Signs: miosis, sedation, slurred speech, respiratory depression (RR < 12), hypotension, QTc prolongation/Torsades de Pointes. Management: airway support, naloxone 0.04-0.4 mg IV titrated to respiratory effort with a continuous infusion often needed, cardiac monitoring, correct electrolytes (K+, Mg2+), and prolonged observation — minimum 8-12 hours, often 24+ hours. For Torsades de Pointes: magnesium sulfate 2g IV, overdrive pacing or isoproterenol if refractory, discontinue methadone and other QT-prolonging drugs. Do NOT discharge early — delayed toxicity is common.

8 · Disposition & OTP linkage

  2. Take-home doses if dispensing under the 72-hour rule: Day 2 40 mg, Day 3 40 mg, clear written instructions, store safely away from children

Verification required before administering. If verified: continue home dose (QTc monitoring if > 100 mg or other risk factors). If unverified: conservative 30-40 mg, verify on next business day. Do not abruptly discontinue — coordinate discharge with OTP.

6 · Monitoring & QTc risk

  • Respiratory rate: q15-30 min x 2h, then q1h x 2h
  • SpO2: continuous or q30 min
  • Sedation: assess response to verbal/tactile stimuli
  • COWS score: before and 2-4 hours after dosing
  • First dose observation minimum 2-4 hours; guest dosing (verified) minimum 30 minutes
  • Red flags: RR < 10, SpO2 < 92%, excessive sedation, slurred speech/ataxia

QTc | Risk

< 450 ms | Low risk

450-499 ms | Moderate risk (discuss risks/benefits, monitor)

>= 500 ms | High risk (avoid methadone if possible, cardiology consult)

Obtain an ECG if: dose > 100 mg/day, combining with other QT-prolonging drugs, cardiac disease/syncope/arrhythmia history, family history of long QT, or electrolyte abnormalities (hypoK, hypoMg). ECG NOT required for a single dose <= 40 mg in an otherwise healthy patient, a known stable maintenance dose without prior QTc issues, or guest dosing at verified usual dose.

QT-prolonging class | Examples

Antiemetics used for withdrawal nausea | Ondansetron, loperamide (especially high/abused doses), prochlorperazine, promethazine

Antipsychotics | Haloperidol, quetiapine, ziprasidone

Antidepressants | Citalopram, escitalopram, amitriptyline

Antibiotics | Fluoroquinolones, azithromycin

CYP3A4 effect | Examples

Inhibitors (increase methadone levels) | Fluconazole, ketoconazole, erythromycin, clarithromycin, ritonavir/other HIV PIs, grapefruit juice

Inducers (decrease methadone levels) | Rifampin (most significant), phenytoin, carbamazepine, phenobarbital, efavirenz, nevirapine

7 · Respiratory depression & overdose

Peak effect 3-4 hours after PO dosing. Half-life 8-59 hours. No ceiling effect (unlike buprenorphine). If it occurs: stimulate patient, support airway, give naloxone 0.04-0.4 mg IV titrated to RR — NOT full reversal (precipitates severe withdrawal, likely AMA). Infusion 0.25-0.5 mg/hour may be needed. Monitor at least 8-12 hours after the last dose.

Signs: miosis, sedation, slurred speech, respiratory depression (RR < 12), hypotension, QTc prolongation/Torsades de Pointes. Management: airway support, naloxone 0.04-0.4 mg IV titrated to respiratory effort with a continuous infusion often needed, cardiac monitoring, correct electrolytes (K+, Mg2+), and prolonged observation — minimum 8-12 hours, often 24+ hours. For Torsades de Pointes: magnesium sulfate 2g IV, overdrive pacing or isoproterenol if refractory, discontinue methadone and other QT-prolonging drugs. Do NOT discharge early — delayed toxicity is common.

8 · Disposition & OTP linkage

  • OTP appointment scheduled within 72 hours
  • Take-home doses if dispensing under the 72-hour rule: Day 2 40 mg, Day 3 40 mg, clear written instructions, store safely away from children
  • Naloxone kit prescribed or provided
  • Return precautions: difficulty breathing, excessive drowsiness, chest pain/palpitations, return of severe withdrawal
  • Documentation: 72-hour rule use, OTP referral information, doses administered and dispensed

Admission criterion

First-dose observation with any respiratory depression

QTc > 500 ms or arrhythmia

Concurrent medical condition requiring inpatient care

Concurrent intoxication requiring monitoring

No safe discharge environment

Suicide risk

Unable to arrange 72-hour OTP linkage

Transportation — OTPs require daily in-person visits initially

Capacity — wait lists at many programs

Hours — most open early morning only

Cost — insurance/Medicaid coverage varies

Distance — rural areas may have limited OTP access

  • OTP referral made (program name, appointment date/time)
  • Patient counseling performed (overdose risk, safe storage, naloxone)

  • For guest dosing: OTP name/phone, name of verifier, verified dose and last dose date
  • Naloxone — prescribe or provide
  • Overdose risk counseling: tolerance decreases rapidly during abstinence, risk highest in first 2 weeks, never use alone, avoid mixing with benzodiazepines/alcohol

Continue methadone dosing (hospital pharmacy can administer). Telemetry if QTc concerns. Addiction medicine/psychiatry consultation. Social work for OTP linkage and barriers assessment. Do not abruptly discontinue.

9 · Do NOT

Critical pitfalls

Do NOT give a patient's claimed OTP dose without verification — call the OTP directly.

Do NOT give methadone with QTc >= 500 ms or a Torsades history — avoid or get cardiology consult.

Do NOT combine methadone with benzodiazepines or other sedative-hypnotics — additive respiratory depression (FDA black box warning).

Do NOT expect immediate respiratory depression — peak effect is delayed 3-4 hours after a PO dose, and the effect is prolonged (half-life 8-59 hours).

Do NOT give a full naloxone reversal dose — titrate to respiratory rate; full reversal precipitates severe withdrawal and likely AMA departure.

Do NOT discharge methadone toxicity/overdose early — minimum 8-12 hours observation, often 24+ hours; delayed toxicity is common.

Do NOT abruptly discontinue methadone in a hospitalized or pregnant patient — precipitates severe withdrawal, AMA risk, or fetal distress/preterm labor.

Do NOT give high-dose methadone to a patient not currently enrolled in the claimed OTP without verified tolerance — risk of fatal respiratory depression.

Do NOT discharge without a documented OTP referral and appointment within 72 hours under 21 CFR 1306.07(b).

Do NOT skip safe-storage counseling — methadone is lethal to opioid-naive individuals, including children, at a single adult dose.
