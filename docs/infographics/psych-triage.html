Psych Triage: Route by Primary Presenting Concern

Adults presenting to the ED with an acute psychiatric symptom (agitation, suicidal ideation, altered mental status, psychosis, anxiety/panic, withdrawal, catatonia, medication reaction, or eating-disorder presentation). Gateway/triage consult — routes to the matching myMedKitt specialty consult for detailed management. Excludes pediatric psychiatric presentations and formal psychiatric admission criteria beyond MARSIPAN medical-stability thresholds.

⚠ Safety first — secure the environment before beginning any psychiatric assessment.
⚠ Rule out organic causes in ALL psychiatric presentations, especially new psychiatric symptoms first appearing after age 45, fluctuating mentation, abnormal vital signs, or focal neurologic findings.
⚠ Agitation: verbal de-escalation (BETA protocol) before chemical restraint — chemical restraint is a last resort, not a first response.
⚠ Suicidal ideation/attempt: 1:1 continuous visual observation and remove ligatures, sharps, and medications from the room immediately.
⚠ Withdrawal: early benzodiazepines prevent withdrawal seizures (peak 12-48h) and delirium tremens (peak 48-96h, mortality 5-15%).
⚠ Catatonia: do NOT give antipsychotics initially — can trigger malignant catatonia or NMS. Lorazepam challenge is first-line.
⚠ Medication reaction: NMS, serotonin syndrome, and dystonia require different treatments — differentiate before treating.
⚠ Eating disorder: MARSIPAN criteria (BMI <13, K+ <3.0, PO4 <0.5, HR <40, BP <90 systolic, QTc >450 ms, syncope, hypoglycemia) signal medical instability requiring admission.

2 · Agitation / aggression

Verbal de-escalation before chemical restraint
Verbal de-escalation (BETA protocol) is first-line; chemical restraint is reserved for de-escalation failure. Rule out organic causes — hypoglycemia, hypoxia, intoxication — before treating as primary psychiatric agitation. Key decision: is this delirium/medical or primary psychiatric? Continue in the Acute Agitation consult for the BETA protocol and chemical restraint options.[2]

3 · Suicidal ideation / attempt

1:1 observation, remove means
1:1 continuous visual observation. Remove ligatures, sharps, and medications from the room immediately, and complete an environmental safety checklist. Screen with ASQ or C-SSRS. Risk stratification drives disposition — discharge vs voluntary vs involuntary hold. Continue in the Suicidal Patient consult.[3]

4 · Altered mental status

Critical question: delirium or primary psychosis? Use history, vitals, exam, mentation, and the red flags below to decide medical workup intensity. Continue in Psych Medical Stability.[4]

  1. Acute onset (hours-days)
  2. Fluctuating course
  3. Inattention — cannot focus on interview
  4. Visual hallucinations
  5. Abnormal vital signs
  6. Age >45 with first psychiatric symptoms

5 · Bizarre behavior / psychosis

Rule out organic causes first
Start with Psych Medical Stability, then continue to Acute Psychosis / First Break. Rule out organic causes FIRST when red flags are present: anti-NMDA receptor encephalitis, stimulant intoxication, medication toxicity, encephalitis/meningitis, metabolic derangement. New-onset psychosis in a patient older than 45 is a medical cause until proven otherwise. If agitated, use Acute Agitation for chemical restraint options.[4][5]

6 · Anxiety / panic

Panic attack vs medical emergency
Before diagnosing panic, rule out pulmonary embolism (PERC, Wells), acute coronary syndrome (especially with risk factors), arrhythmia (check ECG), hyperthyroidism, pheochromocytoma, and stimulant intoxication. If true panic attack: reassurance and a calm environment, breathing exercises, a benzodiazepine if severe, and outpatient psychiatry/therapy referral.[1]
Lorazepam
0.5-1 mg PO/SL
For severe panic attack, after medical mimics (PE, ACS, arrhythmia, hyperthyroidism, pheochromocytoma, stimulant intoxication) are excluded. Not a linked drug-store indication in this tree — see source-conflict note.

7 · Withdrawal symptoms

Early benzodiazepines prevent seizures and DTs
Early treatment prevents withdrawal seizures (peak 12-48h) and delirium tremens (peak 48-96h, mortality 5-15%). High-risk features: prior seizures/DTs, PAWSS ≥4, prolonged heavy use. Continue in the Alcohol Withdrawal consult.[6]
SubstanceAssessment / treatment
Alcohol / BenzodiazepinesCIWA-Ar, benzodiazepines, phenobarbital
OpioidsCOWS score, buprenorphine initiation, clonidine
StimulantsSupportive care, benzodiazepines for agitation

8 · Unresponsive / mute / rigid (catatonia)

Recognize catatonia early — it is treatable. Bush-Francis catatonia signs: immobility/stupor, mutism, staring/fixed gaze, posturing/waxy flexibility, negativism (resistance to movement). Continue in the Catatonia consult.[7]

Lorazepam
2 mg IV challenge
Improvement within 10-15 min strongly supports the diagnosis. If response: continue lorazepam 2 mg q4-6h. If no response: ECT is the next step. Not a linked drug-store indication in this tree — see source-conflict note.
Avoid antipsychotics initially
Antipsychotics given before the catatonia is recognized can trigger malignant catatonia or NMS.[7]

10 · Not eating / purging

Eating disorder emergency — focus on medical stability. Check immediately: electrolytes (K+, PO4, Mg2+ — refeeding risk), ECG (QTc prolongation, bradycardia), glucose, and vitals (bradycardia, orthostatic hypotension).[10]
