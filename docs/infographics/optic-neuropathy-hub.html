Optic Neuropathy Hub — ED Triage

Clinicians evaluating acute or subacute vision loss / optic neuropathy in the ED. Organized by time-to-permanent-damage (vascular, inflammatory, compressive, toxic) rather than by mechanism — Module 2 is the tree's own 'pick the worst fit' triage step, not a mutually exclusive checklist. CRAO is included as the most common sudden-painless-monocular-vision-loss DDx trap even though it is a retinal, not optic-nerve, occlusion.

Sick Check First

5 DO NOT MISS:
1. Arteritic AION (giant cell arteritis) — age >50, jaw claudication, scalp tenderness → immediate high-dose steroids.
2. Methanol / toxic optic neuropathy — bilateral loss, high anion-gap acidosis, exposure.
3. Pituitary apoplexy / compressive lesion — sudden loss + headache + ophthalmoplegia.
4. Optic neuritis (MS/NMOSD) — pain on eye movement, RAPD, dyschromatopsia.
5. Posterior reversible encephalopathy / occipital stroke — cortical bilateral pattern, severe HTN.

  • Vitals — HTN crisis? Fever? Tachycardia?
  • Quick neuro — a bilateral cortical pattern from PRES / occipital stroke / methanol does NOT stop at the optic nerve
  • VA both eyes — count fingers / hand motion / LP / NLP is fine; do NOT waste time on a Snellen chart in a code stroke
  • RAPD — the single most useful sign (present = optic nerve/retina disease, absent = cortical or symmetric pre-chiasmal disease)
  • Color desaturation (red top of a med bottle works)
  • Disc exam — pale, swollen, hyperemic, or normal
  • Pupil, IOP, lid, extraocular movements
RAPD is the key discriminator
RAPD + central scotoma + color desaturation = optic nerve until proven otherwise. Organize by time-to-permanent-damage, NOT by mechanism (vascular / inflammatory / compressive) — what kills the nerve fastest goes first.
Unstable, HTN emergency, methanol, or trauma
Resuscitate in parallel to workup. Do NOT funnel down 'is this AION' — if the patient is unstable, in a hypertensive emergency, methanol is suspected, or there is trauma, treat and image simultaneously.

GCA / Arteritic AION

Clinical clues: age >50 (almost never <50); jaw claudication (highest LR+); scalp tenderness, new temporal HA, PMR symptoms (proximal stiffness / shoulder / hip); constitutional symptoms (weight loss, fever, fatigue); ESR often >50, CRP almost always up (CRP more sensitive than ESR); pale, swollen disc with altitudinal field defect = arteritic AION pattern; amaurosis fugax in the days before frank vision loss is common.

  1. Methylprednisolone 1 g IV NOW — do NOT wait for biopsy, do NOT wait for ESR/CRP if clinical suspicion is high
  2. Send ESR, CRP, CBC (thrombocytosis common), platelets, BMP
  3. Temporal artery biopsy scheduled within 1-2 weeks (steroid does NOT invalidate biopsy in the short window)
  4. Ophthalmology + rheumatology stat
  5. GI prophylaxis PPI + glucose monitoring during high-dose steroid

Traumatic Optic Neuropathy

Clinical clues: new RAPD post-trauma is TON until proven otherwise. Vision loss may be immediate (direct injury) or delayed (canal hematoma / edema). Often paired with other facial / orbital fractures.

  1. CT face + orbits thin cuts (1 mm bone algorithm) — thick cuts miss canal fractures
  2. Ophthalmology + neurosurgery + oculoplastics stat
  3. VA + RAPD + color desat documented at baseline; serial neuro exams
  4. Pain control, head of bed up, avoid Valsalva
  5. Decompression candidates: canalicular fragment + new RAPD + decreasing VA
Steroid decision is institutional
Do NOT give megadose steroid reflexively — CRASH and IOTS data shifted away from routine high-dose methylprednisolone for TBI / TON; institutional decision with consultants.
Missed canal fracture
Missed canal fracture on thick-cut CT (always ask for thin-cut bone algorithm). Don't assume the vision loss is 'just orbital edema' — serial exams matter. Do NOT delay neurosurg / oculoplastics consult on the assumption it's mild.

Pituitary Apoplexy

Clinical clues: sudden severe HA (often retro-orbital or frontal); visual field defect — classically bitemporal hemianopia (chiasm compression) but can be any pattern; ophthalmoplegia (CN III / IV / VI in the cavernous sinus); hypotension out of proportion to clinical state; hyponatremia, hypoglycemia; predisposing pituitary adenoma (often previously undiagnosed).

  1. Hydrocortisone 100 mg IV bolus now (then 50 mg IV q6h or 200 mg/24h continuous if unstable) — apoplexy = adrenal crisis until proven otherwise; steroid BEFORE imaging
  2. Pituitary MRI with gadolinium — dedicated sella cuts (NOT just generic brain MRI)
  3. Neurosurgery + endocrinology stat
  4. Labs: cortisol, ACTH, TSH, free T4, prolactin, IGF-1, LH, FSH, Na, glucose
  5. Visual fields formal once stabilized
  6. Replace electrolytes (Na often low), correct glucose, support BP

Methanol / Toxic Optic Neuropathy

Clinical clues: "Drunk" patient with vision complaints — always get the gas. Bilateral painless vision loss, often progressive over 12-24 h. "Snowfield" vision, central scotoma, eventually optic disc hyperemia then atrophy. High anion gap metabolic acidosis + high osmolar gap. Source: bootleg / moonshine, windshield wiper fluid, antifreeze ingestion (suicidal or accidental peds).

  1. Fomepizole load 15 mg/kg IV over 30 min, then 10 mg/kg q12h × 4 doses, then 15 mg/kg q12h until methanol <20 mg/dL AND pH normal
  2. Ethanol drip alternative if fomepizole unavailable: load 0.6-0.8 g/kg IV/PO, maintain level 100-150 mg/dL
  3. Bicarb drip for pH <7.30
  4. HD threshold: methanol level >50 mg/dL, severe acidosis (pH <7.15 unresponsive), end-organ damage (vision loss IS end-organ), or renal failure
  5. Folinic acid 1 mg/kg IV q4-6h (or folate if folinic unavailable) — accelerates formate metabolism
  6. Send: methanol level, ethylene glycol level, ABG, osm gap, lactate, formate, salicylate / acetaminophen co-ingestion screen
  7. Poison Control stat

Optic Neuritis (Demyelinating)

Classic features: pain with eye movement (>90% of typical MS-ON); color desaturation out of proportion to VA; RAPD, central scotoma; 2/3 retrobulbar (normal disc), 1/3 papillitis (swollen disc); often young female, age 20-50.

  1. MRI brain + orbits with fat-sat + gadolinium
  2. Send AQP4-IgG and MOG-IgG cell-based assays NOW (before steroid — assay is not affected, but send the bloods first)
  3. IV methylprednisolone 1 g IV (1st dose — full course per deep-dive consult)
  4. Neuro-ophthalmology consult; neurology consult
  5. Rule out infectious mimics first: RPR + treponemal, HIV, CXR if TB risk

PRES / Occipital Stroke

This is cortical, NOT an optic nerve problem. Key distinguishing feature: RAPD is ABSENT in cortical / bilateral homonymous patterns (both pathways equally involved) — this is the cleanest bedside discriminator from true optic neuropathy.

  1. BP control to <160 systolic with labetalol or nicardipine drip (PRES) or per stroke protocol
  2. MRI brain (DWI + FLAIR + SWI) — PRES shows posterior parieto-occipital T2 hyperintensity (usually reversible); occipital stroke shows DWI restriction
  3. Stroke pathway in parallel
  4. Magnesium if eclampsia / pre-eclampsia
  5. Look for and remove the trigger: severe HTN, eclampsia, post-transplant immunosuppression (cyclosporine, tacrolimus), chemo, autoimmune flare

Clinical clues for PRES: severe HTN + AMS + seizures + cortical blindness; pregnancy / postpartum (eclampsia / pre-eclampsia); transplant / chemo patients; vision loss is binocular, often described as "blurred" or "tunnel".

Don't lock in "optic nerve"
Don't lock in "optic nerve" without checking RAPD — cortical patterns can be subtle and easy to misroute.

IIH (Papilledema)

Clinical clues: obese young female (classic) — but also OCPs, vitamin A excess, sleep apnea, recent steroid taper, tetracyclines, lithium; bilateral disc swelling (papilledema); HA worse lying down, with Valsalva, in the morning; transient visual obscurations; pulsatile tinnitus; diplopia from CN VI palsy.

  1. MRV first to rule out CVST (same demographic, way worse miss) — this is the most important step
  2. MRI brain with gad (rule out mass / hydrocephalus)
  3. LP with opening pressure measured supine (NOT seated) — >25 cm H2O confirms (>28 cm H2O in obese patients per ICHD-3 / Friedman criteria)
  4. Acetazolamide 500 mg PO BID start after CVST excluded; titrate up to 1-4 g/day per tolerance
  5. Visual field formal
  6. Ophtho + neurology consult

CRAO (Cherry-Red Spot)

This is a RETINAL artery occlusion, not technically optic neuropathy — included in the hub because it presents as sudden painless monocular vision loss and is the most common DDx trap.

  1. Treat as a stroke equivalent — stroke alert / activate stroke team if within tPA window (some centers now thrombolysing CRAO within 4.5 h)
  2. Non-contrast CT head + CTA neck/head (look for carotid embolic source, aortic dissection, ICA stenosis)
  3. ESR + CRP — if elevated and age >50, treat as GCA simultaneously (methylpred 1 g IV)
  4. ECG + telemetry — look for AFib embolic source
  5. Echocardiogram
  6. Hyperbaric oxygen if available within window
  7. Ophthalmology consult

Clinical clues: sudden painless monocular vision loss (often described as "curtain coming down"); cherry-red spot at the fovea (thinned macula reveals choroidal red against the pale ischemic retina); pale retina, attenuated arterioles; RAPD often profound; embolic risk profile (AFib, carotid stenosis, recent vascular procedure).

Brain-equivalent stroke
Treating CRAO as "just an eye problem" instead of a brain-equivalent stroke is a pitfall. The same patient is at high short-term risk for ipsilateral hemispheric stroke — stroke workup is mandatory.

Cervical Artery Dissection

Triad to recognize: Horner syndrome (partial — ptosis + miosis; anhidrosis often absent in carotid dissection because sweat fibers travel with external carotid); neck or face pain (often unilateral, can be severe and migrainous); ischemic event — amaurosis fugax, monocular vision loss, hemispheric TIA / stroke, or cranial nerve palsy (IX, X, XII).

  1. CTA neck + head (or MRA with fat-sat T1 — shows the intramural hematoma)
  2. Antiplatelet vs anticoagulation decision per stroke protocol and clot/hemorrhage status
  3. Stroke + vascular neurology consult
  4. Avoid manipulations of the neck (chiro / massage) and Valsalva activity

Risk factors: recent neck trauma (often trivial — chiropractic, yoga, sports, MVC whiplash), connective tissue disease (Marfan, Ehlers-Danlos, FMD), recent infection, hypertension.

The triad rarely presents all at once
The pain may have started days before the vision loss / TIA — ask carefully about preceding neck or face pain.

Orbital Cellulitis (Compressive Mimicker)

  1. IV broad-spectrum antibiotics covering MRSA + anaerobes — do NOT wait for imaging
  2. CT orbits with contrast (look for subperiosteal or orbital abscess, sinusitis source)
  3. Ophthalmology + ENT consult; neurosurgery if cavernous sinus involvement suspected
  4. VA + EOM + RAPD + pupil + IOP — serial exams
  5. Blood cultures, CBC, CRP

Underdosed empiric antibiotics for orbital cellulitis. Missing cavernous sinus thrombosis — look for bilateral findings, CN III/IV/V1/V2/VI involvement. Not getting CT contrast (non-con CT misses abscess).

Nutritional or Hereditary Optic Neuropathy

No deep-dive consult yet (low ED urgency — workup-heavy, outpatient pathway). Clinical clues: subacute painless central or cecocentral scotoma; bilateral (often simultaneous or sequential within weeks); ETOH abuse, vegan diet, bariatric surgery history, malabsorption, vitamin A excess (paradoxically); family history of Leber's (LHON) — maternal inheritance pattern; slow progressive course (weeks to months).

  1. B12, folate, RBC folate, thiamine (B1), copper, ceruloplasmin (Wilson)
  2. HIV, RPR + treponemal

Rescue / Initial Bundle + Reassess

  • VA both eyes (CF / HM / LP / NLP is fine)
  • Confirm RAPD
  • Ishihara 1-2 plates (color desat documentation)
  • Disc photo via fundus camera if available; dilated exam by ophtho otherwise
  • IOP both eyes — rule out AACG mimicker (AACG hurts, IOP >40, mid-dilated fixed pupil; route to AACG)
  • Formal Humphrey field if available, otherwise confrontation
  • POCUS optic nerve sheath if elevated ICP suspected (adult >5 mm, peds >4.5 mm at 3 mm posterior to globe)
  • ESR, CRP, CBC, BMP, glucose
  • B12, folate, RPR, HIV (demographic-driven)
  • Pregnancy if applicable
Universal
Ophthalmology consult ALWAYS for new optic neuropathy. If ANY suspicion of GCA (age >50, scalp / jaw / PMR / new HA, ESR or CRP up): empiric methylprednisolone 1 g IV NOW — do not wait for biopsy or labs.

Reassess at 60 min: VA stable or improving → continue workup, ophtho disposition. VA dropping or RAPD worsening → STOP, back to Module 2 (something time-critical was missed), re-image, re-consult. New neuro finding → back to Module 2 (PRES / pituitary / methanol / stroke).

Admit

  • Methanol on HD or fomepizole drip — ICU

  • PRES, malignant HTN, occipital stroke — admit per home consult

  • IIH with severe papilledema or VA dropping despite acetazolamide

  • Any unstable patient

  • Single-dose IV methylpred given, outpatient infusion arranged, awaiting ride / transport

Convert to admit if
Worsening VA, new neuro finding, severe side effect of therapy, social barrier preventing safe discharge, or workup result that upgrades acuity (positive AQP4-IgG, severe sinusitis on CT, etc.).

Discharge

  • Stable subacute / chronic presentation with confirmed neuro-ophthalmology follow-up within 1 week
  • Drug-induced with offender stopped (or coordinated stop plan) + outpatient plan
  • Stable nutritional cause with B12 / folate / thiamine replacement started + follow-up
  • Typical MS-ON discharged after first IV methylpred dose with confirmed outpatient infusion
  • VA documented at discharge (compare to arrival)
  • Pain controlled
  • Orthostatic vitals normal (high-dose steroid can drop BP and unmask adrenal issues)
  • No new neuro findings on recheck
  • Follow-up arranged AND confirmed by phone (call the office, do not just hand a phone number)
  • Suicide screen if any vision loss is permanent (acute vision loss has elevated SI rates)
  • Pregnancy plan documented if applicable
  • Patient understands the diagnosis hypothesis and the plan
Return precautions (universal)
Worsening or new vision loss in either eye. Severe headache, fever, neck stiffness. New weakness, numbness, gait change, bladder / bowel. Inability to tolerate oral steroid taper. Severe steroid side effects (mood change, GI bleed signs, glucose crisis).

5 DO NOT MISS:
1. Arteritic AION (giant cell arteritis) — age >50, jaw claudication, scalp tenderness → immediate high-dose steroids.
2. Methanol / toxic optic neuropathy — bilateral loss, high anion-gap acidosis, exposure.
3. Pituitary apoplexy / compressive lesion — sudden loss + headache + ophthalmoplegia.
4. Optic neuritis (MS/NMOSD) — pain on eye movement, RAPD, dyschromatopsia.
5. Posterior reversible encephalopathy / occipital stroke — cortical bilateral pattern, severe HTN.

  • Vitals — HTN crisis? Fever? Tachycardia?
  • Quick neuro — a bilateral cortical pattern from PRES / occipital stroke / methanol does NOT stop at the optic nerve
  • VA both eyes — count fingers / hand motion / LP / NLP is fine; do NOT waste time on a Snellen chart in a code stroke
  • RAPD — the single most useful sign (present = optic nerve/retina disease, absent = cortical or symmetric pre-chiasmal disease)
  • Color desaturation (red top of a med bottle works)
  • Disc exam — pale, swollen, hyperemic, or normal
  • Pupil, IOP, lid, extraocular movements

RAPD + central scotoma + color desaturation = optic nerve until proven otherwise. Organize by time-to-permanent-damage, NOT by mechanism (vascular / inflammatory / compressive) — what kills the nerve fastest goes first.

Resuscitate in parallel to workup. Do NOT funnel down 'is this AION' — if the patient is unstable, in a hypertensive emergency, methanol is suspected, or there is trauma, treat and image simultaneously.

Critical Actions At A Glance (reference)

  • Check RAPD on EVERY new vision loss — the single most useful sign for optic nerve disease.
  • Age >50 + new vision loss + ESR/CRP up = methylprednisolone 1 g IV NOW — do NOT wait for biopsy.
  • Pituitary apoplexy = stress-dose hydrocortisone BEFORE imaging.
  • Methanol / toxic alcohol exposure with vision loss = fomepizole load + hemodialysis threshold check.

GCA / Arteritic AION

Clinical clues: age >50 (almost never <50); jaw claudication (highest LR+); scalp tenderness, new temporal HA, PMR symptoms (proximal stiffness / shoulder / hip); constitutional symptoms (weight loss, fever, fatigue); ESR often >50, CRP almost always up (CRP more sensitive than ESR); pale, swollen disc with altitudinal field defect = arteritic AION pattern; amaurosis fugax in the days before frank vision loss is common.

  • Methylprednisolone 1 g IV NOW — do NOT wait for biopsy, do NOT wait for ESR/CRP if clinical suspicion is high
  • Send ESR, CRP, CBC (thrombocytosis common), platelets, BMP
  • Temporal artery biopsy scheduled within 1-2 weeks (steroid does NOT invalidate biopsy in the short window)
  • Ophthalmology + rheumatology stat
  • GI prophylaxis PPI + glucose monitoring during high-dose steroid

Methylprednisolone: 1 g IV NOW IV — Do not wait for biopsy or ESR/CRP results if clinical suspicion is high. myMedKitt drug-store methylprednisolone entry currently has no GCA-specific indication dosing.

Do NOT delay steroid for biopsy or test results. Cost of overtreating one false-positive with 3 days of IV steroid is trivial vs cost of a permanent contralateral AION (~30% bilateral rate without treatment).

Traumatic Optic Neuropathy

Clinical clues: new RAPD post-trauma is TON until proven otherwise. Vision loss may be immediate (direct injury) or delayed (canal hematoma / edema). Often paired with other facial / orbital fractures.

  • CT face + orbits thin cuts (1 mm bone algorithm) — thick cuts miss canal fractures
  • Ophthalmology + neurosurgery + oculoplastics stat
  • VA + RAPD + color desat documented at baseline; serial neuro exams
  • Pain control, head of bed up, avoid Valsalva
  • Decompression candidates: canalicular fragment + new RAPD + decreasing VA

Do NOT give megadose steroid reflexively — CRASH and IOTS data shifted away from routine high-dose methylprednisolone for TBI / TON; institutional decision with consultants.

Missed canal fracture on thick-cut CT (always ask for thin-cut bone algorithm). Don't assume the vision loss is 'just orbital edema' — serial exams matter. Do NOT delay neurosurg / oculoplastics consult on the assumption it's mild.

Pituitary Apoplexy

Clinical clues: sudden severe HA (often retro-orbital or frontal); visual field defect — classically bitemporal hemianopia (chiasm compression) but can be any pattern; ophthalmoplegia (CN III / IV / VI in the cavernous sinus); hypotension out of proportion to clinical state; hyponatremia, hypoglycemia; predisposing pituitary adenoma (often previously undiagnosed).

  • Hydrocortisone 100 mg IV bolus now (then 50 mg IV q6h or 200 mg/24h continuous if unstable) — apoplexy = adrenal crisis until proven otherwise; steroid BEFORE imaging
  • Pituitary MRI with gadolinium — dedicated sella cuts (NOT just generic brain MRI)
  • Neurosurgery + endocrinology stat
  • Labs: cortisol, ACTH, TSH, free T4, prolactin, IGF-1, LH, FSH, Na, glucose
  • Visual fields formal once stabilized
  • Replace electrolytes (Na often low), correct glucose, support BP

Hydrocortisone: 100 mg IV bolus NOW, before imaging IV — Apoplexy = adrenal crisis until proven otherwise.

Hydrocortisone (maintenance): 50 mg IV q6h OR 200 mg/24h continuous infusion if unstable IV — Taper to oral maintenance over 1-3 days as hemodynamics stabilize.

Missing the adrenal insufficiency and crashing the BP during imaging delays kills more patients than the apoplexy itself.

Methanol / Toxic Optic Neuropathy

Clinical clues: "Drunk" patient with vision complaints — always get the gas. Bilateral painless vision loss, often progressive over 12-24 h. "Snowfield" vision, central scotoma, eventually optic disc hyperemia then atrophy. High anion gap metabolic acidosis + high osmolar gap. Source: bootleg / moonshine, windshield wiper fluid, antifreeze ingestion (suicidal or accidental peds).

  • Fomepizole load 15 mg/kg IV over 30 min, then 10 mg/kg q12h × 4 doses, then 15 mg/kg q12h until methanol <20 mg/dL AND pH normal
  • Ethanol drip alternative if fomepizole unavailable: load 0.6-0.8 g/kg IV/PO, maintain level 100-150 mg/dL
  • Bicarb drip for pH <7.30
  • HD threshold: methanol level >50 mg/dL, severe acidosis (pH <7.15 unresponsive), end-organ damage (vision loss IS end-organ), or renal failure
  • Folinic acid 1 mg/kg IV q4-6h (or folate if folinic unavailable) — accelerates formate metabolism
  • Send: methanol level, ethylene glycol level, ABG, osm gap, lactate, formate, salicylate / acetaminophen co-ingestion screen
  • Poison Control stat

Fomepizole (loading dose): 15 mg/kg IV over 30 min IV

Fomepizole (maintenance): 10 mg/kg IV q12h × 4 doses, then 15 mg/kg IV q12h IV — Continue until methanol/EG level <20 mg/dL and patient asymptomatic. Re-dose immediately after hemodialysis (dialysis removes the drug).

Ethanol (alternative if fomepizole unavailable): Load 0.6-0.8 g/kg IV/PO, maintain level 100-150 mg/dL IV/PO — No genuine myMedKitt drug-store entry for ethanol infusion — dose sourced from consult text only.

Folinic acid (or folate if unavailable): 1 mg/kg IV q4-6h IV — Accelerates formate metabolism. No genuine myMedKitt drug-store entry for folinic acid — dose sourced from consult text only.

mg/dL: Treatment goal reached 0-20; Continue fomepizole + bicarb 20-50; HD threshold reached (>50 mg/dL) 50-100

Fomepizole dose INCREASES AFTER HD — HD removes the drug. Re-dose immediately after the session.

Optic Neuritis (Demyelinating)

Classic features: pain with eye movement (>90% of typical MS-ON); color desaturation out of proportion to VA; RAPD, central scotoma; 2/3 retrobulbar (normal disc), 1/3 papillitis (swollen disc); often young female, age 20-50.

  • MRI brain + orbits with fat-sat + gadolinium
  • Send AQP4-IgG and MOG-IgG cell-based assays NOW (before steroid — assay is not affected, but send the bloods first)
  • IV methylprednisolone 1 g IV (1st dose — full course per deep-dive consult)
  • Neuro-ophthalmology consult; neurology consult
  • Rule out infectious mimics first: RPR + treponemal, HIV, CXR if TB risk

Methylprednisolone: 1 g IV (1st dose — full course per deep-dive consult) IV — myMedKitt drug-store methylprednisolone entry currently has no optic-neuritis-specific indication dosing.

NEVER oral prednisone alone — ONTT showed oral pred monotherapy increases recurrence rate.

Bilateral / severe / no-light-perception / poor recovery → think NMOSD before MS-ON; pulse longer + add PLEX. Always rule out syphilis / TB / HIV before high-dose steroid.

PRES / Occipital Stroke

This is cortical, NOT an optic nerve problem. Key distinguishing feature: RAPD is ABSENT in cortical / bilateral homonymous patterns (both pathways equally involved) — this is the cleanest bedside discriminator from true optic neuropathy.

  • BP control to <160 systolic with labetalol or nicardipine drip (PRES) or per stroke protocol
  • MRI brain (DWI + FLAIR + SWI) — PRES shows posterior parieto-occipital T2 hyperintensity (usually reversible); occipital stroke shows DWI restriction
  • Stroke pathway in parallel
  • Magnesium if eclampsia / pre-eclampsia
  • Look for and remove the trigger: severe HTN, eclampsia, post-transplant immunosuppression (cyclosporine, tacrolimus), chemo, autoimmune flare

Clinical clues for PRES: severe HTN + AMS + seizures + cortical blindness; pregnancy / postpartum (eclampsia / pre-eclampsia); transplant / chemo patients; vision loss is binocular, often described as "blurred" or "tunnel".

Don't lock in "optic nerve" without checking RAPD — cortical patterns can be subtle and easy to misroute.

IIH (Papilledema)

Clinical clues: obese young female (classic) — but also OCPs, vitamin A excess, sleep apnea, recent steroid taper, tetracyclines, lithium; bilateral disc swelling (papilledema); HA worse lying down, with Valsalva, in the morning; transient visual obscurations; pulsatile tinnitus; diplopia from CN VI palsy.

  • MRV first to rule out CVST (same demographic, way worse miss) — this is the most important step
  • MRI brain with gad (rule out mass / hydrocephalus)
  • LP with opening pressure measured supine (NOT seated) — >25 cm H2O confirms (>28 cm H2O in obese patients per ICHD-3 / Friedman criteria)
  • Acetazolamide 500 mg PO BID start after CVST excluded; titrate up to 1-4 g/day per tolerance
  • Visual field formal
  • Ophtho + neurology consult

Acetazolamide: 500 mg PO BID, titrate up to 1-4 g/day per tolerance PO — Start only after CVST excluded. myMedKitt drug-store acetazolamide entry currently has no IIH-specific indication dosing.

cm H2O: Normal opening pressure 0-25; Consistent with IIH (>28 cm H2O in obese patients) 25-40

NOT getting the MRV — CVST mimics IIH and kills; this is the most important diagnostic step. Calling it IIH without ruling out secondary causes (drugs, sleep apnea, endocrine). Discharging without formal visual field — field loss drives surgical urgency (ONSF / shunt).

CRAO (Cherry-Red Spot)

This is a RETINAL artery occlusion, not technically optic neuropathy — included in the hub because it presents as sudden painless monocular vision loss and is the most common DDx trap.

  • Treat as a stroke equivalent — stroke alert / activate stroke team if within tPA window (some centers now thrombolysing CRAO within 4.5 h)
  • Non-contrast CT head + CTA neck/head (look for carotid embolic source, aortic dissection, ICA stenosis)
  • ESR + CRP — if elevated and age >50, treat as GCA simultaneously (methylpred 1 g IV)
  • ECG + telemetry — look for AFib embolic source
  • Echocardiogram
  • Hyperbaric oxygen if available within window
  • Ophthalmology consult

Clinical clues: sudden painless monocular vision loss (often described as "curtain coming down"); cherry-red spot at the fovea (thinned macula reveals choroidal red against the pale ischemic retina); pale retina, attenuated arterioles; RAPD often profound; embolic risk profile (AFib, carotid stenosis, recent vascular procedure).

Treating CRAO as "just an eye problem" instead of a brain-equivalent stroke is a pitfall. The same patient is at high short-term risk for ipsilateral hemispheric stroke — stroke workup is mandatory.

Cervical Artery Dissection

Triad to recognize: Horner syndrome (partial — ptosis + miosis; anhidrosis often absent in carotid dissection because sweat fibers travel with external carotid); neck or face pain (often unilateral, can be severe and migrainous); ischemic event — amaurosis fugax, monocular vision loss, hemispheric TIA / stroke, or cranial nerve palsy (IX, X, XII).

  • CTA neck + head (or MRA with fat-sat T1 — shows the intramural hematoma)
  • Antiplatelet vs anticoagulation decision per stroke protocol and clot/hemorrhage status
  • Stroke + vascular neurology consult
  • Avoid manipulations of the neck (chiro / massage) and Valsalva activity

Risk factors: recent neck trauma (often trivial — chiropractic, yoga, sports, MVC whiplash), connective tissue disease (Marfan, Ehlers-Danlos, FMD), recent infection, hypertension.

The pain may have started days before the vision loss / TIA — ask carefully about preceding neck or face pain.

Orbital Cellulitis (Compressive Mimicker)

  • IV broad-spectrum antibiotics covering MRSA + anaerobes — do NOT wait for imaging
  • CT orbits with contrast (look for subperiosteal or orbital abscess, sinusitis source)
  • Ophthalmology + ENT consult; neurosurgery if cavernous sinus involvement suspected
  • VA + EOM + RAPD + pupil + IOP — serial exams
  • Blood cultures, CBC, CRP

Vancomycin + Ceftriaxone (± metronidazole): Vancomycin + ceftriaxone IV (± metronidazole; ampicillin-sulbactam if MRSA risk low) IV — Tree gives class-level empiric coverage, not orbital-cellulitis-specific mg/kg numbers — no matching orbital-cellulitis indication exists yet in the myMedKitt drug-store for vancomycin or ceftriaxone.

Distinguishes from preseptal cellulitis (eyelid only): proptosis (Hertel measurement if available); painful or restricted EOM; pain with eye movement; decreased VA / color desat; RAPD (compressive optic neuropathy); chemosis; fever / leukocytosis often (but can be absent).

Underdosed empiric antibiotics for orbital cellulitis. Missing cavernous sinus thrombosis — look for bilateral findings, CN III/IV/V1/V2/VI involvement. Not getting CT contrast (non-con CT misses abscess).

Nutritional or Hereditary Optic Neuropathy

No deep-dive consult yet (low ED urgency — workup-heavy, outpatient pathway). Clinical clues: subacute painless central or cecocentral scotoma; bilateral (often simultaneous or sequential within weeks); ETOH abuse, vegan diet, bariatric surgery history, malabsorption, vitamin A excess (paradoxically); family history of Leber's (LHON) — maternal inheritance pattern; slow progressive course (weeks to months).

  • B12, folate, RBC folate, thiamine (B1), copper, ceruloplasmin (Wilson)
  • HIV, RPR + treponemal
  • TSH
  • Heavy metal screen if exposure history (lead, mercury, arsenic)
  • ANA, ENA if autoimmune features
  • If Leber suspected (young male, sequential bilateral, family history): mtDNA testing for 11778, 14484, 3460 (G→A)
  • Pregnancy test if female of reproductive age (B12 deficiency Rx differs)
  • Outpatient neuro-ophthalmology referral — urgent 1-2 weeks

Thiamine: 100 mg IM or IV daily × 3 days then PO IM/IV then PO — Empiric repletion while workup pends (low risk, high yield). myMedKitt drug-store thiamine entry lists Wernicke/hyponatremia/hyperemesis indications, not this specific nutritional-optic-neuropathy regimen.

Vitamin B12: 1000 mcg IM weekly × 4 then monthly IM — No genuine myMedKitt drug-store entry for B12 (cyanocobalamin) — dose sourced from consult text only.

Folate: 1 mg PO daily PO — No genuine myMedKitt drug-store entry for folate — dose sourced from consult text only. Address alcohol use disorder, refer for nutrition.

Leber's is often misdiagnosed as MS-ON early — if recovery doesn't happen at 6 weeks and family history is suggestive, get genetic testing.

Drug-Induced Optic Neuropathy

No deep-dive consult yet (outpatient pathway in most cases).

Drug | Pattern | Reversibility

Ethambutol | Dose-dependent (>15-25 mg/kg/day); bilateral central scotoma, decreased red-green color | Often irreversible past 6 months

Amiodarone | Bilateral, slowly progressive, optic disc swelling | Usually reversible if caught early

Isoniazid (INH) | Similar to ethambutol but rarer | Variable

Sildenafil / tadalafil (PDE-5) | Rare NAION presentation; bilateral or sequential, vascular risk factors common | Variable

Linezolid | Prolonged use >28 days, peripheral neuropathy often co-presents | Variable

Hydroxychloroquine | Retinopathy more common than optic neuropathy; high-dose / long-duration | Variable

Methotrexate, vincristine, cisplatin, tamoxifen | Chemo-related | Variable

Topiramate | Acute angle-closure glaucoma (NOT a true optic neuropathy but mimics) | Reversible with drug cessation

Tacrolimus / cyclosporine | PRES pattern | Variable

  • Stop the offender (call the prescriber — do NOT unilaterally stop critical drugs like amiodarone in a heart failure patient; coordinate)
  • Document baseline VA, color, fields, RAPD
  • Neuro-ophthalmology urgent referral (1-2 weeks)
  • Investigate alternative agents in coordination with the specialty managing the underlying disease
  • Patient education on reversibility (ethambutol often is NOT reversible past 6 months of high-dose exposure)

Assuming reversibility (especially ethambutol). Unilaterally stopping a critical drug without specialist coordination. Missing the drug history — always ask about new prescriptions, supplements, and OTC use.

Rescue / Initial Bundle + Reassess

  • VA both eyes (CF / HM / LP / NLP is fine)
  • Confirm RAPD
  • Ishihara 1-2 plates (color desat documentation)
  • Disc photo via fundus camera if available; dilated exam by ophtho otherwise
  • IOP both eyes — rule out AACG mimicker (AACG hurts, IOP >40, mid-dilated fixed pupil; route to AACG)
  • Formal Humphrey field if available, otherwise confrontation
  • POCUS optic nerve sheath if elevated ICP suspected (adult >5 mm, peds >4.5 mm at 3 mm posterior to globe)
  • ESR, CRP, CBC, BMP, glucose
  • B12, folate, RPR, HIV (demographic-driven)
  • Pregnancy if applicable

Ophthalmology consult ALWAYS for new optic neuropathy. If ANY suspicion of GCA (age >50, scalp / jaw / PMR / new HA, ESR or CRP up): empiric methylprednisolone 1 g IV NOW — do not wait for biopsy or labs.

Reassess at 60 min: VA stable or improving → continue workup, ophtho disposition. VA dropping or RAPD worsening → STOP, back to Module 2 (something time-critical was missed), re-image, re-consult. New neuro finding → back to Module 2 (PRES / pituitary / methanol / stroke).

Admit
