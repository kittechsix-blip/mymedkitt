Unfractionated Heparin (UFH) Infusion Protocol

Adults in the ED/inpatient setting requiring IV unfractionated heparin (VTE/PE, ACS/STEMI adjunct, or a no-bolus/reduced-intensity start). Excludes patients with active major bleeding, suspected/confirmed HIT, or an unresolved contraindication — see Contraindications.

⚠ Before starting: confirm indication, bleeding risk, platelet count, baseline aPTT/anti-Xa availability, recent anticoagulants, and whether a bolus is appropriate.
⚠ Do NOT start UFH with active major bleeding, suspected/confirmed HIT, severe uncontrolled hypertension with bleeding risk, a recent high-risk surgery/procedure not cleared for anticoagulation, ICH/unsecured high-risk lesion (unless specialist-directed), or platelets critically low/rapidly falling without explanation.
⚠ VTE/PE: 80 units/kg IV bolus, then 18 units/kg/hr infusion (actual body weight) — hold bolus and coordinate timing when systemic thrombolysis is planned for massive PE.
⚠ ACS/STEMI adjunct uses LOWER caps than VTE and the STEMI-fibrinolytic and NSTEMI/UA regimens are NOT identical: STEMI+fibrinolytic 60 units/kg bolus (max 4,000 units) / 12 units/kg/hr infusion (initial max 1,000 units/hr); NSTEMI/UA 60-70 units/kg bolus (max 5,000 units) / 12-15 units/kg/hr infusion, aPTT goal 50-70 sec.
⚠ Use a no-bolus/reduced-intensity start when full-bolus bleeding risk is unacceptable (post-thrombolysis restart, recent procedure/ICH/spinal pathology after specialist clearance, very high bleeding risk/frailty, residual anticoagulant effect).
⚠ Major bleeding or urgent reversal: stop UFH, give protamine 1 mg per 100 units UFH received in the prior 2-3 hr (max 50 mg), infused slowly over 10 min — do not give excess protamine.

Overview

Use unfractionated heparin (UFH) when rapid titration, short half-life, procedural flexibility, renal failure, or planned thrombolysis/procedure makes it preferable to LMWH or DOAC therapy.

  • Confirm indication
  • Confirm bleeding risk
  • Baseline platelet count
  • Baseline aPTT/anti-Xa availability
  • Recent anticoagulants received
  • Whether a bolus is appropriate

▶ What to do now

Contraindication / HIT concern — do NOT start
Do not start UFH until clarified. If anticoagulation is still required, discuss non-heparin anticoagulant options with pharmacy/hematology.
VTE / PE — standard dosing
80 units/kg IV bolus, then 18 units/kg/hr infusion (actual body weight unless local protocol specifies adjusted weight). Massive PE with planned thrombolysis: hold bolus and coordinate timing with the thrombolytic protocol; many protocols restart without bolus once aPTT/anti-Xa permits.
STEMI + fibrinolytic
60 units/kg IV bolus (max 4,000 units); infusion 12 units/kg/hr (initial max 1,000 units/hr), per ACC/AHA STEMI guideline. Titrate by institutional aPTT/anti-Xa nomogram.
NSTEMI / unstable angina
60-70 units/kg IV bolus (max 5,000 units); infusion 12-15 units/kg/hr; aPTT goal 50-70 sec, per AHA/ACC NSTE-ACS guideline. Avoid duplicate anticoagulants if the patient already received LMWH/DOAC.
No-bolus / modified start
Use a no-bolus or reduced-intensity nomogram. Document the reason for no bolus and the target monitoring range; pharmacy selects the institutional low-intensity/no-bolus nomogram.

No-bolus / modified start — examples

  • Post-thrombolysis restart
  • Recent procedure/surgery after specialist clearance
  • Intracranial or spinal pathology after specialist clearance
  • Very high bleeding risk or frailty
  • Transition from another anticoagulant with residual effect

Disposition / transition

Admit/ICU based on indication and instability. VTE: transition to DOAC, LMWH, warfarin bridge, or procedural plan when safe. ACS/STEMI: coordinate with cardiology/cath lab and antiplatelets. PE thrombolysis: restart anticoagulation per protocol once safe. HIT concern: stop all heparin products and use a non-heparin anticoagulant strategy. Every handoff should state the target range, next lab time, and bolus/no-bolus rationale.

Management algorithm

UFH Infusion Protocol — UFH is preferred when titratability, reversibility, or renal/procedural flexibility matters. Confirm indication, bleeding risk, platelets, baseline aPTT/anti-Xa, recent anticoagulants, bolus appropriateness before starting.

Which heparin pathway? — Heparin dosing depends on indication and bolus safety.

VTE/PE: 80 u/kg bolus, 18 u/kg/hr infusion — Standard VTE UFH is 80 units/kg bolus then 18 units/kg/hr, adjusted by protocol. Coordinate timing if thrombolysis planned.

ACS/STEMI adjunct: lower bolus/infusion caps — STEMI+fibrinolytic 60u/kg (max 4,000u)/12u/kg/hr (max 1,000u/hr); NSTEMI/UA 60-70u/kg (max 5,000u)/12-15u/kg/hr, aPTT 50-70 sec — two distinct regimens, not identical.

No-bolus / modified start — No-bolus UFH is appropriate when therapeutic anticoagulation is needed but bolus risk is too high (post-thrombolysis, recent procedure, ICH/spinal pathology after clearance, frailty, residual anticoagulant effect).

Contraindication / HIT — do NOT start — Active major bleeding, suspected/confirmed HIT, uncontrolled severe HTN with bleeding risk, recent high-risk surgery not cleared, ICH/unsecured high-risk lesion, or critically low/falling platelets. Discuss non-heparin anticoagulant with pharmacy/hematology.

Monitoring: nomogram aPTT/anti-Xa, platelets, bleeding checks — First level commonly 6 hr after start and 6 hr after dose changes. Platelets daily or per protocol to screen for HIT. Anti-Xa preferred when aPTT unreliable.

Adjustment / handoff per local nomogram — Handoff includes indication/target range, actual weight, bolus given or omitted, current rate, last level/next due, bleeding + platelet trend, transition plan.
