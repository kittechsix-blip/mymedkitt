First Trimester Emergencies — ED Management

Pregnant patients <14 weeks gestation (first trimester) in the ED with abdominal pain, vaginal bleeding, nausea/vomiting, dysuria, or RLQ pain. Excludes second/third-trimester emergencies, labor, and postpartum complications.

⚠ Unstable first-trimester patient = ruptured ectopic until proven otherwise — 2 large-bore IVs, type & crossmatch, activate MTP, bedside FAST/pelvic US; do NOT delay for β-hCG or imaging.
⚠ Ruptured or unstable ectopic requires emergent OB surgical consult (salpingectomy/salpingostomy/laparotomy) — do not delay for additional workup.
⚠ Septic abortion (fever + uterine tenderness + purulent discharge) requires emergent broad-spectrum IV antibiotics AND surgical evacuation in parallel — do not delay evacuation for antibiotics to take effect; may progress rapidly to septic shock.

Disposition & Return Precautions

  • Admit: hemodynamic instability, ruptured ectopic (OR), pyelonephritis (IV antibiotics), appendicitis (surgical), hyperemesis refractory to ED treatment, septic abortion
  • Discharge: hemodynamically stable, diagnosis established or safe outpatient follow-up plan, tolerating oral intake/medications, reliable follow-up (especially PUL — 48h recheck), adequate pain control
  • ALL patients: OB follow-up arranged, clear return precautions documented
  • Soaking >1 pad/hour for 2+ consecutive hours
  • Fever >100.4°F (38°C)
  • Foul-smelling discharge
  • Lightheadedness, dizziness, or syncope

Nonobstetric — UTI, Pyelonephritis, Appendicitis

**Asymptomatic bacteriuria in pregnancy MUST be treated.** Untreated risk of progression to pyelonephritis is **2.4%** vs **0.6%** with treatment. Send urine culture — a negative UA does not reliably exclude bacteriuria.

Amoxicillin: 875 mg PO BID × 7 days PO — First-line option for UTI/asymptomatic bacteriuria in pregnancy.

Cephalexin: 500 mg PO q6h × 7 days PO — Pregnancy-safe across all trimesters.

Nitrofurantoin: 100 mg PO BID × 7 days PO — ACOG: first trimester only "when no other suitable alternatives are available." Safe in 2nd/3rd trimester.

Ceftriaxone: 1 g IV daily IV — Admit ALL pregnant patients with pyelonephritis for IV antibiotics — insufficient evidence for outpatient management. Continue until afebrile 48 hours, then oral step-down guided by cultures.

Imaging | Performance

Ultrasound (first) | Visualization as low as 7%, sensitivity only 18%

MRI without contrast (preferred) | 94% sensitivity, 97% specificity — safe in first trimester

CT abdomen/pelvis | If US indeterminate and MRI unavailable — single study does not exceed fetal-harm threshold

Disposition & Return Precautions

  • Admit: hemodynamic instability, ruptured ectopic (OR), pyelonephritis (IV antibiotics), appendicitis (surgical), hyperemesis refractory to ED treatment, septic abortion
  • Discharge: hemodynamically stable, diagnosis established or safe outpatient follow-up plan, tolerating oral intake/medications, reliable follow-up (especially PUL — 48h recheck), adequate pain control
  • ALL patients: OB follow-up arranged, clear return precautions documented
  • Soaking >1 pad/hour for 2+ consecutive hours
  • Fever >100.4°F (38°C)
  • Foul-smelling discharge
  • Lightheadedness, dizziness, or syncope
  • Severe or worsening abdominal pain, or shoulder pain (referred diaphragmatic irritation)
