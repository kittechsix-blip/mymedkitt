Malaria — ED Diagnosis & Treatment

Adults and children with fever after travel to a malaria-endemic region (within 12 months), ED/inpatient setting. Species identification (thick/thin smear) and WHO 2023 severity criteria drive treatment selection. Excludes chemoprophylaxis-counseling detail and exact pediatric weight-band dosing tables (see Pharmacy modal / full consult).

⚠ Any fever in a traveler returning from a malaria-endemic area (within 12 months) is malaria until proven otherwise — admit ALL P. falciparum cases, even if initially uncomplicated.
⚠ Parasitemia >10% (or >250,000/mcL) = severe malaria — ICU admission and immediate IV artesunate.
⚠ IV artesunate is the ONLY treatment for severe malaria. Obtain via CDC IND protocol — CDC Malaria Hotline 770-488-7788 (M-F 9am-5pm ET) or 770-488-7100 (24/7).
⚠ Quinidine gluconate is NO LONGER available in the US (production ended 2017, last stock expired March 2019) — do not delay treatment; bridge with interim oral ACT while IV artesunate is en route.
⚠ G6PD testing is MANDATORY before primaquine — dose-dependent hemolysis if G6PD-deficient.
⚠ NEVER use artemisinin monotherapy — always combination therapy (ACT); monotherapy promotes resistance.
⚠ Avoid steroids in cerebral malaria — increases mortality, does not help.
⚠ Do NOT use the same drug class for treatment that was used for chemoprophylaxis (e.g., atovaquone-proguanil prophylaxis failure) — resistance is likely.
⚠ Species ID pending? Treat empirically as P. falciparum while awaiting speciation — do not delay treatment.

Initial assessment & diagnostics

Malaria is a life-threatening parasitic infection transmitted by *Anopheles* mosquitoes. Species identification drives all treatment decisions — *P. falciparum* can kill within 24-48 hours if untreated. Call the CDC Malaria Hotline for guidance on any severe or complex case: 770-488-7788 (M-F 9am-5pm ET) or 770-488-7100 (24/7 after hours).

SpeciesIncubation period
P. falciparum7-14 days (most within 30 days)
P. vivax / P. ovale12-18 days (can be >6 months — dormant liver stages)
P. malariae18-40 days
P. knowlesi9-12 days
  • Altered mentation, seizures
  • Respiratory distress, hypoxemia
  • Hypotension, shock
  • Jaundice, dark urine (hemolysis)
  • Severe anemia
  • Acute kidney injury, oliguria

Thick smear concentrates parasites (higher sensitivity for detection). Thin smear allows species identification and parasitemia count. RDT (HRP-2/pLDH antigen) is sensitive for *P. falciparum* but cannot replace smears — no species ID or parasitemia %. Repeat smears q12-24h if initial negative but suspicion remains high.

SEVERE MALARIA → IV artesunate now

Uncomplicated P. falciparum → ACT
Artemether-lumefantrine or atovaquone-proguanil — never artemisinin monotherapy. Admit even though uncomplicated (can deteriorate within 24-48h).
P. vivax / P. ovale → chloroquine + primaquine

P. malariae / P. knowlesi → chloroquine

Severe malaria — IV artesunate

IV artesunate is the ONLY treatment for severe malaria. Call the CDC Malaria Hotline immediately: 770-488-7788 (M-F 9am-5pm ET) or 770-488-7100 (24/7 after hours) to request IV artesunate via the CDC IND (Investigational New Drug) protocol — delivered to bedside within hours.

IV Artesunate
2.4 mg/kg IV at 0, 12, and 24h, then daily
IV
Adults and children ≥20 kg. Continue until can tolerate oral ACT (usually 3-7 days), then complete a 3-day oral ACT course.
IV Artesunate (pediatric)
3 mg/kg IV at 0, 12, and 24h, then daily
IV
Pediatric <20 kg — higher mg/kg dose than adults.
Quinidine gluconate discontinued in the US
Quinidine gluconate is NO LONGER available in the US (production ended 2017, last stock expired March 2019). If IV artesunate will be delayed, begin interim oral artemether-lumefantrine (or another oral ACT) while it is en route from the CDC — do not delay treatment.
  • Cerebral malaria: airway/aspiration precautions, benzodiazepines for seizures, avoid steroids (increases mortality)
  • Hypoglycemia (<40 mg/dL): glucose q4-6h, dextrose boluses PRN, consider D10 infusion
  • Acute kidney injury: fluid resuscitation avoiding overload, dialysis if severe
  • Severe anemia (Hgb <7): liberal transfusion threshold
  • ARDS/pulmonary edema: restrictive fluid strategy, mechanical ventilation if needed
  • Acidosis: correct underlying cause; bicarbonate generally NOT recommended unless pH <7.1
  • Shock: crystalloid resuscitation, vasopressors if fluid-refractory
Post-artesunate delayed hemolysis
Monitor CBC weekly × 4 weeks after IV artesunate — post-artesunate delayed hemolysis occurs in 10-15% of cases, 1-4 weeks after treatment.

Uncomplicated malaria — species-specific regimens

Artemisinin-based combination therapy (ACT) is first-line for uncomplicated chloroquine-resistant *P. falciparum* (most regions worldwide). NEVER use artemisinin monotherapy — promotes resistance.

4 tablets (20 mg artemether / 120 mg lumefantrine per tablet) PO BID × 3 days

PO

Species pending
If RDT positive or high clinical suspicion but speciation is pending: do not delay treatment — treat as *P. falciparum* (most dangerous) pending confirmation, then reassess when species ID returns.

P. vivax / P. ovale — radical cure

*P. vivax* and *P. ovale* form dormant liver-stage hypnozoites that can cause relapse months to years later. Primaquine is REQUIRED for radical cure — without it, relapse occurs in 50-80% of patients.

G6PD MANDATORY before primaquine
G6PD testing is MANDATORY before primaquine — dose-dependent hemolysis in G6PD-deficient patients. If recently transfused, G6PD may be falsely normal — defer primaquine or retest in 3 months.

Special populations

Severe malaria in pregnancy (any trimester): IV artesunate — benefits outweigh risks. Uncomplicated, 1st trimester: quinine + clindamycin × 7 days (no matching myMedKitt drug-reference entry — verify local dosing). Uncomplicated, 2nd/3rd trimester: artemether-lumefantrine, quinine + clindamycin, or mefloquine. P. vivax/ovale: chloroquine now, defer primaquine until after delivery, bridge with chloroquine prophylaxis 300 mg base weekly. Admit all pregnant patients with malaria.

Children have higher risk of severe malaria, hypoglycemia, seizures, and severe anemia. Children <5 years have the highest *P. falciparum* mortality — admit all. IV artesunate pediatric dosing is 3 mg/kg (see above). Exact pediatric weight-band tablet dosing for artemether-lumefantrine, atovaquone-proguanil, and chloroquine is in the Pharmacy modal.

Breakthrough malaria despite chemoprophylaxis does not exclude the diagnosis. Do NOT use the same drug class for treatment that was used for prophylaxis — resistance is likely. If on atovaquone-proguanil prophylaxis, treat with artemether-lumefantrine or quinine + doxycycline instead.

  2. Day 7: should be negative — if still positive, treatment failure (consult ID, switch regimen)
  3. Day 14 and 28: confirm sustained clearance
  4. If IV artesunate was given: CBC weekly × 4 weeks for post-artesunate delayed hemolysis

Malaria unlikely — pursue alternative diagnoses — Rare exceptions: airport malaria, congenital transmission, blood transfusion. Reconfirm travel history if suspicion persists.

Assess symptom timing + red flags for severe disease — Fever, rigors, chills, headache, myalgias, GI symptoms. Incubation 7 days to >6 months depending on species.

Severe malaria — IV artesunate

**IV artesunate is the ONLY treatment for severe malaria.** Call the CDC Malaria Hotline immediately: **770-488-7788** (M-F 9am-5pm ET) or **770-488-7100** (24/7 after hours) to request IV artesunate via the CDC IND (Investigational New Drug) protocol — delivered to bedside within hours.

IV Artesunate: 2.4 mg/kg IV at 0, 12, and 24h, then daily IV — Adults and children ≥20 kg. Continue until can tolerate oral ACT (usually 3-7 days), then complete a 3-day oral ACT course.

IV Artesunate (pediatric): 3 mg/kg IV at 0, 12, and 24h, then daily IV — Pediatric <20 kg — higher mg/kg dose than adults.

**Quinidine gluconate is NO LONGER available in the US** (production ended 2017, last stock expired March 2019). If IV artesunate will be delayed, begin interim oral artemether-lumefantrine (or another oral ACT) while it is en route from the CDC — do not delay treatment.

  • Cerebral malaria: airway/aspiration precautions, benzodiazepines for seizures, avoid steroids (increases mortality)
  • Hypoglycemia (<40 mg/dL): glucose q4-6h, dextrose boluses PRN, consider D10 infusion
  • Acute kidney injury: fluid resuscitation avoiding overload, dialysis if severe
  • Severe anemia (Hgb <7): liberal transfusion threshold
  • ARDS/pulmonary edema: restrictive fluid strategy, mechanical ventilation if needed
  • Acidosis: correct underlying cause; bicarbonate generally NOT recommended unless pH <7.1
  • Shock: crystalloid resuscitation, vasopressors if fluid-refractory

Monitor **CBC weekly × 4 weeks** after IV artesunate — post-artesunate delayed hemolysis occurs in 10-15% of cases, 1-4 weeks after treatment.

Uncomplicated malaria — species-specific regimens

Artemisinin-based combination therapy (ACT) is first-line for uncomplicated chloroquine-resistant *P. falciparum* (most regions worldwide). **NEVER use artemisinin monotherapy** — promotes resistance.

Artemether-Lumefantrine (Coartem): 4 tablets (20 mg artemether / 120 mg lumefantrine per tablet) PO BID × 3 days PO — Take with fatty food — absorption 2-3x higher. Adult dose ≥35 kg; total 6 doses at 0, 8, 24, 36, 48, 60 hours.

Atovaquone-Proguanil (Malarone): 4 tablets (250 mg atovaquone / 100 mg proguanil per tablet) PO daily × 3 days PO — Alternative ACT if artemether-lumefantrine unavailable. Take with food or a milky drink.

Quinine sulfate 650 mg PO TID plus doxycycline 100 mg PO BID (or clindamycin) is a poorly-tolerated fallback when ACT is unavailable. This oral quinine regimen has no matching entry in myMedKitt's drug reference — verify local formulary dosing before use.

Chloroquine Phosphate: 1000 mg (600 mg base) PO initial dose, then 500 mg (300 mg base) at 6, 24, and 48 hours PO — For confirmed chloroquine-sensitive P. falciparum, or P. vivax/ovale/malariae/knowlesi blood-stage treatment.

Chloroquine Phosphate (pediatric): 10 mg base/kg (max 600 mg base) initial, then 5 mg base/kg at 6, 24, 48h PO — Weight-based; initial dose capped at 600 mg base.

If RDT positive or high clinical suspicion but speciation is pending: do not delay treatment — treat as *P. falciparum* (most dangerous) pending confirmation, then reassess when species ID returns.

P. vivax / P. ovale — radical cure

*P. vivax* and *P. ovale* form dormant liver-stage hypnozoites that can cause relapse months to years later. **Primaquine is REQUIRED for radical cure** — without it, relapse occurs in 50-80% of patients.

**G6PD testing is MANDATORY before primaquine** — dose-dependent hemolysis in G6PD-deficient patients. If recently transfused, G6PD may be falsely normal — defer primaquine or retest in 3 months.

Primaquine: 30 mg base (52.6 mg salt) PO daily × 14 days PO — G6PD normal. Start after the chloroquine blood-stage course. Take with food to reduce nausea.

Primaquine (high-dose alternative): 0.5 mg/kg base PO daily × 14 days PO — Alternative regimen — higher cure rates, more GI side effects.

Primaquine (G6PD-deficient regimen): 45 mg base (79 mg salt) PO once weekly × 8 weeks PO — Under specialist guidance. Monitor CBC weekly for hemolysis.

Admit all pregnant patients with malaria.

Children have higher risk of severe malaria, hypoglycemia, seizures, and severe anemia. Children <5 years have the highest *P. falciparum* mortality — admit all. IV artesunate pediatric dosing is 3 mg/kg (see above). Exact pediatric weight-band tablet dosing for artemether-lumefantrine, atovaquone-proguanil, and chloroquine is in the Pharmacy modal.
