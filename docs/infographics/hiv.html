HIV in the Emergency Department

Adults presenting to the ED with known, suspected, or undiagnosed HIV infection. Covers HIV status assessment, acute seroconversion/new diagnosis, well-controlled-HIV complications, immunocompromised/opportunistic-infection workup, ARV medication effects, and PEP/PrEP. Excludes pediatric HIV, perinatal transmission, and routine outpatient ART management.

⚠ Never stop HAART for GI side effects or minor adverse effects — the only clear indications to stop are Stevens-Johnson syndrome and nevirapine-associated hepatic failure; involve the patient's HIV provider for any other change.
⚠ If the patient is HBV co-infected, stopping tenofovir (TDF/TAF), emtricitabine, or lamivudine risks a fatal hepatitis B flare — resume HBV-active ART immediately and consult ID urgently.
⚠ PEP is NOT recommended when care is sought more than 72 hours after exposure — start as soon as possible, ideally within 2 hours.
⚠ Abacavir hypersensitivity is linked to HLA-B*5701 — if positive, abacavir is contraindicated, and after a hypersensitivity reaction it must NEVER be rechallenged (can be fatal).
⚠ PI-associated kidney stones (atazanavir, indinavir) are RADIOLUCENT and may be invisible on standard noncontrast CT — look for hydroureter and perinephric stranding.
⚠ A normal nonenhanced CT does not rule out a CNS opportunistic infection in an immunocompromised patient with neurologic symptoms — contrast CT or MRI is required.
⚠ CD4 <200, detectable viral load, unknown status, or not in care = AIDS-range risk for opportunistic infection — keep a low threshold for admission and start/resume TMP-SMX prophylaxis.

1 · Assess HIV status

HIV is now a manageable chronic disease with HAART, but ~14% of HIV-infected persons remain unaware of their status, driving continued transmission. Approximately ~80% of new transmissions are from persons not yet diagnosed or not in regular care. The ED identifies undiagnosed infection, manages complications of chronic HIV and its treatment, initiates PEP for exposures, and refers PrEP candidates.

Key first step
Determine the patient's HIV disease status first — CD4 count and viral load drive the entire differential.
  • Most recent CD4 count and viral load (or at least whether VL is "undetectable")
  • Current antiretroviral medications and compliance
  • Prior opportunistic infections
  • Recent travel or sick contacts
ALC is a soft signal, not a substitute for CD4
An absolute lymphocyte count <950 cells/mcL may suggest CD4 <200, though guidelines do not advocate this as a reliable estimate. CD4/VL can be ordered from the ED but results are typically not available for real-time decisions.

CDC (2006): routine opt-out screening ages 13-64 where undiagnosed prevalence ≥0.1%. USPSTF (Grade A): routine screening ages 15-65. 4th-generation tests (antibody + p24 antigen) detect infection as early as 2 weeks post-exposure — preferred initial test. A 2018 study of >200,000 ED patients showed a 0.4% new-diagnosis yield with nontargeted 4th-gen screening, of which 14.5% were acute HIV.

75-90% of patients develop an acute illness during seroconversion: fever, headache, malaise, sore throat without exudate, generalized lymphadenopathy, GI symptoms, genital/mucocutaneous ulcers, and a nondescript macular rash. Notably absent: respiratory or pulmonary symptoms. The syndrome lasts weeks.

Well-controlled patients (CD4 >200, undetectable VL, adherent) are living longer and now present with typical diseases of aging, medication adverse effects, and chronic inflammatory-state complications rather than opportunistic infections. HBV reactivation — stopping therapy
Tenofovir alafenamide (TAF), tenofovir disoproxil fumarate (TDF), emtricitabine (FTC), and lamivudine (3TC) all have dual HIV/HBV activity. If a co-infected patient stops one of these, acute HBV flare can occur — fatalities have been reported. ~10% of HIV patients are co-infected with HBV. The only clear indications to stop HAART are Stevens-Johnson syndrome and nevirapine-associated hepatic failure; all other decisions to stop require the HIV provider.

7 · PEP & PrEP

PEP is most effective when given ASAP — ideally within 2 hours of exposure. NOT recommended when care is sought more than 72 hours after exposure. Indications: sexual exposure, needlestick/percutaneous, mucous membrane exposure, sexual assault. Baseline HIV test required. Adherence is a challenge: one study showed <60% follow-up rates and <25% completed the full 28-day course.

[Biktarvy](#/drug/biktarvy/hiv pep) (bictegravir/emtricitabine/tenofovir alafenamide)
1 tablet (50mg/200mg/25mg) PO once daily × 28 days
PO
CDC 2025 preferred single-tablet regimen. Start ASAP, ideally within 2 hours; NOT effective if started >72 hours post-exposure.
[TDF/FTC](#/drug/tdf-ftc/hiv pep) + [Dolutegravir](#/drug/dolutegravir/hiv pep)
TDF/FTC 300mg/200mg + Dolutegravir 50mg PO once daily × 28 days
PO
Equally effective, significantly lower cost than Biktarvy — often institutional preference. Alternative: TDF/FTC + Darunavir 800mg/Ritonavir 100mg daily.

PrEP is highly effective: PROUD trial showed 86% relative risk reduction; IPrEx and DISCOVER studies showed >90% reduction with daily adherence. Refer MSM (especially recent STI), persons who inject drugs and their sex partners, persons exchanging sex, and sex partners of HIV-infected persons with detectable VL.

8 · Disposition

Admit if
Suspected/confirmed OI requiring inpatient treatment; hemodynamic instability/sepsis/respiratory failure; CNS symptoms with concerning imaging/LP; new diagnosis with acute seroconversion + complications; inability to tolerate oral medications; social barriers to safe discharge. Continue home HAART, ID consult, CD4/VL, HBV/HCV screen if not documented, medication reconciliation for ARV interactions.
Discharge if
Stable vital signs, tolerating PO, no OI requiring inpatient treatment, well-controlled HIV with manageable complaint, reliable follow-up available. Continue all HIV medications — do NOT stop HAART. Follow up with HIV provider within 1-2 weeks. The ED visit is a critical linkage-to-care touchpoint: ~80% of new transmissions come from persons not diagnosed or not in care.

Vancomycin (C. difficile): 125mg PO q6h × 10-14 days PO — Severe C. diff: Vancomycin 500mg PO q6h + Metronidazole 500mg IV q8h.

Ganciclovir (CMV colitis): 5 mg/kg IV q12h × 21-42 days IV — Continue until endoscopic resolution. Foscarnet is an alternative if ganciclovir-resistant.

Azithromycin + Ethambutol (disseminated MAC, CD4 <50): Azithromycin 500-600mg PO daily + Ethambutol 15 mg/kg PO daily PO — Lifelong until immune reconstitution (CD4 >100 for >6 months). Add rifabutin for severe disease. MAC prophylaxis: Azithromycin 1200mg weekly if CD4 <50.

Other OIs — constitutional, dermatologic, ocular

Kaposi sarcoma (HHV-8), disseminated VZV, oral hairy leukoplakia (EBV, does not scrape off unlike thrush), oral candidiasis (suggests CD4 <200). **CMV retinitis (CD4 <50):** "pizza pie" fundoscopy, painless vision loss — urgent ophthalmology.

Fluconazole (oropharyngeal candidiasis): 100-200 mg PO daily × 7-14 days PO — Esophageal candidiasis: 200-400mg daily x14-21 days.

Valganciclovir or IV Ganciclovir (CMV retinitis): Valganciclovir 900mg PO BID or Ganciclovir 5 mg/kg IV q12h PO or IV — Induction 14-21 days, then valganciclovir 900mg daily maintenance. Sight-threatening emergency — urgent ophthalmology referral.

**Low threshold for admission.** Admit for suspected/confirmed OI needing IV treatment, instability/sepsis, CNS symptoms with abnormal imaging/LP, inability to tolerate PO, or social barriers. Do NOT stop HAART — involve the HIV provider/ID consultant.

[TMP-SMX](#/drug/tmp-smx/pjp prophylaxis) (OI prophylaxis): 1 double-strength tablet (160mg TMP/800mg SMX) PO once daily PO — Start/resume if CD4 <200; continue until CD4 >200 for 3+ months on ART. Alternative: Dapsone 100mg daily or Atovaquone 1500mg daily.

Fluconazole + Azithromycin (additional OI prophylaxis): Fluconazole 100-200mg daily (CD4 <100-150) + Azithromycin 1200mg weekly (CD4 <50) PO — Fluconazole for recurrent mucocutaneous candidiasis or cryptococcal secondary prophylaxis. Azithromycin for MAC prophylaxis.

6 · ARV medication effects — quick reference

Drug class | Key ED-relevant toxicity | Action

NRTI/NtRTI | Tenofovir (TDF) Fanconi syndrome; abacavir hypersensitivity 3-5% (HLA-B*5701) — NEVER rechallenge; retrospective MI association. | Screen HLA-B*5701 before abacavir. TAF has reduced renal/bone toxicity vs TDF.

NNRTI | Efavirenz neuropsychiatric effects (vivid dreams → psychosis/suicidality); nevirapine hepatotoxicity (FDA black box, first 6 weeks). | Immediate discontinuation of efavirenz if severe psychosis/suicidality. Doravirine has fewer CNS effects.

PI (boosted) | Atazanavir: indirect hyperbilirubinemia/jaundice (benign) + RADIOLUCENT kidney stones. CYP3A4 inhibition — major drug interactions. | Check drug interactions before prescribing anticoagulants, statins, antiarrhythmics, corticosteroids.

INSTI | Best-tolerated class; ~90% undetectable VL through 96 weeks. Dolutegravir: neural tube defect signal (Botswana data). | Review dolutegravir NTD risk before use in women of childbearing potential for PEP.

HBV-active NRTIs (TDF/TAF/FTC/3TC) | Stopping in an HBV co-infected patient risks a fatal hepatitis flare (~10% of HIV patients are HBV co-infected). | NEVER stop — resume immediately and consult ID urgently. See scope warning above.

Tenofovir alafenamide (TAF), tenofovir disoproxil fumarate (TDF), emtricitabine (FTC), and lamivudine (3TC) all have dual HIV/HBV activity. If a co-infected patient stops one of these, acute HBV flare can occur — fatalities have been reported. **~10%** of HIV patients are co-infected with HBV. The only clear indications to stop HAART are **Stevens-Johnson syndrome and nevirapine-associated hepatic failure**; all other decisions to stop require the HIV provider.

7 · PEP & PrEP

PEP is most effective when given ASAP — ideally within **2 hours** of exposure. **NOT recommended when care is sought more than 72 hours after exposure.** Indications: sexual exposure, needlestick/percutaneous, mucous membrane exposure, sexual assault. Baseline HIV test required. Adherence is a challenge: one study showed **<60%** follow-up rates and **<25%** completed the full 28-day course.

[Biktarvy](#/drug/biktarvy/hiv pep) (bictegravir/emtricitabine/tenofovir alafenamide): 1 tablet (50mg/200mg/25mg) PO once daily × 28 days PO — CDC 2025 preferred single-tablet regimen. Start ASAP, ideally within 2 hours; NOT effective if started >72 hours post-exposure.

[TDF/FTC](#/drug/tdf-ftc/hiv pep) + [Dolutegravir](#/drug/dolutegravir/hiv pep): TDF/FTC 300mg/200mg + Dolutegravir 50mg PO once daily × 28 days PO — Equally effective, significantly lower cost than Biktarvy — often institutional preference. Alternative: TDF/FTC + [Darunavir](#/drug/darunavir/hiv pep) 800mg/[Ritonavir](#/drug/ritonavir/hiv pep) 100mg daily.

PrEP is highly effective: PROUD trial showed **86%** relative risk reduction; IPrEx and DISCOVER studies showed **>90%** reduction with daily adherence. Refer MSM (especially recent STI), persons who inject drugs and their sex partners, persons exchanging sex, and sex partners of HIV-infected persons with detectable VL.

TDF/FTC (Truvada): 300mg/200mg PO once daily PO — FDA-approved for all genders. Requires negative HIV test and CrCl >60 before starting. Refer to PrEP provider — do NOT start from the ED.

TAF/FTC (Descovy): 25mg/200mg PO once daily PO — Approved for MSM and transgender women ONLY (cisgender women not studied). Requires CrCl >30.

8 · Disposition

Suspected/confirmed OI requiring inpatient treatment; hemodynamic instability/sepsis/respiratory failure; CNS symptoms with concerning imaging/LP; new diagnosis with acute seroconversion + complications; inability to tolerate oral medications; social barriers to safe discharge. Continue home HAART, ID consult, CD4/VL, HBV/HCV screen if not documented, medication reconciliation for ARV interactions.

Stable vital signs, tolerating PO, no OI requiring inpatient treatment, well-controlled HIV with manageable complaint, reliable follow-up available. Continue all HIV medications — do NOT stop HAART. Follow up with HIV provider within 1-2 weeks. The ED visit is a critical linkage-to-care touchpoint: **~80%** of new transmissions come from persons not diagnosed or not in care.
