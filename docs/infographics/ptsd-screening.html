PTSD / ASD Screening in the ED: Screen, Suicide-Screen, Brief-Intervene, Disposition

Adults ≥18 in the ED with an identified or suspected DSM-5-TR Criterion A trauma exposure — acute (<1 month, ASD pathway) or established (>1 month, PTSD pathway). Excludes: pediatric patients (different disclosure/consent and caregiver-guidance protocols), formal CAPS-5 diagnostic interview (screening tools only, not a diagnostic substitute), and initiation of longitudinal outpatient psychiatric medication titration (ED role is bridge dosing + warm-handoff, not chronic management).

⚠ Never prescribe benzodiazepines for acute trauma or established PTSD — worsens trajectory, impairs extinction learning, and is associated with increased risk of developing PTSD when given after trauma (Guina 2015 meta-analysis, 18 trials, 5236 participants).[21]
⚠ Never offer single-session psychological debriefing (CISD) — Cochrane review shows OR 2.51 (95% CI 1.24-5.09) for PTSD at 1 year. WHO and 2023 VA/DoD CPG strongly recommend against it.[15][16]
⚠ Never diagnose PTSD before 1 month post-trauma — use the Acute Stress Disorder pathway for the 3-day–1-month window; ASD is a total-count (≥9 of 14) diagnosis, not cluster-based like PTSD.[3]
⚠ Always run the C-SSRS suicide screen on every PTSD/ASD patient. Severity 4-5 or any "yes" to the behavior section in the past 3 months = HIGH risk → psychiatric admission pathway.[10][11]
⚠ Always offer warm-handoff referral to trauma-focused CBT (CPT, PE, or EMDR) — a paper referral alone is not enough; warm handoff substantially outperforms it, especially in at-risk populations.[17]
⚠ IPV screening must occur in private — separate the patient from any accompanying partner BEFORE asking any IPV question.[25]
⚠ Active IPV with no safe place to go requires shelter coordination before discharge; never share the safety plan with the partner or in EHR notes accessible to the partner.[25]
⚠ Sexual assault presenting within 120 hours: activate SANE, offer forensic-kit consent, and address HIV PEP / emergency contraception / STI prophylaxis before disposition — the kit is valid up to 120h, ideally <72h.[23][24]
⚠ Psychiatric admission indications: C-SSRS severity 4-5, recent suicidal behavior (past 3 months), homicidal ideation with means, acute psychosis/severe dissociation impairing safety, or inability to maintain safety post-discharge (e.g., severe IPV with no safe place).[26]

1 · Triage & time window

DSM-5-TR Criterion A (qualifying trauma): directly experiencing (A1), witnessing in person (A2), learning it happened to a close family/friend and it was violent/accidental (A3), or repeated/extreme occupational exposure to aversive details — first responders, ED staff (A4). A4 does not apply through media/TV unless work-related. Subjective horror/helplessness response was removed from DSM-5/DSM-5-TR.[3]

Time since traumaPathway
0-3 daysAcute Stress Reaction (ASR) — ICD term, not a DSM-5-TR diagnosis
3 days - 1 monthAcute Stress Disorder (ASD) — ≥9 of 14 symptoms
>1 monthPTSD pathway — PCL-5 cluster scoring
>6 months delayed onsetPTSD with delayed-expression specifier

Select the chief presentation — each pathway carries specific time-sensitive actions: recent trauma <1mo+symptomatic, trauma >1mo with intrusion/avoidance/hyperarousal, sexual assault <120h, IPV/DV survivor, or a high-risk routine screen (veteran, first responder, refugee, post-ICU/arrest).

Never diagnose PTSD before 1 month post-trauma.[3][5] ASD's predictive value for later PTSD is limited — Bryant's review found ASD identifies some cases but misses the majority of eventual PTSD; a negative ASD screen does not rule out future PTSD.[6]

2 · Screening tools

PC-PTSD-5 — JCAHO/Joint Commission and VA/DoD-endorsed primary screen (Prins 2016): AUC >0.92, kappa ≥0.75. Cutoff ≥3 = optimally sensitive (94.8%) — best for ED screening; ≥4 = optimally balanced. Stem question first — if the patient never experienced a Criterion A trauma, do not score symptom items. Avoid during acute crisis states (transient elevations can be false positives).[7]

PCL-5 — 20-item self-report, fully aligned with DSM-5-TR. Each item 0-4; total range 0-80. Probable-PTSD cutoff 31-33 (Bovin 2016), population-specific variation 23-49. Two scoring methods: total severity ≥31-33, OR the DSM-5-TR cluster rule (items rated ≥2 count as endorsed: ≥1 of intrusion, ≥1 of avoidance, ≥2 of negative-cognitions/mood, ≥2 of arousal). Treatment monitoring: ~10-point reduction = clinically meaningful; <28 after treatment = clinically significant change (Marx 2022). PCL-5 is not a stand-alone diagnostic tool — gold standard remains the CAPS-5 structured interview.[8][9]

Every band converges on the same next step
Regardless of severity band (probable / subthreshold / below-threshold), the tree routes all three to the mandatory C-SSRS suicide screen next — severity does not gate whether the screen happens.

Acute Stress Disorder (3d-1mo): requires ≥9 of 14 symptoms across 5 categories (any combination, total count — not cluster-based like PTSD): intrusion (5), negative mood (1), dissociative (2), avoidance (2), arousal (5). Functional impairment required. Distinct from ASR (ICD, 0-3d, not a DSM-5-TR diagnosis).[3]

Trauma-focused CBT — the gold-standard referral3 modalities

2023 VA/DoD CPG: three trauma-focused psychotherapies are first-line OVERALL for PTSD, preferred over pharmacotherapy: Cognitive Processing Therapy (CPT), Prolonged Exposure (PE), and Eye Movement Desensitization and Reprocessing (EMDR). 4 · C-SSRS suicide screen — mandatory

Every PTSD/ASD patient gets a suicide screen. Columbia Suicide Severity Rating Scale (Screen Version) — FDA-recommended, validated in ED including PTSD/veteran populations. In 18,684 psychiatric ED patients, the optimal cutoff carried an adjusted OR 4.7 for suicide death within 1 week (Legarreta 2015).[10][11]

6 · Special populations

Time-sensitive multi-track response. Forensic: activate SANE immediately, forensic-kit consent + chain of custody (valid up to 120h, ideally <72h). Pharmacology: HIV PEP within 72h (tenofovir-emtricitabine + raltegravir or per local protocol × 28d); emergency contraception within 120h (levonorgestrel 1.5mg × 1, ulipristal 30mg × 1, or copper IUD); STI prophylaxis (CDC 2021: ceftriaxone 500mg IM [1g if ≥150kg] + doxycycline 100mg BID × 7d + metronidazole 2g × 1); hepatitis B PEP if non-immune; tetanus if wounded. 7 · Disposition

Combine PCL-5 / PC-PTSD-5 / ASD result + C-SSRS + social context to choose disposition.

Voluntary preferred when the patient agrees and is engaged. Involuntary hold (varies by state) generally requires imminent danger to self or others, or grave disability, PLUS refusal of voluntary admission and a current mental-illness diagnosis. ED actions: 1:1 observation, lethal-means restriction, remove belongings (belt, shoelaces, sharp objects), document risk assessment thoroughly (Joint Commission sentinel-event review if suicide within 7 days of ED discharge).[26]

Discharge criteria (ALL must be met): C-SSRS low risk, adequate social support, safe living environment, no active intoxication impairing decisions, patient willing/able to engage, appointment scheduled (ideally ≤2 weeks), crisis plan reviewed. Warm handoff is the standard — paper referral alone is insufficient. Provide 988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline, PTSD Coach app, and population-specific hotlines (Veterans: 988 press 1 / Vet Center 1-877-WAR-VETS; sexual assault: RAINN 1-800-656-HOPE; IPV: NDV 1-800-799-SAFE; disaster: SAMHSA 1-800-985-5990). Stepped-care monitoring: re-screen at 1 and 3 months.[17][27]

For moderate distress with insufficient outpatient access, or active SI without plan/intent who can engage with support: warm 988 transfer connecting to local mobile crisis, Crisis Stabilization Unit if available, peer-support warm handoff, follow-up appointment within 24-48 hours, lethal-means counseling — then routes to the same outpatient warm-handoff pathway.

Confirm patient consent, run the Campbell DA lethality assessment (firearm in home, choking history, weapon threats, escalation), call NDV Hotline 1-800-799-SAFE to coordinate local shelter, social-work consult for transport/paperwork/custody, safety plan (code word, documents, money, clothes) never shared with the partner. Children witnessing IPV → mandatory child-abuse report (varies by state, generally yes). Avoid benzodiazepines (impair safety judgment during a safety-critical window); hydroxyzine 25-50mg qhs if sleep support needed. 24-72h follow-up call from the advocacy organization.[25]

Discharge criteria (ALL must be met): C-SSRS low risk, adequate social support, safe living environment, no active intoxication impairing decisions, patient willing/able to engage, appointment scheduled (ideally ≤2 weeks), crisis plan reviewed. Warm handoff is the standard — paper referral alone is insufficient. Provide 988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline, PTSD Coach app, and population-specific hotlines (Veterans: 988 press 1 / Vet Center 1-877-WAR-VETS; sexual assault: RAINN 1-800-656-HOPE; IPV: NDV 1-800-799-SAFE; disaster: SAMHSA 1-800-985-5990). Stepped-care monitoring: re-screen at 1 and 3 months.[17][27]

For moderate distress with insufficient outpatient access, or active SI without plan/intent who can engage with support: warm 988 transfer connecting to local mobile crisis, Crisis Stabilization Unit if available, peer-support warm handoff, follow-up appointment within 24-48 hours, lethal-means counseling — then routes to the same outpatient warm-handoff pathway.

Confirm patient consent, run the Campbell DA lethality assessment (firearm in home, choking history, weapon threats, escalation), call NDV Hotline 1-800-799-SAFE to coordinate local shelter, social-work consult for transport/paperwork/custody, safety plan (code word, documents, money, clothes) never shared with the partner. Children witnessing IPV → mandatory child-abuse report (varies by state, generally yes). Avoid benzodiazepines (impair safety judgment during a safety-critical window); hydroxyzine 25-50mg qhs if sleep support needed. 24-72h follow-up call from the advocacy organization.[25]

8 · One-glance algorithm

Trauma exposure identified — DSM-5-TR Criterion A: direct/witness/learned-of/repeated exposure. Time window drives pathway: 0-3d ASR, 3d-1mo ASD, >1mo PTSD.

Chief presentation? — Recent <1mo+symptomatic / trauma >1mo+intrusion-avoidance-hyperarousal / sexual assault <120h / IPV survivor / high-risk routine screen.

Time since trauma? — 0-3d / 3d-1mo / >1mo — never diagnose PTSD before 1 month.

PC-PTSD-5: score ≥3? — 5-item VA/JCAHO screen. Cutoff ≥3 optimally sensitive.

Sexual assault, acute (<120h) — SANE + forensic kit + HIV PEP + EC + STI ppx + hep B + RAINN warm handoff.

IPV / domestic violence survivor — Screen in PRIVATE — separate from accompanying partner first.

PCL-5 administered — any band → suicide screen — 20-item, 0-80 total. All 3 severity bands route to the same next step: C-SSRS.

ASD: ≥9 of 14 symptoms + impairment? — Total count across 5 categories, not cluster-based.

ASR (0-3d) — PFA + reassurance, no Dx — Avoid benzos + CISD. 2-4wk follow-up re-screen.

ASD — PFA + TF-CBT referral + sleep support — Hydroxyzine or trazodone for sleep; C-SSRS; warm handoff 1-2wk.

C-SSRS suicide screen — MANDATORY — Severity 4-5 or recent behavior = HIGH risk = psychiatric admission pathway.

Psychological First Aid (5 elements) — Listen/validate, inform, practical help, connect, refer. Never CISD.

Pharmacology decision — Chronic SSRI, nightmares (prazosin), sleep-only, avoid list, or skip to disposition.

Pharm selected: SSRI / prazosin / sleep bridge — Choose the scenario above — distinct dosing shown per choice, not interchangeable.

What to AVOID: benzodiazepines, propranolol ppx, CISD — Benzos worsen PTSD trajectory and increase PTSD risk after trauma.

Disposition decision — Combine screen result + C-SSRS + social context.

Psychiatric admission pathway — Voluntary preferred; involuntary needs danger-to-self/others or grave disability. TX: APOWW/OPC/MHW.

Outpatient — warm-handoff referral — All discharge criteria met; appointment ≤2wk; 988 + PTSD Coach provided.

Crisis services / mobile crisis — 988 warm transfer, mobile crisis, CSU, 24-48h follow-up.

IPV shelter coordination — NDV 1-800-799-SAFE; safety plan never shared with partner.

9 · Do NOT

Critical pitfalls

Do NOT prescribe benzodiazepines for acute trauma or established PTSD — worsens trajectory, impairs extinction learning.

Do NOT offer single-session psychological debriefing (CISD) — Cochrane shows OR 2.51 for PTSD at 1 year.

Do NOT diagnose PTSD before 1 month post-trauma — use the ASD pathway for the 3d-1mo window.

Do NOT skip the C-SSRS on any PTSD/ASD patient — severity 4-5 triggers a psychiatric admission pathway.

Do NOT rely on a paper referral alone for trauma-focused CBT (CPT/PE/EMDR) — warm handoff is the standard.

Do NOT screen for IPV with the partner present — separate the patient first, every time.

Do NOT discharge active IPV with no safe place to go — coordinate shelter before discharge; never share the safety plan with the partner.
