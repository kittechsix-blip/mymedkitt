## How to read the locators

- Locator = short name + **PDF page** + (journal page where the PDF shows one) + section heading as printed. For ALAD 2026, AMR 2026 and BLAD 2026 the printed footer page number equals the PDF page. CLAD 2019 PDF p.N = journal p.(492+N); AMR 2016 PDF p.N = journal p.(396+N); Nomenclature 2007 PDF p.N = journal p.(1228+N).
- Quotes are verbatim from the PDF text layer, each under 40 words; ligature or hyphenation artefacts were normalised only where the text layer split a word. "(ungraded)" = the document prints no grade. These documents do not use GRADE letters except where stated; the only "grades" are Delphi scores or % agreement, quoted as printed.
- **Tables / figures read from rendered page IMAGES (verified visually):** ALAD 2026 Figure 2 (PDF p.9); AMR 2026 Table 1 (p.5) and Table 2 (p.6); AMR 2016 Table 1 (p.4); CLAD 2019 Table 2 and Table 3 (p.4). These are marked "(read from image)".

## 1. Acute lung allograft dysfunction (ALAD): definition, thresholds, differential, evaluation

**1. Why the term exists** (ALAD 2026, p.2, Introduction; ungraded)
> "LTx recipients may additionally experience acute and potentially reversible episodes of lung function decline before or after the criteria for CLAD are met."

**2. Definition: spirometric** (ALAD 2026, p.3, Section I; Delphi item 6: consensus, 3.67 + 1.63, p.4 Box 1)
> "ALAD may be defined spirometrically, with a 10% or greater decline in FEV1 (in liters) from the maximum FEV1 value over the preceding 3-6 months."
Box 1 item 6 (text layer) specifies the **3-month** maximum (3.67 + 1.63); item 5, the 6-month version, also reached consensus (3.00 + 1.84). p.4: "We recommend caution with the diagnosis of ALAD in recipients with a 10% decline relative to a time point greater than 3 months." If no FEV1 in the window: "the most recent value should be used" (p.3; Box 1 item 7, 3.92 + 1.34).

**3. Definition: clinical, when spirometry is not available** (ALAD 2026, p.3, Section I)
> "if spirometry is not available or feasible, ALAD can be defined clinically as a new or increased supplemental oxygen requirement, acute respiratory symptoms or physical exam findings, or new radiographic abnormalities"
Box 1 scores (text layer, p.4): new or increased oxygen requirement 4.57 + 0.65 (consensus); respiratory symptoms and exam findings 2.63 + 2.05 (consensus); ground-glass opacities on imaging "in the appropriate clinical context" 2.54 + 1.66 (consensus). p.6: "The group reached weak consensus that ALAD could be defined based on radiographic findings in an appropriate clinical context."

**4. Signs and symptoms listed** (ALAD 2026, p.6, Section I; ungraded)
> "Signs and symptoms of ALAD may include cough or dyspnea, hypoxemia or need for oxygen supplementation, and opacities on CT scan or chest radiograph."

**5. Severity grades** (ALAD 2026, p.6, Table 1, text layer; Box 1 items 8-10: 3.86 + 1.21; 3.72 + 1.67; 4.58 + 0.61)
- Mild: "Spirometric ALAD without other clinical signs or symptoms of graft dysfunction"
- Moderate: "Spirometric ALAD with additional clinical signs or symptoms of graft dysfunction, not meeting criteria for severe ALAD"
- Severe: "New or increased oxygen requirement or need for respiratory hospitalization, irrespective of spirometry"

**6. Trajectory labels** (ALAD 2026, p.6, Table 1; Box 1 items 11-15, text layer)
- Recovered: return "within 3 months of onset to within 10% of the reference FEV1".
- Progressive: "a subsequent further 10% decline in FEV1 from the time of ALAD onset within 3-months".
- Rapidly progressive: "a subsequent further 10% decline in FEV1 or need for hospitalization occurs within 30-days of ALAD onset".
- Persistent: neither recovery nor progression at 3 months.
- Box 1 item 14 (4.33 + 0.99): "Patients with rapidly progressive ALAD have high risk of death." (p.4; also p.6)

**7. Cause need not be known; no required workup** (ALAD 2026, p.6 and p.9)
> "the cause of ALAD does not need to be determined for a patient to meet criteria for ALAD"
> "there is no required evaluation to meet the diagnostic criteria for ALAD." (p.9, Section III)
Idiopathic ALAD = no cause "after investigative evaluation and/or assessment of response to empiric therapy" (Table 1, p.6; Box 1 item 20, 4.73 + 0.49).

**8. When ALAD can be diagnosed after transplant** (ALAD 2026, p.7, Section I; Box 1 items 21-23, p.4)
> "ALAD cannot be assessed in the first 72 h following transplant, given the overlap with PGD"
Item 21 (72 h): consensus 4.82 + 0.52. Items 22 (not <1 month) and 23 (not <6 months): "did not reach consensus" (1.44 + 3.19; -1.53 + 3.13). p.7: "the group thought that ALAD could likely be diagnosed in this period [first 6 months], but there was uncertainty of how to interpret relative spirometric changes when post-transplant FEV1 is low".

**9. Home spirometry** (ALAD 2026, p.3-4; Box 1 item 16)
In-lab vs home spirometry for diagnosis: "did not reach consensus, 1.23 + 2.43". The group recommended spirometry "per ATS/ERS guidelines" (p.3).

**10. Aetiology classes** (ALAD 2026, p.7-8, Section II, Box 2; Figure 2 p.9)
> "The etiology of ALAD can be sub-categorized as alloimmune, non-alloimmune, or idiopathic." (Box 2 item 1, consensus 4.70 + 0.51)
Alloimmune: ACR (item 2, 4.88 + 0.33), acute AMR (item 3, 4.84 + 0.37). Non-alloimmune with consensus (text layer): infection (4.84 + 0.37), aspiration (4.50 + 1.49), fluid overload (3.78 + 1.97), pleural effusion (3.62 + 2.09), pneumothorax (3.44 + 2.05), airway complications (4.04 + 1.68). "Alloimmune and non-alloimmune ALAD can occur simultaneously" (item 4, 4.76 + 0.48).
**Did not reach consensus** as ALAD causes: diaphragm dysfunction (0.71 + 2.97), respiratory muscle weakness (0.47 + 2.90), frailty (-0.09 + 2.71), acute pain (0.77 + 2.83), weight gain (0.61 + 2.77) (items 11-15). p.8: these "should be considered in the evaluation for ALAD etiologies, though assessment for each of these contributors is not required for ALAD diagnosis."

**11. Pulmonary embolism: stated, but not voted** (ALAD 2026, p.8, Section II)
> "pulmonary thromboembolism is an important cause of an acute deterioration in lung allograft function, and should be considered in the evaluation of ALAD."
"Although it was not specifically addressed with a Delphi statement".

**12. Framework figure (diagnostic subgroups)** (ALAD 2026, p.9, Figure 2; **read from image**)
Top box "ALAD, Spiromeric or Clinical" -> evaluation box "History/physical, Bronchoscopy, DSA, Chest imaging, Therapy response" -> four categories: Alloimmune (acute cellular rejection: TBB+, infection-, DSA-, other testing-; antibody mediated rejection: TBB+/-, infection-, DSA+, other testing-); Non-alloimmune (acute infection: TBB-, infection+, DSA-, other-; non-infectious causes: TBB-, infection-, DSA-, other testing+); Idiopathic (no cause identified: all negative); Mixed ("Coexistent +/- ACR +/- AMR +/- infection +/- other"). Caption: "Multiple categories of ALAD may co-exist. ... full diagnostic criteria should be applied for acute cellular and antibody-mediated rejection, but are omitted from this figure for clarity."

**13. History and examination** (ALAD 2026, p.9, 4.1; Box 3 item 1, 4.74 + 1.16)
> "Clinicians should assess for and quantify dyspnea, cough, gastroesophageal reflux, fever, weight gain, peripheral edema, or other symptoms of respiratory disease."
Helpful history listed: sick contacts, travel, known pathogen colonization, CLAD or BLAD status, previous DSA, previous ACR/AMR, "recent immunosuppressive therapy, adherence to medical recommendations, current infection prophylaxis and adherence, and recent transfusion of blood products". p.9: "The initial clinical features of an ALAD presentation are often non-specific".

**14. Blood tests** (ALAD 2026, p.9-10, 4.2.1; Box 3 items 2, 6, 7)
> "There was consensus that all patients with ALAD should have a complete blood count, assessment of immunosuppressive drug levels, and quantification of donor-specific antibodies"
Box 3 item 2 (CBC with differential + immunosuppressive drug level): 4.88 + 0.39. Item 6 (HLA DSA): 4.06 + 1.48. Non-HLA antibody (item 7): "did not reach consensus, -0.17 + 3.17". Basic chemistry: "renal function, liver enzymes, and electrolytes are worthwhile though rarely diagnostic". "should be considered": blood quantitative CMV PCR, HLA antibodies, trough immunosuppressive levels (p.10).

**15. Chest X-ray** (ALAD 2026, p.10, 4.2.2; Box 3 item 3, 4.74 + 0.60)
> "All cases of ALAD should undergo chest imaging with at least posterior-anterior and lateral CXR when feasible."
CXR "can be helpful to evaluate for non-rejection elements including lobar opacities or interstitial changes, effusions, edema, pneumothorax, atelectasis, consolidation, or masses."

**16. CT chest** (ALAD 2026, p.10, 4.2.2; Box 3 items 4-5)
> "CT scans are most appropriate in moderate or severe ALAD, or where there are specific features on CXR that require further evaluation."
Item 4 (CT for moderate or severe ALAD): consensus 4.72 + 0.73. Item 5 (mild ALAD may not need CT): "did not reach consensus, 1.82 + 2.75". CT can assess "inflammatory changes associated with severe rejection; proximal and distal airway complications; specific features of fungal infection; and CLAD features". "The role of contrast in CT scans should be considered on an individual basis, balancing the diagnostic utility and risk of potential IV contrast exposure."

**17. Bronchoscopy: who and when** (ALAD 2026, p.10-11, 4.2.3; Box 3 items 8-11)
> "we suggest a for-cause bronchoscopy in all cases of ALAD. For mild ALAD, it may be possible to defer bronchoscopy and institute empiric treatment"
Item 8 (moderate or severe): consensus 4.52 + 0.71. Item 9 (all ALAD): consensus 3.40 + 2.13. Item 10 (mild may not need): "did not reach consensus, 1.90 + 2.61". Item 11 (include transbronchial biopsy "with adequate sampling"): 3.88 + 1.84. Testing for respiratory viruses "should be considered in most cases of ALAD". **No time frame (hours or days) for bronchoscopy is given anywhere in the document.**

**18. Bronchoscopy contraindicated by physiology** (ALAD 2026, p.11, 4.2.3)
> "In patients with advanced respiratory failure on invasive mechanical ventilation or extracorporeal membrane oxygenation (ECMO), the risks of bronchoscopy may preclude this investigation and empiric treatment should be considered"
Also p.8: "significant hypoxemia might preclude invasive assessments of allograft dysfunction."

**19. What bronchoscopy looks for; biopsy adequacy** (ALAD 2026, p.11, 4.2.3)
Direct visualisation "to assess for malacia, stenosis, or ischemic exudate". Biopsy aim: at least five evaluable parenchymal fragments; risks "bleeding and pneumothorax". Negative result: "non-diagnostic or negative biopsies do not exclude ACR or AMR and that empiric treatment may be considered in selected cases."

**20. Specialised tests, not routine** (ALAD 2026, p.11, 4.2.4)
Aspiration work-up (video fluoroscopic swallow, BAL pepsinogen A4 and bile salts); non-HLA antibodies: no consensus; dd-cfDNA "may also have utility"; surgical lung biopsy "may be considered in complex or atypical cases". p.16: dd-cfDNA has "excellent negative predictive value but limited specificity"; "real-world clinical utility ... requires further evaluation."

**21. Treatment response as a diagnostic clue** (ALAD 2026, p.8 and p.11; Box 2 item 17, 4.32 + 0.79)
> "improvement with antibiotics suggests a bacterial etiology, while improvement with lympholytic therapy implies an immune etiology like ACR."
Box 4-related items 12-13 (recovery with pulse steroids alone confirms rejection; antimicrobials alone confirms infection): both "did not reach consensus" (1.32 + 2.55; 2.30 + 2.37).

## 2. Acute cellular rejection (ACR)

**22. ACR as a cause of ALAD** (ALAD 2026, p.8, Section II; ungraded)
> "ACR that is accompanied by a decline in spirometry is associated with a higher risk of CLAD and death."
Same page: ACR is "a frequent complication of LTx and is associated with worse overall clinical outcomes and increased incidence of CLAD".

**23. ACR presentation: only the generic ALAD presentation is given.** No document states a symptom, sign or imaging pattern specific to ACR. ALAD 2026 p.10: CXR "may only rarely suggest specific features in the setting of ACR or AMR". p.9: initial clinical features "are often non-specific". Nomenclature 2007 is purely histological and describes no clinical presentation.

**24. Histologic grading: the scheme** (Nomenclature 2007, p.2 (journal 1230), Table 1; text layer)
> "A: Acute rejection Grade 0-none, 1-minimal, 2-mild, 3-moderate, 4-Severe; B: Airway inflammation Grade 0, 1R, 2R, X; C: ... obliterative bronchiolitis 0-absent, 1-present; D: Chronic vascular rejection"
(This quote condenses the table rows.) Abstract (p.1): "'R' denotes revised grade"; B1R replaces 1996 B1+B2, B2R replaces B3+B4.

**25. Diagnosis of ACR is histological** (Nomenclature 2007, p.2, Section A; ungraded)
> "A diagnosis of acute rejection is based exclusively on the presence of perivascular and interstitial mononuclear cell infiltrates."
Grade is the most advanced pattern present (p.2). Biopsy adequacy (p.11): "at least five pieces of well-expanded alveolated lung parenchyma are required for an assessment of acute rejection."

**26. Grade definitions, short** (Nomenclature 2007, p.3-5)
A1 minimal: scattered perivascular cuffs, "Eosinophils and endothelialitis are not present". A2 mild: more frequent infiltrates, "no obvious infiltration by mononuclear cells into the adjacent alveolar septa or air spaces". A3 moderate: "extension of the inflammatory cell infiltrate into perivascular and peribronchiolar alveolar septa and airspaces". A4 severe: "diffuse perivascular, interstitial and air-space infiltrates ... with prominent alveolar pneumocyte damage and endothelialitis"; may show hyaline membranes and hemorrhage.

**27. ACR must be read with clinical and microbiology data** (Nomenclature 2007, p.6, Section A)
> "The diagnosis of acute lung rejection therefore requires integration with clinical and particularly microbiologic data."
p.11: BAL "has no clinical role in the diagnosis of acute rejection" but is "useful for the exclusion of infection".

**28. Mimics of ACR on biopsy** (Nomenclature 2007, p.12, Differential Diagnosis; ungraded)
> "Perivascular mononuclear infiltrates are not specific for acute rejection and many other conditions may simulate or mimic alloreactive lung injury."
Listed: CMV pneumonitis, Pneumocystis jiroveci pneumonia, post-transplant lymphoproliferative disease, recurrent primary disease (sarcoidosis), reperfusion injury early. "Granulomatous inflammation ... should always raise the possibility of mycobacterial or fungal, including pneumocystis, infection."

**29. How the 2007 grades relate to the 2026 ALAD framework.** ALAD 2026 never mentions the A/B grades, and never says ACR must be graded or what grade qualifies. Its only link to the 2007 paper is a citation for biopsy adequacy (reference 48: "at least five evaluable parenchymal fragments", p.11) plus the LASHA reporting framework: "histologic changes may be reported according to the Lung Allograft Standardized Histological Analysis (LASHA) framework" (p.11). In Figure 2, ACR is simply "TBB+". See Ambiguities A1.

**30. Grade A1 and later outcome** (Nomenclature 2007, p.6; ungraded)
> "the potential long-term significance of Grade A1 minimal acute rejection has emerged"; the group chose "to retain this minimal grade for further evaluation".
No treatment threshold by grade is given ("different clinical groups have different therapeutic algorithms", p.6).

**31. ACR treatment statements: all that exist** (ALAD 2026, p.12-13, Section IV; Box 4)
- "75% of ACR events are thought to be responsive to corticosteroids" (p.13, 5.3).
- Item 12 (consensus 4.21 + 0.72): "For treatment of possible alloimmune ALAD, pulse steroids, intravenous gamma globulin (IVIg), lymphocyte-depleting agents (e.g., anti-thymocyte globulin or alemtuzumab), or plasma exchange may be considered."
- p.13: "The choice of therapy should be guided by local availability, the clinical context, severity of presentation and patient-specific factors." No dose, duration, or agent order is stated.
- Item 7 (consensus 2.69 + 1.57): "For ALAD without a known cause, empiric augmentation of immunosuppression should be initiated."
- Item 8 (consensus 2.69 + 1.57): "In rapidly progressive ALAD, empiric augmentation of immunosuppression to treat rejection should be initiated, without delay for diagnostic testing."
- AMR 2026 p.9: DSA plus ACR, no AMR histology: interpret as AMR "only if ACR is persistent or is severe (grade > A3) and refractory to steroid therapy". (The text prints "> A3"; see Ambiguities A5.)

## 3. Antibody-mediated rejection (AMR)

**32. AMR as a cause of ALAD** (ALAD 2026, p.8)
> "Antibody-mediated rejection (AMR) can also be associated with a decline in spirometry. Indeed, clinical AMR can have a variety of clinical presentations consistent with the proposed ALAD definition."

**33. 2026 GAP definition: concept** (AMR 2026, p.2 Abstract and p.4 Definition; ungraded)
> "This Graft, Antibody, and Pathology (GAP) definition is based on the presence of Graft dysfunction, the presence and characteristics of Antibodies, and Pathological findings."
Graft dysfunction in AMR (p.4): "new or worsening respiratory symptoms, > 10% decline in spirometry from baseline, new or worsening hypoxemic respiratory failure, or new or worsening imaging abnormalities." The document says this was chosen "To align with the ISHLT statement on acute lung allograft dysfunction (ALAD)".

**34. GAP codes** (AMR 2026, p.5, Table 1; **read from image**)
G: 0 not present / 1 present (core). A (antibodies): 0 no HLA DSA / 1 HLA DSA present / 2 weak (MFI < 2500) / 3 intermediate (MFI 2500-8000) / 4 strong (MFI > 8000) (core); non-HLA antibody not present Y / present Z (non-core). P (pathology): X biopsy not done or inadequate; 0 findings frequently detected in AMR (DAD, OP, AFOP, capillary inflammation, septal widening, endothelialitis, microthrombi) not present; 1 present; 2 present in addition to ACR, chronic rejection or lymphocytic bronchiolitis (core); p-S6RP immunostaining Y negative / Z positive (non-core). C (complement activation, non-core): 0 not present (DSA C1q and/or C4d negative) / 1 present.

**35. Which combinations are AMR** (AMR 2026, p.6, Table 2; **read from image**)
- G1 A1 P1, G1 A1 P2: "Clinical AMR". G0 A1 P1, G0 A1 P2: "Subclinical AMR". G1 A1 P0: "AMR - Consider sampling problem/limitation, especially in cases with intermediate and strong HLA-DSA (A 3, A 4)."
- G1 A0 P1, G1 A0 P2: "NFA" (need further assessment): "Graft dysfunction with histologic findings frequently detected in AMR but no HLA DSA. Consider other etiologies (e.g., infection). Consider testing for non-HLA antibodies, C4d staining, p-S6RP staining."
- G1 A0 P0: "Not AMR ... Consider other etiologies (e.g., infection)."; G0 A1 P0: "Not AMR ... Consider close follow-up for intermediate and strong HLA-DSA (A 3, A 4)."; G0 A0 P1/P2: "Not AMR".

**36. AMR can coexist with ACR and infection** (AMR 2026, p.3 and p.4; Delphi key statement 2: 4.27 ± 0.74)
> "AMR can co-exist with other causes of allograft injury, including acute cellular rejection (ACR) and infection."
p.4: the 2016 definition's "exclusion of other causes of graft dysfunction" criterion is dropped; "emerging evidence that infection can induce the development of DSA and AMR."

**37. DSA strength and C4d** (AMR 2026, p.4-5 and p.9)
> "finding weak ... DSA does not make the diagnosis less conclusive as a substantial amount of DSA could be bound to the graft"
> "It is now widely accepted that C4d deposition is not a sensitive marker for pulmonary AMR." (p.5)
p.9: the workgroup "did not support the routine use of C4d immunostaining in surveillance transbronchial lung biopsies". p.4: "the HLA community has not endorsed specific cutoffs" for MFI ranges because of laboratory variability.

**38. Histology frequently found** (AMR 2026, p.3 and p.9; Delphi item 5: 4.00 ± 0.96)
"diffuse alveolar damage (DAD), acute fibrinous and organizing pneumonia (AFOP), capillary injury, endothelialitis, microthrombi, and septal widening."

**39. Surveillance and monitoring** (AMR 2026, p.3 and p.10-11; Delphi items 1, 8, 9)
> "Scheduled surveillance monitoring for AMR should be performed after lung transplantation." (clinical AMR 4.32 ± 0.82; subclinical 4.21 ± 0.88)
Scheduled DSA testing 4.32 ± 0.9; scheduled transbronchial biopsies 3.26 ± 1.43. p.11: "testing for DSA after a sensitizing event" and "AMR can occur beyond the first year after transplant". "Surveillance protocols for AMR have not been studied" (p.4).

**40. Treatment statements** (AMR 2026, p.11; Delphi items 12-13, p.10)
> "AMR with allograft dysfunction should always be treated with multiple drugs or interventions." (3.75 ± 0.85)
> "De novo DSA that is accompanied by otherwise unexplained allograft dysfunction should be treated." (3.88 ± 0.93)
p.11: "there have been no randomized-controlled trials, systematic reviews, or head-to-head comparisons of different treatments"; "could not recommend a specific treatment regimen". Without graft dysfunction, "could not recommend treatment for de novo DSA, even if the DSA was specific to HLA-DQ". Success defined as "stable graft function over 6 months ... or as clearance of DSA, improvement in spirometry, and reversal of pathological changes."

**41. Treatment agents named in the lung-AMR documents.** AMR 2026 names no drug for treatment (pre-transplant desensitisation discussion only). ALAD 2026 lists "pulse steroids, IVIg, lymphocyte-depleting agents (e.g., anti-thymocyte globulin or alemtuzumab), or plasma exchange" for possible alloimmune ALAD (item 31 above). AMR 2016 p.6 (journal 402): "Treatment has generally consisted of interventions that aim to deplete circulating antibodies, suppress B-cells and mitigate further antibody-mediated allograft injury." Its desensitisation text (p.6) mentions IVIg, plasmapheresis, bortezomib, rituximab for waitlist protocols, not for acute AMR.

**42. Outcomes** (AMR 2026, p.2; ungraded)
> "Approximately 20% of patients die within 30 days, 50% die within 1 year, and 80% die within 2 years of the diagnosis of AMR."
"73% developed severe CLAD ... within 1 year of the diagnosis." ALAD 2026 p.13: "ALAD due to acute AMR may stabilize or improve with treatment even though long-term outcomes are often poor."

**43. 2016 AMR definition (superseded)** (AMR 2016, p.4, Table 1 **read from image**; p.5 AMR staging)
Table 1 columns: allograft dysfunction / other causes excluded / lung histology / lung biopsy C4d / DSA. "Definite" = all five present. "Probable" rows: any one missing (dysfunction present in every row). "Possible" rows: two missing. Footnote a: "There is building evidence that antibody-mediated rejection can be diagnosed confidently in the absence of positive C4d staining, hence this group is recognized separately."
> "'Definite AMR' has all criteria present and other possible causes excluded, noting that ACR and AMR may coexist." (p.4, Diagnostic certainty)
Clinical vs subclinical (p.5): "Clinical AMR: The presence of allograft dysfunction (defined as alterations in pulmonary physiology, gas exchange properties, radiologic features or deteriorating functional performance)". Subclinical AMR "in the absence of allograft dysfunction". (Subclinical Table 2: text layer only.)

**44. Where the 2026 statement supersedes 2016** (AMR 2026, p.2 and p.4; ungraded)
> "The 2016 definition relied on a probabilistic approach, which categorized cases based on hierarchical levels of diagnostic certainty. This yielded arbitrary categories that comprised heterogeneous cases"
So: definite/probable/possible categories replaced by GAP codes; "other causes excluded" is no longer a criterion; C4d not necessary; MFI bands now supplied for DSA; p-S6RP added (non-core). AMR 2026 states it "used the 2016 ISHLT consensus report as a foundation" (p.12). It does not say whether the 2016 labels should stop being used in clinical notes; see Ambiguities A2.

**45. Antibody levels, MFI caution (2016)** (AMR 2016, p.2, Immunology of AMR)
> "DSA level and function should not be assessed by the mean fluorescent intensity (MFI) of the single antigen bead (SAB) assay, because the MFI does not represent the titer"
Compare AMR 2026 p.5, which nonetheless assigns codes by MFI band (2500 / 8000) "if DSA strength is assessed using MFI". See Ambiguities A6.

**46. Severe AMR and respiratory support** (AMR 2016, p.6 (journal 402), Grading severity of AMR)
> "severe pulmonary AMR may cause acute hypoxemic respiratory failure requiring oxygen replacement therapy and other supportive measures, ranging from non-invasive or invasive mechanical ventilation to extracorporeal life support."

**47. 2007 position on AMR (historical)** (Nomenclature 2007, p.10-11 (journal 1238-1239))
> "In 2006, no histologic features for antibody-mediated rejection in the lung were agreed upon."
"Caution is urged in the diagnosis of acute antibody-mediated rejection in the lung ... a multidisciplinary approach is again recommended in view of the wide differential diagnosis and the potential toxicity of treatments." Table 2 (humoral response stages I-IV, text layer) is borrowed from other organs.

## 4. CLAD: definition, phenotypes, thresholds, acute decline on top of CLAD

**48. Definition** (CLAD 2019, p.2 (journal 494), Definition of CLAD; ungraded)
> "CLAD is defined as a substantial and persistent decline (>=20%) in measured FEV1 value from the reference (baseline) value. The baseline value is computed as the mean of the best 2 post-operative FEV1 measurements (taken >3 weeks apart)."
(Text layer prints the inequality as a ligature symbol; "20%" is the printed value, confirmed against Table 2 in the image.)

**49. Timing and confirmation** (CLAD 2019, p.2)
"occur >3 months after LTx" (p.2, intro). Probable CLAD: "lung function parameters remain impaired on a second reading at least 3 weeks after the first >=20% fall from baseline and after adequate treatment of secondary causes such as infection, acute cellular/antibody-mediated rejection, or airway stenosis". Confirmed: "if the physiologic abnormalities (i.e., FEV1 decline of >=20%) persist for 3 months after the first value is taken". If function "returns to >80% of baseline after therapy, then the diagnosis of CLAD is not sustained".

**50. Investigation trigger** (CLAD 2019, p.2 and p.4)
> "it is now advised to start investigations whenever the FEV1 declines by >=10% from baseline."
p.4: "Patients with a first drop in FEV1 of >=10% should be investigated thoroughly and reassessed within 4 to 6 weeks, especially if a new treatment has been initiated." The 0-p stage was dropped (p.2).

**51. Staging** (CLAD 2019, p.4, Table 2; **read from image**)
CLAD 0 current FEV1 >80% of baseline; CLAD 1 >65-80%; CLAD 2 >50-65%; CLAD 3 >35-50%; CLAD 4 <=35%.

**52. Phenotypes** (CLAD 2019, p.4, Table 3; **read from image**; p.4 text)
BOS: obstruction (FEV1/FVC <0.7) yes, restriction (TLC decline >=10%) no, CT opacities no. RAS: obstruction no, restriction yes, opacities yes. Mixed: yes / yes / yes. Undefined: yes/no/yes and yes/yes/no.
> "RAS ... is defined physiologically by: (1) a persistent >=20% decline in FEV1 ... (2) a decrease in total lung capacity (TLC) to <=90% compared with baseline ... and (3) the presence of persistent opacities on chest imaging"
Also: "Up to 30% of patients with CLAD develop a restrictive defect." "we strongly recommend measuring TLC by body plethysmography to confirm a suspected diagnosis".

**53. Non-CLAD causes of chronic function loss** (CLAD 2019, p.3, Table 1, text layer)
Section B (cannot be easily told from CLAD; no baseline reset): infiltration with tumour, disease recurrence, "Drug or other induced pulmonary toxicity (e.g., sirolimus, methotrexate, amiodarone, radiotherapy)", "Pulmonary arterial strictures or emboli", "Acute/subacute generalized infection", "Acute/subacute cellular or antibody-mediated rejection", "Acute/subacute effects of aspiration". Section A (baseline may be reset if stable 6 months): ageing, surgical factors, persistent pleural effusion, persistent lung oedema from kidney/heart/liver failure, airway stenosis, myopathy/neuropathy, weight gain, native lung hyperinflation, localised infection with scarring.

**54. Acute decline on top of established CLAD** (ALAD 2026, p.3 and p.6; Delphi item 18: 4.45 + 0.94; item 17: 4.08 + 1.57)
> "ALAD may develop in an individual with CLAD who previously had stable FEV1 values." (Box 1, item 18)
> "Patients with pre-existing CLAD are eligible for a diagnosis of ALAD; for these patients, the reference FEV1 for spirometric ALAD determination are unchanged." (p.6)
> "ALAD in CLAD may be a sign of subsequent CLAD progression or an 'acute on chronic' physiology." (p.6)
Figure 1C (p.5 caption): a patient "developed an additional ALAD episode with a > 10% decline in FEV1" several months after CLAD onset. Aside from this, no document gives a distinct acute-on-CLAD treatment plan. CLAD 2019 p.4: "An acutely reversible condition, such as acute infection and even acute rejection, may exist in conjunction with developing early CLAD."

**55. ALAD vs CLAD** (ALAD 2026, p.6; BLAD 2026, p.9)
ALAD 2026: "ALAD may indicate the onset of CLAD" (item 17). BLAD 2026 p.9: "a decline in lung function at any time is classified as acute lung allograft dysfunction (ALAD) or, if persistent despite appropriate treatment of potential causes, as CLAD." ALAD 2026 p.15: "more than 50% of ALAD cases progressing to CLAD in some reports".

**56. CLAD in a patient too sick for spirometry** (CLAD 2019, p.9 (journal 501), Limitations)
> "Some patients have clear (acute-onset) lung allograft dysfunction but are too sick to have spirometry. Our consensus group has determined that the term CLAD should not be employed in these instances."

**57. CLAD treatment statements** (CLAD 2019, p.7 (journal 499), Treatment of CLAD)
> "In CLAD (BOS), sustained administration of high-dose corticosteroids should be avoided due to harmful side effects and lack of efficacy."
Other options listed: "conversion of cyclosporine to tacrolimus; a trial of azithromycin for >=8 weeks; and fundoplication for documented gastroesophageal reflux in selected cases". "Effective pharmacologic therapy of CLAD remains an unmet medical need." "There are no formal treatment guidelines for RAS". Retransplantation "may be the only therapeutic option for advanced CLAD in well-selected patients". Also p.2: before confirming CLAD after infection/rejection/aspiration "we would advise a prolonged course (at least 8 weeks) of neomacrolide therapy (usually azithromycin) in patients not already on such therapy."

**58. CLAD imaging and bronchoscopy** (CLAD 2019, p.6-7 (journal 498-499))
CT: "The initial CT scans ... without contrast media are recommended in all LTx patients at 6-month follow-up"; repeat CT when CLAD first diagnosed; "opacities on CXR that are not explained by obvious causes ... are indicators for a repeat chest CT." Transbronchial biopsy and BAL "should be performed at the start of the diagnostic process to investigate the decline in lung function not explained by obvious, non-CLAD causes" and "have little role in follow-up of CLAD patients" once diagnosed. Persistent BAL neutrophilia (>15%) or eosinophilia (>=2%) are associated with later CLAD.

**59. Baseline vs BLAD** (BLAD 2026, p.3, Section I; p.6 Table 2, text layer)
> "BLAD refers to a state of objectively measurable, abnormally low peak lung function following transplantation."
Definition: baseline FEV1 and FVC "not >= the lower limit of normal (LLN) or >= 80% predicted, by 1-year post-transplant and beyond" (p.3, paraphrase of the printed definition; wording is "failure to reach normal post-transplant lung function ... where the normal threshold is defined as a baseline FEV1 and baseline FVC >= the lower limit of normal (LLN) or >= 80% predicted"). Baseline is the CLAD 2019 baseline: "the average of the 2 highest post-operative FEV1 values separated by a minimum of 3 weeks" (Table 2 item 3). Grading (Table 2 item 15): grade 1 FEV1 >= 65% predicted; grade 2 >= 50% < 65%; grade 3 < 50%. "BLAD grade cannot worsen, as this would indicate a previous higher baseline and a progressive loss in keeping with a different physiologic graft dysfunction syndrome." (p.9). Reported prevalence 35-55% (p.7). For the ED, BLAD means a low baseline FEV1 is not by itself an acute event; the 10% ALAD trigger is judged against the recipient's own recent maximum (item 2).

## 5. Infection and airway complications as causes of decline

**60. Infection is named but organism- and timing-specific guidance is minimal** (ALAD 2026, p.7-8, Section II; Box 2 item 5: 4.84 + 0.37)
> "Community-acquired respiratory viral infections can be associated with a decline in FEV1 and the development of ACR and CLAD."
> "In some LTx recipients, viral infections associated with ALAD may result in a recovery of lung function, while others may develop accelerated CLAD."
> "Superimposed bacterial and fungal infections have also been associated with a decline in spirometry and CLAD."
p.13: "infectious etiologies - most notably pneumonia - represent a common and potentially treatable cause of ALAD".

**61. Organisms named in the documents** (all passages; ungraded)
- ALAD 2026 p.13: empiric coverage "with particular consideration for coverage against resistant organisms such as Staphylococcus aureus and Pseudomonas aeruginosa, guided by institutional susceptibility patterns"; p.13: empiric therapy "can be challenging or even contraindicated, particularly in light of the increasing prevalence of fungal colonization/infection and multidrug-resistant organisms."
- ALAD 2026 p.10: blood CMV PCR "to assess for CMV viremia, which may be associated with pneumonitis"; CT can show "specific features of fungal infection"; respiratory-virus testing "in most cases of ALAD, especially if ... cough, coryza, or malaise".
- Nomenclature 2007 p.12: CMV pneumonitis, Pneumocystis, mycobacteria and fungi as biopsy mimics of ACR (item 28). CLAD 2019 p.7: a randomised trial in recipients "with respiratory syncytial virus" showed interventions targeting a specific infection "may reduce the prevalence of new-onset or progressive BOS" (the intervention is not named).
No document gives a time-after-transplant infection timetable.

**62. Empiric antibiotics by severity** (ALAD 2026, p.12-13, Box 4 items 2-6; 5.1)
- Mild: "did not reach consensus, 0.28 + 2.37". Moderate: "consensus, 2.52 + 2.11". Severe: "consensus, 3.90 + 1.73".
> "De-escalation of antibiotics could be considered after 72 hours if culture results are negative." (item 5, 3.71 + 1.64)
> "For ALAD, empiric treatment may be initiated before diagnostic testing." (item 6, 3.40 + 1.84)
p.13 text: "De-escalation should be considered within 24 h of receiving definitive culture results or reassessed by 72 h after initiation of empiric therapy."
No drug, dose, or route is stated. Empiric antibiotic selection "should be guided by local microbiological surveillance data and the patient's infectious history."

**63. Infection after immunosuppression is raised** (ALAD 2026, p.13 and Box 4 item 13: 4.69 + 0.51)
> "Patients receiving augmented immunosuppression should be closely monitored for secondary opportunistic infections."
"Careful balancing of the potential risks and benefits of empiric augmentation of immunosuppression are critical".

**64. Airway complications** (ALAD 2026, p.8, p.11; Box 2 item 10: 4.04 + 1.68)
> "Airway complications including ischemic airway injury, stenosis and malacia can also result in allograft dysfunction, inflammation, ACR, and predisposition to infections."
p.11: bronchoscopy permits direct visualisation "to assess for malacia, stenosis, or ischemic exudate as causes of ALAD, particularly early post-transplant." BLAD 2026 p.13: bronchoscopy "can be therapeutic, allowing for airway clearance, foreign body removal, or dilation of strictures if present." CT "can assess for ... proximal and distal airway complications" (ALAD p.10). **"Anastomotic dehiscence" is not mentioned in any of the six documents; "anastomotic stenosis" appears only as "anastomotic stenosis after bilateral LTx" in a spirometry-interpretation note (CLAD 2019 p.5, FEV1/FVC may remain <0.7).** No management of stenosis or malacia is stated beyond the BLAD dilation sentence.

**65. Aspiration** (ALAD 2026, p.8, Box 2 item 6: 4.50 + 1.49; Nomenclature 2007 p.12)
> "gastroesophageal reflux and markers of aspiration including pepsinogen A4 and bile salts in bronchoalveolar lavage (BAL) are associated with elevated BAL neutrophilia, lung dysfunction, and early CLAD"
Nomenclature 2007 p.12: "The pulmonary allograft is not protected by a cough reflex and patients are highly predisposed to recurrent aspiration."

**66. Non-infectious, non-immune acute causes with ED-treatable examples** (ALAD 2026, p.12, Section IV)
> "When other etiologies of ALAD are identified, appropriate therapies (for example, diuresis for volume overload or thoracentesis for pleural effusion) should be instituted."
Fluid overload, pleural effusion and pneumothorax are consensus ALAD causes (item 10). The BLAD document adds cardiac evaluation (ECG, echocardiogram, NT-proBNP) for fluid overload (BLAD 2026 p.13) in the baseline-function context.

## 6. Urgency, admission, contacting the transplant centre, oxygen/ventilation, imaging, bronchoscopy

**67. Urgency** (ALAD 2026, p.11, Section IV; ungraded)
> "Prompt recognition and management of ALAD provides a critical opportunity, as targeted treatment may prevent progression to irreversible graft injury"
p.8: "delays may result in non-recovery of the allograft or death." p.13: "the urgency of therapeutic intervention often takes precedence over definitive etiologic clarification".

**68. Hospitalisation** (ALAD 2026, p.4 and p.6)
Respiratory hospitalisation is part of the definition of severe ALAD (item 5 above), and "need for hospitalization" within 30 days defines rapidly progressive ALAD (item 6). **No document gives criteria for who should be admitted, observed or discharged.**

**69. Contacting the transplant centre.** **Not stated.** The only related wording is that the diagnostic evaluation "is an essential element of LTx care, and one of the most critical specialized roles of the LTx clinician" (ALAD 2026 p.8) and that AMR needs "a multidisciplinary approach" (AMR 2026 p.4; Nomenclature 2007 p.14). No document tells an emergency clinician to call the transplant centre, or when.

**70. Oxygen and ventilation** (ALAD 2026, p.13, 5.3; Box 4 items 14-15)
> "In patients with severe ALAD, early initiation of aggressive respiratory support should be considered, including low- and high-flow nasal cannula, non-invasive or invasive mechanical ventilation (MV), and/or ECMO"
Item 14: consensus 4.56 + 0.62. Item 15 (ECMO should be considered): 3.96 + 1.33. "ECMO support may be considered in carefully selected individuals with severe ALAD, given rates of recoverability that approach 80%." Ventilation strategy "should be extrapolated from evidence in critical care and PGD literature"; no targets, settings or oxygen saturation goals are given. Item 16 (re-transplantation may be considered for selected patients): 2.98 + 2.23; p.14: "should be approached with considerable caution".

**71. Overarching goals** (ALAD 2026, p.11)
> "1. to deliver appropriate physiologic support to maintain adequate gas exchange and hemodynamic stability; 2. to identify and treat underlying causes ...; and 3. to minimize or prevent permanent damage to the allograft."

**72. Imaging timing (CT)**: see item 16. No time frame given; CT is for moderate/severe ALAD or specific CXR features. In CLAD care, CT is non-contrast by default (CLAD 2019 p.6). For ALAD contrast use is "individual" (item 16).

**73. Bronchoscopy timing**: see items 17-19. "For-cause bronchoscopy in all cases", may be deferred in mild ALAD, may be precluded in ventilated or ECMO patients. No time frame, no statement on bronchoscopy before or after starting treatment except Box 4 item 6 (empiric treatment may start before diagnostic testing).

**74. Sequencing in unstable patients** (ALAD 2026, p.8)
> "In cases of rapidly progressive ALAD, patients may be too unstable to undergo comprehensive diagnostic testing to identify a cause."
> "multiple etiologies may be driving rapidly progressive ALAD such that treatment should be directed at multiple potential etiologies"
Box 2 item 18 (4.42 + 1.39): "Rapidly progressive ALAD may be of any etiology."

**75. Concurrent antimicrobial plus immunosuppression augmentation** (ALAD 2026, p.12, Box 4 items 9-11)
Mild: "did not reach consensus, -0.00 + 2.12". Moderate: "did not reach consensus, 1.85 + 1.90". Severe: "consensus, 3.87 + 1.13": "For severe ALAD treatment, concomitant augmentation of immunosuppression and antimicrobial therapies should be considered in parallel."

## 7. Immunosuppression, drug levels, drug toxicity

**76. Drug levels in the workup** (ALAD 2026, p.9-10; Box 3 item 2)
Immunosuppressive drug level is part of the consensus blood work (item 14). p.10: "current trough immunosuppressive drug levels, which if subtherapeutic may suggest acute rejection"; also "review changes in gastrointestinal function and assess for occurrence of multiple subtherapeutic drug levels, significant variation outside the intended therapeutic range, and/or lack of adherence to the center's protocolized blood testing frequency." No target range for any drug is given.

**77. Adherence and recent changes** (ALAD 2026, p.9): history should include "recent immunosuppressive therapy, adherence to medical recommendations". BLAD 2026 p.11: "medication adherence concerns (especially for immunosuppressive drugs)".

**78. Augmentation** (ALAD 2026, p.12-13): see items 31 and 77. "The group determined that augmentation of immunosuppression should be considered in all patients with ALAD." The statement is tempered: consensus to "recommend" empiric augmentation in severe ALAD; no agreement for mild or moderate (p.13).

**79. Drug toxicity relevant to lung function** (CLAD 2019, p.3, Table 1 B4, text layer)
> "Drug or other induced pulmonary toxicity (e.g., sirolimus, methotrexate, amiodarone, radiotherapy)"
listed among causes that "cannot be differentiated easily from CLAD". CLAD 2019 p.7 also lists "conversion of cyclosporine to tacrolimus" as a CLAD (BOS) option and p.8: "De-novo tacrolimus use, as compared with cyclosporine, was associated with a significantly reduced risk for BOS Grade >=1 at 3 years". No document describes calcineurin-inhibitor toxicity, drug interactions, or what to do with a toxic level.

**80. Steroids** (CLAD 2019, p.7): "sustained administration of high-dose corticosteroids should be avoided" in CLAD (BOS) (item 57). ALAD 2026 treats pulse steroids as acceptable for possible alloimmune ALAD (item 31). No dose is given in either.

**81. IVIg, lymphocyte-depleting agents, plasma exchange** named only as options (ALAD 2026 Box 4 item 12, 4.21 + 0.72); no toxicity statements beyond "It is also particularly important to be cognizant of potential risks of empiric immunosuppression and to carefully balance efficacy and safety" (p.13).

## Ambiguities / internal inconsistencies

**A1. How 2007 grading, ALAD 2026 and CLAD 2019 fit together: the documents do not say.** (a) ALAD 2026 treats ACR and AMR as aetiologies (p.7-8) and cites the 2007 paper only for biopsy sample adequacy (reference 48, p.11); it never states how an A-grade maps to "mild/moderate/severe ALAD", which are defined by symptoms, oxygen or hospitalisation, not histology. (b) Nomenclature 2007 predates ALAD, BLAD and CLAD staging; its Category C (obliterative bronchiolitis) and "BOS ... is the preferred means of diagnosing and monitoring chronic airway rejection" (p.10) predate the 2019 CLAD umbrella term. (c) CLAD 2019 says rejection or infection must be treated before CLAD can be called (p.2), but the ALAD document says ALAD can be diagnosed in someone with CLAD without altering the reference FEV1 (p.6). (d) BLAD 2026 states ALAD and CLAD are the only possible labels for any decline (p.9), but ALAD requires a decline from the max of the **preceding 3-6 months** (ALAD) whereas CLAD uses the **best-two baseline** (>=20%); a patient with a 10-19% decline against baseline but >= 3-6 months of slow loss is not clearly classed (ALAD p.4 advises "caution"; ALAD p.6 "chronicity unknown"). (e) ALAD 2026 is dated 2026 but its Figure 2 caption says full ACR and AMR criteria "are omitted from this figure for clarity"; the criteria themselves are not reproduced anywhere in the ALAD document.

**A2. 2016 vs 2026 AMR.** AMR 2026 says it "update[s] the 2016 definition" (p.2) but does not explicitly say that "definite/probable/possible" should be retired. Under 2016, "other causes excluded" was a criterion; under 2026 AMR "can co-exist" with infection. A chart note saying "probable AMR" (2016) cannot be translated mechanically into a GAP code.

**A3. Stray reviewer comment in ALAD Box 2 item 17.** The text layer reads: "Treatment response or lack of response can assist in determination of ALAD etiology. [Very strange statement - unclear too me what is meant] (consensus, 4.32 + 0.79)". The bracketed editorial remark is in the proof ("Article in press"); the statement was still scored as consensus. Meaning of the statement is also explained in the narrative (p.8, item 21 above).

**A4. ALAD text vs Delphi on empiric immunosuppression.** p.13: "The group determined that augmentation of immunosuppression should be considered in all patients with ALAD" but the same paragraph says no agreement for mild or moderate. Box 4 item 7 (without known cause) and item 8 (rapidly progressive) carry **identical printed scores** (2.69 + 1.57), which may be a copy error. In-text numbering is also off: p.13 says "consensus to recommend empiric augmentation ... in severe ALAD (4.)" but item 4 is empiric antibiotics for severe ALAD. Items 9-10 (concomitant therapy mild or moderate) are cited as the immunosuppression items. Treat the Delphi items as authoritative for wording and the narrative for intent, and do not infer a dose or trigger.

**A5. "Consensus" thresholds differ.** ALAD 2026 defines consensus as mean >= 2.5 or <= -2.5 (p.3) only; items with SD larger than the mean (e.g. 2.63 + 2.05; 2.54 + 1.66; 2.52 + 2.11) are labelled "consensus". AMR 2026 required "an SD that did not cross zero" (p.3), which ALAD 2026 does not. BLAD 2026 used % agreement (>= 75%) or mean >= 1.00 on a 5-point scale. Scores are therefore not comparable across the three 2026 documents. Also AMR 2026 p.9 prints "grade > A3" for severe ACR; strictly "> A3" would mean A4 only, and the document does not clarify whether A3 is meant.

**A6. DSA strength: MFI.** AMR 2016 says MFI "does not represent the titer" (p.2). AMR 2026 acknowledges MFI limits (p.4) but codes A2-A4 by MFI bands (<2500, 2500-8000, >8000) while saying "the HLA community has not endorsed specific cutoffs". Table 2 of AMR 2026 says to "Consider close follow-up for intermediate and strong" DSA (A3, A4) even in G0 P0, an action that is not defined.

**A7. Definition of severe ALAD.** Table 1 and Box 1 item 10: "need for respiratory hospitalization irrespective of spirometry"; p.6 narrative: "hospitalization for respiratory failure". It is not stated whether a recipient admitted for non-respiratory reasons, or for pneumonia with a stable FEV1 and no oxygen need, counts as severe.

**A8. De-escalation timing.** Box 4 item 5: "after 72 hours if culture results are negative"; text p.13: "within 24 h of receiving definitive culture results or reassessed by 72 h after initiation".

**A9. Persistent ALAD.** Table 1: "Neither recovery to within > 90% of the reference FEV1 ... nor progression"; Box 1 item 15: "neither recovery nor progression at 3 months". Recovered ALAD: "within 10% of the reference" (item 6). The "> 90%" symbol in the table is as printed; an FEV1 exactly 90% is unaddressed.

**A10. Spirometry vs clinical definition.** The ALAD text recommends "the spirometric definition of ALAD for research studies" (p.6) because the clinical definition "involves clinical judgment"; the clinical criteria (including symptoms and exam findings alone, ground-glass alone) reached only weak consensus (2.63; 2.54) and are the criteria an ED clinician would most often use.

**A11. CLAD 2019 internal inconsistency on onset.** The definition says CLAD is confirmed at 3 months (p.2) but the Limitations say onset is the first test showing >=20% decline (p.8). The text says criteria for "possible" CLAD is >=20% fall but investigations are triggered by >=10% (p.2). Table 3 footnote d says transitions between BOS and RAS are "mixed"; Table 3 has two rows labelled "Undefined" with a single heading.

**A12. CLAD 2019 on rejection/infection and baseline reset.** Table 1 section B: no resetting of baseline after acute infection or rejection; p.8: "the baseline should not be reset after conditions that are known risk factors for CLAD (such as acute cellular or antibody-mediated rejection, and infection)". By contrast ALAD 2026 Figure 1B and p.4 allow a 3- vs 6-month reference window; the ALAD document does not say how an unresolved ALAD should be coded against the CLAD baseline when it persists beyond 3 months, other than "Persistent ALAD" or CLAD.

**A13. ALAD "article in press" proof status.** ALAD 2026 and AMR 2026 PDFs are headed "ARTICLE IN PRESS"; the manifest lists final citations. Wording and scores may differ in the final version; they were quoted from the saved proof.

**A14. Table 1 count in the 2026 BLAD document.** Delphi recommendation 12 appears in Table 3 (aetiologies) rather than Table 2, so Table 2 numbering skips 12; BLAD says 17 actionable recommendations (p.2). Not relevant to ED use, noted for citation accuracy.
