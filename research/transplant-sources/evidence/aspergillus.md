## How to read the locators

- Both are saved IDSA web pages (HTML), no page numbers. Locator = section heading as printed + `[P#]`, where `[P#]` is the Nth paragraph/list item in document order from the top of the saved file (extraction script in the session scratchpad). A text search of the quoted words finds each item. Numbers are per file, so "2026 [P181]" and "2016 [P181]" are different items; each item names its document.
- **Grades.** IDSA prints "(strength recommendation; quality evidence)", e.g. "(strong recommendation; high-quality evidence)" in 2016; 2026 prints "(conditional recommendation, low certainty of evidence)" or "(good practice statement)" / "(no recommendation, knowledge gap)". Copied exactly as printed.

## A. IDSA 2026 (prevention)

### A1. Which organ recipients are at highest risk and when

**A1.1 Heart: incidence and risk factors** (Heart Transplant Recipients, Results [P249]; mapping review, no grade)
> "the pooled incidence of IA was approximately 3.8% (95% CI 2.5 to 5.7%)" (6,005 recipients, 16 studies, no prophylaxis)
> "The most consistently reported and reproducible risk factors were post-operative RRT, reoperation or redo thoracic surgery, cytomegalovirus infection, and augmented immunosuppression for treatment of rejection"
Other reported factors: "prolonged mechanical ventilation or intubation, extracorporeal membrane oxygenation, hypoalbuminemia, multiple pre-transplant hospitalizations, prior Aspergillus colonization, and environmental or programmatic exposures such as hospital construction".

**A1.2 Kidney** (Kidney, Results [P195]): pooled IA incidence "0.8% (95% CI 0.6 to 1.2%)" (35,240 recipients; 34 studies); "The one-year all-cause mortality among 124 cases with available survival data was 45%." Factors: "low volume transplant centers, older age, preceding bacterial pneumonia, Candida colonization (which may be a surrogate marker for severe immunosuppression), diabetes, chronic lung or heart disease, and leukopenia"; "Other factors that may increase risk include delayed graft function, acute rejection, and RRT."

**A1.3 Pancreas** (Pancreas, Results [P202]): pooled incidence "0.8% (95% CI 0.3% to 1.7%)" (1,234 recipients); "The one-year all-cause mortality was 70%, although only three deaths were clearly attributable to IA." "some reports suggest a higher incidence of IA among patients receiving lymphocyte-depleting agents such as alemtuzumab, available data are insufficient to confirm this association".

**A1.4 Liver high-risk features** (Liver Recommendation 2 Remarks [P162]): "the most common risk factors for IA included: 1) renal replacement therapy in the peri-transplantation period, 2) re-transplantation, and 3) transplantation for fulminant hepatic failure." The 2026 liver incidence figure is in a table not saved (Table 2); not read.

**A1.5 Lung incidence:** not stated as a number in the saved 2026 text except "the very high incidence of IA (>17%) in all four studies" of the older before-and-after cohorts (Lung, Limitations [P218]), which the panel says raises "concerns about their generalizability to current practice".

**A1.6 Timing:** the 2026 text gives no onset-timing statement for any organ. Duration of prophylaxis in liver evidence: "typically until discharge from the initial transplant hospitalization through 28 days post-transplant" (Liver Rec 2 Remarks [P164]).

### A2. Prophylaxis strategy and agents named (so an ED physician can recognise them)

**A2.1 Liver, agents named in the evidence.** Universal prophylaxis studies: "agents examined were anidulafungin, micafungin, itraconazole, and liposomal amphotericin B" compared with fluconazole or none (Liver Rec 1 Remarks [P154]). Targeted: "voriconazole was evaluated as a prophylactic agent in both targeted and universal strategies" [P163]; targeted studies used "caspofungin, liposomal amphotericin B, and amphotericin B lipid complex" [P168].

**A2.2 Liver recommendations.**
- Rec 1 [P152]: "we suggest against using universal anti-Aspergillus prophylaxis (conditional recommendation, low certainty of evidence)."
- Rec 2 [P161]: "we suggest targeted anti-Aspergillus prophylaxis for individuals at high risk of invasive aspergillosis (conditional recommendation, very low certainty of evidence)."
- Rec 3 [P181]: "we suggest using an echinocandin or a newer anti-mold triazole (voriconazole, posaconazole or isavuconazole) rather than amphotericin B formulations or itraconazole (conditional recommendation, very low certainty of evidence)."
Rationale [P187]: "Itraconazole is limited by unreliable absorption and significant drug-drug interactions. Voriconazole and posaconazole also have notable interactions, requiring careful monitoring."

**A2.3 Kidney, pancreas, heart** (Conclusions [P193], [P200], [P247]; no grade, conclusions): "The current available evidence does not support the use of universal IA prophylaxis in kidney transplant recipients"; same wording for pancreas; heart: "does not support routine universal prophylaxis against IA".

**A2.4 Lung** (Lung Recs 1-3 [P207], [P210], [P240]): "we make no recommendation for or against universal anti-Aspergillus prophylaxis (no recommendation, knowledge gap)"; same for targeted prophylaxis or preemptive therapy. Rec 3 [P240]: agent choice "based on ... adverse events profile, drug-drug interactions, ease of administration and tolerability, associated costs and resources, availability, as well as local epidemiology (good practice statement)." Studies reviewed used "voriconazole or aerosolized amphotericin B with or without itraconazole" [P215]. The 2026 text says practice is "significant variability" [P209]. Lung harms listed: "toxicities, such as hepatotoxicity, bronchospasm" and "specific drug interactions with immunosuppressive agents" ([P231]).
(Tables 7 and 8 and the triazole/aerosolized amphotericin comparison tables referenced at [P235], [P237], [P242] are not saved.)

### A3. Azole and calcineurin/mTOR inhibitor interaction (2026)

**A3.1** Liver Rec 3 Remarks [P182] (no separate grade): "This recommendation prioritizes reducing adverse events (e.g., nephrotoxicity and hepatotoxicity), minimizing drug-drug interactions (especially with cyclosporine, tacrolimus, sirolimus and everolimus), and optimizing bioavailability."
**A3.2** Lung Limitations [P227]: "the lack of TDM for azoles in the three studies" and Lung Recs [P231]: "specific drug interactions with immunosuppressive agents". 2026 gives **no dose-adjustment instructions** for tacrolimus, cyclosporine or sirolimus (those would be in the unsaved Appendix A).
**A3.3** Monitoring statement: 2026 [P238] "continuous monitoring with regular assessment of IA rates, mortality, drug tolerability, and resistance patterns is essential."

### A4. Presentation, diagnosis, treatment in the 2026 document
Not covered. The 2026 guideline states its scope is prevention only ([P117]: "These guidelines focus specifically on anti-mold prophylaxis to prevent IA"). Diagnosis and treatment come from the 2016 guideline (section B).

## B. IDSA 2016 (diagnosis and management, full text)

### B1. Risk, who and when

**B1.1 Lung highest** (Epidemiology [P298]): "In SOT recipients, the intensity of immunosuppression to prevent or treat allograft rejection, colonization, and coinfection with CMV drive the risk of IA." "Lung transplant recipients have the highest risk of IA". "approximately one-half of cases of IA in lung transplant recipients were late-onset, occurring 1 year or more after transplantation". "CMV infection is a risk factor for aspergillosis, notably in heart and lung transplant recipients." Cystic fibrosis: pretransplant airway colonization "increases the risk of post-lung transplant IA".

**B1.2 Incidence by organ** (Prophylaxis Evidence Summary [P573]): "Invasive Aspergillus infection occurs in up to 19% of all SOT recipients (estimated 0.65% per year), with recent mortality estimates of approximately 22%." By organ: liver 1%-9.2%; heart 1%-14%; kidney 0.7%-4%; pancreas 3.4%.

**B1.3 Risk factors and timing** ([P573]): "the need for organ retransplantation (liver), posttransplant renal or hepatic failure with renal replacement therapy (liver and kidney), reexploration (liver and heart), pretransplant colonization with Aspergillus spp (heart), concurrent CMV infection (liver and heart), hepatitis C infection (liver), and steroid-based regimens". "Pulse-dosed corticosteroid therapy with lymphocyte depletion is a notable risk in the Aspergillus-colonized individual." "Infections tend to occur both early after transplantation (first month) and late (mean approximately 184 days)."
Recommendation [P571] (strong recommendation; low-quality evidence): "prophylactic strategies in SOT recipients based on the institutional epidemiology of infection and assessment of individual risk factors." "Prospective trials are lacking to address the need for routine anti-Aspergillus prophylaxis other than for lung transplant recipients." Risk factors listed in the recommendation: cardiac (pretransplant colonization, reoperation, CMV infection, renal dysfunction, institutional outbreak); liver (fulminant hepatic failure, reoperation, retransplantation, or renal failure); "others with institutional outbreaks or prolonged or high-dose corticosteroid use".

**B1.4 Tracheobronchial aspergillosis in lung recipients** (Full text [P472]): "TBA is most commonly described in lung transplant recipients, affecting 4%-6% of patients". "TBA typically occurs within 3-6 months of lung transplant". "Most lesions are asymptomatic and diagnosed by surveillance bronchoscopy; they manifest as pseudomembranes, ulceration, black eschar, or plaques." "These lesions can develop despite systemic antifungal prophylaxis."

### B2. Prophylaxis agents named (2016)

- Lung [P227] (strong recommendation; moderate-quality evidence): "antifungal prophylaxis with either a systemic triazole such as voriconazole or itraconazole or an inhaled AmB product for 3 to 4 months after lung transplant".
- [P228] (weak recommendation; low-quality evidence): systemic voriconazole or itraconazole over inhaled AmB for "mold colonization pre- or post-lung transplant, mold infections found in explanted lungs, fungal infections of the sinus, and single-lung transplant recipients".
- [P229] (strong recommendation; moderate-quality evidence): "reinitiating antifungal prophylaxis for lung transplant recipients receiving immunosuppression augmentation with either thymoglobulin, alemtuzumab, or high-dose corticosteroids".
- Full-text [P569]: "A benefit to continuing antifungal prophylaxis beyond 3-4 months after lung transplant has not been established." After that, suggested only for severe rejection needing thymoglobulin or alemtuzumab, or high-dose prolonged corticosteroids.
- [P567]: "An advantage of inhaled AmB is the lack of systemic adverse effects and/or drug-drug interactions; a disadvantage is its inability to prevent extrapulmonary fungal infections." "Azole prophylaxis is complicated by drug interactions with the calcineurin inhibitors, as well as liver toxicity."
- Inhaled formulation recommendation, [P148] (weak recommendation; low-quality evidence): "Aerosolized formulations of AmB may be considered as prophylaxis ... in lung transplant recipients."

### B3. Azole interaction guidance (2016)

**B3.1** [P372] (evidence summary, no grade): triazoles "demonstrate significant drug-drug interactions"; as a class they alter "serum levels of the azoles and of coadministered agents including calcineurin inhibitors and mammalian target of rapamycin inhibitor immunosuppressive agents, anticoagulants, psychiatric and neurotropic medications, barbiturates, glucocorticoids, digoxin ..." and "All of the azoles have important interactions via the CYP enzymes, notably CYP3A4".
**B3.2** [P368] (strong recommendation; moderate-quality evidence): "Clinicians should obtain serum trough drug levels for azole antifungal agents (itraconazole, voriconazole, posaconazole, and possibly isavuconazole) and for potentially interacting drugs such as cyclosporine, tacrolimus, and sirolimus".
**B3.3** [P367] (strong recommendation; moderate-quality evidence): TDM "once the steady state has been reached"; [P373] documentation of levels "in the first 4-7 days after starting therapy".
**B3.4 Trough targets named** (Evidence summary, no grade, "primarily ... single-center, retrospective studies and can only be considered a general guide"): voriconazole "trough of >1-1.5 ug/mL for efficacy but <5-6 ug/mL to minimize toxicity"; itraconazole "trough concentrations >0.5-1 ug/mL" (combined with hydroxyitraconazole >1.5 ug/mL) [P376], [P377], [P375].
**B3.5** The saved 2016 text gives **no dose-reduction rule** for tacrolimus/cyclosporine/sirolimus when an azole is started (Table 2 not saved).
**B3.6 Steroid effect** in the same guideline (strong recommendation context, [P372]): triazoles alter levels of "glucocorticoids" (no dose guidance).

### B4. Presentation and diagnosis (2016, mostly general, not SOT-specific)

- [P137] (strong recommendation; high-quality evidence): "GM is not recommended for screening in SOT recipients or patients with chronic granulomatous disease (CGD)". (Galactomannan applies to hematologic malignancy/HSCT [P135].)
- [P138] serum (1->3)-beta-D-glucan recommended "in high-risk patients (hematologic malignancy, allogeneic HSCT), but are not specific for Aspergillus (strong recommendation; moderate-quality evidence)". SOT not named.
- [P140] (strong recommendation; high-quality evidence): "We recommend performing a chest computed tomographic (CT) scan whenever there is a clinical suspicion for IPA regardless of chest radiograph results".
- [P144] (strong recommendation; moderate-quality evidence): "We recommend performing a bronchoscopy with bronchoalveolar lavage (BAL) in patients with a suspicion of IPA"; send BAL "for routine culture and cytology as well as non-culture-based methods (eg, GM)". Peripheral nodules: "percutaneous or endobronchial lung biopsy should be considered."
- [P130] (strong recommendation; high-quality evidence): submit "tissue and fluid specimens ... for simultaneous histopathologic/cytologic and culture examination".
- Imaging features (full text [P330], text only): "Typical features of IPA on CT imaging include nodules, consolidative lesions, and wedge-shaped infarcts." The halo sign is described as characteristic of angioinvasion "typically ... in severely neutropenic patients" [P331].
- A clinical symptom list for SOT patients (fever, cough, etc.) is **not** given in the saved 2016 text.

### B5. First-line treatment (2016)

- [P161] (strong recommendation; high-quality evidence): "We recommend primary treatment with voriconazole".
- [P162] (strong recommendation; high-quality evidence): "Early initiation of antifungal therapy in patients with strongly suspected IPA is warranted while a diagnostic evaluation is conducted".
- [P163] alternatives: "liposomal AmB (strong recommendation; moderate-quality evidence), isavuconazole (strong recommendation; moderate-quality evidence), or other lipid formulations of AmB (weak recommendation; low-quality evidence)".
- [P165] (strong recommendation; moderate-quality evidence): "Primary therapy with an echinocandin is not recommended".
- [P166] (strong recommendation; low-quality evidence): "continued for a minimum of 6-12 weeks".
- [P169] (strong recommendation; low-quality evidence): "Reducing doses of, or eliminating altogether, immunosuppressive agents, when feasible, is advised as a component of anti-Aspergillus therapy".
- [P167] (strong recommendation; moderate-quality evidence): "For patients with successfully treated IPA who require subsequent immunosuppression, secondary prophylaxis should be initiated".
- TBA in lung transplant [P191] (strong recommendation; moderate-quality evidence): "treatment with a systemic antimold antifungal for TBA, including saprophytic forms ... adjunctive inhaled AmB in the setting of TBA associated with anastomotic endobronchial ischemia"; duration "at least 3 months or until TBA is completely resolved, whichever is longer".
- Breakthrough on prophylaxis [P233] (weak recommendation; moderate-quality evidence): "Antifungal therapy should be empirically changed to an alternative class of antifungal with Aspergillus activity"; verify "serum azole levels"; "reduction of underlying immunosuppression if feasible".
- Triazoles overall [P152] (strong recommendation; high-quality evidence): "Triazoles are preferred agents for treatment and prevention of IA in most patients".
- Lung colonization (full text [P593], [P594]; weak; moderate and low quality): preemptive antimold therapy for asymptomatic airway colonization "within 6 months of lung transplant or within 3 months of receiving immunosuppression augmentation for rejection".
- Doses of voriconazole/isavuconazole/liposomal AmB for treatment are **not** in the abridged recommendations or the sections read.

## Conflict between the two documents (do not merge silently)

For **lung** transplant recipients: 2016 [P227] "We recommend antifungal prophylaxis ... for 3 to 4 months after lung transplant (strong recommendation; moderate-quality evidence)"; 2026 [P207] "no recommendation for or against universal anti-Aspergillus prophylaxis (no recommendation, knowledge gap)". The 2026 page lists the 2016 guideline as "Related Content" and calls itself an update ("first section"), so for prophylaxis the 2026 statement is the newer; the saved text does not say whether the 2016 lung recommendation is withdrawn.
For **liver**: 2016 [P571] gives no routine recommendation and "Individual risk factors" only; 2026 suggests targeted prophylaxis in high-risk patients and an echinocandin or newer anti-mold triazole.

## Not stated

- Any tacrolimus, cyclosporine, sirolimus or everolimus dose adjustment or target trough when an azole is started or stopped (both documents say only to monitor levels and that interactions are important).
- Isavuconazole interaction details; the posaconazole/isavuconazole prophylaxis efficacy in liver (2026 says no peer-reviewed studies).
- 2026 incidence for liver and lung, onset timing for any organ, any presentation or diagnostic approach (2026 is prevention-only).
- A SOT-specific clinical symptom list; galactomannan/beta-D-glucan accuracy in SOT beyond "GM not recommended for screening in SOT".
- Treatment doses (voriconazole loading/maintenance, liposomal AmB mg/kg) in the sections read.
- Contents of 2026 Tables 1-8 and Appendix A; contents of 2016 Tables 2-3 (not saved).
- Inhaled amphotericin product, dose or schedule (2026 and 2016 name "inhaled AmB product" and aerosolized formulations only).

## Ambiguities

- 2016 [P227] lists "voriconazole or itraconazole" and "inhaled AmB" but not posaconazole or isavuconazole for lung prophylaxis; 2026 lists posaconazole and isavuconazole only in the liver recommendation.
- 2016 incidence ranges (liver 1%-9.2%, heart 1%-14%) are from older studies; 2026 pooled heart 3.8%, kidney 0.8%, pancreas 0.8% use a different method (mapping review, no prophylaxis). The two are not directly comparable.
- Lung late-onset: 2016 says half of IA cases were 1 year or more after transplant [P298] while 2016 prophylaxis is 3-4 months; 2016 states benefit of prophylaxis beyond 3-4 months "has not been established".
- 2026 [P162] risk factor list for liver is "the most common risk factors for IA included", based on studies reviewed, not a formal risk score.
- The 2016 GM statement ("not recommended for screening in SOT recipients") concerns screening of asymptomatic patients; use of GM on BAL in symptomatic SOT patients is not addressed separately in the saved text (BAL GM is mentioned generally at [P144]).
- Paragraph numbers [P#] are per saved file; the 2016 grades use the older "quality of evidence" wording, the 2026 uses "certainty".
