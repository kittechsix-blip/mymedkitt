## Ten priority rows (full text and omitted qualifiers)
- K1 (Ch 6 Background, ungraded, p34): "An acute rejection is clinically suspected in patients experiencing an increase in serum creatinine, after the exclusion of other causes of graft dysfunction (generally with biopsy)." No creatinine threshold given. Rationale, not a recommendation.
- K2 (6.1, 1C, p34): "We recommend biopsy before treating acute rejection, unless the biopsy will substantially delay treatment." Claim complete. Same page: no RCTs show biopsy improves outcomes; BK nephropathy would be treated differently (rationale).
- K41 (p40 rationale, ungraded): "Causes of kidney allograft dysfunction that require rapid intervention for treatment to be effective include acute rejection, obstruction, urine leak, vascular compromise and some recurrent diseases, for example FSGS. These causes are more common in the first few days to weeks after kidney transplantation than in subsequent months to years." Rationale, no grade.
- K49 (9.1, 1C, p43): "We recommend kidney allograft biopsy when there is a persistent, unexplained increase in serum creatinine." Qualifiers in rationale: first exclude dehydration, urinary obstruction, acute CNI toxicity (high blood levels) before biopsy (K57); a 25-50% rise is "often arbitrarily used in studies" (K58). 9.2 (2D), 9.3 (2C), 9.4 (2D), 9.5 (2C) are separate rows.
- K38 (8.4, 2C, p40): "We suggest including a kidney allograft ultrasound examination as part of the assessment of kidney allograft dysfunction." Claim complete. Rationale (p42, K84, K85): finds arterial occlusion, venous thrombosis, urinary obstruction, urine leak, perinephric hematoma, post-biopsy AV fistula; mild to moderate calyceal distension can be normal.
- K155 (14.1.2, 2C, p72): "For allograft pyelonephritis, we suggest initial hospitalization and treatment with intravenous antibiotics. (2C)" Claim exact. Qualifiers (rationale, ungraded): "at least initially"; "particularly true in early infections (first 4-6 months)"; 14 days anticipated adequate absent a kidney abscess; relapsing infection considered for longer. No antibiotic named.
- K166 (14.2.3, 1C, p73): "We recommend that KTRs with PCP diagnosed by bronchial alveolar lavage and/or lung biopsy be treated with high-dose intravenous trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole, corticosteroids, and a reduction in immunosuppressive medication. (1C)" Claim exact, including the diagnostic condition. Corticosteroid qualifier: 14.2.3 names corticosteroids without restriction, but 14.2.4 (1C) recommends them for moderate to severe PCP (PaO2 <70 mm Hg in room air or alveolar gradient >35 mm Hg) and the rationale says "for KTRs with significant hypoxemia". The two statements are not reconciled. Reduction of immunosuppression is part of the same sentence at 1C; the guideline does not say who decides. Usual duration 2-3 weeks; IV pentamidine if proven TMP-SMX allergy (rationale).
- K141 (13.4.2, p63): "We recommend that KTRs with systemic HSV 1, 2 infection be treated (1B) with intravenous acyclovir and a reduction in immunosuppressive medication. (1D)" 13.4.2.1: IV until clinical response (1B), then oral to complete 14-21 days (2D). Rationale: continue IV until fever, hypoxia and hepatitis signs resolve; hospitalization warranted.
- K316 (23.1.2, 1B, p115): "We recommend avoiding allopurinol in patients receiving azathioprine." Rationale: profound life-threatening pancytopenia; "extreme caution, or not at all"; if used together reduce azathioprine by at least 50% with frequent blood counts. K317 (23.1.3, 2D): "We suggest avoiding NSAIDs and COX-2 inhibitors whenever possible." Claims exact. Parent 23.1 (2D) is treating hyperuricemia when complications exist.
- K249 (14.3.2, 1C, p74): "We recommend monitoring CNI and mTORi blood levels in patients receiving rifampin." 14.3.2.1 (Not Graded): "Consider substituting rifabutin for rifampin to minimize interactions with CNIs and mTORi." Rationale: rifampin strongly induces CYP3A4; CNI doses have needed a two- to threefold rise. No numeric target.
