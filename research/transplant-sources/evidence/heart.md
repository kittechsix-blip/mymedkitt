# Heart transplant recipient care: evidence pack

Source of record: Velleca A, Shullo MA, et al. *The International Society for Heart and Lung Transplantation (ISHLT) guidelines for the care of heart transplant recipients.* J Heart Lung Transplant 2023;42(5):e1-e141. Saved file: `ISHLT_heart_recipient_care_2023_guideline.pdf` (141 PDF pages; PDF page N = printed page eN, checked against page footers).

## How to read the locators and grades (important for the verifier)

- **Locator** = printed page (e-number) + section heading as printed (Task Force = TF, Topic = T).
- **Grades** are printed as "Class I/IIa/IIb/III, Level of Evidence A/B/C". Quoted exactly as printed. Where the guideline gives no class it is marked **ungraded** (narrative text).
- **Two-column recommendation tables.** Almost every recommendation sits in a table with a left column "2010 Prior Guideline Recommendation" and a right column "2023 Guideline Update Recommendation". If the right column says "Continuing approval without change", the **2010 wording and its grade are the current recommendation**. If both columns carry text, the right column replaces the left. A heading "New Recommendation" means the 2023 column only. Extracted PDF text puts the columns one after the other, which is easy to misattribute: on e22 the doses of TMP/SMX, pentamidine, dapsone and atovaquone are in the **2010** column only, and the 2023 column has no doses. Items below are attributed by column; the pages read from images are listed next.
- **Tables / recommendation pages read from rendered page IMAGES** (single-page PDF rendered to PNG and viewed): Table 4 (e40), Table 5 (e44), Tables 9 and 10 (e57), Table 15 and the recommendation columns on e96, recommendation columns on e20, e22, e52, e91. Table 15 turned out to be in the 2010 column only (see 7.12).
- **Read from extracted text, not images (column attribution inferred from pairing and "Continuing approval" markers; treat as less certain):** Table 3 CMV prevention (e15), Table 11 CAV nomenclature (e66), Table 14 anaesthesia (e98), recommendation pages e18-19, e59-60, e65, e69, e72-80, e96-97 (except column checks above), e85, e92.
- Adults only. Pediatric-only recommendations were ignored. "HT" = heart transplant; CS = corticosteroid; CNI = calcineurin inhibitor; TAC = tacrolimus; CYA = cyclosporine; PSI/mTORi = proliferation signal / mTOR inhibitor; EMB = endomyocardial biopsy.
- **Scope warning.** This is a guideline for the transplant team. It is not written for emergency care. Much of the arrhythmia and denervation text sits in perioperative sections (TF1 early post-op care, TF4 intercurrent surgery). Where I use it for a late recipient I say so.

## 1. Acute cellular rejection (ACR) and antibody-mediated rejection (AMR)

**1.1 Rate of treated rejection** (e51, TF2 T4 narrative; ungraded)
> "13% of patients experienced a treated rejection episode between the time of hospital discharge to their 1-year follow-up visit"
(2010-2016 cohort; 24% in 2004-2006.)

**1.2 Rejection is often silent** (e34, TF2 T1 "Noninvasive monitoring for acute rejection"; ungraded)
> "rejection often occurs in asymptomatic patients"
Also (e34, "Post-transplant rejection surveillance"): surveillance biopsies in asymptomatic patients on CNI/mycophenolate regimens yield "between 1 and 2%" for moderate or higher ACR; clinically indicated biopsies for signs or symptoms of graft dysfunction yielded "18% in one study".

**1.3 Symptoms that guide intensity of treatment** (e51, TF2 T4 narrative; ungraded)
> "symptoms of congestion (shortness of breath, abdominal bloating, orthopnea) or low cardiac output (fatigue, low blood pressures, and decreased urine output)"
Same paragraph: LV or RV systolic dysfunction on echo "even in the absence of symptoms, is typically treated as symptomatic rejection."

**1.4 Biopsy if rejection suspected** (e51, T4 Symptomatic ACR; 2010 rec, "Continuing approval without change"; **Class I, Level of Evidence: C**)
> "An EMB should be performed as early as possible if there is suspicion of symptomatic acute heart allograft rejection."

**1.5 Admit; ICU for haemodynamic compromise** (e51, same table; 2023 wording; **Class I, Level of Evidence: C**)
> "Patients with hemodynamic compromise (hypotension, low cardiac output, or marked elevation of pulmonary capillary wedge pressure) should be treated in the ICU, or an intermediate-care unit with the ability to perform continuous hemodynamic monitoring and administer inotropes."
Preceding sentence of the same rec: "The HT recipient with symptomatic acute cellular rejection should be hospitalized."

**1.6 First-line treatment of symptomatic ACR** (e52, image read; 2010 rec, "Continuing approval without change"; **Class I, Level of Evidence: C**)
> "High-dose IV CS should be first-line therapy for symptomatic acute cellular rejection irrespective of ISHLT EMB grade (1R, 2R, or 3R)."
No dose is printed in the ACR recommendation.

**1.7 Cytolytic therapy for haemodynamic compromise** (e52, image read; 2010 rec, continued; **Class I, Level of Evidence: C**)
> "Cytolytic immunosuppressive therapy with anti-thymocyte antibodies should be administered in addition to IV CS if hemodynamic compromise is present, and especially if there is no clinical improvement within 12 to 24 hours"

**1.8 Haemodynamic support** (e52; continued; **Class I, Level of Evidence: C**)
> "IV inotropes and vasopressors should be used as necessary to maintain adequate CO and systemic blood pressure until recovery of heart allograft function occurs."

**1.9 Prophylaxis during rejection therapy** (e52; continued; **Class I, Level of Evidence: C**)
> "Antimicrobial prophylaxis against opportunistic infections should be administered when high-dose CS and/or cytolytic therapy are used for the treatment of rejection."

**1.10 Do not use IL-2 receptor blockers** (e52; continued; **Class I, Level of Evidence: C**)
> "IL-2 receptor blockers should not be used to reverse acute cellular rejection."

**1.11 Low-grade ACR with compromise: think AMR and CAV** (e52, image read; 2023 wording; **Class I, Level of Evidence: C**)
> "In a patient with low-grade acute cellular rejection and hemodynamic compromise, the possibility of AMR (see AMR section) and/or CAV should also be entertained."

**1.12 Follow-up biopsy** (e52, image read; 2023 wording; **Class I, Level of Evidence: C**)
> "Follow-up EMB should be performed 2 to 4 weeks after initiation of therapy for acute cellular rejection, unless there is a compelling indication for earlier histologic evaluation."

**1.13 Adjust maintenance drugs** (e52; continued; **Class I, Level of Evidence: C**)
> "Appropriate adjustments of maintenance immunosuppressive therapy should be made to decrease the risk of recurrent rejection."
Includes "ascertainment of compliance with current therapy".

**1.14 Asymptomatic ACR** (e52-53, T4 "Asymptomatic ACR"; continued 2010 recs)
- 3R: "should be treated even in the absence of symptoms" and "High dose IV CS should be given" (**Class I, Level of Evidence: C**).
- 2R: "can be treated with either IV or oral CS" (**Class I, Level of Evidence: C**).
- 1R: 2023 wording "does not require treatment in most cases, but maintenance doses of immunosuppressive agents should be adjusted" (**Class IIa, Level of Evidence: C**).
- 2R after 12 months: "may not require treatment" with close surveillance (2023 wording, **Class IIb, Level of Evidence: C**).

**1.15 Recurrent or resistant ACR** (e53; continued **Class I, Level of Evidence: C** for antithymocyte; 2023 **Class IIa, Level of Evidence: B** for photopheresis/TLI)
> "Recurrent or resistant acute cellular rejection that occurs after treatment with CS and cytolytic immunosuppressive therapy and optimization of the patient's maintenance immunosuppressive regimen can be treated with photopheresis or total lymphoid irradiation"
Also (2023, **Class I, Level of Evidence: C**): evaluation of EMB for concomitant AMR and anti-HLA antibodies "is recommended."

**1.16 AMR: varied presentation** (e54, T5 narrative; ungraded)
> "the variability in presentation of AMR from rare hyperacute to variable intensity of acute rejection and finally asymptomatic or subclinical phenotypes."

**1.17 AMR timing** (e58, T5 narrative; ungraded)
> "More commonly AMR occurs in the first weeks and months after HT, although late subclinical AMR is increasingly recognized"
Hyperacute (e54): "within minutes or hours after HT" and "typically results in cardiogenic shock."

**1.18 AMR is a major cause of late rejection** (e60, T6 narrative; ungraded)
> "AMR as an important cause of late rejection, accelerated CAV, and graft failure."
and "patients presenting with signs or symptoms of graft dysfunction late after HT should undergo an evaluation for AMR with EMB and immunostaining for complement activation or antibody binding before steroid treatment and should be tested for the presence of circulating HLA donor-specific antibodies"

**1.19 Late rejection: AMR commoner than ACR; test before treating** (e61, T6 table; 2023 new; **Class IIa, Level of Evidence: C**)
> "EMB with ISHLT immunopathologic evaluation, as well as measurement of circulating HLA donor-specific-antibodies should be obtained before initiating treatment."

**1.20 Late rejection: adherence, co-medications, triggers** (e61; 2023 new; **Class IIa, Level of Evidence: B**)
> "Careful assessment of adherence to medications, co-medications or supplements interfering with IS and triggering events (infections, GI disorders) is recommended in any case of late rejection"

**1.21 Risk factors for late rejection** (e60, T6 narrative; ungraded; also e60 table, **Class IIa, Level of Evidence: C**)
> "younger recipient age, prior history of acute rejection episodes or episodes occurring > 6 months after transplantation, African American ethnicity, presence of HLA donor-specific antibodies, donor and recipient sex mismatch, calcineurin-inhibitor (CNI) reduced or free immunosuppression, and a history of medication non-adherence."
(The T6 table list differs slightly; see Ambiguities.)

**1.22 AMR diagnosis categories** (e54, T5 narrative; ungraded): pAMR 0, pAMR 1(H+), pAMR 1(I+), pAMR 2, pAMR 3 (severe: interstitial haemorrhage, capillary fragmentation, mixed infiltrates, endothelial pyknosis/karyorrhexis, marked oedema plus immunopathologic findings).
AMR diagnosis rec (e59, image-not-viewed; 2023): "When AMR is suspected EMB examination should include AMR pathology assessment and classification according to the ISHLT grading consensus." **Class I Level of Evidence: C.** Serum: "Serum samples should be drawn before initiation of therapy to avoid assay interference from therapeutic agents." (**Class IIa, Level of Evidence C**).

**1.23 AMR treatment options** (e59, T5 table; 2023 column; **Class IIa, Level of Evidence C**)
> "high-dose IV CS; (2) cytolytic immunosuppressive therapy. (3) Rituximab (4) Bortezomib or carfilzomib (5) Eculizumab"
Also, antibody removal "plasmapheresis; (2) immune apheresis (immunoadsorption); (3) IV Ig" and "IV inotropes and vasopressors; (2) MCS" (continued from 2010, **Class IIa, Level of Evidence C**). "Systemic anticoagulation may decrease intravascular thrombosis in the heart allograft." (e60, "Continuing approval without change", grade not printed in the shown cell).

**1.24 AMR doses and schedule that the guideline does print** (e55, e57; narrative and Table 10 **read from image**; ungraded)
- Methylprednisolone: "500-1000 mg daily" for "3 consecutive days" (e55; the sentence also gives "10mg/kg/day for children").
- Table 10 (e57, image): Plasmapheresis "1-2 plasma exchanges", daily or every other day for "3-5 days", or "3 times per week" for "1-4 weeks", or "once weekly" "2-4 weeks". IV immunoglobulin "100-2000 mg/kg", "low dose 1-3 times per week, often given after each plasmapheresis" or "Immune modulating dose (2 g/kg) after last plasmapheresis cycle q 4 weeks". Rituximab "375 mg/m2" once weekly for "1-4 weeks".
- Table 9 (e57, image): eculizumab adverse effect "Meningococcal infection (Vaccination recommended)".
No ATG dose is printed.

**1.25 Timing trap for AMR therapy** (e57, narrative; ungraded)
> "Polyclonal and monoclonal antilymphocytic antibodies, IV immunoglobulin or rituximab should not be given shortly before plasmapheresis or immune apheresis, as they are removed by this process."

**1.26 AMR with compromise** (e57; ungraded)
> "IV inotropic agents and vasopressors and at times MCS may be required to maintain adequate organ perfusion"
Refractory: "bortezomib, eculizumab, and total lymphoid irradiation may be effective" (limited data).

**1.27 Follow-up and asymptomatic/mixed AMR** (e58; ungraded unless noted): follow-up EMB "2 to 4 weeks after initiation of therapy for acute AMR". Mixed rejection with compromise: "aggressive therapy with high-dose IV CS, and cytolytic therapy is appropriate."

**1.28 Hyperacute rejection** (e59, T5 table; 2023 column; **Class I, Level of Evidence: C**)
> "Treatment for hyperacute rejection should be initiated as soon as the diagnosis is made, preferably when the HT recipient is still in the operating room."
Options listed: MCS, high-dose IV CS, plasmapheresis, IVIG, rituximab, cytolytic therapy, eculizumab, IV CNI with increased target levels, inotropes and vasopressors, heparin.

**1.29 Retransplantation after acute rejection** (e60, 2023 column; **Class III, Level of Evidence: C**)
> "Emergent retransplantation following acute rejection is associated with unfavorable outcomes."

**1.30 Who gives treatment.** The guideline places rejection treatment in a hospital with transplant expertise: hospitalisation is required (1.5), and PTLD care is "at the transplant center" (8.12). It does **not** state that an emergency physician may start steroids or ATG, nor who should give them in an emergency. See Not stated.

## 2. The denervated heart

**2.1 Resting heart rate** (e96, image read, 2010 column, "Continuing approval without change"; **Class I, Level of Evidence C** printed on e97)
> "The resting heart rate is usually higher in HT recipients. Although most allografts have a resting heart rate of approximately 90 beats/min, some have resting sinus rates as high as 130 beats/min, which do not require treatment."

**2.2 Atropine and bradycardia** (e96, image read, same recommendation)
> "a relative, symptomatic, bradycardia that requires treatment will not respond to atropine. Isoproterenol infusion and pacing are the usual modes of management of HT bradyarrhythmias."

**2.3 Atropine and glycopyrrolate, reversal agents** (e98, Table 14, text read; ungraded table)
> "The indirect acting anticholinergics (atropine, glycopyrrolate) and anticholinesterases (neostigmine, edrophonium) have no effect on the heart rate of the cardiac allograft"
Same row: no "instances of severe bradycardia or cardiac arrest" in a large study of neuromuscular reversal; sugammadex "devoid of any direct cholinergic effects".

**2.4 Autonomic drugs, catecholamines, phenylephrine** (e98, Table 14; ungraded)
> "Given the complete cardiac denervation, drugs that work on the autonomic nervous system have minimal effects on the transplanted heart."
- "Indirect-acting sympathomimetics such as ephedrine are therefore not very effective for treating hypotension and maintaining cardiac output; and ketamine may not display hemodynamic stability in heart transplant patients in extremis."
- "Direct-acting sympathetic agents, like norepinephrine, epinephrine, isoproterenol, and dopamine, are effective, although the beta-adrenergic inotropic effects are attenuated early after HT."
- "Phosphodiesterases have been shown to increase inotropy in the transplanted heart. The alpha-adrenergic response of phenylephrine is effective, but the reflex bradycardia is absent."

**2.5 Adenosine: sensitivity** (e96, image read, same recommendation as 2.1; **Class I, Level of Evidence C**)
> "the denervated heart is highly sensitive to adenosine, and the use of standard doses to treat atrial tachyarrhythmias may result in prolonged asystole."

**2.6 Adenosine: low-dose rule** (e20, image read; 2023 new; **Class IIb, Level of Evidence C**)
> "Adenosine use may be considered in HT recipients with supraventricular tachyarrhythmias if they are closely monitored and low doses are administered (25 mcg/kg: 1.5 mg if ≥ 60 kg)."
Narrative (e14, TF1 perioperative arrhythmia; ungraded): "Use of adenosine post-transplant was previously a relative contraindication due to presumed risk of prolonged AV block in the denervated heart. However, a recent study suggests minimal risk when low initial doses are used (25 mcg/kg; 1.5 mg if ≥ 60 kg) and therapy is gradually increased."
Narrative (e79, TF3 T8; ungraded): adenosine "should only be administered with caution at low doses (e.g., 3 mg in adults) due to increased risk of severe bradycardia or asystole". **The two printed adult doses differ (1.5 mg vs 3 mg): see Ambiguities.**

**2.7 Chronotropic drugs** (e19, TF1 T4 table; 2023 column; **Class I, Level of Evidence B**, perioperative setting)
> "Pharmacologic chronotropic agents, including isoproterenol, theophylline, terbutaline, and albuterol can be used in the perioperative setting to increase heart rate."

**2.8 Pacing targets early after HT** (e19; continued 2010 rec; **Class I, Level of Evidence B**)
> "After HT, temporary pacing should be initiated in the setting of relative bradycardia to maintain heart rates of > 90 beats/min."
Narrative (e14): "pacemaker implant is indicated if symptomatic bradycardia persists after 3 weeks post-transplant." Table text: "Recommendations for permanent pacing exist for inappropriate chronotropic response 3 weeks after HT."

**2.9 Late bradyarrhythmia** (e79, TF3 T8 narrative; ungraded)
> "Although acute rejection may result in bradyarrhythmias, late sinus node dysfunction and AV block are often not related to acute rejection."
> "In general, conventional indications for permanent pacemakers should be applied in these patients."
AV block "may be more common than sinus node disease in heart transplant patients with late bradyarrhythmias." Incidence of late (>3 months) pacing "about 4.4%" in one single-centre study.

**2.10 Permanent pacing** (e80, TF3 T8; 2023 new): "Permanent pacing is recommended regardless of symptoms in HT recipients with second-degree Mobitz type II atrioventricular block, high-grade atrioventricular block, or third-degree atrioventricular block." (**Class I, Level of Evidence: B**).

**2.11 Beta-blockers and calcium-channel blockers: rate control** (e20, image read; 2010 rec, "Continuing approval without change"; **Class IIa, Level of Evidence B**)
> "Non-dihydropyridine calcium channel blockers (CCBs) and b-blockers may be used in HT recipients for rate control."
e14 narrative: "Beta blockers, calcium antagonists, adenosine and amiodarone can be used safely after HT". AF rate control (e80; 2023 new; **Class IIa, Level of Evidence: B**): non-dihydropyridine CCBs and beta-blockers "may be used in HT recipients with preserved ejection fraction for ventricular rate control of AF."

**2.12 Digoxin.** Not found. A search of the whole text for "digoxin" returns nothing in any recommendation or narrative.

**2.13 Anginal pain / chest pain in the recipient.** The guideline does **not** state that the denervated heart lacks anginal pain, nor that CAV or MI is painless. The only chest-pain statement is the "inform the transplant center" list: "chest pain other than musculoskeletal symptoms" (e91, 2023 item 12; see 9.1).

**2.14 Catecholamine / vasoplegia and RV support, perioperative** (e14-e15, e17; early post-op): "Norepinephrine is considered the first-line agent for treatment of vasoplegia, followed by vasopressin." RV dysfunction: "inotropic support (such as epinephrine and isoproterenol)", milrinone, inhaled nitric oxide or prostacyclin. Perioperative only; applying to late presenters is the app author's inference.

## 3. Arrhythmias in the recipient

**3.1 Atrial arrhythmias: causes and first step** (e78, TF3 T8 narrative; ungraded)
> "Atrial arrhythmias may be related to underlying CAV, acute rejection, and acute illness such as sepsis. Therefore, endomyocardial biopsy and coronary angiography should be considered in heart transplant recipients with new-onset atrial arrhythmias."
Incidence "may be as high as 30%". "The majority of atrial arrhythmias are right atrial macro-reentrant atrial arrhythmias such as atrial flutter (AFl)." "Atrial fibrillation (AF) appears to be less common." "The electrocardiographic morphology of the AFl cannot reliably determine the nature of the AFl."

**3.2 Biopsy and angiography for atrial or ventricular arrhythmia** (e79, T8 table; 2023 new; **Class I, Level of Evidence: B**)
> "Clinicians should consider endomyocardial biopsy and coronary angiography in HT recipients with atrial or ventricular arrhythmias to exclude acute rejection and assess for CAV."

**3.3 Echo; electrophysiology study** (e79; 2023 new; **Class I, Level of Evidence: B**): consider echo "to assess cardiac function"; consider electrophysiological evaluation "in HT recipients with atrial arrhythmias or SVT to determine the nature of the arrhythmias."

**3.4 Persistent tachyarrhythmias** (e19, TF1 T4; continued 2010 rec; **Class I, Level of Evidence B**)
> "Persistent tachyarrhythmias, whether atrial or ventricular, should prompt investigation of possible rejection and electrophysiological evaluation if rejection is absent."
Also "Treatment of tachyarrhythmias should be aimed at rate control." (**Class I, Level of Evidence B**).

**3.5 Sustained ventricular tachycardia** (e19; continued; **Class I, Level of Evidence B**)
> "Sustained ventricular tachycardia (SVT) should be evaluated with both an angiogram and an endomyocardial biopsy (EMB)."
(The guideline abbreviates ventricular tachycardia as "SVT", the same abbreviation it uses elsewhere for supraventricular tachycardia.)

**3.6 Ventricular arrhythmia and sudden death** (e79, narrative; ungraded)
> "Ventricular arrhythmias and aborted sudden death in heart transplant recipients of any age should prompt urgent investigations including endomyocardial biopsy and coronary angiography."
Also: "Acute rejection and CAV are major risk factors for sudden death"; risk of sudden death within 2 years of CAV diagnosis "as high as 8%"; reduced LVEF "(<40%)" predicts sudden death.

**3.7 AF/flutter: anticoagulation and cardioversion** (e79-80, T8; 2023 new)
- "Clinicians should consider anticoagulation therapy in HT recipients with AF/flutter to reduce the risk of systemic thromboembolism guided by CHADS-VASc score." (**Class I, Level of Evidence: C**)
- "Clinicians should consider DC cardioversion in HT recipients with sustained atrial arrhythmias or supraventricular tachycardia to restore sinus rhythm and improve symptoms." (**Class IIa, Level of Evidence: A**)
- Radiofrequency ablation for symptomatic sustained atrial arrhythmias or SVT (**Class IIa, Level of Evidence: B**); for AF (**Class IIb, Level of Evidence: C**).

**3.8 Antiarrhythmic drugs** (e19-20 and e80)
- 2010 rec, continued (e19-20): "The Class III anti-arrhythmics sotalol and amiodarone can be safely used in HT recipients and have minimal interaction with immunosuppressive agents." (**Class IIa, Level of Evidence C**)
- 2023 new (e80): "sotalol and amiodarone can be safely used in HT recipients for rhythm control, but close monitoring of immunosuppressive agents is recommended." (**Class IIa, Level of Evidence: C**)
- Surgery section (e96-97, image read, 2010 rec continued; **Class I, Level of Evidence C**): "Amiodarone is recommended as the drug of choice for atrial tachyarrhythmias in HT recipients."
- Interaction: e14 "Amiodarone and calcium antagonists can increase calcineurin inhibitor (CNI) levels. Therefore, CNI dose adjustment and level monitoring are required." Table 5 (7.9) lists amiodarone, diltiazem, verapamil as raising levels.
The statements that amiodarone has "minimal interaction" and that it "increase[s] CNI levels" are both printed; see Ambiguities.

**3.9 SVT mechanisms** (e79, narrative): AVRT or AVNRT "are uncommon and related to an underlying electrophysiological substrate within the donor heart"; symptomatic cases "can be managed with radiofrequency ablation". Adenosine: see 2.5, 2.6.

**3.10 ICD** (e79-80): "ICD may be reasonable if meaningful survival of greater than 1 year is expected" in severe CAV with LV dysfunction (e80; **Class IIb, Level of Evidence: C**). Narrative: reduced LVEF "(<35%)" generally guides primary prevention.

**3.11 DOAC and CNI interaction** (e80, T9 table; 2023 new; **Class IIb, Level of Evidence: B**)
> "DOACs should be prescribed cautiously to heart transplant patients receiving cyclosporine-based immunosuppression regimen and in those with advanced kidney disease"
DOAC as alternative to VKA for AF stroke prevention with "a tacrolimus- or PSI-based immunosuppressive regimen" (**Class IIa, Level of Evidence: B**). "Endomyocardial biopsy can be completed in a patient receiving a DOAC provided that the anticoagulant is held at least 48 hours before procedure." (**Class IIa, Level of Evidence: B**).

## 4. Cardiac allograft vasculopathy (CAV)

**4.1 Importance** (e66, T3 narrative; ungraded)
> "CAV remains highly prevalent and a leading cause of death after heart transplantation."

**4.2 Detection** (e66-69, T3; narrative and table)
- Angiography is "the gold standard" (2010); IVUS or OCT add vessel-wall detail. Table 11 (e66, text read): ISHLT CAV0 to CAV3; CAV3 includes "LVEF ≤45% usually in the presence of regional wall motion abnormalities" or "significant restrictive physiology".
- "Coronary angiography should be performed to assess the development of CAV and angiograms should be graded according the 2010 ISHLT nomenclature." (e69; **Class 1, Level of Evidence: B**, printed "Class 1").
- "Annual or biannual coronary angiography evaluation should be considered." (**Class I, Level of Evidence: C**).
- Noninvasive tests (e67-68): dobutamine stress echo had "only 7% sensitivity for angiographic disease" in the largest cohort; "the presence of ischemia on DSE did not predict clinical outcomes." SPECT "up to 84% sensitivity and 78% specificity for detecting ≥50% stenosis" in one series, but AUC "only 0.65" for CAV2-3 in a later study. "the choice of noninvasive surveillance is largely dependent on local center expertise."
- CMR limits (e68, narrative): "allograft denervation with high resting heart rates reducing image quality".

**4.3 CAV as a cause of late graft dysfunction** (e60, e88)
> "CAV remains a frequent cause of late graft dysfunction and should be considered in the differential diagnosis." (e60 narrative)
> "All heart transplant patients presenting with new onset heart failure should be evaluated for acute cellular and antibody mediated rejection and new or progressive CAV should be considered. Concurrent evaluation for CAV is particularly relevant if an acute coronary syndrome is present." (e88, TF3 T11 narrative; ungraded)
2023 rec (e61, T6; **Class IIa, Level of Evidence: C**): "Coronary angiography and IVUS or optical coherence tomography [OCT] should be considered in these patients during long-term follow up."

**4.4 Late-onset diastolic failure** (e88; ungraded): "New onset diastolic heart failure late (>10 years) after heart transplant can be due to CAV, chronic rejection, and allograft fibrosis without a clear history of rejection."

**4.5 Management statements** (e68-69, T3 table)
- Prevention: "strict control of cardiovascular risk factors ... as well as strategies for the prevention of CMV infection" (**Class I, Level of Evidence: C**).
- Statins: "statin therapy has been shown to reduce CAV and improve long-term outcomes regardless of lipid levels" (**Class I, Level of Evidence: A**). Adult statins (e49): "statin doses should generally be lower than those recommended for hyperlipidemia" because of CNI interaction (**Class I, Level of Evidence: A**); CK and liver enzymes should be monitored (**Class IIa, Level of Evidence: C**).
- PSI: "Substitution of MMF or CNI with a PSI should be considered to prevent and delay progression of CAV, especially within 2 years of HT." (**Class I, Level of Evidence: A**).
- PCI: "Follow-up coronary angiography is recommended at 6 months after a PCI for evaluation of in stent restenosis and progression of CAV." (**Class I, Level of Evidence: C**). Narrative: PCI "can be undertaken for obstructive focal CAV disease" (e68). Aspirin: "reasonable to consider routine use of aspirin early after heart transplant for prevention of CAV." (e78; **Class IIb, Level of Evidence: C**).
- Retransplant: "Patients with severe CAV not amenable to medical or surgical therapy with asymptomatic moderate to severe LV dysfunction may be considered for retransplantation." (e88; **Class IIb, Level of Evidence: C**).
The guideline gives **no ED-specific CAV presentation** (see Not stated).

## 5. Primary graft dysfunction, RV failure, pericardial effusion (late presenters only)

**5.1 PGF and RV failure are written as perioperative topics** (e14-e19). Late-presenter content is minimal.
- Persistent instability after early MCS (e19, 2010 rec continued; **Class IIa, Level of Evidence C**): "If there is no evidence of graft functional recovery within 3 to 4 days, hyperacute and antibody-mediated rejection should be excluded"
- Tamponade first (e18-19; continued; **Class IIa, Level of Evidence C**): "In the presence of hemodynamic instability, cardiac tamponade should be excluded by direct surgical exploration. The presence of hyperacute/antibody-mediated rejection should also be excluded."

**5.2 Pericardial effusion** (e18, TF1 T4 table; continued 2010 recs)
- "Pericardial effusions occurring after HT should be monitored by echocardiogram." (**Class I, Level of Evidence C**)
- "Percutaneous or surgical drainage should be done when the pericardial effusion causes hemodynamic compromise." (**Class I, Level of Evidence C**)
- "Pericardial effusions that are not hemodynamically compromising do not require drainage unless there is a strong suspicion of an infectious etiology." (**Class IIa, Level of Evidence C**)
mTOR inhibitor link (e45, e55-56 trials; ungraded): "higher rates of adverse drug events including pericardial effusion" with everolimus/sirolimus "particularly when introduced early post transplant"; Table 8: everolimus "= more pericardial effusion".

**5.3 Heart failure in a late recipient** (e88): see 4.3. Treatment (e88, ungraded): "In transplant recipients who do not respond to oral heart failure therapy, inotropic therapy such as milrinone and/or dopamine, have been used to provide symptomatic improvement." "Care must be taken to minimize exacerbation of chronic kidney disease, which is almost universally present in the heart transplant population."

## 6. Infection

**6.1 Timing statements that exist** (ungraded narrative, e16): PJP "risk ... is highest within the first 6 months of transplant, but certain risk factors, including prolonged use of high-dose corticosteroids (CS), may augment the risk." Coccidioidomycosis: "Most infections occur within the first year of transplant". The guideline does not give a general infection-timeline (first month / 1-6 months / late).

**6.2 CMV prophylaxis regimens** (Table 3, e15, text read; ungraded table): D+/R-: "Ganciclovir 5mg/kg IV daily or valganciclovir 900 mg po daily* for 3-6 months"; R+: same drugs "for 3 months"; "Some HT centers will add CMV immune globulin for high-risk patients". Rec (e21, 2010 rec; **Class I, Level of Evidence A**): "Prophylaxis against CMV should be initiated within 24 to 48 hours after HT." and duration "3-6 months for D+/R- and 3 months for R+". 2023: "Antiviral prophylaxis is recommended over pre-emptive therapy for recipients at high-risk for CMV infection (D+/R-)." (e22 image read; **Class IIa, Level of Evidence C**).

**6.3 HSV** (e21; **Class I, Level of Evidence B**): "Prophylaxis with acyclovir, valacyclovir, or famciclovir should be considered for heart transplant recipients who are CMV -/-, seropositive for HSV-1 and/or HSV-2"

**6.4 EBV** (e22, image read; 2023 new; **Class IIa, Level of Evidence C**): "insufficient evidence to support the use of antiviral prophylaxis for post-transplant lymphoproliferative disorder prevention in EBV-mismatched (D+/R-) heart transplant recipients."

**6.5 Pneumocystis (PJP) prophylaxis** (e22, image read)
- 2023 column: "The preferred agent for prophylaxis against Pneumocystis jiroveci is trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole." (**Class I, Level of Evidence A**). Alternatives "dapsone, atovaquone, clindamycin, pyrimethamine, and inhaled pentamidine" (**Class I, Level of Evidence B**), no doses in this column.
- 2023 duration: "Prophylaxis against Pneumocystis jiroveci is recommended for at least 6-12 months following heart transplant." (**Class I, Level of Evidence B**).
- **2010 column only (doses, not restated in 2023):** TMP 80 mg/SMZ 160 mg "1 single- or double-strength tablet per day"; aerosolised pentamidine "300 mg every 3-4 weeks"; dapsone "50-100 mg/day"; atovaquone "1,500 mg PO QD". Listed for "sulfa allergy or glucose-6-phosphate dehydrogenase deficiency".

**6.6 Candida** (e22, image read): 2023 "insufficient evidence to support universal prophylaxis against Candida spp." (**Class IIa, Level of Evidence C**). The nystatin 4-6 mL and clotrimazole lozenge 10 mg regimen is **2010 column only**.

**6.7 Coccidioides** (e22, image read; 2023 new; **Class I, Level of Evidence C**): "residing in areas endemic for Coccidioides should receive 6-12 months of prophylaxis with an oral azole". Donor prior/active cocci: "6-12 months of pre-emptive fluconazole followed by either lifelong step-down therapy or serologic and clinical monitoring."

**6.8 Aspergillus risk** (e16, ungraded): risk factors "airway colonization with Aspergillus spp., reoperation, post-transplant hemodialysis, need for ECMO, CMV disease"; "targeted antifungal prophylaxis may be considered, though the optimal duration is unclear."

**6.9 Toxoplasma** (e85, TF3 T10 table; 2023 new)
- "Toxoplasma D+/R- heart transplant recipients should receive targeted prophylaxis early post-transplant. Standard one-year TMP/SMX pneumocystis prophylaxis regimens (TMP 160 mg/SMX 800 mg orally three times weekly or TMP 80 mg/SMX 400 mg orally daily) is recommended." (**Class I, Level of Evidence: B**)
- "Lifelong Toxoplasma prophylaxis is recommended in high-risk (D+/R-) heart recipients." (**Class IIa, Level of Evidence: C**). Sulfa-allergic: "dapsone 50 mg daily, plus pyrimethamine 50 mg weekly plus folinic acid 10 mg weekly can be considered ... after checking for glucose 6 phosphate dehydrogenase deficiency." (**Class IIa, Level of Evidence: C**).

**6.10 Other organism-specific statements** (e85, new, TF3 T10): Chagas PCR monitoring "weekly for the first 2 months post-transplant, every 2 weeks in the third month, and then monthly for at least 6 months" (**Class I, Level of Evidence: C**). Suspected donor-derived cryptococcosis: serum and CSF cryptococcal antigen and cultures (**Class I, Level of Evidence: C**). Histoplasmosis donor: "itraconazole for 1 year" (**Class I, Level of Evidence: C**).

**6.11 BK virus** (e73, narrative; ungraded): "Evaluation for BKV should be considered in patients with progressive renal dysfunction without an otherwise identifiable cause." Management "generally involves decreasing immunosuppression while carefully monitoring for rejection".

**6.12 Antibiotic choice caution** (e96, image read; 2023; **Class I, Level of Evidence C**)
> "Aminoglycoside antibiotics and those that are potent CYP-enzyme inhibitors, such as erythromycin and clarithromycin, are best avoided, because of the risk of worsening renal dysfunction when used in combination with CYA or TAC."

**6.13 Endocarditis prophylaxis** (e105; continued; **Class IIa, Level of Evidence: C**): "the use of antibiotic prophylaxis for dental procedures is considered reasonable in patients with valvulopathies."

**6.14 Live vaccines** (e114 narrative; e117 new rec; page numbers from PDF position, see note in Ambiguities): "Live-viral vaccinations should NOT be given to heart transplant recipients for travel" (LAIV, oral typhoid, oral polio, varicella or live zoster, MMR, BCG, yellow fever). Grade not read.

**6.15 Novel pathogens** (e115): "a multidisciplinary approach involving transplant infectious diseases is imperative", including "alterations in immunosuppressive therapy, and timing of biopsies and other invasive procedures."

**6.16 Prophylaxis when treating rejection:** see 1.9. **Fever/infection and immunosuppression changes:** no statement on whether to reduce immunosuppression in sepsis. See Not stated.

## 7. Immunosuppression

**7.1 Standard regimen** (e49, TF2 T3; continued; **Class IIa, Level of Evidence: B**): "Calcineurin inhibitor-based therapy remains the standard in immunosuppressive protocols used after HT." MMF, EVL or SRL "should be included in contemporary immunosuppressive regimens" (**Class IIa, Level of Evidence: B**). Steroids (e50): "CS avoidance, early CS weaning or very low dose maintenance CS therapy are all acceptable" (**Class IIa, Level of Evidence: B**). TAC vs CYA: "TAC-based regimens may be associated with lower rejection rates but not with superior survival" (**Class IIb, Level of Evidence: B**).

**7.2 Induction** (e50): "ATG may be beneficial in patients at high risk for acute rejection" (**Class IIb, Level of Evidence: C**); routine rituximab induction not recommended in non-sensitised recipients (**Class III, Level of evidence: B**); belatacept and tocilizumab "evolving ... rescue therapy" (**Class IIb, Level of evidence: C**).

**7.3 Cyclosporine targets, non-mTOR regimens** (e41, 2010 rec, continued; **Class IIa, Level of Evidence: C**): trough "325 ng/mL (range 275-375 ng/mL) for the first 6 postoperative weeks, 275 ng/mL (range 200-350 ng/mL) for weeks 6 to 12, 225 ng/mL (range 150-300 ng/mL) for month 3 to month 6; and 200 ng/mL (range 150-250 ng/mL) from month 6 onward" (assay-dependent; "Abbot TDX assay (or equivalent)").

**7.4 Tacrolimus targets, non-mTOR regimens** (e41, continued; **Class IIa, Level of Evidence: C**): "between 10 and 15 ng/mL during the early postoperative period (Days 0-60); between 8 and 12 ng/mL for the next 3 to 6 months; and between 5 and 10 ng/mL in stable patients 6 months after HT". Monitoring: "12-hour trough for twice-daily TAC and a 24-hour trough concentration for once-daily TAC".

**7.5 Targets with mTOR inhibitors** (Table 4, e40, **read from image**; adults): CNI + mTORi: everolimus 3-8 ng/mL, sirolimus 4-12 ng/mL, cyclosporine "75-200 (3-6 months)" and "50-100 (> 6 months)", tacrolimus "3-8 (> 6 months)". CNI-free (mTORi + MMF): everolimus 6-10, sirolimus 8-15. Recs (e41-42): TAC with mTORi "After 6 months, a target TAC trough concentration between 3 and 8 ng/mL may be considered." (**Class IIa, Level of Evidence: C**); EVL with CNI 3-8 (**Class IIa, Level of Evidence: B** with CYA; **C** with TAC); "Levels should be measured at least 5 days after adjustment of the dose" (e41; **Class IIa, Level of Evidence: B**).

**7.6 MPA levels** (e43; 2023; **Class IIb, Level of Evidence: C**): "a MPA level of < 1.5 mg/L is considered subtherapeutic" in graft dysfunction; "Trough MPA levels should not be used to guide dosing in patients receiving EC-mycophenolate sodium."

**7.7 Formulation, brand and route** (e50-51, e43; 2023): switching TAC formulations needs "Close monitoring of TAC concentrations" (**Class IIb, Level of Evidence: C**); "Maintaining the same brand of immunosuppressant is preferable and repeated brand substitution should be avoided." (**Class IIa, Level of evidence: C**). Sublingual TAC: "up to a 50% dose reduction of the current or anticipated oral dose" (**Class IIb, Level of Evidence: C**); narrative (e48): "30 to 100% of the oral dose has been suggested", staff should "wear at least two pairs of gloves, respiratory protection, and a non-permeable gown."

**7.8 IV immunosuppression** (e96, image read; 2023 column, **Class I, Level of Evidence C**): IV cyclosporine "has been associated with an anaphylactic-type reaction"; convert to "25 to 33% of the oral dose"; IV tacrolimus "10% to 33% of the oral daily dose"; sublingual "50% to 70% of the oral dose" (IR capsule contents); "Mycophenolate and azathioprine; same as the oral dose." Narrative (e48): PVC-free containers; "Anaphylactic reactions have been reported with intravenous formulations for the CNI's".

**7.9 Drugs that change CNI/mTOR levels (Table 5, e44, READ FROM IMAGE; heading "Drugs That Affect the Levels of Tacrolimus, Cyclosporine, Sirolimus, or Everolimus")**
- **Decrease** immunosuppression levels (left column): antiepileptics carbamazepine, fosphenytoin, phenobarbital, phenytoin; caspofungin, nafcillin, rifabutin, rifampin, rifapentine; efavirenz, etravirine, nevirapine; tocilizumab; bosentan; aprepitant, deferasirox, modafinil, St. John's wort, thalidomide, ticlopidine.
- **Increase** immunosuppression levels (right column): antifungals clotrimazole, fluconazole, isavuconazole, itraconazole, ketoconazole, posaconazole, voriconazole; clarithromycin, erythromycin, metronidazole and tinidazole; protease inhibitors (general), cobicistat, darunavir, fosamprenavir, indinavir, nelfinavir, ritonavir, saquinavir, tipranavir; letermovir; hepatitis C direct-acting antivirals (daclatasvir, glecaprevir-pibrentasvir, grazoprevir-elbasvir, ledipasvir-(sofosbuvir), velpatasvir-(sofosbuvir), voxilaprevir-velpatasvir-(sofosbuvir)); **amiodarone, diltiazem, verapamil**; cimetidine, fluvoxamine, glipizide, glyburide, imatinib, nefazodone, rilonacept, **theophylline**, turmeric; nutraceuticals bitter orange, grapefruit.
- Rule (e40 narrative): "if an interacting agent (Table 5) is added or withdrawn to existing therapy, close monitoring of immunosuppressant drug levels and dose adjustments are made to avoid any adverse outcomes."
- Statins, macrolides, azoles and rifampin: statin interaction is stated in recs (7.1/4.5); statins are **not** in Table 5. Azoles, macrolides, rifampin and anticonvulsants are in Table 5 as above.
- Other named interactions: combined hormonal contraception "inhibits the CYP-450 3A4 pathway" (e100; **Class I, Level of Evidence C**); DOACs with cyclosporine (3.11); ezetimibe and PCSK9 inhibitors do not appear to alter levels (e75, narrative); statins "limit dose titration or lead to intolerance" with CNIs (e75).

**7.10 Toxicity statements**
- CNI nephrotoxicity (e72, narrative): "The acute toxicities of CNI (e.g., vasoconstriction, toxic tubulopathy, and in some cases thrombotic microangiopathy)" and long-term "irreversible changes".
- CNI hypertension (e76): "largely attributed to CNI therapy."
- CNI neurotoxicity (e65, T2 table; **Class I, Level of Evidence: C** for both): "Management of HT recipients with new onset seizures not due to stroke or structural brain disease should include reduction of CNI doses (taking into consideration the risk of inadequate immunosuppression) and correction of hypomagnesemia, if present." "PRES in HT recipients should be managed with gradual blood pressure reduction and withdrawal or reduction of CNI along with substitution for an alternate immunosuppressive agent or an alternate CNI." "The occurrence of encephalopathy late after HT should prompt neurological consultation and imaging" (**Class I, Level of Evidence: C**). Persistent seizures after CNI reduction: substitute a PSI (**Class IIb, Level of Evidence: C**). Antiseizure drug choice should weigh interactions (**Class IIa, Level of Evidence: C**).
- mTORi adverse effects (e45, e55-56): pericardial effusion, "oral ulcers, interstitial edema", pneumonia; higher early mortality with early everolimus "mainly due to infection, particularly in patients treated with rATG"; Table 7: "EVL/redCYA = higher total cholesterol & HDL".
- Belatacept: "FDA Black Box warning against use in EBV seronegative patients due to the risk of PTLD" (e47).
- Rituximab induction "was associated with accelerated coronary vasculopathy" (e46).

**7.11 Etomidate and steroid stress dosing** (e97, 2023 new; **Class I, Level of Evidence C**): "Individualize perioperative corticosteroid 'stress dose' based upon patient's history of corticosteroid intake combined with type and duration of surgery." "The use of etomidate in patients at risk for adrenal suppression and crisis should be carefully considered, given its inhibitory effect on steroid synthesis and potential acute adrenal insufficiency." (Table 14, e98: single-dose etomidate lowers cortisol "for at least 24 hours" but "this has not been shown to be clinically relevant.")

**7.12 Table 15 (IV conversion), e96, READ FROM IMAGE.** The table is printed in the **2010 column**: cyclosporine "One-third of oral daily dose"; tacrolimus "One-fifth of the oral daily dose as a continuous infusion over 24 hours"; mycophenolate and azathioprine "Same as oral dose". The 2023 column gives different text ("25 to 33%", "10% to 33%"). See Ambiguities.

## 8. Long-term (chronic) complications

**8.1 Hypertension** (e76-77): "Hypertension ... largely attributed to CNI therapy." Treat to the general-population goals (**Class I, Level of Evidence: C**, continued). 2023 combined rec (**Class I, Level of Evidence: C**): "ACEi and calcium channel blockers may be preferred as first line therapy in patients with diabetes and as a CAV prevention strategy, while hydrochlorothiazide could be considered to specifically counteract CNI-induced hypertension." "Calcium channel blockers are most widely used" (2010 text). Narrative: "thiazide-based diuretics may have benefit for those with CNI-induced hypertension."

**8.2 Chronic kidney disease** (e72-74): "CNI is a major, but not the sole, cause of post-transplant CKD." Renal function "at least twice a year" with albuminuria (**Class I, Level of Evidence: C**). Nephrology referral if eGFR "< 30 mL/min/1.73 m2", albuminuria "≥ 300 mg/g", or "Rapidly declining GFR (>5 mL/min/1.73 m2 per year)" (**Class I, Level of Evidence: C**). 2023: "strict glucose control and blood pressure control with use of an ACEI or ARB, and SGLT2 inhibition for those with diabetes and CKD" (**Class I; Level of Evidence: C**). ESAs "should not be used to increase Hgb levels >11.5 g/dL" (**Class I; Level of Evidence: C**). Conversion to PSI "may improve renal function".

**8.3 Diabetes** (e75-77): screening by "fasting plasma glucose levels or with an oral glucose tolerance test ... and HbA1c" (**Class I, Level of Evidence: C**); "diabetogenic immunosuppressive medications should be minimized" (**Class I, Level of Evidence: C**). Narrative: SGLT2 inhibitors "can be considered as an option to manage post-transplant diabetes mellitus in select heart transplant recipients" based on small series. Metformin "remains an excellent agent in patients without advanced renal failure."

**8.4 Dyslipidaemia** (e78): "It is reasonable to target LDL levels below 100 mg/dL (2.5 mmol/l) ... but there must be close monitoring for potential interactions between lipid lowering therapies and immunosuppressive agents." (**Class IIa, Level of Evidence: C**). Lipids measured "Biannual" (**Class I, Level of Evidence: C**). PCSK9 inhibitors and ezetimibe adjuncts (Adults: **Class IIb, Level of Evidence: B**).

**8.5 Malignancy, skin cancer** (e70-72): "skin cancer remains the most common post-transplant malignancy, followed by prostate and lung cancer, with lymphoma being uncommon." Cumulative prevalence "16% in 5-year survivors and 28% in 10-year survivors." "It is recommended that HT recipients have close skin cancer surveillance, including education on preventive measures and yearly dermatological exams." (e72, continued; **Class I, Level of Evidence: C**). Screening for solid tumours as in the general population (**Class I, Level of Evidence: C**). "There is no evidence to support a reduction in immunosuppression in patients with solid tumors unrelated to the lymphoid system." (**Class I, Level of Evidence: C**).

**8.6 PTLD** (e71-72): "Initial evaluation and therapeutic plan for PTLD in HT recipients should be done at the transplant center by physicians familiar with transplant-associated malignancies." (**Class I, Level of Evidence: C**). "Use of rituximab monotherapy for treatment of adult and pediatric CD20+ PTLD that is not responsive to a reduction in immunosuppression is reasonable." (Adults: **Class IIa, Level of Evidence: B**). mTORi conversion in malignancy or PTLD "should be considered" (e50; **Class IIb; Level of evidence C**). The guideline gives no PTLD presenting features for adults.

**8.7 Bone disease** (e92-93): adults "should begin anti-resorptive therapy with bisphosphonates immediately after HT and continue it at least throughout the first postoperative year." (**Class I, Level of Evidence B**); calcium "1,000-1,200 mg" and vitamin D "600-800 IU" (2023, **Class I, Level of Evidence C**); BMD assessment ongoing for those on glucocorticoids or CNIs (**Class I, Level of Evidence, B**).

**8.8 Gout** (e78, T7; continued; **Class I, Level of Evidence: C**): "antihyperuricemic therapy and concurrent risk reduction may be used to prevent recurrent attacks of gout". Same recommendation: "It is important to assess for contraindications and drug interactions when medically treating complications of immunosuppression." No specific gout drug or colchicine/NSAID statement is printed.

**8.9 Anaemia, BK, neurologic:** see 8.2, 6.11, 7.10.

## 9. Emergency care, surgery and procedures, contacting the centre, pregnancy

**9.1 When local clinicians should tell the transplant centre** (e91, image read; 2023 column; **Class I, Level of Evidence C**)
> "(1) hospitalization for any reason; (2) change in medication, including the addition of any antibiotic, anti-fungal, or anti-viral therapy for confirmed or presumed infection; (3) hypotension or an unexplained drop in systolic blood pressure ≥ 20 mm Hg from baseline; (4) increase in resting heart rate > 10 beats/min over baseline"
Continues: fever "≥ 101°F (38°C) or any unexplained fever ≥ 101°F (38°C) for ≥ 48 hours", "≥ 2-pound weight gain in 1 week", "unexplained weight loss of > 5 pounds", "increased shortness of breath", "pneumonia or any respiratory infection", "syncope", "chest pain other than musculoskeletal symptoms", "decline > 10% in forced expiratory volume in 1 second", "abdominal pain", "nausea, vomiting or diarrhea", "cerebral vascular event, seizure, or mental status changes", "suspicion/diagnosis of pregnancy", "epidemiologic exposure to a contagious infectious agent". (2010 column had 16 items.)

**9.2 Emergency access to the centre** (e113, TF4 T13 narrative; ungraded): "The patient and primary care physician should know how to reach the transplant center in case of emergency." Goal of the follow-up schedule includes "(6) the need to reduce emergency department visits and hospitalizations." (e90, **Class IIa, Level of Evidence C**).

**9.3 Intercurrent surgery** (e96-97, image read for e96; **Class I, Level of Evidence C**)
- "HT recipients requiring intercurrent surgical procedures should have a full preoperative assessment in collaboration with the transplant team" (continued).
- "Immunosuppression should not be discontinued or modified without discussion with the HT team. However, it may be prudent to omit the dose of CNI on the morning of surgery to avoid potentiating the detrimental effect of dehydration on renal function. Thereafter, immunosuppression should be continued as normal" (e97, 2023).
- Fluids: "decreased intravascular volume will exacerbate renal dysfunction, and fluid excess may not be well tolerated by HT recipients." (e97; continued).
- mTORi before elective surgery: "consider interrupting high-exposure PSI administration at least 1 month before elective surgery" (e97; 2023 new; **Class I, Level of Evidence B**); "In the event of urgent surgery, severe open wound complications, or urinary fistulas ... could justify the withdrawal of mTOR inhibition."
- Blood: "blood products used in HT recipients should be leukocyte poor"; "In emergent situations, washed type O RBCs and type AB FFP and platelets are always safe." (e96, image read; the ABO sentence concerns ABO-incompatible recipients). CMV-seronegative recipients: "low-risk CMV packed red blood cells should be available."

**9.4 Biopsy and anticoagulation:** see 3.11 (hold DOAC 48 h before EMB).

**9.5 Pregnancy** (e98-100; **Class I, Level of Evidence C** unless noted): "CNIs and CS should be continued in a pregnant HT recipient, but MMF (class D) should be discontinued." "Blood levels of CNI should be monitored closely during pregnancy". "Azathioprine can be used as a substitute for MMF, but this should be decided based on the balance of maternal and fetal risk." EMB: "surveillance EMB done under fluoroscopy should be avoided." Contraception: "Combined hormonal contraception inhibits the CYP-450 3A4 pathway, and immunosuppressant drug blood levels should be monitored carefully". Breastfeeding "safe" with prednisolone, methylprednisolone, cyclosporine, tacrolimus, azathioprine; avoid with "mycophenolic acid products, sirolimus, everolimus, and/or belatacept". Pregnant recipient care is "best achieved at the primary transplant institution in collaboration with local or referring physicians." Male recipients: mycophenolate label "discontinued 90 days before having unpro-tected sex"; "The evidence for this recommendation is lacking."

**9.6 Blood product and adherence:** adherence and "repeated education on the critical importance of adherence to treatment and early reporting of symptoms contribute to the prevention and early recognition of late acute rejection" (e60, continued; **Class IIa, Level of Evidence: C**).

## Not stated (ED-relevant questions the guideline does not answer)

1. No emergency-department section. No stepwise ED approach, triage level, or list of "must-do" actions for a recipient presenting acutely.
2. Whether an emergency physician should give IV methylprednisolone or ATG before transplant-team input, and no steroid dose for symptomatic ACR (only AMR: methylprednisolone 500-1000 mg daily, 3 days).
3. Whether a recipient with fever or suspected sepsis should have immunosuppression reduced or held, or stress-dose steroids given outside surgery. No sepsis statement.
4. Whether denervation abolishes angina; no statement that ACS, CAV or MI may present without chest pain. No acute-coronary-syndrome pathway for a recipient (only "Concurrent evaluation for CAV is particularly relevant if an acute coronary syndrome is present").
5. Digoxin: nothing. Beta-blocker and non-dihydropyridine CCB doses, and any caution on their use in rejection or low output: nothing beyond "may be used ... for rate control".
6. Atropine dose alternatives, isoproterenol or theophylline doses for late bradycardia, and theophylline interaction advice: nothing beyond the statements above (theophylline appears in Table 5 as raising CNI levels; the guideline does not link this to its use as a chronotrope).
7. Which ECG or troponin/BNP findings should be treated as rejection: the guideline says biomarkers may be used to identify higher-risk patients (**Class IIb, Level of Evidence C**) but gives no thresholds.
8. Thresholds for toxic or low tacrolimus, cyclosporine, sirolimus or everolimus levels in an ill patient; target levels for months 0-6 on mTORi regimens are "not been adequately established" for TAC early.
9. Management of acute CNI nephrotoxicity, hyperkalaemia, hypomagnesaemia (beyond seizure management), or acute kidney injury in the late recipient.
10. Adult PTLD or malignancy presentation, and gout drug choice in a patient on a CNI.
11. General infection timeline, empirical antimicrobial regimens for late fever or pneumonia, and CMV treatment doses (the document gives only prophylaxis in this section).
12. Imaging and contrast guidance, cardiac arrest or ACLS modifications, defibrillation, transfusion triggers beyond "leukocyte poor", and any statement about vasopressor choice for shock in a late recipient (vasoplegia text is perioperative).
13. Primary graft dysfunction and RV failure in late presenters: perioperative only.
14. How quickly to contact the centre (immediately vs next day); the list in 9.1 gives events but no urgency.
15. Pregnancy emergencies (ectopic, pre-eclampsia, labour) specific to recipients.

## Ambiguities / internal inconsistencies

1. **Adenosine adult dose.** e20 (image read) and e14: "25 mcg/kg: 1.5 mg if ≥ 60 kg", Class IIb C. e79 narrative: "low doses (e.g., 3 mg in adults)". e96 (2010 column, still current): "standard doses ... may result in prolonged asystole." Three different positions (1.5 mg, 3 mg, "standard doses harmful") coexist; the 3 mg figure is not a graded recommendation.
2. **Amiodarone.** "Minimal interaction with immunosuppressive agents" (e19-20, 2010, continued) vs "Amiodarone and calcium antagonists can increase calcineurin inhibitor (CNI) levels" (e14) vs Table 5 lists amiodarone, diltiazem and verapamil as increasing levels vs 2023 rec "close monitoring of immunosuppressive agents is recommended" (e80). "Drug of choice for atrial tachyarrhythmias" (e96, Class I C) is a 2010 perioperative-surgery statement.
3. **Diltiazem/verapamil.** The recipient-arrhythmia rec says non-dihydropyridine CCBs "may be used" for rate control (Class IIa B) without mentioning that they raise CNI levels; the interaction appears only in Table 5 and e14.
4. **Fever threshold.** e91 (image read): 2010 "≥ 100.5°F" for 48 h; 2023 "≥ 101°F (38°C) or any unexplained fever ≥ 101°F (38°C)" (both versions print "(38°C)" although 101°F is about 38.3°C, and the 2023 item contains a stray hyphen "101˚-F" in the text version). A separate version of the same list at e90-91 repeats the thresholds with item numbering that differs by two items (16 vs 18).
5. **"Decline > 10% in forced expiratory volume in 1 second"** is on the heart-transplant contact list (item 13), a lung-transplant criterion with no explanation.
6. **PJP duration.** 2010 "minimum of 3 months up to a maximum of 24 months" (Class IIb C) vs 2023 "at least 6-12 months" (Class I B). Toxoplasma D+/R-: TMP/SMX "one-year" then "Lifelong ... may be considered" (Class I B) vs "Lifelong Toxoplasma prophylaxis is recommended" (Class IIa C).
7. **Table 15 vs text.** Table 15 (2010 column, image read) says tacrolimus IV is "One-fifth of the oral daily dose"; the 2023 text says "10% to 33%". Cyclosporine "One-third" vs "25 to 33%". e48 narrative says "approximately one third" for CYA and "10 to 33%" for TAC.
8. **Rejection risk factors** differ between e60 narrative (African American ethnicity, donor/recipient sex mismatch) and the e60 table (recipient female gender, no ethnicity in 2023). e36 surveillance rec adds "reduced immunosuppression due to post-transplant malignancy or chronic infection, African American descent".
9. **Sustained ventricular tachycardia abbreviated "SVT"** (e19), the same abbreviation used for supraventricular tachycardia (e79).
10. **CKD referral threshold:** the 2010 column says GFR decline "> 4 mL/min/1.73 m2 per year"; the 2023 column says ">5". The "Class I" grade for hemoglobin target: 2010 "between 11 and 13 g/dL" vs 2023 "should not be used to increase Hgb levels >11.5 g/dL".
11. **Cyclosporine targets with mTORi:** the e40 narrative gives "150 and 350 ng/mL within the first 2 months" for CYA with everolimus; Table 4 starts at 3-6 months (75-200). The 2023 rec (e41) says early-period TAC targets with mTORi "have not been adequately established".
12. **Grade presentation.** On some pages the grade after a "Continuing approval without change" cell is printed only in the 2010 column; on others the "Class" line is attached to the first paragraph of a two-paragraph cell (e.g., e21-22 PJP). Where I could not view the page image I attributed grades by position.
13. **Page numbers for vaccines (6.14) and e105-e117 items** were located from extracted text ordering and were not confirmed on the rendered page; verify before citing.
14. **Perioperative vs late.** Items in 2.7, 2.8, 2.14, 3.4, 3.5 are in TF1 "Early Postoperative Care" and are written for the perioperative period; the guideline does not say whether they apply to a recipient years after transplant.
