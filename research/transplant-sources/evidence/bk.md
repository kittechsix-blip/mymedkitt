## 1. Definitions

**1.1 Working definitions** (Introduction [P107]-[P111]; ungraded definitions)
> "Possible BKPyV-nephropathy: high-level urine BKPyV loads defined as BKPyV-DNAuria >10 million copies/mL (c/mL; or equivalent) or decoy cells or PyV virions by electron microscopy, but undetectable plasma BKPyV-DNAemia."
> "Probable BKPyV-nephropathy: plasma BKPyV-DNAemia >1000 c/mL (or equivalent) sustained for >2 wk."
> "Presumptive BKPyV-nephropathy: plasma BKPyV-DNAemia >10 000 c/mL (or equivalent)."
> "Biopsy-proven BKPyV-nephropathy: detection of compatible cytopathic effects plus immunohistochemistry and a specific diagnostic test identifying BKPyV as opposed to JC polyomavirus (JCPyV)."

**1.2 Viruria (high-level vs low-level).** High-level viruria = urine BKPyV-DNA >10 million c/mL or decoy cells (1.1). Low-level (Diagnostics, Virologic Methods [P164]): in the study quoted, low-level viruria before transplant was "similar to healthy blood donors (ie, <100 000 c/mL; 5 log10)". Qualitative urine PCR is not enough [P164]: "a positive result in urine cannot distinguish kidney transplant patients with high-level viruria from those with low-level viruria".

**1.3 DNAemia replaces "viremia"** ([P112]): "BKPyV-DNAemia and plasma BKPyV-DNA loads replaced the widely used term BKPyV viremia".

**1.4 Table 2 footnote a (image)** ties the DNAemia definitions to the risk table: DNAemia "Defined as >1000 c/mL (or equivalent) for >2wk (probable BKPyV-nephropathy) or increasing >10 000 c/mL or equivalent (presumptive BKPyV-nephropathy)" ([P118], text under Table 2).

## 2. Timing after transplant

**2.1 Natural course in the first year** (Virologic Methods [P164]; ungraded epidemiology)
> "new-onset high-level viruria ... is seen in approximately 20% to 40% of patient posttransplant, followed by new-onset plasma BKPyV-DNAemia in 10% to 20%, and biopsy-proven BKPyV-nephropathy in 8% (range, 1%–15%) of patients in the first 12 mo"
(the source text reads "high-level viruria defined by urine BKPyV loads of >10 million c/mL or the presence of 'decoy cells'" before the quoted words; wording above is contiguous within [P164] after that definition).

**2.2 Later years** ([P164]): "Subsequently, BKPyV-DNAemia and biopsy-proven BKPyV-nephropathy are seen in another 10% to 20% for the next 24 mo and another 1% to 10% for the next 3 to 5 y."

**2.3 Viruria precedes DNAemia** ([P165]): "high-level viruria precedes plasma BKPyV-DNAemia on average by approximately 6 wk (range, 1–12 wk)". Figure 2 caption [P182]: "BKPyV-DNAemia loads are identified in plasma by QNAT approximately 2 to 6 wk after high-level BKPyV-DNAuria." (see Ambiguity 1)

**2.4 Screening window = the at-risk window** (Table 5 (image); strong, B): "screening kidney transplant recipients for plasma BKPyV loads monthly until mo 9, then every 3 mo until 2 y posttransplantation". Abstract [P103]: monthly "until month 9, then every 3 mo until 2 y posttransplant (3 y for children)".

**2.5 Recurrence after rejection treatment** (Considerations for Screening [P174]): "BKPyV recurrence may arise late after transplantation following treatment for rejection, with the risk depending on the type of antirejection treatment and other factors."

**2.6 Risk factors** (Table 2 (image), no recommendation): tacrolimus (compared with cyclosporine A) "High, A" for both DNAemia and biopsy-proven nephropathy; older age "Moderate, B" (DNAemia) / "Low, C" (nephropathy); male sex same; corticosteroids "Moderate, B"; acute rejection "Low, C"; lymphocyte-depleting agents "Low, C"; ureteric stents "Low, C"; mTOR inhibitors "(decrease risk)" "Low, C"; BKPyV-seropositive donor into seronegative recipient "Moderate, B" (DNAemia). Abstract [P103] lists "tacrolimus, acute rejection, and higher steroid exposure".

## 3. Presentation

**3.1 The guideline does not describe symptoms.** It describes BKPyV-nephropathy through virology, histology and graft function. Statements that bear on presentation:

**3.2 Graft dysfunction triggers biopsy** (Pathology [P143]; Table 4 (image))
> "The general indications for a renal allograft biopsy remain in the case of altered renal function or signs of pathology (eg, a significant rise in serum creatinine of >15% from baseline, hematuria, or proteinuria)."
Table 4 (image), first item (strong, A): "in the context of detectable BKPyV-DNAemia, a kidney biopsy be performed as clinically indicated (eg, rise in serum creatinine, proteinuria, hematuria".

**3.3 Dysfunction is the commonest reason for biopsy** (Differential Diagnosis, Rejection [P150]): "Allograft dysfunction is the most frequent indication for a diagnostic biopsy in the context of BKPyV-DNAemia."

**3.4 Creatinine rises when allograft involvement is extensive** (Figure 2 caption [P182]): "Serum creatinine concentration may increase when allograft BKPyV loads and the associated interstitial inflammation become more extensive." Also [P149]: "Peaking of serum creatinine concentration can be observed in approximately 50% of the patients." (in the biopsy-findings paragraph; subject population as in the source paragraph).

**3.5 Rationale for screening before dysfunction** (Abstract [P103]): "To facilitate early intervention with limited allograft damage". [P174]: test regularly "to identify BKPyV-nephropathy early at the stage of limited viral allograft involvement."

**3.6 Declining renal function in non-kidney recipients** (Table 5 (image), weak, C): "For non-kidney solid organ transplant recipients presenting with declining renal function, in the absence of other reasons for the renal compromise, we suggest testing for BKPyV-DNAemia and looking for BKPyV-nephropathy if a renal biopsy is performed".

**3.7 Rising creatinine as the only presenting feature: not stated** as such. The guideline never says BKPyV-nephropathy is asymptomatic or presents only as a creatinine rise; the closest are 3.2-3.5.

## 4. The test and thresholds

**4.1 Test of choice** (Virologic Methods [P164]): "quantitative detection of BKPyV-DNA by molecular tests and the determination of viral DNA loads in urine and blood by QNAT expressed as c/mL (or equivalent) have demonstrated broad utility and are the key diagnostic tools". Plasma is the screening sample (Table 5 (image): "screening kidney transplant recipients for plasma BKPyV loads").

**4.2 Why plasma over urine** ([P165]): "BKPyV-DNAemia loads have a higher positive predictive value for biopsy-proven BKPyV-nephropathy than high-level urine BKPyV loads or 'decoy cells.'"

**4.3 Confirmatory timing** (Table 5 (image), weak, B): "If plasma BKPyV-DNA loads are 1000–10 000 c/mL (or equivalent), we suggest confirmatory testing within 2–3 wk". [P174]: "testing should be repeated within 2 to 3 wk to confirm whether BKPyV-DNAemia has spontaneously resolved or is persistent".

**4.4 Action thresholds** ([P174]): "a threshold BKPyV-DNAemia of >10 000 c/mL (or equivalent) identifies presumptive BKPyV-nephropathy necessitating prompt reduction of immunosuppression if laboratory errors are unlikely." "Persistent BKPyV-DNAemia at 1000 to 10 000 c/mL (or equivalent) identifies kidney transplant patients with probable BKPyV-nephropathy". Caveat same para: "persistent BKPyV-DNAemia at levels <10 000 c/mL has been reported in patients eventually diagnosed with biopsy-proven BKPyV-nephropathy."

**4.5 Threshold uncertainty** ([P166]): thresholds listed in guidelines "as >10 000 c/mL in a single sample or as >1000 c/mL persisting for >2 wk, some single-center studies have reported lower or higher viral loads." Within "the 0.5 log10 c/mL range accepted as not significantly different in amplification-based QNATs".

**4.6 Monitoring after a positive** (Table 5 (image), weak, D): "In kidney transplant recipients with sustained plasma BKPyV-DNA loads >1000 c/mL (or equivalent), we suggest monitoring BKPyV-DNAemia every 2–4 wk to assess dynamics and response to the intervention".

**4.7 Same assay and specimen** (Table 5 (image), strong, B): "the same specimen type and assay be used in the same diagnostic laboratory to avoid uncertainty because of assay variability when monitoring the dynamics of BKPyV-DNAemia". Also (strong, C): QNAT "that target conserved BKPyV genome sequences"; amplicon "<150 bp to avoid significant underquantification".

**4.8 Urine as the fallback** (Table 5 (image)): decoy-cell cytology is the minimal screen in resource-limited settings (strong, B); "If urine decoy cells or urine BKPyV-DNA loads of >10 million copies/mL (or equivalent) are detected, we recommend measuring plasma BKPyV-DNA loads to guide clinical management (strong, B)". Resource-limited [P175]: "undetectable decoy cells on properly processed urine cytology ... can virtually exclude BKPyV-DNAemia and biopsy-proven BKPyV-nephropathy."

**4.9 Whole blood** ([P167]): extraction from "EDTA-anticoagulated whole blood rather than plasma" is described as an adaptation; "the available clinical data ... are limited".

**4.10 Definitive diagnosis is histology** (Table 4 (image), strong, A): "immunohistochemistry (clone PAb 416 against SV40 large T-antigen) for confirming the diagnosis of biopsy-proven PyVAN". Biopsy can miss it ([P143]): "renal allograft biopsies fail to detect intragraft replication foci in 10% to 30% of the cases with BKPyV-DNAemia during early onset or when biopsy-proven BKPyV-nephropathy is resolving". DNAemia alone does not mandate biopsy ([P143]): "BKPyV-DNAemia per se is not an indication for renal allograft biopsy in a kidney transplant patient with baseline renal function and standard immunological risk." Abstract [P103]: "In adults without graft dysfunction, kidney allograft biopsy is not required unless the immunological risk is high."

**4.11 Not BKPyV: JC virus** ([P153]): JCPyV-nephropathy "indistinguishable" morphologically; "Unlike for BKPyV screening, plasma JCPyV loads are not a reliable marker". Adenovirus: "Cystitis is a frequent HAdV complication in hematopoietic cell transplantation that is exceptional in BKPyV-nephropathy" ([P154]).

## 5. Management

**5.1 Principle** (Management [P179]; ungraded)
> "Given that there are no effective antivirals to prevent or treat BKPyV replication, the primary therapeutic intervention for sustained BKPyV-DNAemia or biopsy-proven BKPyV-nephropathy in kidney transplant patients aims at reducing maintenance immunosuppression"
Abstract [P103]: "Current studies do not support the usage of leflunomide, cidofovir, quinolones, or IVIGs." (IVIG wording differs from Table 7; see Ambiguity 2.)

**5.2 When to reduce** (Table 7 (image))
- "We recommend reducing maintenance immunosuppression as the primary treatment of sustained BKPyV-DNAemia/-nephropathy in kidney transplant patients without high immunologic risk or concurrent acute rejection (strong, B)".
- "We suggest reducing immunosuppression when BKPyV-DNAemia is between 1000–10 000 copies/mL (or equivalent) on 2 measurements within 2–3 wk (weak, B)".
- "We recommend reducing immunosuppression based on 1 measurement BKPyV-DNAemia >10 000 copies/mL (or equivalent) or if biopsy-proven BKPyV-nephropathy (strong, B)".
- "We recommend reducing immunosuppression for biopsy-proven BKPyV-nephropathy even if plasma BKPyV-DNA load results needed to confirm the diagnosis are still pending (strong, B)".

**5.3 Who decides and how: the guideline's own wording**
- Institutional, not individual: Table 7 (image) "We suggest each transplant center to develop an institutional algorithm and standard operating procedure of how to reduce immunosuppression in patients with BKPyV-DNAemia (weak, D)".
- No agreed method ([P180]): "The optimal strategy to reduce immunosuppression has not been defined; it varies among transplant centers and is often individualized because there are no randomized controlled trials (RCTs) directly comparing different protocols."
- Table 6 first step (image, weak, C): "first confirming that all immunosuppressive drug doses and concentrations are within the institutional target range".
- The guideline names no specific clinician (nephrologist, transplant physician, ED) who performs the reduction. The text refers to "transplant centers" and "the treating" setting only.

**5.4 Table 6 (image): practice guidance for reducing immunosuppression**
- **Strategy 1, antimetabolite first:** "Reduction of the dose of antimetabolite by at least 50%"; if DNAemia "does not decrease by 10-fold at 4 wk or does not clear below lower limit of detection (weak, C)": "Discontinuation of the antimetabolite and tapering of corticosteroid dose to 5–10 mg/d of prednisone or equivalent"; add prednisone 5-10 mg/d "for patients who are not on corticosteroids to avoid CNI monotherapy (weak, C)"; then "a stepwise reduction of the CNI dose (tacrolimus trough target 5 ng/mL; cyclosporine trough target 100 ng/mL; weak, C)".
- **Strategy 2, CNI first:** "Reduction of the dose of CNI by 25%–50% in 1 or 2 steps to target trough concentrations of tacrolimus of 3–5 ng/mL and cyclosporine trough concentrations of 75–125 ng/mL"; if no 10-fold fall at 4 wk: "Reduction of the antimetabolite by 50% and tapering of corticosteroid dose to 5–10 mg/d", then "Discontinuation of the antimetabolite".
- **Lower targets are statement only:** "tacrolimus target trough concentrations of 3 ng/mL and cyclosporine target trough concentrations 75 ng/mL followed by tacrolimus target trough of 1.5 ng/mL; cyclosporine target trough of 50 ng/mL (no recommendation—statement only)".
- **Monitoring** (strong, B): "BKPyV-DNAemia should be monitored every 2–4 wk until clearance"; lowest-immunosuppression patients with DNAemia "below <1000 c/mL": "follow-up of BKPyV-DNAemia and serum creatinine concentration every 3 mo (weak, D)".
- The text ([P190]) says to start "by reviewing and promptly adjusting the doses and trough concentrations of immunosuppressive drugs if found to be in excess of the respective predefined institutional target range."

**5.5 What follows reduction** ([P189]): reduction "led to successful clearance of BKPyV-DNAemia below the LOD in 80% to 100% of cases"; "acute cellular rejection and subsequent de novo DSAs have been reported in around 10% to 30% of the patients during or after clearance of BKPyV-DNAemia in some studies". "recurrent BKPyV-DNAemia has been observed in 10% to 20% of patients in the context of reincreasing immunosuppression" ([P190]).

**5.6 Re-increasing and rejection** (Table 7 (image)): "We suggest to judiciously reincrease maintenance immunosuppression based on the individual immunologic risk after confirmed BKPyV-DNAemia clearance, with appropriate screening for BKPyV-DNAemia (weak, D)". Statement (expert opinion): "most experts apply high-dose steroid therapy followed by resuming close monitoring of renal allograft function and at least monthly monitoring of BKPyV-DNAemia for the next 3 to 6 mo". Also Table 5 (image, weak, D): after increased immunosuppression or antirejection therapy "resuming monthly screening for BKPyV-DNAemia for the next 3 mo". Also Table 7 (weak, D): test persistent-DNAemia patients "for de novo DSA if there is evidence of renal dysfunction".

**5.7 mTOR-inhibitor and belatacept regimens** (Table 7 (image)): "There is insufficient data to evaluate the efficacy of switching to mTOR inhibitors for treating BKPyV-DNAemia or biopsy-proven BKPyV-nephropathy (no recommendation—statement only)". On mTOR+CNI, possible approaches (expert opinion): "first reduce the dose of calcineurin inhibitor followed by a reduction of the dose of mTOR inhibitor if needed". On belatacept, possible approaches (expert opinion): "first reduce or discontinue the antimetabolite"; "increase the interval of belatacept administration to every 6–8 wk"; "switch to a low-level calcineurin-based or mTOR inhibitor-based immunosuppressive regimen".

**5.8 Adjuncts and the guideline's position (Table 7 (image), Adjunctive therapies)**
- IVIG: "We suggest consideration of intravenous immunoglobulin administration as adjuvant therapy in kidney transplant recipients with insufficient response to reduced immunosuppression to facilitate viral clearance (weak, D)"; also to prevent rejection in high-immunological-risk recipients when reduction is necessary (weak, D). Dosing "little evidence regarding which IVIG dosing is most appropriate" ([P199]); the three scenarios listed there are low 0.1-0.3 g/kg every 2-4 wk, high 0.5-2 g/kg 1-3 times per week, or "adjusted dosing as needed for correcting hypogammaglobulinemia".
- Cidofovir: "We recommend to not use cidofovir ... (strong, B)". [P200]: "No RCTs provide evidence that cidofovir is beneficial"; risk "nephrotoxicity and ophthalmologic toxicity".
- Leflunomide: "We recommend to not use leflunomide ... (strong, B)". [P201]: "Leflunomide has a long half-life and has been associated with hepatotoxicity."
- Fluoroquinolones: "We recommend to not use fluoroquinolones to prevent or treat BKPyV-DNAemia or BKPyV-nephropathy in kidney transplant recipients (strong, A)". [P202]: one RCT found ciprofloxacin "did not reduce BKPyV-DNAemia rates but increased the rate of fluoroquinolone-resistant infections."
- Statins: "We recommend to not use statins ... (strong, A)".
- Experimental (Abstract [P103]): antivirals, vaccines, neutralizing antibodies and adoptive T cells "should be enrolled in clinical trials". [P198] "Very limited clinical data suggest benefits of currently available adjuvant therapies".

**5.9 Biopsy during treatment** (Table 4 (image), weak, D): "to not perform an allograft biopsy during the course or resolution of BKPyV-DNAemia/-nephropathy unless rejection or another renal disease is a matter of concern and its detection will alter management".

**5.10 Graft loss and retransplant** (Abstract [P103]): "Kidney retransplantation subsequent to BKPyV-nephropathy is feasible in otherwise eligible recipients if BKPyV-DNAemia is undetectable; routine graft nephrectomy is not recommended."

## 6. Ureteric stenosis and haemorrhagic cystitis
**Not covered.** Neither term, and no ureteral obstruction or hemorrhagic cystitis in kidney recipients, is discussed. The only related statements are: ureteric stents are a risk factor for DNAemia and nephropathy (Table 2 (image), "Low, C"); "Obstructive uropathy as primary renal disease of pediatric recipients" is a pediatric risk factor (Table 2); adenovirus cystitis is "exceptional in BKPyV-nephropathy" ([P154], 4.11).

## 7. Differential from rejection

**7.1 Why it is hard** (Differential Diagnosis ... Rejection [P150]): "A combination of the 2 poses a dilemma because, currently, both conditions cannot be treated at the same time."

**7.2 TCMR cannot be diagnosed on histology in BKPyV-nephropathy** ([P152]): "TCMR confined to the tubulointerstitium (Banff grades IA/B) ... cannot be reliably diagnosed in cases of biopsy-proven BKPyV-nephropathy because no morphological, immunohistochemical, or molecular features distinguish both entities with certainty." "The Banff rules to diagnose TCMR grade IA/B should not be applied to kidney transplant patients with BKPyV-DNAemia or biopsy-proven BKPyV-nephropathy." Table 4 (image, strong, B): "concomitant interstitial TCMR (Banff grade IA/B) is not diagnosed on the basis of inflammation and tubulitis; instead, an explanatory diagnostic comment incorporating interdisciplinary discussion should be used".

**7.3 Vascular rejection and AMR are still diagnosable** ([P152]): "intimal arteritis, as well as chronic allograft arteriopathy seen in both AMR and TCMR, should be reported as possible concomitant rejection." Table 4 (image, strong, C): "antibody-mediated rejection be diagnosed in a patient with detectable BKPyV-DNAemia if Banff diagnostic criteria are met". [P151]: "Except for peritubular capillaritis, the pattern of active AMR does not overlap with the key features of biopsy-proven BKPyV-nephropathy."

**7.4 Creatinine rise: virus-clearing immunity vs rejection** (Figure 2 caption [P182]): "Increase in serum creatinine may arise because of antiviral immune reconstitution or acute rejection, whereby the former may be transient, unlike the latter."

**7.5 Rejection risk after reducing immunosuppression** (Figure 2 caption [P182]): reducing immunosuppression "while increasing the risk of antidonor immunity and allograft rejection". Rejection and DNAemia are linked as a risk factor (Table 2 (image): acute rejection "Low, C").

**7.6 Biopsy findings can mimic** ([P149]): "these biopsy findings cannot be interpreted with confidence as borderline rejection, T cell–mediated rejection (TCMR), or antibody-mediated rejection (AMR) in kidney transplant patients with ongoing high-level BKPyV replication."

**7.7 No rule-in/rule-out molecular test** ([P157]): "no biopsy-based machine learning or gene expression profiling test is currently available to confidently rule in or rule out the clinical diagnosis of probable and presumptive BKPyV-nephropathy."

## 8. Non-kidney organ recipients and combined organs

**8.1 Rare** (Non-kidney SOT Recipients [P177]): "BKPyV-DNAemia and biopsy-proven BKPyV-nephropathy are rarely reported in non-kidney SOTs." Lung: "identified BKPyV-DNAemia in approximately 4% of patients, with biopsy-proven BKPyV-nephropathy in 1%." Pancreas-only: "higher risk of pancreas allograft failure with BKPyV-DNAemia"; "the impact of reduction of immunosuppression on the pancreas allograft in the setting of BKPyV-DNAemia needs further study."

**8.2 Screening** (Table 5 (image)): "For non-kidney solid organ transplant recipients, we recommend to not routinely screen for BKPyV-DNAemia (strong, B)". Combined kidney/other organ (weak, C): "extending screening for BKPyV-DNAemia every 3 mo up to 36 mo posttransplant".

**8.3 Management** (Table 7 (image), weak, D): "For multiorgan transplant recipients, including kidney or non-kidney solid organ transplant recipients with BKPyV-DNAemia or biopsy-proven BKPyV-nephropathy, we suggest a careful reduction of immunosuppression as per above, with close clinical and laboratory monitoring, weighing the risks and benefits of rejection and graft loss". [P197]: "there is not sufficient evidence to guide immunosuppression reduction for treating other multiorgan transplant recipients". Simultaneous pancreas-kidney: "Reduction of immunosuppression has been effective and reported a low risk of pancreas allograft loss."

**8.4 Other organs in the reduction decision** ([P180]): among factors "the presence of other SOTs (eg, pancreas, liver, heart, lung)".

## Not stated (do not invent)

1. Any symptom list, or that BKPyV-nephropathy is asymptomatic or presents only as a rising creatinine (3.7). No fever, pain or urinary symptom description.
2. Ureteric stenosis or obstruction and haemorrhagic cystitis in kidney recipients (Section 6).
3. Who carries out the reduction of immunosuppression (a named clinician or service), and whether an ED or non-transplant clinician should change immunosuppression. The guideline defers to a transplant-centre algorithm.
4. ED-specific guidance: what to send, when to call the transplant team, admission or discharge criteria, or empiric action on a creatinine rise.
5. A creatinine threshold for acting on BKPyV beyond the biopsy-trigger example ">15% from baseline" (3.2) and the 25-50% CNI reduction steps; no eGFR criteria.
6. Specific doses for prednisone beyond "5-10 mg/d", for mycophenolate beyond "at least 50%" reduction or discontinuation, and for IVIG beyond the three scenarios quoted.
7. How to treat acute rejection in BKPyV beyond the expert-opinion statement of high-dose steroid with close monitoring (5.6); no regimen detail.
8. Which immunosuppressive agent to prefer in a patient on a non-standard regimen beyond "possible approaches" (5.7).
9. Urinary BKPyV thresholds for acting on urine alone (the guideline says independent studies are lacking, [P165]); no action threshold for decoy cells alone beyond measuring plasma.
10. Duration of monitoring after clearance ("the optimal duration of monitoring is unknown", [P174]).
11. BKPyV screening or thresholds in liver, heart, lung or pancreas recipients with renal dysfunction beyond Table 5 (declining renal function: test DNAemia, weak, C).
12. Antiviral or other drug treatment of BKPyV outside the listed adjuncts; no dose for cidofovir or leflunomide (recommended against).

## Ambiguities or internal inconsistencies

1. **Interval from high-level viruria to DNAemia:** "approximately 6 wk (range, 1–12 wk)" in text [P165] vs "approximately 2 to 6 wk" in the Figure 2 caption [P182].
2. **IVIG:** Abstract [P103] says "Current studies do not support the usage of leflunomide, cidofovir, quinolones, or IVIGs"; Table 7 (image) says IVIG may be considered (weak, D) and recommends against only cidofovir, leflunomide, fluoroquinolones and statins. The abstract does not mention statins.
3. **Table 6 CNI targets:** Strategy 1 step III says "tacrolimus trough target 5 ng/mL; cyclosporine trough target 100 ng/mL"; Strategy 2 step I says tacrolimus "3–5 ng/mL" and cyclosporine "75–125 ng/mL". Then "no recommendation" statements describe 3 ng/mL and 1.5 ng/mL as further steps.
4. **Order of "Probable/Presumptive" labels in Table 2 footnote a:** DNAemia ">1000 c/mL ... for >2wk (probable) or increasing >10 000 c/mL" while the definition ([P109]-[P110]) states probable as ">1000 c/mL sustained for >2 wk" and presumptive as ">10 000 c/mL"; [P174] uses "Persistent ... 1000 to 10 000 c/mL" for probable. Slightly different phrasing; no numeric conflict.
5. **Strength labels:** [P174] "a threshold BKPyV-DNAemia of >10 000 c/mL ... necessitating prompt reduction" (text) while Table 7 (image) rates reduction on one measurement >10 000 as "strong, B" and on 1000-10 000 on two measurements as "weak, B".
6. **Biopsy on stable function:** Table 4 (image) "weak, D" to "consider" biopsy in high-risk patients with DNAemia and stable function; Abstract says biopsy "not required unless the immunological risk is high" (consistent) but Table 4 adds "high virologic risk", which the abstract omits.
7. **"Possible BKPyV-nephropathy"** is defined (high-level viruria with undetectable DNAemia) but no management is stated for it; Table 5 says plasma testing should follow (strong, B).
8. **"Early" vs "late" intervention:** [P189] "earlier intervention at lower levels of BKPyV-DNAemia required fewer intervention steps and resulted in fewer rejection episodes" yet [P192] "a retrospective study found that >20% reduction in CNI concentration 1 mo after the initial BKPyV-DNAemia was associated with an increased rate of acute rejection".
9. **Table 1 GRADE key and Figures 1-2 bodies** were not read; any recommendation shown only in a figure is not captured.
10. **P# convenience numbering:** counted from the page top in the saved HTML; Tables carry their own `(strong/weak, A-D)` grades which are the authoritative citation form.
