## 3. What the source says bearing on "no immunosuppressant is stopped, held, reduced or converted in the ED without the transplant team"

Nothing in either guideline addresses emergencies, sepsis, or CNI toxicity. Verified by search (withdraw, withhold, hold, discontinu, reduc*, minimi*, sepsis, toxic*, vomit, missed dose, NPO, emergency): no ED statement exists. What does exist:

- G Q14 (rationale): "complete immunosuppression withdrawal **should not be undertaken** except in a closely monitored, highly selected group of LT recipients." Supports the note.
- N Q24 (statement, Strong, Level 5): a transplant professional should be involved in immunosuppression management before and after elective surgery. Rationale: "It is not recommended to hold CNI for the surgery or after the surgery"; "If patients are on high-dose steroids, these should be reduced if possible"; "consider holding mTORi (everolimus or sirolimus) ~4-6 weeks before elective surgery"; transplant providers and pharmacists guide mTORi restart. Elective surgery only.
- N Q23: reduce CNI dose in CKD in the first year, with an adjunct (Strong, Level 1); "minimized but not eliminated" first year (Strong, 1); "minimized but not stopped" beyond 1 year (Strong, 3); delayed CNI with induction after early AKI "may be considered" (Weak, 2). Outpatient strategies.
- N PTLD: "management of PTLD involves a reduction in immunosuppression." N Q12-13: minimisation/substitution for cardiovascular disease and metabolic disease, MPA stopped before pregnancy.
- G chronic AMR text: "Initial treatment ... includes increasing plasma tacrolimus levels"; G rejection: augment immunosuppression, switch cyclosporine to tacrolimus. These are team decisions after biopsy, consistent with the note.
- G CNI "Nephrotoxicity ... is the main indication for discontinuing CNI" (G, mTOR section).

Reading: the note is consistent with the source in direction (every change the source describes is planned and team-directed, and withdrawal is discouraged) but it is not a source statement; it is stronger than the source (blanket, all agents, all emergencies). The source does endorse holding an mTOR inhibitor and reducing high-dose steroids around elective surgery. Safe to keep as an Editor note with the disposition bullet quoted in full (finding 5). No source statement supports "hold in sepsis" or "continue in sepsis"; the page correctly says so.

## 5. Omissions: stated by the source, ED-relevant, absent from the page

1. G Q5: endovascular treatment "can confer some risk of hepatic artery injury and rupture in the very early postoperative period, but it can be the first-line therapy when the patient's condition precludes surgery"; early HAT "usually corrected surgically or endovascularly, with anticoagulation, depending on the patient's condition and the transplant center's practice".
2. G Q15: late TCMR treated the same as acute TCMR; mild acute TCMR "may resolve without additional immunosuppression"; 60-90% respond to high-dose steroids, lower for late TCMR; "Patient adherence to immunosuppression should be reinforced."
3. G Q17 CDR: steroids and anti-thymocyte globulin "not effective"; treat early (<50% duct loss). Chronic AMR: augment immunosuppression (Strong, 5); IVIG/PLEX/anti-CD20 not recommended (Strong, 4).
4. N infection rationale: early post-LT bacterial infection incl. multidrug-resistant (25%) and fungal (40%), mortality 25-67%; fluconazole prophylaxis 2-4 weeks with >=2 risk factors (Strong, 2); azole interactions noted on page but prophylaxis not.
5. N influenza/COVID/RSV: influenza is "more severe", linked to rejection and graft dysfunction; neuraminidase inhibitor treatment "may be effective in hospitalized and immunocompromised patients beyond" 48 h; baloxavir not routinely recommended; RSV supportive only; live vaccines avoided.
6. N CMV: syndrome presentation (fatigue, myalgias, fever, neutropenia, viraemia); typically within 1-2 months of stopping prophylaxis and within 3 months of transplant or rejection treatment; oral valganciclovir preferred, IV ganciclovir if viral load >=100,000 IU/mL, impaired gut absorption, or severe infection especially pneumonia (Strong, 1). (Doses exist; page rule bars doses unless ED-started; the route criteria carry none.)
7. N Q25 (hernia): incisional hernia 5-35% in 2 years; "severe pain and bowel obstruction when abdominal contents become incarcerated and/or strangulated"; mTOR inhibitor is a risk factor. A real acute-abdomen presentation for this population.
8. N: herbal/dietary supplements "may affect CNI metabolism or lead to drug-induced liver injury"; statin-CNI interaction "increased risk of muscle toxicity" (hydrophilic statins preferred); amlodipine/felodipine minimal CNI interaction (finding 11).
9. G Q1: early allograft dysfunction definition (AST/ALT >2000 within 7 days, bilirubin >=10 or INR >=1.6 on day 7) and "no routine pharmacological prevention" (only relevant to early return).
10. G Table 4: cyclosporine, everolimus and sirolimus trough ranges; MMF exposure reduced by PPIs/antacids; corticosteroid levels raised by nirmatrelvir-ritonavir; drug names and side-effect lists (not doses).
