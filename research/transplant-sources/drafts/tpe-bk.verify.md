## 2. Values read from source / image

- Definitions (Introduction, text; Table 2 footnote a repeats them): possible = urine DNA >10 million c/mL or decoy cells or virions by EM with undetectable plasma DNA; probable = plasma >1000 c/mL (or equivalent) sustained >2 wk; presumptive = plasma >10 000 c/mL (or equivalent); biopsy-proven = compatible cytopathic effects + immunohistochemistry + a test identifying BK vs JC. PAGE MATCHES (page omits "virions by EM" in "possible", immaterial).
- Screening schedule (Table 5; Figure 1 image): monthly until month 9, then every 3 months until 2 years (strong, B). Children: 3 years. Combined kidney/other organs incl. pancreas: every 3 months to 36 months (weak, C). Non-kidney: no routine screening (strong, B); test if renal function declines unexplained (weak, C). PAGE MATCHES for adults and non-kidney; omits the combined-organ extension.
- Confirmatory testing 1000-10 000 c/mL within 2-3 wk (weak, B); monitor every 2-4 wk (weak, D; Table 6 "recommend ... every 2-4 wk until clearance (strong, B)"). PAGE gives the first; omits monitoring interval.
- Creatinine rise: Pathology text "The general indications for a renal allograft biopsy remain in the case of altered renal function or signs of pathology (eg, a significant rise in serum creatinine of >15% from baseline, hematuria, or proteinuria)". PAGE MATCHES ("for example", "more than 15%"). Table 4 itself gives no number ("rise in serum creatinine, proteinuria, hematuria; strong, A").
- Epidemiology (Virologic Methods): high-level viruria ~20-40%, DNAemia 10-20%, biopsy-proven 8% (range 1-15%) in 12 mo; "another 10% to 20% for the next 24 mo and another 1% to 10% for the next 3 to 5 y". PAGE MATCHES.
- Biopsy misses virus in "10% to 30% of the cases with BKPyV-DNAemia during early onset or when biopsy-proven ... is resolving" (Pathology). MATCHES. ACR and de novo DSA "around 10% to 30%" (Management). MATCHES. Viruria-to-DNAemia lag "approximately 6 wk (range, 1-12 wk)" (text) vs Figure 2 caption "approximately 2 to 6 wk": the page shows both correctly.
- IVIG: abstract "Current studies do not support the usage of leflunomide, cidofovir, quinolones, or IVIGs"; Table 7 "We suggest consideration of intravenous immunoglobulin administration as adjuvant therapy ... insufficient response to reduced immunosuppression (weak, D)". Page shows the inconsistency correctly.
- Fluoroquinolone/cidofovir/leflunomide/statin grades (strong A / strong B / strong B / strong A): PAGE MATCHES.

## 3. Links

`adult-uti` / `uti-start` exists ("Adult UTI Assessment"). `adult-uti.ts` lists "Renal transplant" under ALWAYS complicated and "Renal transplant recipient" under escalate-to-ID; its default complicated-UTI treatment is "FQ or culture-guided" (`uti-fluoroquinolones`), outpatient pyelonephritis first line is a fluoroquinolone, and discharge advice is "FQ (ciprofloxacin or levofloxacin) x 7 days, OR TMP-SMX". The BK source's fluoroquinolone statement is limited to preventing or treating BK DNAemia/nephropathy (strong, A); it is silent on UTI treatment, so there is no direct conflict, but the consult has no BK or kidney-transplant text and the BK page's Editor note sends a transplant recipient to a consult whose default is a fluoroquinolone with no cross-reference. Report only; no judgement on UTI choice. The consult does not mention BK anywhere.
