## 2. Full account of the four statements

### (a) Atropine, bradycardia, isoproterenol, theophylline, pacing
- e96-e97 (Topic 4 "Management of Intercurrent Surgery", anaesthesia recommendation; 2010 column text, 2023 column "Continuing approval without change", Class I, C printed e97): "Anesthesia can be safely induced if there is clear understanding that the HT is denervated. The resting heart rate is usually higher in HT recipients. Although most allografts have a resting heart rate of approximately 90 beats/min, some have resting sinus rates as high as 130 beats/min, which do not require treatment. It must be remembered that a relative, symptomatic, bradycardia that requires treatment will not respond to atropine. Isoproterenol infusion and pacing are the usual modes of management of HT bradyarrhythmias. ... the denervated heart is highly sensitive to adenosine ... Amiodarone is recommended as the drug of choice for atrial tachyarrhythmias in HT recipients."
- e98 Table 14 (special anaesthetic considerations, ungraded): "The indirect acting anticholinergics (atropine, glycopyrrolate) and anticholinesterases (neostigmine, edrophonium) have no effect on the heart rate of the cardiac allograft, and the safety of neuromuscular reversal has been demonstrated in a large-scale study with no instances of severe bradycardia or cardiac arrest." (the page leaves out the second clause.)
- e19 (Topic 4 Early Postoperative Care): 2023 column, Class I, B: "Pharmacologic chronotropic agents, including isoproterenol, theophylline, terbutaline, and albuterol can be used in the perioperative setting to increase heart rate." (2010 column listed isoproterenol and theophylline.) "After HT, temporary pacing should be initiated in the setting of relative bradycardia to maintain heart rates of > 90 beats/min" (2010 col, continuing, I, B). Pacing-guideline paragraph (I, C): "Recommendations for permanent pacing exist for inappropriate chronotropic response 3 weeks after HT." e15 (perioperative narrative): "pacemaker implant is indicated if symptomatic bradycardia persists after 3 weeks post-transplant." e17: isoproterenol 1 to 10 mcg/min is printed only as postoperative inotropic support (I, C). The guideline gives no ED dose for isoproterenol or theophylline.
- e79 (late, ungraded) and e80 (Topic 8, 2023, new): "late sinus node dysfunction and AV block are often not related to acute rejection"; "conventional indications for permanent pacemakers should be applied"; permanent pacing regardless of symptoms for Mobitz II, high-grade or third-degree AV block (I, B); also permanent pacing reasonable for marked first-degree or Mobitz I block with symptoms attributable to it (IIa, C) and dual-chamber over single-chamber ventricular pacing (I, A), both omitted by the page.
- Fair? The body is fair and discloses that the atropine, isoproterenol and pacing statements are surgery-section and early-postoperative statements. Unfair places: critical action 2, the entry summary and the brady summary drop that caveat (finding 6); "Early after transplant only" (finding 13). No statement in the guideline says atropine is unsafe, only that it "will not respond".

### (b) Adenosine: every statement (only four places in the document; no paediatric adenosine dose exists in it)
1. e15 (narrative, "Perioperative management of cardiac arrhythmias", ungraded): "Use of adenosine post-transplant was previously a relative contraindication due to presumed risk of prolonged AV block in the denervated heart. However, a recent study suggests minimal risk when low initial doses are used (25 mcg/kg; 1.5 mg if >= 60 kg) and therapy is gradually increased." Population: adult (>=60 kg), recipients generally, perioperative section.
2. e20 (Topic 4 Early Postoperative Care, 2023 New Recommendation, Class IIb, C): "Adenosine use may be considered in HT recipients with supraventricular tachyarrhythmias if they are closely monitored and low doses are administered (25 mcg/kg: 1.5 mg if >= 60 kg)." Adult by the 60 kg cut-off; table is the early postoperative one.
3. e79 (Topic 8 Arrhythmias narrative, ungraded): "Adenosine may be considered to terminate SVT in heart transplant recipients, but should only be administered with caution at low doses (e.g., 3 mg in adults) due to increased risk of severe bradycardia or asystole (refer to Task Force 1 for guideline recommendations for adenosine use in heart transplant recipients)." Explicitly adult; the "refer to Task Force 1" is the e20 recommendation. "e.g." makes 3 mg an example, not a contradiction of e20; the page is right to show both and choose neither.
4. e96-e97 (anaesthesia, 2010 col, continuing, Class I, C): "the denervated heart is highly sensitive to adenosine, and the use of standard doses to treat atrial tachyarrhythmias may result in prolonged asystole."
Page represents these correctly except for context labelling (finding 1), the dropped "gradually increased" and "severe bradycardia or asystole" (finding 14), and "low dose only". The proposed drug-store row ("25 mcg/kg: 1.5 mg if >= 60 kg", closely monitored, IIb C) matches e20; it must also carry the 3 mg locator (e79) and "gradually increased" (e15), and the existing card needs reconciling (section 3).

### (c) Rejection with haemodynamic compromise: first-line therapy and the "biopsy/DSA before treatment" statements
- First-line (e52, 2010 col, 2023 "Continuing approval without change", each Class I, C): "High-dose IV CS should be first-line therapy for symptomatic acute cellular rejection irrespective of ISHLT EMB grade (1R, 2R, or 3R)." "Cytolytic immunosuppressive therapy with anti-thymocyte antibodies should be administered in addition to IV CS if hemodynamic compromise is present, and especially if there is no clinical improvement within 12 to 24 hours of IV CS administration." "IV inotropes and vasopressors should be used as necessary ... until recovery of heart allograft function occurs." "In a patient with low-grade acute cellular rejection and hemodynamic compromise, the possibility of AMR and/or CAV should also be entertained" (2023 wording, I, C).
- Other compromise statements (page omits): e55 narrative, AMR outside the immediate transplant period: "Initial therapy, especially when hemodynamic alterations are present may require inotropic or mechanical support and medical therapy and should include high-dose IV CS (methylprednisolone, 500-1000 mg daily ...)... Cytolytic therapy ... can be used as escalation"; e57 "IV inotropic agents and vasopressors and at times MCS may be required"; e58 mixed rejection: "When hemodynamic compromise is present, aggressive therapy with high-dose IV CS, and cytolytic therapy is appropriate." e59 AMR recommendation (IIa, C): "high-dose IV CS; cytolytic immunosuppressive therapy" among treatments.
- Biopsy/DSA before treatment: e61 (Topic 6 late acute rejection, 2023 New Recommendation, IIa, C): "AMR is more commonly identified in late acute rejection compared to acute cellular rejection and should be considered in the differential diagnosis of HT recipients presenting with signs or symptoms of heart allograft dysfunction. EMB with ISHLT immunopathologic evaluation, as well as measurement of circulating HLA donor-specific-antibodies should be obtained before initiating treatment." e60 narrative: "...evaluation for AMR with EMB and immunostaining ... before steroid treatment and should be tested for the presence of circulating HLA donor-specific antibodies." e59 (IIa, C): "When AMR is suspected recipient serum should be evaluated for ... anti-donor (HLA) antibodies. Serum samples should be drawn before initiation of therapy to avoid assay interference from therapeutic agents." e52 first line also says EMB "as early as possible" (e51, I, C).
- Real conflict? It is a sequencing tension, not a direct contradiction. e52 applies once the diagnosis is symptomatic acute CELLULAR rejection; e61/e60 apply to a late (after year one) recipient with signs of graft dysfunction whose cause is not yet separated (AMR, ACR, CAV). The stated reason for early sampling (e59) is assay interference, which a blood draw before the first dose avoids. The guideline never says what to do in an unstable late patient before biopsy; the page's sentence "does not say who gives the first dose" is true. The page's "Source conflict" label and critical action 6 ("conflicts with first-line steroid") go further than the text (finding 5). Fair otherwise: classes and locators are right.

### (d) Amiodarone, diltiazem, verapamil, CNI/mTOR levels
- e15 (perioperative narrative, ungraded): "Beta blockers, calcium antagonists, adenosine and amiodarone can be used safely after HT ... Amiodarone and calcium antagonists can increase calcineurin inhibitor (CNI) levels. Therefore, CNI dose adjustment and level monitoring are required."
- e19-e20 (Early Postoperative table; 2010 col, 2023 "Continuing approval without change"): "The Class III anti-arrhythmics sotalol and amiodarone can be safely used in HT recipients and have minimal interaction with immunosuppressive agents." (IIa, C). "Non-dihydropyridine CCBs and b-blockers may be used in HT recipients for rate control." (IIa, B).
- e80 (Topic 8, 2023, new): "Non-dihydropyridine calcium channel blockers and beta-blockers may be used in HT recipients with preserved ejection fraction for ventricular rate control of AF." (IIa, B). "The Class III antiarrhythmics sotalol and amiodarone can be safely used in HT recipients for rhythm control, but close monitoring of immunosuppressive agents is recommended." (IIa, C).
- e96-e97 (anaesthesia, I, C): amiodarone "recommended as the drug of choice for atrial tachyarrhythmias in HT recipients."
- e44 Table 5 "Drugs That Affect the Levels of Tacrolimus, Cyclosporine, Sirolimus, or Everolimus" read in full. Increase: antifungals (clotrimazole, fluconazole, isavuconazole, itraconazole, ketoconazole, posaconazole, voriconazole); clarithromycin, erythromycin, metronidazole and tinidazole; antiretrovirals (protease inhibitors general, cobicistat, darunavir, fosamprenavir, indinavir, nelfinavir, ritonavir, saquinavir, tipranavir; letermovir); HCV direct-acting antivirals (daclatasvir, glecaprevir-pibrentasvir, grazoprevir-elbasvir, ledipasvir-sofosbuvir, velpatasvir-sofosbuvir, voxilaprevir-velpatasvir-sofosbuvir); cardiovascular: amiodarone, diltiazem, verapamil; others: cimetidine, fluvoxamine, glipizide, glyburide, imatinib, nefazodone, rilonacept, theophylline, turmeric; bitter orange, grapefruit. Decrease: carbamazepine, fosphenytoin, phenobarbital, phenytoin; caspofungin, nafcillin, rifabutin, rifampin, rifapentine; efavirenz, etravirine, nevirapine; tocilizumab; bosentan; aprepitant, deferasirox, modafinil, St. John's wort, thalidomide, ticlopidine. The rule (e40, ungraded): "if an interacting agent (Table 5) is added or withdrawn ... close monitoring of immunosuppressant drug levels and dose adjustments are made."
- Fair? Yes on the conflict (minimal interaction e19-20 vs "required" e15 vs Table 5 vs "close monitoring is recommended" e80); neither side chosen; locators right. The page's list of what Table 5 raises and lowers has no error; it omits glipizide, glyburide, rilonacept (raise) and aprepitant, deferasirox, modafinil, thalidomide, ticlopidine (lower). The page says the CCB recommendations "do not mention" the CNI effect: true of e20 and e80. The 2010 "minimal interaction" sentence sits in the Early Postoperative table and e15 in the perioperative section; the page cites it without that context (minor). Note Table 5 lists only diltiazem and verapamil; dihydropyridines are not in it.
